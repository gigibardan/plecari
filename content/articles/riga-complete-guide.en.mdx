There's an unspoken prejudice about Baltic capitals: that they're small, remote, and there isn't enough to fill more than a day. Riga dismantles it completely.

Latvia's capital has an architectural identity unlike anything else in Europe. Not because it has one spectacular monument or a famous square — but because the entire urban fabric of its centre is a work of art. Over 800 Art Nouveau buildings packed into a few boulevards, their façades adorned with masks, mythological creatures, floral garlands and sculpted balconies. It is the world's densest Art Nouveau district, full stop.

Add to this a flawlessly preserved medieval Old Town inscribed on UNESCO's list, a central market installed in five enormous former zeppelin hangars, a black local liqueur made from 24 secret ingredients, and a café culture of lattes and honey cake at prices that feel unreal compared to any other European capital.

Riga is one of Europe's best value city breaks. Here's everything you need to know.

## Riga at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Latvia, EU member since 2004, Schengen Area and Eurozone (since 2014).

**Population:** approximately 600,000 — the largest city in the Baltic states.

**Currency:** Euro (EUR) — no currency exchange needed.

**Language:** Latvian is the official language. English is well understood in the tourist area. Russian is common — Latvia has a large Russian-speaking community (around 25% of the population).

**UNESCO:** The Old Town (Vecrīga) and the Art Nouveau district have been on the UNESCO list since 1997.

**Direct flights from Bucharest:** Wizz Air and Ryanair, OTP→RIX, ~2h15, from 40 EUR.

**Average budget:** 60–80 EUR/day (mid-range), well below the average for Western European capitals.

## How to Get from Romania to Riga

### Direct Flight (Recommended)

Wizz Air and Ryanair operate direct flights Bucharest (OTP) → Riga (RIX) several times a week. Duration: 2h10–2h20.

**Indicative prices 2026:**
- Early booking (6–8 weeks ahead): 40–60 EUR return
- Mid-range (2–4 weeks ahead): 70–100 EUR return
- Last minute or peak season (July–August): 120–180 EUR return

**Airport → city centre transfer:**
- **Bus no. 22** → Abrenes iela stop (near Central Station): 30 minutes, 1.50 EUR — cheapest option
- **Bolt/Uber**: 12–18 EUR, 20–25 minutes — recommended at night or with heavy luggage
- **Unlicensed taxis at the airport**: avoid — they charge 2–3 times the normal rate

## What to See in Riga

### The Old Town (Vecrīga) — Riga's Medieval Heart

The Old Town is the mandatory starting point. UNESCO inscribed it in 1997, and the reason is clear: it's one of the best-preserved medieval centres in north-eastern Europe.

**The House of the Blackheads (Melngalvju nams)** is Riga's landmark building — a flamboyant Gothic façade with Renaissance ornamentation from 1334, faithfully reconstructed after wartime destruction. Entry inside: 7 EUR, but the exterior from Town Hall Square is free and spectacular.

**The Three Brothers (Trīs brāļi)** — three medieval houses joined together on Mazā Pils street, built between the 15th and 17th centuries. They represent different architectural styles from the medieval period and are among the most photographed buildings in Riga. Free exterior access.

**The Swedish Gate (Zviedru vārti)** — the only medieval gateway remaining from the city's old fortifications, dating from 1698 during the Swedish occupation. According to legend, a girl was walled alive inside it after being caught in a relationship with a Swedish soldier. Free.

**Riga Cathedral (Rīgas Doms)** — the largest cathedral in the Baltic states, built in 1211. Famous for its organ with 6,768 pipes, one of the largest in Europe. Organ concerts are held regularly — check the schedule on site (tickets 10–15 EUR).

**St. Peter's Church Tower** — Riga's classic panoramic viewpoint. The lift takes you to 72 metres, where you can see the Old Town rooftops, the Daugava River and the Art Nouveau district. Entry: 9 EUR adults.

## Walking Tour of the Old Town — The Best Introduction to Riga

### The Art Nouveau District — The World's Densest Collection

If the Old Town is Riga's medieval soul, the Art Nouveau district is its crown. A 10–15 minute walk from Vecrīga, on Elizabetes boulevard and Alberta street, you enter the area with the highest concentration of Art Nouveau architecture of any European city — over 800 buildings.

**Why so many?** Around 1900, Riga was one of the fastest-growing industrial cities in the Russian Empire. German-Baltic merchant families competed to build prestige apartment blocks in the most fashionable style of the era. The result was a construction boom precisely during the peak decade of European Art Nouveau. The Soviet period then built on the outskirts rather than demolishing the centre — leaving the pre-war fabric unusually intact.

**Alberta iela (Alberta Street)** is the district's masterpiece — eight buildings designed by Mikhail Eisenstein (father of director Sergei Eisenstein) between 1901 and 1906. Each façade is different: masks with open mouths, atlantes supporting balconies, sphinxes and mythological creatures. Access is completely free — it's a public street, not a museum.

**The Art Nouveau Museum (Alberta iela 12)** — an apartment reconstructed exactly as it looked in 1903, with original furniture, wallpaper, lighting and everyday objects. 45–60 minutes to visit, entry 6 EUR. Essential for understanding how people lived in these spectacular buildings.

**Elizabetes iela** — the parallel boulevard with equally impressive but less photographed buildings. Walk both streets.

### Central Market (Centrāltirgus) — Inside Zeppelin Hangars

Riga's Central Market is a uniquely European place. Housed in five enormous metal hangars originally built for German airships (zeppelins) in the First World War and converted into a market in 1930, the Centrāltirgus is Europe's largest covered market and a UNESCO site in its own right.

Each hangar has its speciality: dairy and bread, meat, fish, fruit and vegetables, textiles and miscellaneous goods. Go to eat — prices are 30–40% lower than restaurants, and you have the chance to taste genuinely local products: black rye bread, Latvian cheeses, Baltic smoked meats, pickles and wild berries.

**Tip:** the fish and meat hangars are the most visually spectacular. Baltic fish — smoked sprats, marinated herring — is a local speciality at 2–4 EUR for a generous portion.

**Hours:** Monday–Saturday 7:00–18:00, Sunday 7:00–16:00. Most lively on Sunday mornings.

### Freedom Monument (Brīvības piemineklis)

Riga's main monument — a 42-metre column topped by a female figure holding three stars representing Latvia's three historic regions. Built in 1935, it survived Soviet occupation (the USSR didn't demolish it, unlike other national monuments in occupied countries). Free, visible from outside. The changing of the guard takes place daily at fixed hours.

### The Museum of Occupation (Latvijas Okupācijas muzejs)

One of the most important — and most unsettling — museums in Eastern Europe. It documents Latvia's two successive occupations: Soviet (1940–1941 and 1944–1991) and Nazi (1941–1944). Entry is free, though a donation is strongly encouraged.

The exhibition is extremely well organised, with personal testimonies, photographs, original documents and objects from the Siberian deportation period. Allow at least 1h30 — you'll leave with a completely different perspective on 20th-century history in this part of Europe.

### Miera iela Quarter — Bohemian Riga

A 15-minute walk from the Old Town, Miera iela (Peace Street) is the favourite local neighbourhood — independent cafés, artisan bakeries, bookshops, art galleries and unpretentious restaurants without tourist markup. Prices are 20–30% lower than in Vecrīga.

Stop for a coffee and a slice of honey cake (medus torte) — Latvia's national dessert, a multi-layered cake of honey pastry and cream, 3–5 EUR a slice.

## Food and Drink — A Practical Guide

### What to Eat

**Black rye bread (rupjmaize)** — the base of the Latvian diet, dense, acidic, extremely aromatic. Eaten with butter and smoked fish. Fresh at the Central Market for 1–2 EUR.

**Smoked sprats (Rīgas šprotes)** — Baltic smoked sprats in tin are a locally recognised European speciality. Fresh at the market: 3–5 EUR per portion.

**Pea soup with smoked meat (zirņu zupa)** — traditional Latvian soup, served in a bread bowl at some restaurants. 5–8 EUR.

**Honey cake (medus torte)** — the national dessert. Found in any café, 3–5 EUR a slice.

**Pancakes with sour cream and wild berry jam** — classic Latvian breakfast, 4–7 EUR.

### What to Drink

**Riga Black Balsam** — the black herbal liqueur with 24 ingredients, 45% ABV. Best drunk in coffee (Balsam Coffee) or with blackcurrant juice. A 0.5-litre bottle: 8–12 EUR at a supermarket.

**Local beer** — Aldaris and Valmiermuiža are the main brands. A pint at a bar: 2.50–4 EUR, well below Western European prices.

**Coffee** — coffee culture is serious in Riga. Quality espresso at 2–3 EUR, cappuccino 3–4 EUR.

### Recommended Restaurants

**Budget (8–15 EUR/person):**
- Central Market — maximum authenticity, minimum prices
- **Lido** — Latvian self-service chain, large portions of traditional food, 6–10 EUR per full meal
- Cafés on Miera iela

**Mid-range (15–30 EUR/person):**
- **Folkklubs Ala Pagrabs** — pub-restaurant in a medieval cellar, traditional Latvian food, live folk music at weekends
- **Restaurācija Rozengrāls** — medieval restaurant in a 13th-century vault, unique experience (reservation recommended)

**Watch out for the "bread and water" charge:** some restaurants in Vecrīga automatically bring a bread basket and water you didn't order and add them to the bill (2–4 EUR). If you don't want them, send them back immediately.

## Recommended Itinerary — 3 Days in Riga

### Day 1 — Old Town and the Daugava River

- **Morning:** walking tour of Vecrīga — House of the Blackheads, Three Brothers, Swedish Gate
- **Lunch:** Folkklubs Ala Pagrabs or a café in Town Hall Square
- **Afternoon:** St. Peter's Church Tower (panoramic view), Riga Cathedral, walk along the Daugava
- **Evening:** Black Balsam tasting, dinner in Vecrīga

- **Early morning:** Central Market (most lively in the morning) — breakfast with local products

- **Afternoon:** Museum of Occupation (2h), Freedom Monument, city park

## Practical Tips

**The Old Town bar scam is real and persistent.** If someone (usually a young woman or a "friendly" group) approaches you on the street and invites you to a bar for a drink, politely decline. The final bill can run to hundreds of euros and there are reports of intimidation at the point of payment. It's the most widely reported tourist scam in Riga.

**The Bolt app is the safest transport option.** Unlicensed taxis at the airport or outside hotels can charge 3–4 times the normal rate.

**The Rīgas Satiksme card** for public transport — buy it at machines (2 EUR for the card + 1.50 EUR per journey). Trams and buses are clean, punctual and cover the centre well.

**Don't pay for the Art Nouveau Museum before seeing Alberta iela** — the street is public and free. The museum is worth visiting, but you can photograph the façades without any charge.

**Baltic amber** is Riga's classic souvenir. Best value: the Central Market or shops on Miera iela, not the Vecrīga stores where prices are 40–60% higher.
