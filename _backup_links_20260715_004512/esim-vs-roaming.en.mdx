Figuring out the cheapest way to stay connected abroad is one of those trip-planning tasks that feels more complicated than it should be. The short version: it depends entirely on whether you're traveling within or outside the EU. Here's the clear breakdown.

## Inside the EU: roaming is already free

Since 2017, EU regulations guarantee "roam like at home" — meaning you can use your existing mobile plan in all 27 EU member states plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway without paying anything extra. If you have 20 GB at home, you have 20 GB in Greece, Spain, or Portugal too.

Two important exceptions that catch people off guard:

**Switzerland and the United Kingdom** are NOT covered by EU roaming rules. The UK left the EU in 2020, and Switzerland was never part of it. Roaming in either country uses your carrier's non-EU rates, which can be steep.

Some plans also have a **fair use limit** for roaming that's lower than your domestic allowance. Check with your carrier before you travel — but for most standard plans, the full allowance applies.

**Bottom line for EU travel:** most people don't need an eSIM. You already have data. The exception is if your plan has very little data or you'll exceed the fair-use roaming cap.

## Outside the EU: this is where eSIM saves real money

Turkey, Egypt, Dubai, the US, Thailand, the UK, Japan — anywhere outside the EU, carrier roaming charges can escalate fast. This is where a prepaid eSIM completely changes the math.

| Option | Typical cost | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carrier roaming (outside EU) | €10–15 per GB | Very short trips, minimal data use |
| Prepaid eSIM | €5–15 for 3–5 GB | Any trip longer than 2–3 days |
| Local physical SIM | Cheapest per GB | Long stays, if you have time to buy one |

A prepaid eSIM for Turkey (5 GB, 7 days) typically costs $8–15 — several times less than the equivalent amount of carrier roaming data. For the US or Japan, the savings are even more pronounced.

## How eSIM works

An eSIM is a digital SIM card already built into your phone. You buy a plan from an app or website, scan a QR code, and your phone connects to a local network at your destination. No physical card to swap, no queuing at airport kiosks.

The key advantage is **dual SIM capability**. Your physical SIM card stays active with your regular number — you can still receive calls, texts, and bank verification codes — while the eSIM handles your mobile data on a cheap local network. You get the best of both: your number stays reachable, and your data costs drop by 60–80%.

## Setting up an eSIM: step by step

The process is simpler than most people expect:

**Before your trip** (ideally 1–2 days before departure):

Check that your phone supports eSIM and is carrier-unlocked. Go to Settings → Network/SIM — if you see an option to add an eSIM or digital SIM, you're good.

Buy a plan from a provider like Airalo, Holafly, or Nomad. Pick your destination country or region, choose a data amount and duration, and complete the purchase.

You'll receive a QR code. Scan it in your phone's eSIM settings (do this on Wi-Fi at home). The profile installs but doesn't activate yet.

**At your destination:** the eSIM activates automatically when your phone detects the local network. Turn on data roaming for the eSIM line specifically, and you're connected — usually within seconds of landing.

## Which option for which destination

| Destination | Recommendation | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EU countries | Use your regular plan | Free roaming, no setup needed |
| UK | eSIM | Not covered by EU roaming since Brexit |
| Switzerland | eSIM | Not in the EU; roaming is expensive |
| Turkey | eSIM | Carrier roaming costs 5–10× more |
| US / Canada | eSIM | Roaming rates are very high |
| Thailand / Southeast Asia | eSIM or local SIM | eSIM is easiest; local SIM is cheapest for long stays |
| Egypt / North Africa | eSIM | Much cheaper than roaming |
| Japan / South Korea | eSIM | Excellent eSIM coverage, expensive roaming |

## What to check before buying

A few things that trip people up:

**Phone compatibility.** Not every phone supports eSIM. Generally: iPhone XS (2018) and later, Samsung Galaxy S20 (2020) and later, Google Pixel 3 and later. Your phone also needs to be network-unlocked — if you bought it tied to a specific carrier, check whether it's been unlocked.

**Country coverage.** Make sure the plan covers your exact destination. Multi-country plans (like "Europe" or "Southeast Asia" bundles) can be convenient for multi-stop trips but sometimes exclude smaller countries.

**Validity period.** Plans range from 7 to 30 days. If your trip is 10 days, a 7-day plan won't cut it — buy the 15 or 30-day option.

**Hotspot/tethering.** Not all eSIM plans allow you to share your connection with other devices. If you need to tether a laptop, check the plan details before purchasing.
