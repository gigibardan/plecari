"eSIM or roaming?" is one of the first questions before any international trip. The short answer: within the EU, roaming is free and you don't need anything extra. Outside the EU, a prepaid eSIM costs 50-200 times less than your carrier's roaming rates.

Below you'll find the complete comparison with real prices from August 2026, for every popular destination, plus everything you need to know to choose, install, and use an eSIM without hassle.

## Inside the EU: roaming is already free — no eSIM needed

Since 2017, the EU's "Roam Like at Home" (RLAH) regulation gives you the right to use your domestic mobile plan across all 27 EU member states plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway at no additional cost. If you have 50 GB at home, you have 50 GB in Greece, Spain, or Italy too.

The regulation has been extended through **June 30, 2032**, so there's no risk of it disappearing anytime soon.

### Fair Use Policy — the data cap you should know about

There's a maximum amount of data you can use while roaming in the EU, but for most plans it's generous. The formula:

**Fair-use cap (GB) = (domestic plan price in EUR / EUR 1.00) x 2**

For a EUR 10/month plan, the roaming cap is 20 GB. For EUR 25/month, it's 50 GB. Since January 2025, the wholesale rate dropped to EUR 1.00/GB (from EUR 1.30), meaning higher fair-use caps for travelers.

| Plan type | Monthly cost | Estimated roaming cap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget SIM-only | EUR 5-8 | ~12 GB |
| Mid-range plan | EUR 15-20 | ~34 GB |
| Premium / unlimited | EUR 30-45 | Effectively unlimited |
| Prepaid top-up | EUR 10 (one-time) | ~20 GB |

If you exceed the cap, your carrier charges a maximum of EUR 2.00/GB — still reasonable. You'll receive notifications at 80% and 100% of your allowance.

### Countries NOT covered by free EU roaming

This is where confusion costs real money:

- **Switzerland** — surrounded by the EU but NOT in it and NOT in the EEA. Roaming here uses non-EU rates
- **United Kingdom** — since Brexit (2020), the UK is no longer part of RLAH. Charged as a non-EU destination
- **Turkey** — one of the most popular vacation destinations from Europe, but non-EU with expensive roaming
- **Albania, Serbia, Montenegro, Bosnia, Kosovo, North Macedonia** — popular Balkan destinations, ALL non-EU
- **Egypt, Tunisia, Morocco** — non-EU
- **United Arab Emirates (Dubai), USA, Thailand, Japan** — non-EU

**Simple rule:** if the country isn't in the EU or EEA (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway), roaming is NOT free.

## Outside the EU: what carrier roaming actually costs

The numbers are shocking. European carriers charge non-EU roaming at rates that seem from another era:

### Typical European carrier roaming rates (non-EU)

| Zone | Typical countries | Data rate | Cost per GB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zone 2 (closer non-EU) | Turkey, USA, Canada, UK | EUR 5-15 per MB | EUR 5,000-15,000 |
| Zone 3 (rest of world) | UAE, Asia, Africa | EUR 8-20 per MB | EUR 8,000-20,000 |

Read that again: **a single GB in carrier roaming can cost EUR 5,000-15,000.** If your phone downloads a 500 MB app update in the background, that's a EUR 2,500-7,500 bill. A 5 GB eSIM for the same destination costs USD 12-18.

### Why this happens

European carriers don't have favorable wholesale agreements with networks outside the EU. The rates reflect real interconnection costs plus significant margins. It's a business model from the 2000s that hasn't adapted to the smartphone era — which is exactly why eSIMs have exploded in popularity.

## What is an eSIM and how it works

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a digital SIM card already built into your phone. It's not a physical object you insert — it's a software profile you download.

### How it works, step by step

1. Buy a data plan online (website or app) before your trip
2. Receive a QR code via email or in the app
3. Scan the QR code in your phone's settings — the eSIM profile installs
4. Upon landing, your phone automatically connects to the local partner network
5. You have internet at local rates, up to 200 times cheaper than roaming

### The key advantage: Dual SIM

The vast majority of modern phones support two SIMs simultaneously — physical SIM + eSIM. This means:

- **Your physical home SIM** stays active for calls, texts, and bank verification codes (OTPs)
- **The eSIM** handles internet exclusively, at local rates
- **Both work simultaneously** — no need to remove one card to insert another

Setup takes 2-3 minutes in your phone's settings. Once installed, the eSIM activates automatically when you arrive at your destination.

## How to install an eSIM — step-by-step guide

### Before your trip (1-2 days before departure)

#### 1. Check phone compatibility

Go to **Settings > Network / SIM / Mobile Data**. If you see an option to "Add eSIM" or "Add data plan," your phone is compatible.

**eSIM-compatible phones (2026):**

| Brand | Compatible models |
| --- | --- |
| **Apple iPhone** | XS, XR, and all newer models (including SE 2, SE 3, 11-17, iPhone Air) |
| **Samsung Galaxy** | S20 and newer, Z Flip/Fold all generations, A54, A55 |
| **Google Pixel** | 3 and all newer models (including Pixel 9 Pro Fold, Pixel 10) |
| **Xiaomi** | 13, 14, 14T, 15, 15T, 17, 17T Pro |
| **OnePlus** | 11, 12, 13, 13R, 15 |
| **Motorola** | Razr (2019+), Edge 50+, Edge 70 |
| **Sony** | Xperia 10 IV+, Xperia 1 V+, Xperia 5 V |
| **Huawei** | P40 Pro, Mate 40 Pro |
| **Nothing** | Phone 1, Phone 2, Phone 2a |

**Essential requirement:** your phone must be **carrier-unlocked**. If you bought it on a carrier plan, check whether it's been unlocked. Phones purchased outright or from independent retailers are typically unlocked.

#### 2. Buy the eSIM plan

Go to the website or app of your chosen provider (Airalo, Saily, Yesim):

1. Select your destination country or region
2. Choose a plan (GB + duration)
3. Pay with card (Visa, Mastercard, PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay)
4. Receive the QR code via email and in the app

#### 3. Install the eSIM profile

**On iPhone:**
- Settings > Mobile Data > Add eSIM > Scan QR Code
- Or: Settings > Mobile Data > Add Data Plan > Scan QR Code
- Label the profile: "Travel" or "Vacation" (so you can identify it easily)
- Set mobile data to use the eSIM (not your physical SIM)

**On Samsung / Android:**
- Settings > Connections > SIM Manager > Add eSIM
- Scan the QR code
- Label the profile
- Set mobile data to eSIM

**Important:** do this **at home, on Wi-Fi**. Not at the airport, not on the plane, not at your destination. If something goes wrong, you have time to contact support.

### At your destination

1. **Enable data roaming** on the eSIM profile (Settings > Mobile Data > eSIM > Data Roaming = ON). This sounds counterintuitive, but without this setting, the eSIM won't connect
2. Your phone detects the local network automatically — usually within seconds of landing
3. Verify that mobile data is set to eSIM, not your home SIM
4. Done — you have internet

### When you return home

- Disable the eSIM profile
- Set mobile data back to your home SIM
- You can delete the eSIM profile or keep it for your next trip to the same region

## Destination-specific quirks — what you need to know

### Turkey — BTK website blocks

Since **July 2025**, Turkey's telecommunications authority (BTK) has blocked access to the websites and apps of major eSIM providers: **Airalo, Holafly, Saily, Nomad, Instabridge, Alosim, BNESIM, Mobimatter, Ubigi, and Roamless**. The block only applies to users inside Turkey — the websites work normally from any other country.

What this means in practice:
- **eSIMs purchased before arrival work perfectly** — the block doesn't affect already-installed eSIMs
- **You CANNOT buy or top up an eSIM from within Turkey** — the websites won't load
- **Buy 20-30% more GB than you think you need** — you have no plan B if you run out
- A VPN might bypass the block in some cases, but it's not guaranteed

### Switzerland — the geographic trap

Switzerland is surrounded by the EU but is NOT part of free EU roaming. Many travelers flying to Geneva or Zurich assume roaming is included — and receive bills for hundreds of euros. A 3-5 GB eSIM for Switzerland costs USD 10-15 and eliminates the risk entirely.

### Albania and the Western Balkans

Albania, Serbia, Montenegro, Bosnia, Kosovo, and North Macedonia are extremely popular summer destinations. NONE are in the EU, so roaming is expensive. A regional "Europe" plan from Airalo or Saily usually covers these countries, but check each country individually — Albania and Kosovo can be missing from some packages.

### USA — watch out for rural areas

US eSIM networks (T-Mobile, AT&T) are excellent in cities and along highways but can be weak in national parks, rural western areas, and some mountain zones. For a road trip, download offline maps and count on Wi-Fi in transit towns.

### Dubai (UAE) — VoIP blocked

In the United Arab Emirates, VoIP services (WhatsApp Calling, FaceTime, Skype) are partially blocked or restricted. You can send WhatsApp text messages, but voice/video calls may not work without a VPN.

## 10 common eSIM mistakes

**1. Buying the eSIM at the airport or after landing**
In Turkey it's impossible (websites are blocked). In other countries, the stress of configuring at queues isn't worth it. Buy and install from home.

**2. Confusing Switzerland or UK with the EU**
Both are non-EU. Roaming is not free. An eSIM eliminates the risk.

**3. Forgetting to enable "Data Roaming" on the eSIM**
Without this setting, the eSIM won't connect. It's the most common cause of "my eSIM isn't working."

**4. Leaving mobile data on your home SIM**
After installing the eSIM, you must set mobile data to the eSIM profile, not your home carrier. Otherwise, you're consuming expensive carrier roaming.

**5. Buying too little data**
A 1 GB plan sounds cheap but runs out in 1-2 days if you use social media. Rule: minimum 3 GB per week for moderate users.

**6. Not downloading offline maps**
Google Maps uses 100-200 MB/hour during navigation. Offline maps = 0 MB. Save 1-2 GB over a one-week vacation.

**7. Leaving photo sync on**
Google Photos and iCloud can automatically upload photos to the cloud, consuming GBs without you realizing. Disable cloud upload on mobile data.

**8. Comparing "unlimited" with "unlimited"**
Many "unlimited" plans have a fair use policy: after 2-5 GB per day, speeds drop to 512 kbps (unusable for navigation or social media). A fixed 5-10 GB plan is more transparent.

**9. Choosing a regional plan when a local one works**
Regional plans (Europe, Asia) cost more per GB than single-country plans. If you're visiting one country, the local plan is always cheaper.

**10. Ignoring free Wi-Fi**
Hotels, cafes, restaurants, airports, metro systems — free Wi-Fi is everywhere in 2026. Use it for downloads, streaming, and video calls. The eSIM is for when you're on the move.

**Tip:** for kids on planes or at the hotel, download Netflix/YouTube/Disney+ content on Wi-Fi before departure.

### Multi-country trips (3+ countries)

If you're visiting multiple countries on a single trip:
- A **regional plan** (Europe 39 countries, Asia, etc.) is more convenient than separate country plans
- **Yesim global** (5 GB / 30 days at USD 28.80, 147 countries) or **Airalo global** are solid options
- The per-GB price is higher than local plans, but you gain simplicity

### Cruises

On a cruise ship, onboard internet is expensive (USD 10-25/day) and slow. An eSIM works **only in ports**, when the ship is docked and your phone connects to the terrestrial network. At sea, the eSIM has no signal.

**Optimal strategy:** buy a regional plan covering the countries in your itinerary. Use onboard internet only for urgent messages. At each port, activate the eSIM and do everything you need (upload photos, messages, navigation).

## WiFi Calling — the free alternative to roaming calls

WiFi Calling is a feature supported by most modern phones and carriers. When enabled, your phone uses Wi-Fi instead of the cellular network for calls and texts.

### Why it matters for travelers

When you're outside the EU with an eSIM active for data only:
- **Incoming calls** on your home SIM can generate roaming charges (your carrier charges for call forwarding)
- **WiFi Calling eliminates these charges** — calls travel over the internet, not the cellular network
- Works on hotel Wi-Fi and on the eSIM's data connection

### How to enable

**On iPhone:** Settings > Phone > WiFi Calling > Enable on This iPhone
**On Android:** Settings > Connections > WiFi Calling > ON

**Note:** not all carriers support WiFi Calling while roaming. Check with your carrier before departure.

### Alternative: WhatsApp/Telegram calls

If WiFi Calling isn't available, voice calls through WhatsApp or Telegram use only 35-50 MB/hour and are completely free. Audio quality is excellent on 4G/LTE. The only limitation: both parties need the app installed.

## eSIM vs Pocket WiFi — which to choose

| Criterion | eSIM | Pocket WiFi |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cost** | USD 4-30 | USD 5-15/day (rental) |
| **Connected devices** | 1 (+ hotspot) | 5-10 |
| **Battery** | Phone battery | Separate battery (4-8 hours) |

**eSIM wins** for: solo travelers, couples, anyone who wants maximum simplicity.
**Pocket WiFi wins** for: large groups (3+ people), families with many devices, anyone who needs stable internet for work.

## Security and privacy — what to know

### Are eSIMs secure?

Yes, equally secure as a physical SIM. The eSIM profile is stored in the phone's secure chip and cannot be cloned or copied without physical access to the device.

### VPN — worth it with an eSIM?

**Yes, in certain situations:**
- **Public Wi-Fi** (airport, cafe, hotel) — a VPN protects your data from interception
- **Dubai/UAE** — VoIP (WhatsApp Calling, FaceTime) is blocked without a VPN
- **China** — access to Google, WhatsApp, Instagram is blocked without a VPN

**Saily** includes basic VPN protection in its price. **Yesim** includes VPN on iOS. For full protection, a separate NordVPN or ExpressVPN subscription is recommended.

### Is my personal data safe?

Reputable eSIM providers (Airalo, Saily, Yesim, Holafly) don't monitor or sell your browsing data. But — as on any network — unencrypted connections (HTTP, not HTTPS) can theoretically be intercepted. Use a VPN on public networks and check for HTTPS in your browser.

## Additional FAQ

### What's better: an eSIM or a local physical SIM?

**eSIM wins on convenience:** buy from home, no SIM swapping, keep your home number active. **Local SIM wins on price:** in some countries (Thailand, Vietnam, India), airport SIM cards are extremely cheap (USD 2-5 for 15-30 GB). But you need to remove your home SIM, lose incoming calls and texts, and sometimes need passport registration.

### Does an eSIM work on a plane?

Not during flight (no cellular network at altitude). It activates automatically upon landing. Some planes have onboard Wi-Fi, but that's a separate service offered by the airline, unrelated to eSIM.

### Can I have multiple eSIMs installed at once?

Yes. Most phones support 5-10 installed eSIM profiles, but you can only have 1-2 active simultaneously. You can have an eSIM for Turkey, one for the UK, and one for the US — activate them one at a time depending on your destination.

### Does the eSIM drain extra battery?

Minimally. Two active SIM profiles (physical + eSIM) consume ~5-10% more battery per day than a single profile. The difference is negligible in practice.

### What do I do if the eSIM doesn't connect at my destination?

1. Verify that "Data Roaming" is enabled on the eSIM profile
2. Check that mobile data is set to eSIM (not your home SIM)
3. Restart your phone
4. Manually select the network (Settings > Network > Manual operator selection)
5. Contact the provider's support via chat (on airport Wi-Fi)

### Does the eSIM expire if I don't use it?

Validity typically starts at first network connection (upon landing), not at the time of purchase. Some plans also have an absolute expiration date (e.g., 90 days from purchase). Check the plan terms.
