## Why Prague casts a spell

Prague survived WWII almost untouched — one of the few European capitals that wasn't bombed. The result: a complete medieval city layered with Gothic, Baroque, Art Nouveau, and Cubist architecture, all in a walkable area smaller than most city centres. Add the best beer culture in the world (Czechs drink more beer per capita than anyone) and prices that still feel like a secret, and you have a city that's very hard to leave.

## Prague Castle — the world's largest

The Guinness-recognized largest castle complex in the world: 70,000 m² of palaces, churches, gardens, and towers, perched on a hill above the Vltava river. The castle has been the seat of Czech rulers for 1,100 years.

**[Book guided tour on GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/prague-castle-tour-t53490/?partner_id=EKAA80H)** (€20-35, skip-the-line + guide who explains the history — without context, the castle feels like a lot of rooms). Or DIY: **Circuit B ticket: €14** (St. Vitus Cathedral, Old Royal Palace, Golden Lane, St. George's Basilica).

**St. Vitus Cathedral** (included in castle ticket) — the Gothic masterpiece that took 600 years to complete. Mucha's Art Nouveau stained glass window is worth the visit alone.

**Golden Lane** — tiny colourful houses built into the castle wall. Franz Kafka lived in #22 (now a bookshop).

**Timing:** castle grounds open 6 AM-10 PM (free to walk). Interior open 9 AM-5 PM. Changing of the Guard: hourly, the most elaborate at noon.

## Charles Bridge — the 600-year-old runway

30 Baroque statues lining a 621-metre stone bridge over the Vltava. It's Prague's signature image — and its most crowded spot. In summer, 30,000 people cross daily.

**The trick:** go at **6:00 AM**. The bridge is empty, the morning mist rises from the river, and the light on the Old Town towers is golden. By 10 AM, it's shoulder-to-shoulder.

**Alternatively:** view the bridge FROM the river. Rent a paddleboard or pedal boat on the Vltava (€10-15/hour from Žluté Lázně) — unique perspective, no crowds.

## Old Town Square

The heart of Prague since the 10th century. The **Astronomical Clock** (1410, oldest working astronomical clock in the world) performs an hourly show — apostles appear in windows above the clock face. It's charming for 45 seconds and then everyone disperses.

**Tip:** the clock tower has a viewing gallery (€10, elevator). The best elevated view of Old Town Square.

**Church of Our Lady before Týn** — the two Gothic spires that define Prague's skyline. Free entry, limited hours.

## Beer culture — the Czech art form

Czechs invented Pilsner (in Plzeň, 1842). They drink more beer per capita than any nation on Earth (140 litres/year). Czech beer isn't just good — it's a religious experience.

**Where to drink:**

**U Fleků** — brewing since 1499. The dark lager (unavailable anywhere else) is served in a medieval hall. Touristy but historically essential. €4/beer.

**Lokál** — modern Czech pub with Pilsner Urquell tank beer (unpasteurised, served from tanks delivered weekly). €2.50/beer. The food is equally excellent — Czech classics at €8-12.

**Letná Beer Garden** — open-air terrace in Letná Park with THE best panoramic view of Prague (bridges, river, Old Town, Castle). €2-3/beer. Best at sunset.

**U Sudu** — underground maze of rooms, each with a different vibe. Student-priced drinks (€2/beer). Gets lively after 10 PM.

## The neighbourhoods beyond the centre

### Malá Strana (Lesser Town)

Between the castle and Charles Bridge. Baroque palaces, embassy gardens, quiet squares, and John Lennon Wall (continuously graffitied since the 1980s). Walk up Nerudova Street to the castle — the most atmospheric approach.

### Vyšehrad

The "other castle" — older than Prague Castle but far less visited. High above the river with spectacular views, a Romanesque rotunda, and the national cemetery (Dvořák, Smetana, Mucha buried here). Free entry. 30 minutes by metro from centre.

### Vinohrady + Žižkov

Where locals actually live and drink. Vinohrady has Art Nouveau architecture and the best restaurant scene outside the centre. Žižkov has the highest density of pubs per capita in Prague (and possibly the world) + the Žižkov TV Tower with crawling baby sculptures.

## Where to eat

**Svíčková** — the national dish. Braised beef in creamy vegetable sauce with bread dumplings and cranberry sauce. €8-10 at any good hospoda.

**Trdelník** — chimney cake (spiral pastry) sold at tourist stands everywhere. €4-5. Originally Slovak, not Czech — but delicious regardless. Skip the ones filled with ice cream (they're for Instagram, not for eating).

**Chlebíčky** — open-faced sandwiches from traditional delis. €1-2 each. Sisters Bistro near Old Town Square does excellent modern versions.

**Full Czech meal at a hospoda:** roast duck with cabbage and dumplings, or pork knee with mustard. €8-12 with beer. The beer is €2-3. Total damage: €10-15 for a feast.

## Getting around

**Metro:** 3 lines (A, B, C). Single ticket: 30 CZK (~€1.20). 24h pass: 120 CZK (~€5). The system is efficient and clean.

**Trams:** excellent network covering areas the metro doesn't. Same tickets. Tram #22 runs from the centre through Malá Strana to the castle — scenic and practical.

**Walking:** Prague is one of the most walkable cities in Europe. Old Town Square to Prague Castle: 25 minutes (including Charles Bridge crossing).

**Airport:** Václav Havel Airport. Bus 119 to Nádraží Veleslavín metro (30 min, €1.20) then metro to centre. Bolt/Uber: €15-20.

## Practical tips

**Currency:** Czech Koruna (CZK). €1 ≈ 25 CZK. Cards accepted almost everywhere. **Do NOT exchange money at the stands on tourist streets** — some display rates that look good but hide a 15% commission in the fine print. Use ATMs inside banks ([avoid scam guide](/en/asigurari/travel-scams-europe-2026)).
