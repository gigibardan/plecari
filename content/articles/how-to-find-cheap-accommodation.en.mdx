Accommodation is typically the single largest expense in any trip — and it is also the category with the most room to pay less without sacrificing quality. A few deliberate choices can reduce what you spend by 30–50%, leaving money for the parts of travel that actually matter. Here is what works, and why.

## 1. Book in the shoulder season (the biggest single saving)

Peak season pricing is one of the most dramatic distortions in travel. The same hotel room that costs €180/night in the first two weeks of August costs €95 in the first two weeks of June. Same room. Same hotel. Same view. The weather in June across most of Southern Europe (Greece, Spain, Italy, Croatia) is excellent — warm, sunny, and with significantly fewer crowds.

**The numbers:**
- July–August in Greek islands: index price 100%
- June and September: 60–70% of peak price
- May and October: 50–60% of peak price
- November to March: 35–50% (excluding Christmas/New Year)

Even within peak season, timing matters. The first and last weeks of August tend to be cheaper than mid-August — that is when most local school holidays peak in Western Europe.

**City breaks follow a different pattern.** Business hotels in city centres are expensive Monday–Thursday (business travel) and cheaper Friday–Sunday. A city break from Thursday to Sunday evening often costs significantly less than one from Monday to Friday. The reverse is true for resort destinations, where weekend premium applies.

## 2. Compare across multiple platforms — prices genuinely differ

For the same hotel, on the same night, you will regularly find price differences of 5–20% between platforms. This is not a glitch — it reflects different commission structures, promotional agreements, and pricing algorithms.

| Platform | Best for |
|---|---|
| Booking.com | Widest selection in Europe, free cancellation options, Genius loyalty discounts |
| Agoda | Asia, Middle East, and exotic destinations; often lower net prices |
| Hotels.com | Reward programme (10 nights = 1 free night); good for frequent travellers |
| Hostelworld | Budget accommodation, hostels, dorm beds |
| Hotel direct website | Often 5–15% cheaper than aggregators for independent properties |

**The practical routine:** find your preferred property on Booking.com or a similar aggregator → note the price → open the hotel's own website in a new tab → compare the total including taxes. Two minutes, occasional 10–15% saving.

**One important caveat:** for large hotel chains (Marriott, Hilton, Accor, IHG), their own loyalty programme rates are often competitive with or better than aggregators, especially once you accumulate status. If you use the same chain frequently, enrol in their programme.

## 3. Always check the hotel's own website

Booking.com and similar platforms charge hotels a commission of 15–25%. Many independent hotels — particularly smaller family-run properties, boutique hotels, and guesthouses — quietly offer a lower direct rate to avoid paying that commission. They cannot advertise this openly (aggregator contracts often prohibit rate parity violations), but they can honour it if you call or email.

**How to find the direct rate:** go to the hotel's website and look for a booking widget. Many show the rate directly. If not, email or call — "Do you offer a direct booking rate?" is a perfectly normal question.

**When it works best:** independent hotels, boutique properties, small family guesthouses. Large chains rarely have better direct rates because their aggregator relationships are more structured.

**When "sold out" on Booking doesn't mean sold out:** aggregators work from an allotment of rooms the hotel allocates to them. When that allotment is exhausted, the property shows as "fully booked" on the platform — but the hotel itself may have rooms available for direct bookings. Always check directly before giving up on a preferred property.

## 4. Stay in zone 2–3, not the city centre

The price difference between a hotel in the historic centre and one 10–15 minutes away by metro is typically 30–50%. The actual experience difference for most travellers is close to zero — you spend your time walking around the city, eating in restaurants, visiting attractions, not sitting in your room looking at the view from the window.

**Real examples:**

| City | Centre price (3★, per night) | Zone 2–3 price | Saving | Metro time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paris | €180–250 | €110–150 | 35–40% | 10–15 min |
| Rome | €150–220 | €90–130 | 35–40% | 15–20 min |
| Barcelona | €160–230 | €100–140 | 35–40% | 10–15 min |
| London | €220–350 | €130–200 | 40–45% | 15–25 min |
| Amsterdam | €170–240 | €100–140 | 35–40% | 10–15 min |

In London, Earl's Court and Hammersmith are 15–20 minutes from Westminster by tube but cost 35–40% less. In Barcelona, staying in Eixample rather than the Gothic Quarter saves money and reduces the noise from Barri Gòtic nightlife. In Rome, Trastevere is genuinely more atmospheric than the centro storico and costs less.

**What to avoid:** the immediate vicinity of train stations. Hotels next to major train stations (Termini in Rome, Gare du Nord in Paris, Keleti in Budapest) have captive demand and charge accordingly — while also being in areas that are often less safe and less pleasant than they look on a map.

## 5. Apartment over hotel for groups and longer stays

For one or two people on a 2–3 night city break, a budget hotel or hostel is often the simplest and most economical option. For groups of 3 or more, or stays of 5+ nights, a self-catering apartment consistently beats hotel rooms on both price and practicality.

**The maths for a family of four, 7 nights in Athens:**
- Two double hotel rooms (3-star centre): €130/night × 2 × 7 = €1,820
- Two-bedroom apartment (same area): €120/night × 7 = €840
- Saving: €980 — before accounting for the kitchen

The kitchen is where the real saving happens. Breakfast at a hotel: €12–18/person. Breakfast from the local supermarket: €3–5/person for the same calories. Over 7 mornings for a family of four, that is a €100–200 saving just on breakfast. Lunch from the market instead of a restaurant adds another €80–120.

**Platforms for apartments:**
- Booking.com (has a significant apartment/apartment hotel inventory)
- Airbnb (better for distinctive properties, neighbourhood flats)
- VRBO / Homeaway (good for larger houses and villas)
- Local property management companies (often cheapest for resort destinations — search "[destination] holiday rentals" directly)

**The Airbnb cleaning fee trap:** always add the cleaning fee to the nightly rate before comparing. A flat listed at €60/night with a €80 cleaning fee costs €171/night for a 2-night stay — more than many hotels.

## 6. Set price alerts and let the algorithm work for you

Accommodation prices fluctuate daily based on demand, remaining availability, and platform algorithms. You do not need to check manually every day — set alerts and let them do the work.

**Booking.com:** on any property page, click "Save" and enable price alerts. The platform notifies you by email when the price drops.

**Google Hotels:** search your destination, find the property, and click "Track prices." Google sends alerts when it detects a significant drop.

**Kayak and Trivago:** both have price tracking for accommodation across platforms, with historical price charts that show whether the current price is high or low relative to typical rates for that property and period.

**When price alerts pay off:** mid-week searches often surface promotional rates that were not available at the weekend. Last-minute price drops (48–72 hours before check-in) happen regularly for business hotels in city centres, particularly on days with lower-than-expected demand.

## 7. Read recent reviews — not the overall score

A property with an 8.5 overall score on Booking might have recent reviews averaging 6.5 if quality has declined. The inverse is also true: a newly renovated property might have a lower overall score because older reviews from the pre-renovation period are pulling it down.

**The right way to read reviews:**
- Sort by most recent (last 3 months), not "most relevant"
- Read 5–10 negative reviews specifically — what do unhappy guests complain about?
- Look for mentions of: cleanliness (or its absence), noise level, actual location vs the map, Wi-Fi speed, and how the hotel handled problems
- Ignore reviews that discuss things you do not care about (someone complaining about no pool when you are not interested in swimming)

**Specific things to check for Mediterranean destinations:** air conditioning (many cheaper properties have poor or broken AC — not relevant in March, critical in August), blackout curtains (relevant if you sleep in), and noise (rooms facing the street in city centres can make sleep difficult).

## 8. Understand and calculate hidden costs before comparing

The price shown in search results is almost never the price you pay. The gap between advertised and actual price is widest in popular tourist destinations.

**Resort fee / destination fee:** common in US hotels (€20–45/night) and increasingly in Mediterranean resorts (€3–15/night). Listed separately and charged at check-in. Not always visible in search results.

**City tourist tax:** varies enormously by destination.

| City | Tourist tax (approx.) |
|---|---|
| Amsterdam | 16% of room rate |
| Venice | €5–10/night per person |
| Barcelona | €3.25–6.75/night |
| Rome | €3–7/night |
| Paris | €0.65–5/night (by category) |
| Athens | €0.50–4/night |
| London | No tourist tax (as of 2026) |

**Breakfast:** €10–18/person at hotel if not included. Calculate this for your party and stay duration — a "cheap" hotel with no breakfast can work out more expensive than a slightly pricier property with breakfast included, depending on where you are and how many people are in your group.

**Parking:** €10–25/night in city centres. A hotel showing €90/night with parking at €20/night costs €110 before you add tourist tax. Search specifically for "hotel with free parking" if you are driving.

**Cancellation policy:** the cheapest rate is almost always non-refundable. A slightly higher rate with free cancellation is almost always worth it if your travel dates have any uncertainty — and it gives you leverage to rebook at a lower price if prices drop after your initial reservation.

## 9. Early booking for peak season, flexibility for off-peak

The timing of your booking matters, and the optimal strategy differs by season:

**Peak season (July–August for Mediterranean, December for Christmas markets, Easter long weekends):**
- Book 2–4 months ahead for best availability and price
- For very popular destinations (Santorini, Cinque Terre, Dubrovnik), book earlier — 4–6 months for preferred properties
- Free cancellation rates are worth the small premium — book early, cancel and rebook if you find better

**Shoulder and off-peak season:**
- 3–6 weeks ahead is usually fine for most destinations
- Last-minute (under 1 week) can produce genuine discounts as properties try to fill remaining rooms
- Monitor prices for your intended stay over 2–3 weeks — you will see the pattern

**The early booking calculation:** if you find a non-refundable rate that is 25% cheaper than the flexible rate, the question is whether you are confident enough in your travel plans to commit. For most leisure trips with fixed flight bookings, a non-refundable hotel at a good price makes sense — pair it with trip cancellation insurance for the rare scenario where you genuinely cannot travel.

## What to pack in your research toolkit

A quick summary of the tools that make this process efficient:

**For price comparison:** Google Hotels (best interface, covers most aggregators), Booking.com (widest European inventory), the hotel's own website (check for direct rate)

**For price tracking:** Booking.com save/alert, Google Hotels price tracking, Kayak

**For reviews:** Booking.com (highest review volume in Europe), TripAdvisor (useful for cross-referencing), Google Maps reviews (often brutally honest from real guests)

**For apartments:** Airbnb, Booking.com apartments, VRBO

**For hostels:** Hostelworld (best hostel-specific inventory), Booking.com (also has extensive hostel listings)

## Putting it together: a realistic accommodation research process

1. Decide your destination and firm dates (or ±3 day flexibility if you have it)
2. Search Google Hotels for an overview of prices, zones, and what is available
3. Filter by zone (avoid the tourist trap areas immediately around major train stations)
4. Shortlist 3–4 properties — open each one's direct website in a separate tab
5. Compare total prices including taxes, breakfast, and any mandatory fees
6. Read the 10 most recent reviews for each, focusing on cleanliness and location accuracy
7. Book on whichever channel gives the best total price — with free cancellation if the price difference is under 10%
8. Set a price alert on the property anyway — if prices drop, you can cancel and rebook

This process takes 30–45 minutes for any destination. The saving over impulse booking or taking the first result on an aggregator is typically €50–150 for a week's stay. For a longer trip or more expensive destination, significantly more.
