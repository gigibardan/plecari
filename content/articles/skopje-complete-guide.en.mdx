No other city in Europe looks like Skopje. Not even Skopje from 20 years ago looked like the Skopje of today.

<cite index="19-1">The centre of North Macedonia's capital presents one of the world's most fascinating urban experiments: over 130 colossal statues, neoclassical façades applied over communist brutalist buildings, pirate ships moored on the Vardar River in a landlocked country, and a 21-metre triumphal arch — the full visual apparatus of an empire, built by a republic of 2 million people.</cite>

This is the "Skopje 2014" project — a 500-million-euro investment in reimagining national identity through granite, bronze and marble. Ridiculous or fascinating? Depends on the day, the angle and the mood. Probably both.

But Skopje is not only the 2014 project. A few hundred metres from the colossal statues lies one of the most authentic and most vibrant Ottoman bazaars in the Balkans. <cite index="15-1">The Old Bazaar (Čaršija) dates from the Ottoman Empire and is one of the largest in the Balkans</cite> — a completely different world, with cobblestone lanes, mosques, caravanserais and craftsmen. And 17 km from the centre, Matka Canyon offers a natural landscape with nothing to do with the downtown architectural extravagance.

<cite index="19-1">Skopje remains one of the cheapest destinations in the Balkans — a coffee 1–2 EUR, a hearty restaurant meal that rarely exceeds 5–7 EUR.</cite>

## Skopje at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** North Macedonia — not in the EU (candidate), not in Schengen.

**Population:** approximately 600,000.

**Currency:** Macedonian Denar (MKD). <cite index="19-1">1 EUR ≈ 61.5 MKD in 2026.</cite>

**Language:** Macedonian (South Slavic language). Albanian is the second official language. English is well spoken in the tourist area.

**Visa:** EU and Romanian citizens enter without a visa — valid ID or passport, max 90 days.

**Direct flights from Bucharest:** Wizz Air, OTP→SKP, ~1h45.

**Average budget:** <cite index="19-1">25–45 EUR/day — one of Europe's cheapest capitals.</cite>

## The "Skopje 2014" Project — A Complete Guide to Europe's Most Controversial Urban Centre

Before visiting Macedonia Square, you need to understand the context. Otherwise it simply seems absurd.

**What happened:** <cite index="16-1">After North Macedonia's independence, Skopje entered a new phase of nation-building expressed through monuments, museums and redesigned public spaces — a project of national identity affirmation through architecture.</cite>

**The reason:** North Macedonia faced a dispute with Greece over the country's name (resolved in 2019 through the Prespa Agreement) and felt the need to assert its historical heritage, including through links to Alexander the Great.

**The result:** <cite index="19-1">over 130 statues, neoclassical arcades and Baroque façades applied over communist-era buildings, plus a triumphal arch, pirate ships on the Vardar and London-style red buses — all built between 2006 and 2014 for approximately 500 million EUR.</cite>

**"Warrior on a Horse" statue** — the 14-metre equestrian statue in the centre of Macedonia Square, depicting a leader with raised sword. Not officially named Alexander the Great (due to the dispute with Greece) but everyone knows who it represents. <cite index="15-1">It is the centrepiece of the square, from which the Stone Bridge leads to the Old Bazaar.</cite>

**Triumphal Arch "Porta Macedonia"** — a free interpretation of Rome's Arch of Titus, 21 metres high.

## What to See in Skopje

### Macedonia Square — The Grand Kitsch Spectacle

<cite index="20-1">Macedonia Square is the heart of modern Skopje, dominated by the "Warrior on a Horse" statue, fountains and neoclassical buildings</cite> — a remarkable blend of Romanesque aesthetics over Soviet concrete. In the evening, illuminated fountains and theatrical lighting make the square even more surreal.

**Entry:** free 24/7. **Best moment:** at night, when the lighting conceals the kitsch and leaves only the spectacle.

### Stone Bridge (Kamen Most) — Between Two Eras

<cite index="16-1">The Stone Bridge is built of stone blocks on semicircular arches, connecting Macedonia Square with the Old Bazaar — a direct link between controversial modern Skopje and authentic Ottoman Skopje.</cite> Free.

### Old Bazaar (Čaršija) — The Authenticity That Survived

A few hundred metres from the Baroque circus of Macedonia Square, the Old Bazaar is a completely different world.

<cite index="15-1">The Old Bazaar is one of the largest and best-preserved Ottoman bazaars in the Balkans — a network of lanes with craftsmen, jewellers, traditional restaurants and mosques, dating from the Ottoman Empire.</cite> <cite index="18-1">It is one of the few parts of Skopje that escaped destruction in the 1963 earthquake, and has a completely different vibe from the rest of the city.</cite>

**What you'll find:**
- Mustafa Pasha Mosque (15th century) — Skopje's largest mosque, with a flower garden. Free entry.
- Kapan Han caravanserai (15th century) — once a merchant caravan hub, now a cultural space
- Daut Pasha Hammam (15th century) — medieval Turkish bath converted into an art gallery. <cite index="17-1">~150 MKD entry.</cite>
- Jewellers' workshops, spice shops, Turkish coffee cafés

### Kale Fortress — Panoramic View Above the City

<cite index="15-1">Kale Fortress rises above the Old Bazaar with panoramic views over all of Skopje and the Vardar.</cite> Dating from the 6th century (rebuilt in multiple phases), with partially restored defensive walls.

<cite index="20-1">Visiting the fortress exterior and panoramic views is free.</cite> Interior museum: ~100 MKD.

**Tip:** go to Kale at sunset — the panorama of the illuminated Macedonia Square in the background and the Old Bazaar in the foreground is unique.

### Mother Teresa Memorial House

<cite index="24-1">On Macedonia Boulevard stands the Mother Teresa Memorial House</cite> — Agnes Gonxha Bojaxhiu was born in Skopje on 26 August 1910. The exhibition documents her life and missionary work.

**Free entry.** 30–45 minutes. <cite index="20-1">One of the few places in the world directly linked to Mother Teresa's birthplace.</cite>

### Millennium Cross and Mount Vodno

On the summit of Mount Vodno (1,066 m) rises the Millennium Cross — a 66-metre cross, one of the world's largest. The cable car from the city centre reaches halfway up the mountain, with a minibus to the summit.

Complete panoramic views of Skopje, the Vardar valley and the surrounding mountains.

## Matka Canyon — The Natural Jewel 17 km from the Centre

<cite index="27-1">Matka Canyon is 17 km from Skopje's centre and is one of North Macedonia's most spectacular natural landscapes — a dramatic gorge with emerald lakes, medieval monasteries clinging to cliffs and Vrelo Cave with underwater stalactites.</cite>

**What to do at Matka:**
- **Boat tour** through the canyon (~200 MKD/3.25 EUR, 45 minutes)
- **Vrelo Cave** — one of the world's deepest underwater caves (separate ticket, ~100 MKD)
- **Hiking** along the Treska River banks, with the monasteries of St Andrew and St Nicholas
- **Matka Monastery** (14th century) — hikers' starting point

<cite index="27-1">Guided canyon tour from Skopje includes transport, riverside walk and boat tour: 20–35 EUR per person.</cite>

**Getting there independently:** taxi from Skopje (~400–500 MKD, 15 minutes) or minibus 60 from the centre.

## Food and Drink in Skopje

### What You Must Eat

**Burek** — Balkan pastry with cheese or meat. At a Bazaar bakery: 50–80 MKD (~1 EUR).

**Tavče Gravče** — baked beans in tomato and pepper sauce in a clay pot — North Macedonia's national dish. 200–350 MKD (3.25–5.70 EUR).

**Ajvar** — roasted red pepper paste — the emblematic Balkan condiment. Buy a jar at the Bazaar: 150–250 MKD.

**Kebapi** — Macedonian grilled meat rolls served with bread and onion. 5–8 EUR a portion.

### Where to Eat

<cite index="19-1">A hearty restaurant meal in Skopje rarely exceeds 5–7 EUR — one of the best gastronomic value-for-money experiences in Europe.</cite>

**Budget (150–400 MKD/meal):**
- Burek bakeries in the Old Bazaar
- Traditional "aščinica" restaurants in the Bazaar

**Mid-range (400–900 MKD/person):**
- Restaurants along the Vardar in the Debar Maalo area
- **Debar Maalo** — the café and bar neighbourhood, authentic local Skopje

## Practical Tips

**Macedonian denar** — exchange at local ATMs or city centre exchange offices. Avoid Euronet.

**Bolt works** in Skopje — far more reliable than street taxis which may negotiate inflated tourist prices.

**Summer heat** — Skopje can exceed 38°C in summer. Visit outdoor attractions early morning or evening. Bring water.

**North Macedonia is not in Schengen** — no visa needed as an EU/Romanian citizen, but passport/ID card is mandatory.
