## Why Athens surprises everyone

Athens has a reputation problem. Most people treat it as a two-hour stopover on the way to the islands. That's a massive mistake. This is a city where you touch 2,500-year-old marble in the morning, eat a €3-5 gyros for lunch, sip a freddo cappuccino on a rooftop with the illuminated Parthenon floating above you, and listen to live rebetiko in a Psyrri taverna at midnight — all on a budget that would barely cover breakfast in Paris.

And the most surprising part: Athens is cheap. A full day — transport, food, attractions, evening drinks — costs €50-90. Try that in Rome or Paris. A couple can do a 3-day city break for €400-700 total (excluding flights). And flights from most European cities start from €60-100 return.

Greece has changed several major prices in 2026 — the Acropolis ticket went up, the National Archaeological Museum nearly doubled — and this guide has all the updated figures, not the 2024 numbers you'll find on most blogs.

## The Acropolis — the defining experience of Western civilisation

This isn't hyperbole: the Acropolis is the single place on earth where you can stand on the rock where democracy, theatre, and Western philosophy were born. The Parthenon, the Erechtheion with its Caryatids, the Temple of Athena Nike — all on a limestone hill that has been sacred for 5,000 years.

### When to visit the Acropolis

**Early morning (8:00-9:00) — the only correct answer.** Be at the gate by 7:45. By 10:00, tour groups arrive. By noon in summer, it's punishing: 40°C+ on white stone, zero shade, hundreds of tourists jostling for selfies.

If you missed the morning, the second-best option is after 15:00 — beautiful light for photos, and the crowds thin out. But morning remains the golden rule.

**What nobody tells you:** the south-east entrance gate (near the Acropolis Museum) usually has a shorter queue than the main north-west gate. You'll save 15-30 minutes.

## The Acropolis Museum — the originals that stayed in Greece

At the foot of the Acropolis, in a modern glass building designed by Bernard Tschumi. Here you'll find the original Caryatids (the ones on the Erechtheion are copies), friezes, metopes, and artefacts excavated from the Acropolis — the pieces the British didn't take (the Parthenon Marbles are in the British Museum, and Greece has been asking for them back for decades).

**Important:** The Acropolis Museum is NOT included in the combined archaeological sites ticket. It requires a completely separate ticket. Worth every cent — it's one of the best archaeological museums in the world.

**Insider tip:** The top floor has the exact orientation of the Parthenon — the friezes are displayed in the same position they occupied on the temple, with the actual Acropolis framed through panoramic windows. And the ground floor has glass panels showing excavations beneath the building (an ancient neighbourhood discovered during construction).

**Opening hours:** Monday: 10:00-17:00. Tuesday-Sunday: 9:00-17:00 (winter), 9:00-20:00 (summer, Friday until 22:00 in peak season). Friday evening is magical — nearly empty museum, sunset light on the marble pieces.

## The 7 sites on the combined ticket — what's worth it and what you can skip

You have 5 days to visit all of them with the €30 combined ticket. Here's the real priority order:

### 1. Ancient Agora — where Socrates taught

The public marketplace of ancient Athens where the laws of democracy were debated, where Socrates engaged citizens in philosophical dialogue, and where the Apostle Paul preached Christianity. Less crowded than the Acropolis, equally atmospheric. The Temple of Hephaestus (inside the Agora) is the best-preserved classical Greek temple — more intact than the Parthenon.

**Time needed:** 1-1.5 hours. **When to go:** morning, immediately after the Acropolis.

### 2. Temple of Olympian Zeus

15 massive Corinthian columns (out of the original 104). It took 600 years to build — started in the 6th century BC, finished by Roman Emperor Hadrian in the 2nd century AD. Visible from the road (free), but worth entering with the combined ticket for the perspective and nearby Hadrian's Arch.

**Time needed:** 30-45 minutes.

### 3. Kerameikos — the ancient cemetery and Athens' quietest site

The potters' quarter and monumental cemetery of ancient Athens — 11 acres of funerary monuments in a landscape of pines and cypress trees. Almost nobody comes here. If you want the perfect photo without tourists in the frame, Kerameikos is the spot. The small museum at the entrance has extraordinary funerary vases.

**Time needed:** 45-60 minutes.

### 4. Hadrian's Library

Roman complex from 132 AD with impressive Corinthian columns, hidden behind Monastiraki Square. Passers-by constantly overlook it. With the combined ticket, worth 20-30 minutes.

### 5. Roman Agora + Tower of the Winds

The marble octagon that combined sundial, water clock, and wind vane — an engineering masterpiece of the ancient world. Also includes the Fethiye Mosque (Ottoman period), a surprising cultural layer.

### 6. Aristotle's Lyceum

The most recently opened site (public access since 2014). This is where Aristotle taught for 13 years. The ruins are modest, but the significance is immense.

**My recommended route with the combined ticket:** Day 1: Acropolis (8:00 AM) → Ancient Agora → lunch at Monastiraki. Day 2: Temple of Zeus → Hadrian's Library → Roman Agora → Kerameikos. Day 3+: Aristotle's Lyceum (if you have time). Realistically, you can cover everything in 2 days without rushing.

## National Archaeological Museum — Greece's greatest museum

The world's largest collection of ancient Greek art. The Mask of Agamemnon, the bronze statue of Poseidon/Zeus, the Sounion Kouros, the Antikythera Mechanism (the first known analogue "computer" in history, dated ~100 BC) — every room has pieces that would be the star exhibit in any other museum.

**2026 price: €20** (up from €12, no seasonal variation). Reduced: €10. Children under 18 and EU students: free.

**Time needed:** minimum 2 hours, ideally 3. It's large and dense. If time is limited, focus on the Mycenaean Antiquities Collection (room 4, with the Mask of Agamemnon) and the Bronzes (room 15).

**Location:** 44 Patission Street, 20 minutes' walk from Syntagma or 2 metro stops (Line 1, Victoria station).

**Insider:** The museum is undergoing building renovations — check which rooms are open before your visit. But the major pieces remain on display throughout.

## Athens neighbourhoods — where the city actually lives

Athens isn't just the Acropolis and ruins. Each neighbourhood has its own personality, and jumping from one to the next is like jumping between different worlds — all within 10-15 minutes on foot.

### Plaka — the village beneath the Acropolis

The oldest continuously inhabited neighbourhood in Athens. Cobblestone streets, neoclassical houses with bougainvillea-draped balconies, tiny Byzantine churches, and the Acropolis always looming above. It's touristy but genuinely charming — especially in the evening, when the lights transform everything.

**What to do:** The walk from Syntagma Square through Plaka to the foot of the Acropolis is the most beautiful evening route. Stop at a rooftop taverna for dinner with views. Visit the Greek musical instruments museum (free) and the Choragic Monument of Lysicrates — the oldest known structure with Corinthian columns in the world (334 BC), on a quiet side street.

**Anafiotika — Plaka's secret:** A micro-neighbourhood of whitewashed blue-and-white houses built by workers from the island of Anafi in the 19th century, who reproduced the island style on the Acropolis slope. It looks like a miniature Santorini, 5 minutes from the metro stations. Very few tourists find it.

### Monastiraki — energy, bazaar, street food

Modern Athens' central square. Permanent bazaar with everything from antiques to cheap t-shirts. On Sundays, the Avyssinias Square flea market (arrive before 9:00 AM, before the crowds) is an experience in itself — old vinyl records, ceramics, coins, jewellery, everything.

**The food:** Thanasis and Savvas on Monastiraki Square — Athens' most famous gyros (€3-5 for a wrap that'll keep you going half the day). Dare: order "gyros pita me apo ola" (with everything) and eat standing up in the square, like the locals.

**Rooftop bars:** A for Athens and 360 Cocktail Bar have the best views of the illuminated Acropolis at night. Cocktail: €10-14.

### Psyrri — food, street art, nightlife

The neighbourhood between Monastiraki and Omonia with Athens' highest concentration of restaurants, cocktail bars, and live music. The walls are covered in street art (Sarri and Protogenous streets are open-air galleries).

**Dinner in Psyrri:** €10-20 for a full meal with house wine. The tavernas don't look fancy — that's exactly the sign that the food is good. Look for places with chalkboard menus, full of Greeks.

**Rebetiko music:** Greek blues, born in the ports and working-class neighbourhoods of the 1920s. Stoa Athanaton (in the central meat market, open since 1930) has live concerts starting after 22:30 — Athens' supreme cultural experience.

### Koukaki — the locals' favourite

South of the Acropolis, 10 minutes' walk from the entrance. Tree-lined streets, neighbourhood bakeries, independent cafés, and authentic restaurants without tourists. If you want to live like an Athenian, stay in Koukaki. Best value for accommodation near the centre.

### Exarchia — the rebel quarter

Student and bohemian neighbourhood with anarchist identity, politically charged graffiti, and the cheapest meals in central Athens. It's not dangerous (the myth exaggerates), but it has a raw aesthetic that isn't for everyone. Mezedopolia (small-plate restaurants) here are 30-40% cheaper than Plaka.

### Kolonaki — the elegant uptown

The chic quarter with designer boutiques, refined cafés, and "old money" atmosphere. Prices run 20-30% above the rest of the centre. But worth a visit for the Lycabettus funicular (€10 return) to the highest hill in Athens — spectacular 360° panorama, especially at sunset.

### Gazi (Technopolis) — clubbing and art

An 1860s gasworks converted into a cultural venue (free entry during the day). The area around Technopolis is Athens' clubbing centre, with a strong LGBTQ+ scene. Most active after 23:00.

### Pangrati — the neighbourhood by the stadium

Residential and authentic, behind the Panathenaic Stadium (the marble one, site of the first modern Olympics in 1896). Independent bookshops, excellent bakeries, and a Saturday morning farmers' market. €45-90/night for accommodation — the best prices in the extended centre.

## Food tours — food is half the Athens experience

Greek cuisine is far more than gyros, Greek salad, and moussaka. Food tours take you to places Google won't find — family tavernas passed down through generations, neighbourhood bakeries, local markets, and urban wine cellars.

### What you'll taste on a food tour

Freshly baked spanakopita (spinach and feta pie), authentic pork souvlaki from the grill, tzatziki with garden cucumbers, loukoumades (Greek doughnuts with honey and cinnamon), pasteli (sesame bars), local cheeses (Cretan graviera, kaseri, manouri), Kalamata olives, olive pastes, baklava, and — the surprise — excellent Greek wine from indigenous varieties (assyrtiko, agiorgitiko, xinomavro).

### Where food tours go

**Plaka + Central Market (Varvakios Agora)** — the classic option, perfect for first-time visitors. The central meat and fish market is a sensory spectacle (not for sensitive stomachs in the morning). Includes tastings of ouzo, mezes, cheeses, and a full lunch.

**Monastiraki + Psyrri** — the modern option with street food, rooftops, and a cooler vibe. Good for couples and younger groups.

**Koukaki** — the local option, no tourists, authentic tavernas. The least known but most authentic.

## Day trips from Athens

### Meteora — the most spectacular sight in Greece

Medieval monasteries built on vertical sandstone pillars, 200-400 metres high. The landscape is so surreal it was used as a filming location in James Bond (For Your Eyes Only) and Game of Thrones. UNESCO World Heritage since 1988.

The day trip from Athens is long — 350 km, 12-14 hours total — but it's one of those experiences that stays with you for life.

| Option | Price | What's included | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organised coach tour | €85-120 | Transport + guide + 2-3 monasteries | 12-14h |
| Train + local tour | €80-130 | Train (€25-40/way) + local tour (€30-50) | 12-14h |
| Private tour | €400-600 | Fully customisable | 12-14h |
| Overnight (2 days) | €180-250 | Accommodation + meals + extended tour | 2 days |

**Monastery entrance:** ~€3/monastery (paid separately at each).

**Dress code is mandatory:** Covered shoulders, covered knees. Women need a long skirt or will be given one at the entrance. Men: long trousers. Non-negotiable — they'll turn you away.

**What the organised tour doesn't tell you:** You spend more time on the bus than at the monasteries. If you have time and budget, stay overnight in Kalabaka (the town at the base of the rocks) — the morning, when mist rises through the stone pillars, is the magical moment day-trippers miss.

### Delphi — the Oracle that ruled the ancient world

The ancient Greeks believed Delphi was the centre of the world (omphalos — "navel of the earth"). The archaeological site sits in a natural amphitheatre on Mount Parnassus, overlooking the Pleistos Valley planted with millions of olive trees — the landscape is as spectacular as the ruins.

**Delphi entrance 2026:** ~€12 combined site + museum ticket. Reduced ~€6 (students, EU seniors). Free: EU citizens under 25, same free days as the Acropolis.

**Bonus stop — Arachova:** Most tours stop in Arachova, a mountain village with stone houses, boutiques, and tavernas where you'll have lunch. It's charming, but don't overspend on souvenirs — prices are inflated for tourists.

### Saronic islands cruise — Hydra, Poros, Aegina

The most relaxing day trip: three different islands, each with a distinct personality.

**Hydra** — cars are banned; the only vehicles are donkeys and boats. Stone streets, shipowners' mansions, artists' studios. The most photogenic island in the Saronic Gulf.

**Poros** — green, with pine trees reaching the shore, peaceful. Perfect for a short stroll and a lemonade at the harbour.

**Aegina** — the largest of the three. Famous for pistachios (genuinely the best in the world), the Temple of Aphaia (€8 entry, spectacular), and fresh-fish tavernas at the harbour.

**Insider:** If you prefer independence, take the regular ferry from Piraeus to a single island (Hydra or Aegina) — €15-30 return, 1-2 hours crossing, and spend the whole day there at your own pace. Flying Dolphins (hydrofoils) are faster but pricier and you can't sit on deck.

### Cape Sounion — sunset at the Temple of Poseidon

The shortest and most romantic day trip. The Temple of Poseidon (440 BC) on a cliff 60 metres above the Aegean, with the most photographed sunset in Greece. 70 km from Athens, about 2 hours along the Riviera coast.

**Organised tour price:** €30-50, afternoon tour (4-5 hours). **Temple entry:** ~€10.

**When to go:** Sunset tours depart around 15:00-16:00 (varies by season). The sun sets over the islands on the horizon — it's spectacular.

**Insider:** On the road to Sounion, along the Athens Riviera coast, you pass local beaches — if you have a rental car, stop at Vouliagmeni Lake (thermal lake, €12 adults, €6 children, warm water year-round at 22-25°C).

## Cruise port experience — for cruise ship passengers

If you arrive in Athens by cruise (Piraeus port, the largest in the Eastern Mediterranean), you typically have 6-10 hours. Every minute counts.

### The 6-hour plan (minimum)

Port transfer → Acropolis (30 min) → Guided Acropolis tour (1.5h) → Walk down through Plaka (30 min) → Quick gyros lunch at Monastiraki (30 min) → Transfer back to port (30 min) → Safety buffer (2h).

### The 8-10 hour plan (ideal)

Port transfer → Acropolis (30 min) → Guided Acropolis + Agora tour (3h) → Plaka rooftop lunch (1h) → Acropolis Museum (1.5h) → Monastiraki shopping (1h) → Transfer back (30 min) → Buffer.

**Cruise-specific tour price:** €50-80/person (includes port-to-centre-to-port transfer).

**The "no ship left behind" guarantee:** Organised cruise tours guarantee you'll make it back to the ship on time. If the tour runs late due to their fault, they'll taxi you to the ship or even to the next port at their expense. Always communicate your exact embarkation time to the guide.

**DIY transport:** The metro from Piraeus (Line 1, green) reaches Monastiraki in 20 min (€1.20). Fast and cheap, but you lose time navigating the metro station. Taxi from port to Acropolis: €15-25.

**Recommendation:** If you arrive during metro hours — take the metro. It's fast, cheap, and drops you right at Syntagma (the heart of Athens). If you arrive at night — the X95 bus is the only public option, runs 24/7, and costs just €5.50.

**Taxi — fixed fare:** €40 daytime (05:00-00:00), €55 nighttime (00:00-05:00). The fare is set by law — if the driver asks for more, refuse. Only take taxis from the official queue at Exit 3, Arrivals Level. Or book Bolt/Uber from the app — often €5-10 cheaper.

### Taxis and ride-sharing in Athens

**Bolt and Uber** both work in Athens and are usually cheaper than taxis. Average ride in the centre: €4-8. **Beat** (formerly Taxibeat, now Free Now) is the popular Greek app that works the same way.

**Watch out:** Traditional taxis can have "surprises" — some take roundabout routes, others "forget" to start the meter. Apps eliminate these problems.

## Athens with kids — family guide

Athens works surprisingly well with children, even in summer, if you plan smartly.

### Family-friendly attractions

**Panathenaic Stadium** — entirely marble, site of the first modern Olympics (1896). Kids can run on the Olympic track. €10 with audio guide, under 6 free. Takes 45 minutes.

**National Garden** — park in the centre, next to Syntagma. Playgrounds, a small zoo, duck pond. Free entry. Perfect for the afternoon break.

**Vouliagmeni Lake** — thermal lake with warm water year-round (22-25°C). €12 adults, €6 children. 30-40 min by tram from the centre. Clean, calm water — ideal for young children.

**Museum of Illusions** — €12 adults, €8 children. Interactive installations, fun photos. Good for an hour when kids are tired of ruins.

**Athens Riviera beaches** — Voula Beach (€5 entry, shallow water, good for children) 30-40 min by tram from Syntagma.

### Practical tips with kids

**Early morning (8:00-11:00) is the only window for the Acropolis in summer** — after 11:00, it's too hot. Strollers/pushchairs are impossible on the Acropolis — bring a baby carrier. Children under 18 enter the Acropolis and most archaeological sites FOR FREE.

**Gyros pita is the perfect kids' food** — simply cooked meat, chips, yoghurt. Spanakopita (spinach pie) and tiropita (cheese pie) are other safe options. Pasta is on every Greek restaurant menu.

**Museum > ruins for kids.** The Acropolis Museum is air-conditioned, interactive, and has the glass floor showing excavations beneath — kids love it.

## Open-air cinema — the Athens summer experience

One of Athens' most beautiful traditions: open-air summer cinemas, running from May to October, with the screen under the stars. Some have Acropolis views.

**Cine Thissio** — the most famous. English-language films (Greek subtitles), with the illuminated Acropolis in the background. €8-9 per ticket. You sit on a wooden chair, drink a cold beer (€4-5), and watch a classic film with the Parthenon over the wall.

**Cine Paris** — in Plaka, on a rooftop, with panoramic views. Romantic atmosphere.

**Cine Dexameni** — in Kolonaki, in a hidden square. Frequented mainly by locals.

**Insider:** Open-air cinemas have a mid-film interval (traditional) — head to the bar for a cocktail or grab a gyros from the corner kiosk. It's part of the experience.

## Lycabettus Hill, Pnyx and other free viewpoints

### Lycabettus — the supreme panorama

The highest point in central Athens (277 m). 360° views — Acropolis, sea, mountains, the entire city. Walk up (30 min, easy trail) or take the funicular from Kolonaki (€10 return). At the top: the Chapel of St George (19th century) and a restaurant (tourist prices, but the view is worth a coffee).

**Best time:** Sunset. Arrive 30-40 minutes before. Bring something to drink.

### Pnyx — where democracy was born

The hill next to the Acropolis where the Ecclesia (the democratic assembly of ancient Athens) gathered. Pericles, Demosthenes, and Themistocles spoke here. Less well-known than Lycabettus, but it offers the most beautiful angle of the Acropolis at sunset — the sun descends behind the temples.

**Free entry, always.** Short walk from Thissio metro station.

### Filopappou Hill

Pines, forest paths, and perfect Acropolis frames through the trees. The Filopappou Monument (114 AD) at the top. Less crowded than Lycabettus.

### Strefi Hill

The locals' viewpoint in Exarchia. Same view as Lycabettus, without a single tourist. Also has a free open-air summer cinema.

**Insider:** The best evening walk route: Thissio metro → Apostolou Pavlou street (pedestrian, with the Acropolis always in view) → Pnyx Hill at sunset → down through illuminated Plaka → dinner in Psyrri. 90 minutes of walking, zero euros, priceless experience.

## Markets, shopping and souvenirs

### Varvakios Agora (Central Market)

Athens' central meat and fish market — operating since 1886. The meat halls with hanging carcasses and shouting vendors are a sensory spectacle. Not for everyone, but authentic. Small tavernas in the basement serve patsas (tripe soup) and souvlaki at local prices (€3-5).

### Sunday flea market (Avyssinias)

Avyssinias Square in Monastiraki transforms on Sundays into a massive flea market — vinyl records, ceramics, old furniture, coins, jewellery, everything. Come before 9:00 AM for the best pieces and prices.

### What's actually worth buying in Athens

**Authentic products (not bazaar souvenirs):** extra virgin olive oil (€8-15 for a 500ml bottle), Aegina pistachios (€5-8/100g), thyme or lime blossom honey (€6-12), natural olive oil soap (€3-5), dried Greek herbs (oregano, mountain thyme — €2-4), artisanal ceramics (€10-30), natural sponges (€5-15 depending on size).

**Avoid:** "Made in Greece" items from souvenir shops on the main streets — most are manufactured in China. Look for shops with their own production or local markets.

## Festivals and events 2026

**Athens & Epidaurus Festival (June-September)** — Greece's most important cultural festival. Ancient theatre, opera, and contemporary music performances at the Odeon of Herodes Atticus (5,000-seat Roman amphitheatre at the foot of the Acropolis) and the ancient theatre of Epidaurus (2h from Athens). Tickets: €15-80. Seeing a Greek tragedy in a 2,300-year-old theatre is unique on this planet.

**Apokries (Greek Carnival, January/February)** — parades, costumes, water fights, street parties in Plaka and Monastiraki.

**Athens International Film Festival (October)** — screenings at historic venues, director Q&A sessions.

**Panathenaea (July/August)** — celebration of the goddess Athena with traditional processions and music.

**Insider:** Check the Odeon of Herodes Atticus programme before your visit — if there happens to be a performance on your evenings in Athens, it's an experience you need to have. The amphitheatre's acoustics are perfect — you can hear every word from the last row, without amplification.

Athens isn't just a city — it's also the gateway to the Greek islands. From Piraeus, Rafina, and Lavrio, ferries depart for virtually every island in the Aegean.

## Athens at sunset — the best spots

Sunsets in Athens are spectacular because of the Mediterranean light quality (dry air, clear sky, reflections on white marble). Here's the ranking:

1. **Cape Sounion** — Temple of Poseidon with the sun descending into the sea. Most dramatic. But requires a 2h trip.
2. **Pnyx Hill** — Acropolis in golden light on the right, sunset on the left. Free, 10 min from Thissio.
3. **Lycabettus** — 360° panorama, the entire city in golden hour light.
4. **Areopagus (Mars Hill)** — rock at the foot of the Acropolis where St Paul preached. Crowded in the evening, but the view is worth it.
5. **A for Athens rooftop** — cocktail + illuminated Acropolis. Commercial, but it works perfectly.

## Things nobody tells you about Athens

**1. Metro stations are museums.** Syntagma station has display cases with artefacts discovered during excavation. Acropolis station has frieze replicas. Monastiraki has Roman-era exhibits. You rush past them, but if you stop for 5 minutes, it's fascinating.

**2. The changing of the guard at Syntagma is more than a tourist show.** Sundays at 11:00 have the full ceremony with military band. But the regular change (every hour) is equally interesting — the Evzones (presidential guard) wear traditional costumes with fustanella (the kilt with 400 pleats, one for each year of Ottoman occupation), 3 kg pom-pom shoes, and ritualised movements lasting 15 minutes.

**3. Greeks don't eat dinner before 21:00-22:00.** If you're at a restaurant at 19:00 and it's empty — it's not a bad sign. It's a sign you're a tourist. Restaurants fill up after 21:30. If you want the authentic experience, adapt.

**4. "Oxi" (no) is said with an upward head tilt.** And "Nai" (yes) with a slight downward nod. Opposite to what you're used to. This creates hilarious confusion when ordering food.

**5. Tap water is safe to drink in Athens.** It comes from Lake Marathon and it's perfectly fine. Bring a reusable bottle and save €3-5/day on bottled water.

**6. Menu prices include VAT and service.** Tipping is not mandatory in Greece, but it's appreciated. Round up the bill or leave 5-10% for good service. You don't need to leave 15-20% as in the US.

**7. WiFi is excellent almost everywhere.** Cafés and restaurants have free WiFi as standard. But get an eSIM for GPS navigation and Bolt — it's more reliable than public WiFi.

**8. Ancient ruins appear literally everywhere.** Not just on the Acropolis. Walking down the street, a metal grating in the pavement reveals excavations beneath your feet. Enter a shopping centre, a glass wall reveals a Roman aqueduct. Under Syntagma Square is a museum of sites discovered during metro construction. Athens is literally built on layers of civilisation.

**9. The stray dogs are semi-adopted by the city.** They're chipped, vaccinated, and neutered by the municipality. They're friendly, laze in the shade near archaeological sites, and locals feed them. Don't be scared — they're part of the Athenian urban landscape.

**10. The best tavernas look the worst.** If a restaurant has laminated menus with photos and someone at the door inviting you in — run. If it has paper tablecloths, a chalkboard menu, and is full of Greeks — go in. The golden rule of culinary Athens.
