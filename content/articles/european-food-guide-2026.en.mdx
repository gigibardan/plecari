## Why food is the real reason to travel Europe

Museums close. Landmarks get scaffolding. Weather disappoints. But food never lets you down. The meal you eat at a tiny trattoria in [Rome](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-roma) — handmade pasta, a glass of house wine, the waiter who treats you like family — becomes the memory that defines the trip. Not the Colosseum (though that's spectacular too). The pasta.

This guide covers what to eat in 12 European countries, what it actually costs, where to find the authentic version (not the tourist version), and the unwritten rules locals follow.

## Italy — the undisputed champion

### The dishes

**Cacio e pepe** — [Rome's](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-roma) signature pasta: pecorino cheese, black pepper, and pasta water emulsified into a creamy sauce without cream. Three ingredients, infinite skill. €10-12 at a proper trattoria. **Da Enzo al 29** (Trastevere) does the definitive version.

**Pizza Napoletana** — soft, charred, puffy crust with San Marzano tomatoes and fresh mozzarella. Naples invented pizza and nobody has topped it in 300 years. A margherita costs €5-7 in Naples, €8-12 in [Rome](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-roma).

**Gelato** — not ice cream. The Italian version has less fat, more intensity, and a texture that's denser and silkier. Look for "produzione propria" (made on site) and natural colours (neon pistachio = artificial). €3-4 for two scoops.

**Pizza al taglio** — Rome's street food: rectangular pizza sold by weight. Point at what looks good, they cut and weigh. €3-6 for a generous serving. **Bonci Pizzarium** (near Vatican) is world-famous for a reason.

### The rules

- **Coffee at the bar (standing):** €1-1.50. Sitting at a table: €3-5. The surcharge is legal and traditional.
- **Cappuccino is a morning drink.** After 11 AM, Italians drink espresso only. Ordering a cappuccino after lunch marks you as a tourist (but nobody will refuse to make one).
- **"Coperto" (cover charge):** €2-4/person at restaurants. It's legal, normal, and covers bread + table setting. Not a scam — [actual scams are different](/en/asigurari/travel-scams-europe-2026).

## Spain — tapas culture is a way of life

### The dishes

**Pintxos** — Basque Country's version of tapas: small bites on bread, displayed on the bar counter. You take what you want, each piece is €2-3. The tradition: eat 2-3 at one bar, move to the next, repeat. A full evening of bar-hopping costs €15-25 and you've eaten like royalty.

**Jamón ibérico** — cured ham from acorn-fed pigs. The best food product in Spain (arguably in Europe). Sliced thin, eaten with bread and olive oil. €15-25/plate at a restaurant, or €3-5 for a tasting at a market.

**Paella** — Valencia's rice dish with saffron, seafood (or rabbit + snails for the original). €12-18/portion. DO NOT eat paella in [Barcelona](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-barcelona) tourist restaurants — it's usually reheated. Eat it in Valencia, or at a reputable restaurant that makes it fresh.

### The rules

- **Lunch is the main meal** (2-3 PM). Many restaurants offer a "menú del día" (set menu): starter + main + dessert + drink for €10-15. The best value in European dining.
- **Dinner starts at 9-10 PM.** Restaurants that are full at 7 PM are full of tourists. Go at 9:30 for the local experience.
- **Pintxos bar-hopping:** never eat more than 3 items at one bar. The tradition is to MOVE — each bar has its specialty.

## Greece — simplicity as genius

### The dishes

**Souvlaki/Gyro** — meat on a spit, wrapped in pita with tzatziki, tomato, onion, and chips. €3-4 in [Athens](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-atena). The most satisfying €4 you'll spend in Europe. **O Thanasis** (Monastiraki) is the classic.

**Greek salad (horiatiki)** — tomato, cucumber, onion, olives, feta, olive oil. No lettuce (that's not Greek). Deceptively simple, insanely good when the tomatoes are sun-ripened. €5-7.

**Moussaka** — layered aubergine, minced meat, and béchamel. Greek comfort food. €8-12 at a taverna.

**Fresh fish** — grilled whole, by the kilo, at a harbour taverna. €12-20/portion. The simplicity is the point — fresh fish needs only olive oil, lemon, and fire.

### The rules

- **Eat at tavernas with menus in Greek.** English-only menus = tourist restaurant = 30-40% more expensive, 50% less authentic.
- **Sharing is expected.** Greeks order communally — 3-4 dishes for the table, everyone eats from everything.
- **Tsipouro/ouzo with meze** — the Greek aperitif tradition. Order a bottle of tsipouro (€5-8) and it comes with free small plates of cheese, olives, and bread.

## Turkey — the feast for €5

### The dishes

**Turkish breakfast (kahvaltı)** — the full spread: 10-15 small plates of cheese, olives, honey, kaymak (clotted cream), eggs, tomatoes, cucumbers, bread, and unlimited tea. €5-8/person at a breakfast café in [Istanbul](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-istanbul). The most generous breakfast tradition in the world.

**Kebabs** — not just döner. İskender (over bread with tomato sauce and yogurt), Adana (spicy minced), and beyti (rolled with cheese). Full meal: €5-8 at local spots.

**Simit** — sesame bread ring. €0.30 from street carts. Turkey's most democratic food — everyone from CEO to taxi driver eats simit.

**Baklava** — layers of filo pastry with pistachios and honey syrup. €3-5/portion. **Karaköy Güllüoğlu** in Istanbul is the benchmark.

### The rules

- **Tea is social currency.** Accept every offer — declining tea is declining friendship.
- **Street food is not "lesser" food.** Some of Turkey's best eating happens at €1-3 from carts and hole-in-the-wall shops.
- **Bargain at markets, never at restaurants.** Restaurant prices are fixed.

[Book an Istanbul food tour](https://www.getyourguide.com/istanbul-l56/?partner_id=EKAA80H) (€30-50, 3 hours, 8-10 tastings across 2-3 neighbourhoods).

## Hungary — paprika-powered soul food

**Goulash (gulyás)** — a SOUP, not a stew (that's a common confusion). Rich, paprika-red, with beef, potatoes, and caraway. €4-5 at a local étkezde (canteen) in [Budapest](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-budapesta).

**Lángos** — fried dough topped with sour cream and grated cheese. €3-4 at Central Market Hall. Hungary's most addictive street food.

**Kürtőskalács (chimney cake)** — spiral pastry rolled in cinnamon-sugar, walnut, or coconut. €3-4. Originally from Transylvania ([Romania](/en/city-breaks/bucharest-city-break-guide) claims it too — and they're both right).

## Czech Republic — beer and dumplings

**Svíčková** — braised beef in creamy vegetable sauce with bread dumplings and cranberry. €8-10 at any hospoda (pub) in [Prague](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-praga). The national dish.

**Czech beer** — Czechs invented Pilsner and drink more beer per capita than anyone (140L/year). €2-3 at a local pub. Pilsner Urquell tank beer at **Lokál** in Prague is liquid gold.

## Portugal — Europe's most underrated kitchen

**Pastéis de nata** — custard tarts with caramelised tops. €1.20 each. Perfect with an espresso (€0.80). Eat 3 — it's expected.

**Bacalhau (salt cod)** — Portugal has 365 recipes for salt cod, one for each day. **Bacalhau à Brás** (shredded cod with eggs, potatoes, olives) is the classic.

**Francesinha** — Porto's insane sandwich: layers of meat, covered in melted cheese, drenched in spicy beer sauce. €8-10. Not healthy. Absolutely essential.

## Croatia — slow-cooked Mediterranean

**Peka** — meat or octopus slow-cooked under an iron bell with potatoes and vegetables. €15-20/portion, feeds 2. Croatia's signature dish — order it 2 hours ahead (it takes that long to cook). Essential along the [Dalmatian coast](/en/inchirieri-auto/road-trip-croatia-split-dubrovnik).

**Black risotto (crni rižot)** — risotto made with cuttlefish ink. Looks dramatic (completely black), tastes incredible. €12-15.

## Romania — the undiscovered food destination

**Mici** — grilled minced meat rolls with garlic, thyme, and cumin. €3-5 for a plate with mustard and bread. Romania's national street food. Best at: any grill with smoke pouring out. [Bucharest guide](/en/city-breaks/bucharest-city-break-guide).

**Sarmale** — cabbage rolls with pork and rice. Romania's comfort food, served at every celebration.

**Ciorbă de burtă** — tripe soup with garlic, vinegar, sour cream. Sounds challenging, tastes incredible.

[Book a Bucharest food tour](https://www.getyourguide.com/bucharest-l215/?partner_id=EKAA80H) (€30-50, 3 hours, 5-7 tastings).

## Austria — imperial elegance on a plate

**Wiener Schnitzel** — veal, pounded thin, breaded, fried in butter. €12-16 at a proper Gasthaus in [Vienna](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-viena). **Figlmüller** is the most famous.

**Sachertorte** — dense chocolate cake with apricot jam. €8/slice at **Café Sacher**. Worth every cent.

## Netherlands — more than cheese and tulips

**Hering (Hollandse Nieuwe)** — raw herring with onions and pickles. €3-4 from any haringhandel in [Amsterdam](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-amsterdam). Hold it by the tail, tilt your head back, and eat. The Dutch way.

**Bitterballen** — fried ragout balls, the perfect bar snack. €6-8/portion with mustard.

**Stroopwafel** — two thin waffles with caramel syrup. €2-3 fresh from a market stand.

## The universal eating rules

**1. Walk one street away.** Restaurants on the main tourist drag charge 30-50% more for worse food. One block back = better food, lower prices.

**2. If the menu is only in English, leave.** A menu in the local language (with English translation) means locals eat there. English-only = tourist trap.

**3. Lunch is cheaper than dinner.** Many restaurants offer lunch set menus at 30-40% less than dinner prices (especially in France, Spain, Italy).

**4. Markets beat restaurants for lunch.** [La Boquería](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-barcelona) in Barcelona, Central Market in [Budapest](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-budapesta), Borough Market in London, Albert Cuyp in [Amsterdam](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-amsterdam) — fresher, cheaper, more fun.
