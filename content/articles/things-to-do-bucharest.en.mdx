Bucharest surprises. It's not in any top 10 of European destinations, it doesn't have an Eiffel Tower or a Colosseum, and that's exactly why it's so good: incredibly low prices, excellent food, a raw energy that "tourist" cities lost long ago, and an architectural mix you won't find anywhere else — Belle Époque palaces glued to communist blocks glued to hipster bars with hidden courtyard gardens.

A full breakfast costs 20-35 RON (€4-7). A meal at a traditional restaurant, with everything, comes to 50-100 RON (€10-20). Metro: 5 RON. Specialty coffee: 12-18 RON. You can eat royally, visit museums, and go out at night on a budget that in Vienna or Amsterdam would barely cover lunch.

This guide is written for those who want to explore Bucharest beyond the tourist checklist — with real prices, actual addresses, insider tips, and complete honesty.

## Essential Attractions — What Not to Miss

### Palace of Parliament

The world's second-largest administrative building (after the Pentagon). 340,000 m², 1,100 rooms, 12 floors, 4 underground levels. Built by Ceaușescu between 1984-1997, it's impressive regardless of what you think of the regime that commissioned it.

**Visit details:**

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Adult price | 85 RON (~€17) |
| Student price | 55 RON (~€11) |
| Children 7-18 | 40 RON (~€8) |
| Under 7 | Free |
| Hours | Daily 09:00-17:00 |
| Tour duration | ~45-60 minutes |
| Booking | Required, minimum 24 hours in advance |

**What you need to know:**
- Can **only be visited with an organised tour** — you cannot enter independently
- You need **photo ID** (passport or national ID) at the entrance
- The standard tour covers the main halls. The rooftop terrace tour is available seasonally and worth the extra — the panoramic view of Bucharest from above is spectacular
- In summer and on weekends it fills up quickly — **book online at cdep.ro** or through GetYourGuide
- The entrance is on Bulevardul Națiunilor Unite (the back side, not the façade on Calea 13 Septembrie)

**Insider tip:** the best time is the first hour of the morning (09:00) or the last tour of the day — smaller groups, less crowding.

### Old Town (Lipscani)

Bucharest's historic core: pedestrian streets, the Old Princely Court (built by Vlad the Impaler in 1459), Manuc's Inn (the city's oldest inn, from 1808), Macca-Vilacrosse Passage (a covered arcade with yellow glass vaulting from 1891), and dozens of churches hidden between bars and restaurants.

**Why a guided tour is worth it here:** on your own, you'll see the pedestrian streets and bars. With a guide, you'll learn what's beneath your feet (the ruins of the Princely Court), why the Macca-Vilacrosse passage is forked, the story of the caravanserai inns, and where the Jewish neighbourhoods demolished by Ceaușescu once stood.

**Exploration options:**

| Type | Price | Duration | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| On your own | Free | 2-3 hours | Main streets + visible churches |
| Free walking tour | Tips (20-50 RON / €4-10 recommended) | 2-2.5 hours | History + context + hidden spots |
| Guided walking tour | €15-25 | 2-3 hours | Dedicated guide + inner courtyards |
| Electric retro car tour | €30-50 | 1.5-2 hours | Covers more ground + memorable experience |
| Tuk-tuk tour | €45-60 | 1.5-2 hours | Parliament + Old Town + Arch of Triumph |

**Don't miss in the Old Town:**
- **Old Princely Court** — ruins of Vlad the Impaler's 1459 palace, ~10 RON entry
- **Stavropoleos Church** — the most beautiful church in Bucharest, Brâncovenesc style, free entry
- **Macca-Vilacrosse Passage** — yellow glass vaulting from 1891, terrace for coffee
- **Manuc's Inn** — traditional restaurant in an inn from 1808
- **Strada Francmasonă** — the narrowest street in the centre, easy to miss

### Calea Victoriei

Bucharest's oldest boulevard (2.7 km), from Piața Victoriei to Splaiul Independenței. It's an architecture lesson: the Romanian Athenaeum, CEC Palace, Military Circle, Cantacuzino Palace (Enescu Museum), Casa Capșa, and the Royal Palace (National Art Museum).

**Recommended walk:** start at **Piața Victoriei** (metro M1/M3/M4) and head south. Along the way, stop at the Romanian Athenaeum (15 RON entry), the Enescu Museum (25 RON), and coffee at Casa Capșa (espresso ~15 RON, pastry ~20 RON — worth it for the atmosphere dating from 1852). Complete walk: 1.5-2 hours without stops, 3-4 hours with visits and coffee.

**Insider tip:** on Saturdays and Sundays, sections of Calea Victoriei become pedestrian — ideal for walking without cars.

### Romanian Athenaeum

The neoclassical concert hall that is the symbol of Romanian culture. Interior with a circular fresco depicting Romanian history. The acoustics are legendary — if you catch a concert by the "George Enescu" Philharmonic, buy a ticket.

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Daytime visit | 15 RON (~€3) |
| Concert | 30-200 RON (€6-40) |
| Visit hours | Variable, check online |
| Location | Str. Benjamin Franklin 1-3 |

### Revolution Square

Where the Ceaușescu regime fell in December 1989. The former Central Committee headquarters (from whose balcony Ceaușescu gave his last speech), the Rebirth Memorial, the National Art Museum (in the former Royal Palace), and the Central University Library.

**Communism tour:** one of the best tours in Bucharest. Lasts 2.5-3 hours, costs €20-35, and covers Revolution Square, the Palace of Parliament (exterior), systematised neighbourhoods, and the guide's personal stories about life under communism. Book on GetYourGuide — tours with 4.9+ rating are the good ones.

## Museums — What's Worth It and What You Can Skip

### Must-See Museums

**National Village Museum (Dimitrie Gusti)**

The best museum in Bucharest and one of the finest ethnographic museums in Europe. Over 300 authentic peasant houses, wooden churches, and windmills brought from every region of Romania, arranged on the shores of Herastrau Lake.

| Item | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Adult price | 40 RON (~€8) |
| Student price | 10 RON (~€2) |
| Hours (summer) | 09:00-19:00 daily |
| Hours (winter) | 09:00-17:00 daily |
| Visit duration | 1.5-3 hours |
| Metro | Aviatorilor (M2) |

**Why it's worth it:** if you don't have time to travel through rural Romania, the museum brings rural Romania to you. The houses are authentic (not replicas) — they were dismantled, transported, and reassembled here from the 1930s onward.

**Grigore Antipa Natural History Museum**

The most interactive museum in Bucharest, excellent for families with children. T. Rex skeleton cast, animal dioramas, biodiversity section. Fully renovated with modern exhibitions.

**National Museum of Art of Romania (MNAR)**

Housed in the former Royal Palace on Calea Victoriei. Medieval Romanian art, European art (El Greco, Rembrandt), and modern Romanian art (Grigorescu, Luchian, Brâncuși). First Wednesday of each month: free entry.

Traditional textiles, ceramics, icons, and folk objects. The presentation is unconventional and intimate — more "living art" than "dead history". In the basement: the communism exhibition is one of the best in Romania.

## Parks and Urban Nature

### Herastrau Park (King Michael I Park)

Bucharest's largest park: 187 hectares, lake with boats, Japanese Garden, cycling paths, lakeside terraces, and the Village Museum on its edge.

| Activity | Price | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Park entry | Free | Open 24/7 |
| Rowing boat (1 hour) | 30-40 RON | From the central pier |
| Bicycle rental | 15-25 RON/hour | Multiple stations in the park |
| Japanese Garden | 10 RON | Wed-Sun 10:00-20:00 |

**How to get there:** metro Aviatorilor (M2), 5-minute walk.

**What to do:** walk along the lakeshore (full loop: ~5 km, 1-1.5 hours), cycling, boating, stop at the Village Museum, lunch at one of the lakeside terraces (Hard Rock Cafe, Pescăruș, Berăria H).

### Cișmigiu Gardens

Bucharest's oldest park (1847), right in the centre on Bulevardul Regina Elisabeta. Lake with boats, an avenue lined with busts of Romanian writers, and a terrace for coffee. It's 10 minutes' walk from the Old Town — perfect for a break from the pavement.

### Botanical Garden

Hidden behind Cotroceni, near the Presidential Palace. Tropical greenhouses, rose gardens, and a tranquillity you wouldn't believe exists in Bucharest. Entry: 10 RON (~€2) + 15 RON (~€3) for greenhouses.

### Văcărești Delta

Bucharest's green surprise: a 183-hectare urban nature park, declared a protected area in 2016. A former artificial lake left abandoned and spontaneously transformed into an ecosystem with pelicans, herons, turtles, and frogs. It's Europe's only "urban delta".

**How to get there:** metro Timpuri Noi (M1/M3), then 10-minute walk. Free entry. Best: April-October for birdwatching.

## Therme Bucharest — The Thermal Complex

Europe's largest thermal complex, 21 km from the centre (near the airport, in Balotești). Three zones, each with its own personality.

### Zones

| Zone | What's included | Best for | Age |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Galaxy** | 1.7 km of water slides, wave pool, children's activities | Families | Ages 3-14 welcome |
| **The Palm** | Mineral pools, retractable glass roof, hydromassage, botanical gardens | Adults | 16+ only |
| **Elysium** | 10 themed saunas, spa treatments, fine dining | Premium relaxation | 16+ only |

### Prices 2026

| Duration | Galaxy (Mon-Thu) | The Palm (Mon-Thu) | Elysium (Mon-Thu) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 hours | 71 RON (~€14) | 102 RON (~€20) | 132 RON (~€26) |
| 4.5 hours | 101 RON (~€20) | 132 RON (~€26) | 162 RON (~€32) |
| Full day | 141 RON (~€28) | 172 RON (~€34) | 202 RON (~€40) |

Weekends and holidays: +10-15 RON per category. Overtime: 10 RON per 30 minutes.

### Practical Tips

- **Book online** — at the counter, queues can be 30-60 minutes, especially on weekends
- **Monday-Thursday** is significantly quieter and cheaper
- **Go at opening** or after 19:00 for the fewest people
- **RFID system** — your entrance bracelet works as internal payment for food and drinks
- **Bring swimwear and flip-flops** — rentals available but more expensive
- **How to get there:** Bolt/Uber (~30-40 RON from centre, 25-35 min), bus 442 (3 RON, slower), or car (free parking)

**Recommendation:** adults-only? Get The Palm (best value). With children? Galaxy is a must. Elysium is worth it only if you want premium spa and saunas.

## Bucharest's Neighbourhoods — Where to Go Beyond the Centre

### Cotroceni — "The Bohemian Quarter"

15 minutes from the centre by metro (Eroilor station, M1/M3/M5). Quiet streets with Neo-Romanian villas, the Botanical Garden, tea houses, bookshops, and a "village in the middle of the city" atmosphere. The Cotroceni Palace (presidential residence) is here.

**What to do:** stroll along Dr. Dumitru Bagdasar street and surroundings, Botanical Garden (10 RON), coffee at Acuarela or Ceainăria Infinitea, lunch at a local restaurant.

**Best for:** couples, those who want peace, architecture enthusiasts.

### Floreasca — "The Modern Quarter"

Northeast Bucharest, with the highest concentration of upscale restaurants, rooftop bars, and modern office buildings. Sky Tower (Romania's tallest building) is here. Floreasca Market is a traditional market with fresh produce.

**Best for:** business travellers, modern gastronomy fans, upscale nightlife.

### Dorobanți — "Little Paris"

Embassies, interwar villas, tree-lined avenues, refined cafés. It's the most "Parisian" neighbourhood in Bucharest and the most beautiful for an architecture walk. Strada Dacia and the surrounding streets are ideal.

**Best for:** architecture lovers, quiet strolls, urban photography.

### Obor — "Authentic Bucharest"

Working-class neighbourhood, untouched by tourism, home to Bucharest's oldest market (Piața Obor). This is where you'll eat the best mici in the city at Terasa Obor — where locals go, not tourists.

**What to do:** Obor market in the morning (cheeses, vegetables, flowers, pickles), mici at Terasa Obor (10-15 RON per serving), stroll through the neighbourhood to see unfiltered Bucharest.

**Best for:** foodie adventurers, urban photographers, authenticity seekers.

### Universitate — "The Student Quarter"

University Square, the National Theatre, bookshops, independent cafés, and university-area energy. The Universitate underpass was once a permanent protest site.

**What to do:** Cărturești Verona bookshop (inside a historic villa on Piata Lahovari), coffee at M60, evening drinks on Strada Blănari.

| **Snagov + Mogoșoaia** | ~40 km | 4-5 hours | €25-40 | Island monastery + palace | Optional |

### Bran + Brașov — The Classic Trip

The most popular day trip from Bucharest. Includes **Bran Castle** ("Dracula's Castle" — though the connection to Vlad the Impaler is more legend than history; entry: 60 RON adults, 15 RON children) and **Brașov** (superb medieval centre: Council Square, Black Church, Rope Street — one of the narrowest in Europe). Some tours include **Râșnov Fortress** with panoramic views.

**With an organised tour (€22-55):** transport, guide, and set route included. Castle entries usually separate. Departs early morning (~08:00), returns evening (~20:00).

**By car:** maximum flexibility. A3 motorway + DN1, ~2.5 hours. You can combine with Peleș Castle in the same day if you leave early.

### Peleș Castle + Sinaia

Romania's most beautiful castle (and arguably in all of Southeast Europe). German Neo-Renaissance style, spectacular interior: 170 rooms, Murano stained glass, arms collection, period furniture. Entry: 50 RON adults (standard), 80 RON (full tour).

### Transfăgărășan

The road described by Jeremy Clarkson (Top Gear) as "the best road in the world." Spectacular switchbacks over the Făgăraș Mountains, Bâlea Lake, and a chance to spot wild brown bears.

**Important:** open only **June-October** (depending on snow). Organised tour: €55-65, 12-14 hours, includes stops at Poenari Citadel (Vlad the Impaler's real fortress), Vidraru Dam, and Bâlea Lake.

## Getting Around Bucharest

### Metro

The fastest way to get around. 5 lines (M1-M5), good coverage of the centre and north. Price: 5 RON/ride or 12 RON for a 24-hour pass.

### Bolt / Uber

The most practical option for medium distances or at night. Very low prices compared to Western Europe: centre to Herăstrau (12-20 RON / €2.50-4), centre to Therme (30-40 RON / €6-8), centre to airport (50-80 RON / €10-16).

**Tip:** install Bolt and/or Uber before your trip. Don't take taxis off the street — Bolt/Uber eliminates the problem entirely with fixed prices and mapped routes.

## Practical Tips — Insider Knowledge

### Money and Payments

- **Currency:** Romanian leu (RON). Approximate rate: 1 EUR = ~5 RON
- **Cards:** accepted almost everywhere. Revolut, Wise, regular bank cards all work
- **Cash:** carry 50-100 RON for markets, some kiosks, and tips
- **ATMs:** use bank ATMs (BRD, BCR, ING, Raiffeisen) with small fees. **Avoid** Euronet (high fees + poor exchange rate)
- **Currency exchange:** **don't change** at exchange offices in the Old Town (terrible rates). Use ATMs or Revolut

### Tipping

- **Restaurants:** 10% is standard if service was good. Not obligatory
- **Free walking tours:** 20-50 RON
- **Taxis/Bolt:** tips not expected
- **Hotels:** 5-10 RON for porters

### Language

- **English:** young people (under 35) speak English well, especially in the centre, hotels, restaurants, and cafés
- **Menus:** most restaurants in the centre have English menus
- **Useful words:** "Mulțumesc" (mul-tzu-MESK) = thank you, "Bună ziua" (BU-nah ZI-wa) = hello, "Cât costă?" (kuht KOS-tah) = how much?

### Internet and Phone

- **Wi-Fi:** excellent throughout Romania — hotels, cafés, restaurants, even in the metro. Romania has one of Europe's fastest internet connections
- **EU roaming:** if you have an EU mobile plan, roaming is free
- **eSIM:** if coming from outside the EU, a Saily eSIM costs from €3-5 for 1 GB — dramatically cheaper than your carrier's roaming

## 10 Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Changing money at Old Town exchange offices** — you'll lose 5-15%. Use bank ATMs or Revolut.
2. **Taking taxis off the street** — use Bolt/Uber: fixed price, mapped route, card payment.
3. **Arriving at Parliament without a booking** — required 24 hours in advance. No booking, no entry.
4. **Staying only in the Old Town** — Bucharest's real charm is in Cotroceni, Obor, Floreasca, Dorobanți.
5. **Not learning about communism** — Bucharest is incomprehensible without historical context. A communism tour (€20-35) is the best investment.
6. **Going to Therme on a weekend without booking** — weekdays are a completely different experience.
7. **Not leaving enough time for Bran + Brașov** — it's a full-day trip (10-12 hours). Leave at 08:00 or earlier.
8. **Eating only in the Old Town** — the best restaurants are in Floreasca, Dorobanți, and Cotroceni.
9. **Not checking museum opening days** — most are closed Mondays (some Tuesdays too).
10. **Skipping Cișmigiu Gardens** — 10 minutes from the Old Town, perfect for a midday break.

## The Bottom Line: Why Bucharest Is Worth It

Bucharest isn't beautiful in the classic sense. It's not Prague, it's not Vienna, it's not Paris. It's something better: a city that's alive, with incredible food at absurdly low prices, fascinating history you discover at every corner, and an energy that "perfect" cities lost long ago.

The best way to explore: spend one day on the classic attractions (Parliament, Old Town, Calea Victoriei), one day on museums and parks, and at least half a day in a non-touristy neighbourhood with mici at Obor or coffee in Cotroceni. If you can, add Therme and a day trip to Brașov.

On a budget of €40-80/day, you'll live at a level that would cost double or triple in Western Europe. And the food — ah, the food — is the reason many people come back.
