Istanbul is the only city in the world that spans two continents. Europe on one side, Asia on the other, connected by ferries crossing the Bosphorus every 10 minutes. Layer on top: 2,500 years of history (Roman, Byzantine, Ottoman), the best street food in the Mediterranean basin, and prices that make London or Paris look like a different planet.

A full Turkish breakfast spread costs €5-8. A ferry across the Bosphorus costs ~€1. Turkish tea: €0.40. You can eat like royalty for €15/day.

## Sultanahmet — where empires meet

Sultanahmet is Istanbul's historic nucleus — three empires overlap within a single square kilometre. A Byzantine cathedral turned Ottoman mosque, a Roman hippodrome, an underground cistern, and the sultans' palace — all within walking distance.

### Hagia Sophia

For 1,000 years it was the world's largest cathedral (Byzantine, 537 AD). Then the Ottomans converted it to a mosque. Then Atatürk made it a museum. In 2020, it became a mosque again — and since 2024, non-Muslim visitors pay an entry fee.

**2026 prices:** €25 entry (pay at the ticket machines at the entrance or online). Children under 8 enter free. Hours: summer 8:00 AM-7:00 PM, winter 9:00 AM-7:00 PM. Fridays, closed 12:00-2:30 PM for prayers.

**Critical tip:** arrive at 8:00 AM — by 10:00 there are already 45-60 minute queues. The interior — 55 metres of dome, Byzantine gold mosaics, Islamic calligraphy — is overwhelming in the best way. Without a guide, it's "just" an impressive building. With a [guided tour](https://www.getyourguide.com/istanbul-l56/hagia-sophia-blue-mosque-t597339/?partner_id=EKAA80H) (€20-35), you witness 1,500 years of civilisational shift.

**Visiting rules:** remove shoes, women cover hair (scarves available free at the entrance). Modest dress required (shoulders and knees covered).

### Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed)

Six minarets, 20,000 hand-painted blue Iznik tiles, and 200 stained glass windows. Free entry. Closed during prayer times (5 times daily, ~30 minutes each).

**When to go:** the interior is most impressive at midday when natural light streams through the stained glass and floods the hall. Modest dress required (coverings provided at the entrance).

**Insider:** the mosque is undergoing extensive restoration (started 2024) but remains open to visitors — some sections may be covered by scaffolding.

### Basilica Cistern (Yerebatan Sarnıcı)

Underground Byzantine water reservoir from the 6th century — 336 marble columns, atmospheric lighting, and two mysterious Medusa head bases at the corner columns. A truly magical place, especially in the evening.

**2026 prices:** 1,950 TL (~€35) daytime, 3,000 TL (~€55) evening/night visit (special lighting, less crowded). Museum Pass NOT valid here. Visit takes 20-30 minutes but is unforgettable.

**Tip:** the evening visit is spectacular — the artistic lighting completely transforms the atmosphere, and crowds are minimal. Worth the price difference.

### Topkapi Palace

Residence of the Ottoman sultans for 400 years. Four successive courtyards, each more private and opulent than the last. The Treasury holds the 86-carat Spoonmaker's Diamond.

**2026 prices:** 2,750 TL (~€50) combined ticket (palace + Harem). The Harem (1,050 TL separately) is the absolute highlight — rooms with Iznik tiles, stories of intrigue and power. Closed Tuesdays. Museum Pass valid for the palace but NOT for the Harem.

**Recommendation:** a [guided tour](https://www.getyourguide.com/istanbul-l56/?partner_id=EKAA80H) makes a huge difference — without explanations, the rooms are beautiful but you miss the context. Best time: Tuesday-Thursday early morning.

### The Hippodrome and Obelisk

The open space in front of the Blue Mosque was the Roman hippodrome — the chariot racing arena from the Byzantine era, with a capacity of 100,000 spectators. Today, three ancient monuments mark the course: the Obelisk of Theodosius (brought from Egypt in 390 AD, 3,500 years old), the Serpent Column (from Delphi, 479 BC — the oldest Greek monument outside Greece), and the Walled Obelisk.

Free, always open — worth 15 minutes of attention on your way between Hagia Sophia and the Blue Mosque.

## Galata Tower and Karaköy

### Galata Tower

Medieval Genoese tower from 1348, with a 360° panorama of Istanbul — the Golden Horn, the Bosphorus, Sultanahmet, and the Princes' Islands on the horizon.

**2026 prices:** €30 entry (includes audio guide). Museum Pass valid. Queues can be long — buy a [skip-the-line ticket](https://www.getyourguide.com/istanbul-l56/?partner_id=EKAA80H) during peak season.

**Free alternative:** the café terrace on Galata Tower Street offers nearly the same view with a coffee for 80-150 TL (€1.50-2.70).

### Galata Bridge

The two-level bridge connecting Eminönü to Karaköy. Upper level — fishermen with rods (early morning or sunset is most authentic). Lower level — fish restaurants. The atmosphere is unique — grill smoke, fishermen's calls, and the view towards the New Mosque.

**Tip:** the bridge restaurants are touristy and expensive. Eat balık ekmek (fish sandwich) at the stands near Eminönü — 260-300 TL (€5-6), fresh and authentic.

### Karaköy neighbourhood

From Galata Bridge, walk up the steep streets towards the Tower. Karaköy has transformed in recent years — from port district to hub of speciality coffee shops, contemporary art galleries, and neo-Ottoman restaurants. Traditional sweet shops (Karaköy Güllüoğlu for the best baklava in Istanbul) coexist with third-wave coffee roasters.

## Dolmabahçe Palace

The most opulent palace in Istanbul — residence of the sultans from 1856, when they abandoned Topkapi for this European-style palace on the Bosphorus shore. The 4.5-tonne Baccarat crystal chandelier in the Throne Room is the world's largest. The ceremonial staircase with crystal balustrades is one of a kind.

**2026 prices:** 2,000 TL (~€36). Museum Pass NOT valid. Closed Mondays. The mandatory guided tour (included in price) takes 90 minutes — the Selamlık (reception) and Harem sections are visited separately.

**Tip:** go Tuesday-Thursday morning. Photography inside is prohibited — but the images stay in your mind.

## Grand Bazaar and Spice Bazaar

### Grand Bazaar (Kapalıçarşı)

4,000+ shops on 60+ covered streets — the world's oldest functioning mall, operating since 1461. Leather, ceramics, textiles, jewellery, Turkish lamps, carpets, and everything you can imagine.

**The rules of Turkish bargaining:**

| Rule | How it works |
|---|---|
| Always bargain | The initial price is 2-3x the real price |
| Start at 50% | Offer half the asking price |
| Settle at 60-70% | That's usually the correct price |
| Walk away | They'll call you back with a lower price |
| Don't buy at the first shop | Compare at least 3 shops |
| The deeper inside, the cheaper | Entrance shops have the highest markups |

**Free entry.** Open Monday-Saturday, 8:30 AM-7:00 PM. Closed Sunday. A [guided bazaar tour](https://www.getyourguide.com/istanbul-l56/grand-bazaar-t643510/?partner_id=EKAA80H) (€25-40, 1.5-2 hours) helps you navigate — you'll discover the secret passages, artisan workshops, and Ottoman hans (caravanserais) that most tourists never see.

**What's worth buying:** Iznik ceramics (check if hand-painted — authentic pieces have slight imperfections), mosaic lamps, tea and spices (from the Spice Bazaar — cheaper), Turkish cotton textiles, silver jewellery.

**What's NOT worth it:** "leather" that's actually synthetic (test with your fingernail — real leather doesn't scratch), "Persian carpets" at suspiciously low prices, and any "antique" that's too beautiful to be real.

### Spice Bazaar (Mısır Çarşısı)

Smaller, more concentrated, and infinitely more fragrant than the Grand Bazaar. Built in 1660, originally for trade with Egypt (hence the name — Mısır = Egypt).

**What to buy:** teas (pomegranate tea is excellent — 150-300 TL/100g), spices (sumac, pul biber, za'atar), Turkish delight/lokum (from Hacı Bekir — the oldest producer, since 1777, 200-400 TL per box), Turkish coffee from Kurukahveci Mehmet Efendi (100-200 TL/250g), and saffron (watch for fakes — real saffron is ~50-80 TL/gram).

**Tip:** for spices, buy from the shops behind the bazaar (on the side street) — same products, half the price.

### Arasta Bazaar

The calm alternative — behind the Blue Mosque. Smaller, fixed prices (no bargaining), good quality, zero stress. Slightly pricier than the Grand Bazaar but worth it if you don't have energy for negotiation.

## Bosphorus — the strait between continents

The Bosphorus is the 32-kilometre strait separating Europe from Asia — and Istanbul's most iconic experience. You sail between continents with views of Ottoman waterfront palaces, mosques, fortresses, and suspension bridges.

### What you see from the Bosphorus

European shore (south to north): Dolmabahçe Palace, Ortaköy Mosque beneath the Bosphorus Bridge, Rumeli Hisarı Fortress (built by Mehmed the Conqueror in 4 months in 1452, just before the conquest of Constantinople), and the yalıs (Ottoman waterfront wooden mansions — some worth tens of millions of euros).

Asian shore: the Maiden's Tower (Kız Kulesi — on a tiny islet at the entrance to the Bosphorus), Beylerbeyi Palace, Anadolu Hisarı Fortress, and fishing villages.

## Turkish hammam — the essential experience

A hammam is not a spa — it's a 600-year-old ritual. The traditional process: relax in the hot room on the göbektaşı (central heated marble stone), followed by peeling with the kese glove (intense exfoliation — your skin has never been this clean), foam washing, and optional oil massage.

### Kebab varieties you must try

Döner is just the beginning. Istanbul has dozens of regional kebab varieties:

- **Adana kebab** — spicy minced meat on a skewer, from south-eastern Turkey. Served with salad, rice, and lavash bread.
- **İskender kebab** — döner slices over bread with tomato sauce and yoghurt, drizzled with sizzling melted butter. The original is in Bursa (day trip — see below).
- **Beyti kebab** — kebab rolled in lavash with tomato sauce and yoghurt. Elegant and satisfying.
- **Cağ kebab** — horizontal spit kebab from Erzurum. Lamb carved directly from the spit. Best at Hocapaşa Sokak, near Sirkeci.
- **Tantuni** — finely diced beef cooked on a griddle with spices, served in lavash. Fast, cheap, perfect.

### Where to eat like a local

**Turkish breakfast (kahvaltı)** — the most impressive meal: cheeses (7-8 varieties), olives, honey, kaymak (clotted cream), eggs, tomatoes, cucumbers, fresh bread, and endless tea. 200-400 TL (€4-7) per person at a breakfast spot. **Van Kahvaltı Evi** in Cihangir is the classic.

**Local restaurant (lokal lokanta):** full meal with kebab/home-cooked food, salad, and drink: 250-500 TL (€5-9). Look for places with window displays of trays — the food is cooked in the morning and served throughout the day. Cheap, fast, and delicious.

**Meyhane (tavern):** the Turkish social experience — meze (appetisers) + raki (anise spirit) + fish. Full evening: 800-1,500 TL (€15-27) with alcohol. The Nevizade area (off İstiklal) is packed with meyhanes — the atmosphere is electric.

**Tourist restaurant:** 600-1,500 TL (€11-27). Variable quality — many in Sultanahmet are mediocre at inflated prices. Notable exception: Sultanahmet Köftecisi (legendary meatballs, since 1920).

### Organised food tours

The best areas for food tours:

- **Kadıköy (Asian side)** — fish market, baklava, local kebabs. The most authentic tour, away from tourists. 3-4 hours, €35-60, includes 6-10 stops.
- **Sultanahmet + Eminönü** — the classic route: spice bazaar, simit, balık ekmek, sand-brewed Turkish coffee. 2-3 hours, €30-50.

**Tip:** go on an empty stomach. Seriously — you'll eat a lot.

## Istanbul's neighbourhoods — beyond Sultanahmet

Istanbul is a universe of neighbourhoods, each with its own personality. Limiting yourself to Sultanahmet is like visiting only the old town of any city and thinking you've seen the place.

### Beyoğlu and İstiklal Caddesi

Istanbul's modern heart. İstiklal is the 1.4 km pedestrian boulevard where everything happens — from the nostalgic red tram (purely decorative, but photogenic) to churches hidden in passages, 19th-century embassies, and the densest concentration of cafés, restaurants, and shops in the city.

**Don't miss:** Çiçek Pasajı (Flower Passage) — a former flower arcade, now packed with meyhanes with atmosphere. Hazzopulo Passage — less known, equally charming.

### Balat

The colourful neighbourhood that conquered Instagram — painted Ottoman wooden houses on steep cobblestone streets. But Balat is much more than a photo backdrop: it was the Jewish quarter (Ahrida Synagogue dates from the 15th century), then Greek, then Armenian. Layers of history are visible in every wall.

**When to go:** early morning (8-9 AM), before the influencers. Have tea at one of the tiny cafés on the side streets, and explore the Gül Mosque (former Byzantine church) and the Bulgarian Iron Church (Sveti Stefan — the only church in the world built entirely from prefabricated cast iron, shipped up the Danube from Vienna in 1898).

### Kadıköy (Asian side)

Cross the Bosphorus by ferry from Eminönü (59 TL / ~€1, 20 minutes, spectacular views) and you arrive in a different world — local markets, streets lined with independent cafés, graffiti, vintage shops, and zero tourists. Kadıköy is where the locals go.

**Kadıköy Fish Market** is the centre — fresh fish at local prices, seafood, cheeses, and sweets. Kaymakçı Pando for the best kaymak (clotted cream) in Istanbul.

**The perfect day combo:** ferry Eminönü → Kadıköy + food tour through the market + stroll along the Moda waterfront (the bohemian coastal neighbourhood) + return ferry at sunset.

### Moda

The green peninsula south of Kadıköy — seaside promenade with views of the Princes' Islands, independent cafés, vintage shops on Kadife Sokak (Velvet Street), and the artistic atmosphere of a former Levantine quarter. The Moda waterfront walk at sunset is one of Istanbul's top free experiences.

### Beşiktaş

The local neighbourhood on the European shore — a fish market (more authentic than the touristy ones), university-area energy, and Dolmabahçe Palace. The area is popular with locals for meyhanes with fresh fish.

### Ortaköy

The Ortaköy Mosque "beneath" the Bosphorus Bridge is one of Istanbul's most photographed images. At weekends, the area fills with kumpir stalls (giant loaded baked potato — 350-600 TL), artisan crafts, and waterfront cafés. Relaxed, multicultural, with spectacular views.

### Üsküdar (Asian side)

Istanbul's contemplative side — Ottoman mosques, cypress-lined cemeteries, and the perfect view of the historic peninsula from the Asian shore. Salacak is the ideal spot for the classic photo with the Maiden's Tower (Kız Kulesi) in the background.

### Cihangir

The bohemian hillside neighbourhood — cafés with Bosphorus views, an expat and artist community, never-ending brunches, and a relaxed atmosphere. It's Istanbul's "Montmartre," but more accessible and more authentic.

## Asian side experiences

The Asian side is the half most tourists ignore — and it's a huge mistake. Crossing the Bosphorus by ferry is not just transport, it's an experience in itself.

### Maiden's Tower (Kız Kulesi)

The tower on the tiny islet at the entrance to the Bosphorus — legendary (in Turkish mythology, a sultan locked his daughter here to protect her from a prophecy). Recently restored, it functions as a restaurant and viewpoint. Access boats depart from Üsküdar or Salacak.

### Çamlıca Hill

Istanbul's highest point (268 m) — a complete city panorama: the Bosphorus, the Golden Horn, the Princes' Islands, and on clear days, Mount Uludağ 150 km away. Çamlıca Mosque (2019, Turkey's largest) is impressive architecturally. The park and gardens are perfect for a picnic.

Access: bus or taxi from Üsküdar (~60-100 TL / €1-2).

### Çengelköy

The Bosphorus village — a fishing village that has preserved its character despite the city's growth. Famous for the kokoreç at Çengelköy Tarihi Kokoreççisi and Çengelköy cucumbers (yes, they have their own cucumber variety). Perfect view of the Bosphorus Bridge from below.

## Day trips from Istanbul

### Princes' Islands (Adalar)

An archipelago of 9 islands in the Sea of Marmara — car-free, just bicycles, horse carriages (under debate whether they'll be eliminated), and total tranquillity after Istanbul's chaos. Büyükada (Big Island) is the most popular — Victorian wooden houses, fish restaurants, and beaches.

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Transport | Ferry Kabataş → Büyükada: 137 TL (~€2.50), 75-90 min |
| Alternative | IDO fast ferry: faster but more expensive |
| Activities | Bicycle around the island (50-100 TL/day), beach, fish lunch |
| Day budget | 300-600 TL (€6-11) plus ferry |
| Best days | Tuesday-Thursday — weekends are crowded |

**Tip:** take the 8:30 AM ferry — you have the whole day on the island and avoid queues. Bring food from the market or order fresh fish at local restaurants (200-400 TL for a full meal).

### Bursa (former Ottoman capital)

The former capital of the Ottoman Empire (1335-1453), at the foot of Mount Uludağ. Famous for the original iskender kebab (invented here), the Green Mosque (Yeşil Cami), Bursa silk, and the Çekirge thermal baths.

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Transport | IDO fast ferry Yenikapı → Mudanya: 2 hours + 30-min bus |
| Alternative | Direct bus: 2.5-3 hours |
| What to see | Green Mosque, Ottoman mausoleums, silk market, iskender kebab |
| Day budget | 500-1,000 TL (€9-18) plus transport |

### Edirne

The second Ottoman capital (1365-1453), at the border with Bulgaria and Greece. The Selimiye Mosque (UNESCO, Mimar Sinan's masterpiece — considered by the architect himself as superior to Hagia Sophia) is the main draw. Plus: Edirne white cheese, fried liver (ciğer), and the traditional oil wrestling festival (Kırkpınar, the world's oldest sporting event — 600+ years).

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Transport | Bus: 2.5-3 hours |
| What to see | Selimiye Mosque, Health Museum, Old Town |
| Day budget | 400-800 TL (€7-15) plus transport |

### Cappadocia (2 days)

Further away (1.5-hour internal flight), but on many visitors' lists. The hot air balloon ride at sunrise over the lunar landscape is one of the world's most spectacular experiences.

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Transport | Internal flight Istanbul → Kayseri/Nevşehir: €30-80 |
| 2-day package from Istanbul | €250-500 (flight + hotel + tour + optional balloon) |
| Balloon ride | €150-300 separately |
| What to see | Göreme, underground cities, valleys, cave churches |

**Alternative:** if you have 4+ days in Istanbul, take an early morning internal flight, do the day tour, and return in the evening. But 2 days/1 night is the ideal option.

## Getting around Istanbul — complete guide

### Istanbulkart — essential

The rechargeable card that works on metro, tram, bus, and ferry. It's essential — paying with cash or bank card on transport is much more expensive or impossible.

| Detail | 2026 Info |
|---|---|
| Card price | 165 TL (~€3) — from machines at metro stations |
| Single ride | 46.20 TL (~€0.85) |
| Transfer (within 2 hours) | Progressive discount on rides 2, 3, 4 |
| City ferry | 53-59 TL (~€1) |
| Princes' Islands ferry | 137 TL (~€2.50) — no transfer discount |

**How to buy:** machines at any metro station. Interface has English option. Top up with cash or card. You can use a single Istanbulkart for multiple people (tap multiple times).

### Metro and tram

The network is extensive and modern. Main lines:

- **Tram T1:** runs through Sultanahmet — from Kabataş (for the funicular to Taksim) via Eminönü, Sultanahmet, Grand Bazaar, to Bağcılar. The most useful tourist route.
- **Metro M2:** Taksim → Levent (business district). Connected to T1 via funicular F1 (Kabataş → Taksim).
- **Metro M11:** the new line from Istanbul Airport (IST) to the centre. Fast and cheap.
- **Marmaray:** the tunnel under the Bosphorus — Europe-to-Asia crossing in 4 minutes, no traffic. Functional, fast, not scenic but efficient.

### Ferries

Istanbul's most beautiful transport — and the most practical for crossing the Bosphorus.

| Route | Duration | Istanbulkart price |
|---|---|---|
| Eminönü → Kadıköy | 20 min | 59 TL (~€1) |
| Eminönü → Üsküdar | 15 min | 53 TL (~€1) |
| Beşiktaş → Kadıköy | 20 min | 59 TL (~€1) |
| Kabataş → Kadıköy | 20 min | 57 TL (~€1) |
| Kabataş → Princes' Islands | 75-90 min | 137 TL (~€2.50) |

### Taxi and ride-hailing

Yellow taxis are everywhere, but scams are common: "broken" meter, long routes, switched banknotes. **Use BiTaksi or Bolt** — the price is fixed, the route is on GPS, and payment is electronic.

**Approximate taxi/Bolt prices:**
- Sultanahmet → Taksim: 200-350 TL (€4-6)
- Taksim → Kadıköy (via Bolt): 250-400 TL (€5-7)
- IST Airport → Sultanahmet: 900-1,200 TL (€16-22)

### From the airport

Istanbul Airport (IST) is 40+ km from the centre. Options:

| Transport | Price | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metro M11 | 30-40 TL (~€0.60) | ~60 min + transfer | Cheapest, efficient |
| Havaist (bus) | 170-200 TL (~€3.50) | 60-90 min | Direct to Taksim, comfortable |
| Taxi/Bolt | 900-1,200 TL (€16-22) | 45-90 min | Fast if no traffic |
| Private transfer | €35-50 | 45-90 min | Book through hotel or GYG |

**Recommendation:** Metro M11 + transfer to M2/T1 is fastest and cheapest. Havaist only if you have heavy luggage and are going directly to your hotel.

**Sabiha Gökçen (SAW)** — the second airport, on the Asian side. Further from the centre (60+ km), but low-cost flights land here. Havaist bus: 170-200 TL, 90-120 minutes.

## 10 insider tips for Istanbul

**1. Hagia Sophia at 8:00 AM, not 10:00.** The difference is between a magical experience and an hour in a queue. At 8:00 you have the interior almost to yourself — you can sit on the carpet, gaze up at the mosaics, and feel the weight of 1,500 years of history.

**2. The ferry > the expensive cruise.** The Eminönü → Kadıköy ferry (€1) is just as beautiful as a €25 cruise. No guide, but the same spectacular views. Take it at sunset for maximum effect.

**3. Eat on the Asian side.** Everything on the European side at tourist prices you'll find on the Asian side at half the price and double the quality. Kadıköy is Istanbul's gastronomic mecca — locals eat here.

**4. Bargain everything in the Grand Bazaar — but not food.** In the bazaar, the initial price is 2-3x the real price. At restaurants and street food stalls, the listed price is the real price.

**5. Avoid the restaurants on Galata Bridge.** They're tourist traps — high prices, mediocre quality. Balık ekmek at the Eminönü stands is better and 5x cheaper.

**6. Istanbulkart from day one.** Without it, you pay double or triple for transport. 165 TL (€3) for the card, 46 TL (~€0.85) per ride — pays for itself from day one.

**7. Don't take a taxi from the airport without an app.** Airport taxi drivers are notorious for long routes. Bolt or BiTaksi — the price is fixed, the route is on GPS, zero stress.

**8. A cheap hammam is NOT a bad hammam.** Mihrimah Sultan (€25) or Çinili Üsküdar (€25) are authentic hammams with 0% tourists. The experience is just as good as the €100 ones — just without the perfumed towel and tea served on a silver tray.

**9. Spice Bazaar — buy from the back.** The entrance shops have big tourist markups. Go out to the street behind the bazaar — same spices, 40-50% cheaper.

**10. Accept the tea. Always.** Tea is Turkey's social language. It's offered in shops, during bargaining, in conversation — it's a form of politeness and friendship. It costs nothing — the experience is priceless.

## Practical information

### Documents and entry

**Passport required.** Turkey is NOT in the EU. Most nationalities (US, UK, EU, Canada, Australia) enter visa-free for up to 90 days. Passport must be valid for 6+ months from the date of entry. **National ID cards are NOT accepted** — a common mistake.

### Money

**Currency:** Turkish Lira (TRY). €1 ≈ 55 TRY (August 2026). Cards accepted at hotels, restaurants, and mall shops. Cash essential at bazaars, street food stalls, small taxis, and local hammams.

**Best rates:** [Revolut or Wise](/en/zboruri/budget-travel-tips-europe-2026) — 0% conversion fee. Avoid exchange offices in Sultanahmet (rates 5-10% below market). Major bank ATMs offer fair rates.

### Connectivity

**Turkey is NOT in the EU — free European roaming does NOT apply.** Your carrier will charge €3-15/day. An [eSIM from Airalo](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3) costs €4-5 for 1GB/7 days — dramatically cheaper. Install before departure.

### Travel insurance

**ESSENTIAL.** The European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) does NOT work in Turkey — you are not medically covered without private insurance. A hospital visit in Istanbul can cost 500-2,000 TL (€9-36) for a simple consultation, much more for emergencies. [Full insurance guide](/en/asigurari/travel-insurance-europe-guide). Recommendation: [EKTA](https://ektatraveling.tpo.mx/uU7jZ5jK) — comprehensive coverage, good price.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| March-May | 10-22°C | Medium | Medium | ✅ Ideal — pleasant weather, not crowded |

### Mosque etiquette

The rules are simple but strict: remove shoes (shoe bags available at the entrance), cover shoulders and knees (both sexes), women cover hair (free scarves at the entrance). During prayers (5 times daily, ~30 min), access is restricted. Don't talk loudly, don't eat, don't drink. Photography is permitted but be discreet.

### Tipping

10-15% at restaurants (check if service charge is already included). At hammams: 10-20% of the price. At hotels: 20-50 TL (€0.40-0.90) for the bellboy or cleaner. At taxis: round up.

### Istanbul's classic scams

**The "friend" who invites you to a bar:** a friendly man speaks your language perfectly, invites you to "his bar" for a beer. The bill arrives at €200-500. **Politely decline and walk away.**

**The taxi with a "broken" meter:** the driver says the meter isn't working. **Use Bolt/BiTaksi** or insist on the meter.

**The shoeshine man who "drops" his brush:** he polishes your shoes "by accident," then demands money. **Say no, keep walking.**

**The "special" price just for you:** in the Grand Bazaar, the initial price is 2-3x the real price. **Bargain from 50%.**

## What makes Istanbul different from the rest of Europe

Istanbul is not Europe and not Asia — it's both. This shows in every detail: the rhythm of the day follows prayer times, not office hours; a meal is not an individual act but a social event; "quickly" is not a concept Turks prize; and every transaction — from a tea to a carpet — is an opportunity for conversation.

It's a city that overwhelms on a first visit (the noise, the crowds, the scale) and one you love from the second. Don't try to "tick it off" — let yourself be carried by its rhythm.
