Many travellers assume that their European Health Insurance Card means they're fully covered abroad. It's the most expensive misconception in travel: the EHIC and travel insurance cover fundamentally different things, and the gap between them can mean a bill of tens of thousands of euros.

This guide explains exactly what each covers, where each leaves you exposed, what the difference costs in real money, and why the right strategy is "both" — not "one or the other."

---

## What the EHIC actually is

The European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) is a free document issued by your country's national health insurance authority. It entitles you to **medically necessary** healthcare during a temporary stay in another EU/EEA country — on the same terms as local residents.

### What "same terms as locals" really means

This is the key phrase most people misunderstand. "Same as locals" doesn't mean "free" — it means you pay what they pay:

- **In France:** a 20% co-payment on consultations + €18/day "forfait journalier" for hospital stays. You pay the same with your EHIC.
- **In Germany:** €10 emergency department fee at hospital presentation. The EHIC puts you on equal footing, but doesn't eliminate the co-payment.
- **In Spain:** emergency treatment is free in the public system. The EHIC works well here.
- **In Italy:** genuine emergencies are free, but non-urgent consultations carry a "ticket sanitario" (co-payment) of €25-50.
- **In Greece:** emergency treatment is free, but quality varies significantly between tourist islands and urban centres.
- **In the UK:** the EHIC/GHIC works, and NHS emergency care is free for everyone. But specialist appointments have waiting times of weeks.

**The takeaway:** the EHIC doesn't make you VIP — it makes you equal to locals in the public system. With all its strengths and limitations.

## What the EHIC does NOT cover — and what the gap costs

These situations are never covered by the EHIC, regardless of circumstances:

### 1. Medical repatriation — €15,000-250,000

Medical repatriation means organised transport back to your home country when you can't travel normally (serious fracture, major accident, illness requiring ongoing treatment at home). The EHIC doesn't cover a single euro of this.

**Real repatriation costs in 2026:**

| Route | Transport type | Estimated cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Western Europe → home country | Ground ambulance | €3,000-8,000 |
| Western Europe → home country | Air ambulance | €15,000-30,000 |
| Spain/Portugal → Northern Europe | Air ambulance | €20,000-35,000 |
| Europe → USA | Medicalised flight | $120,000-250,000 |
| Asia → Europe | Medicalised flight | $150,000-300,000 |
| Medical escort on commercial flight | Escort + business class tickets | €5,000-15,000 |

**Real example:** a tourist breaks their femur skiing in Austria. The operation in Austria is covered (partially) by the EHIC. But the medicalised transport back home — the air ambulance, medical equipment, medical staff — costs €18,000-25,000. Without insurance, the family pays in full.

### 2. Private clinics and doctors — variable costs

The EHIC covers only providers within the **public** healthcare system. The problem: in many tourist situations, accessing the public system isn't practical.

**Where you're forced into private care:**

- **Ski resorts:** medical centres on the slopes are almost always private. First aid + helicopter transport = private bill
- **Summer beach resorts:** clinics in resort towns (Crete, Costa del Sol, Algarve) are predominantly private
- **Cruise ships:** the ship's doctor is private; the hospital at the port of call is probably private
- **Greek islands:** on many small islands, the only option is a private clinic. The nearest public hospital may be on another island entirely
- **Rural areas:** in many tourist areas, the nearest public hospital can be 50+ km away

**Cost example:** consultation + X-ray at a private clinic on an Austrian ski slope: €200-500. At a public hospital in Innsbruck (with EHIC): €10 co-payment. But getting from the slope to Innsbruck already involves a transport bill.

### 3. Mountain rescue and search & rescue — €2,000-10,000

Search and rescue operations — including helicopters, ski patrol, mountain rescue teams — are not covered by the EHIC. At all.

**Rescue costs by country in 2026:**

| Country | Helicopter rescue | Ski patrol (on-piste) | Ambulance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Switzerland** | ~CHF 4,500 (~€4,700) average (Rega) | CHF 800-2,000 | CHF 700-2,100 |
| **France** | €2,000-6,000 | ~€300 (stretcher/sled) | €400-800 |
| **Austria** | €3,500-10,000 | €500-1,500 | €400-900 |
| **Italy** | €2,500-8,000 | €400-1,200 | €300-700 |
| **Germany** | €3,000-7,000 | €500-1,000 | €500-1,000 |

**Switzerland example:** you crash on a slope at Zermatt. The helicopter takes you to the hospital in Visp — bill of ~CHF 4,500. Swiss mandatory health insurance covers 50%, capped at CHF 5,000/year — but only for Swiss residents. As a tourist with your EHIC? You pay the full amount.

**The France exception:** public mountain rescue (PGHM — Peloton de Gendarmerie de Haute Montagne) is free in France for life-saving rescue — but only if life is in danger and you're on public land. On-piste (the resort's private domain), the resort's ski patrol charges €300-2,000. And a private helicopter is billed at €2,500/hour.

### 4. Trip cancellation and travel disruption — zero coverage

The EHIC is a health card — it has nothing to do with your trip itself. If your flight is cancelled, your luggage is lost, or you need to cancel your holiday for medical reasons, the EHIC covers nothing.

**What travel insurance covers (and the EHIC doesn't):**

| Situation | EHIC coverage | Insurance coverage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flight cancellation by airline | ❌ | ✅ Reimbursement + accommodation |
| Lost baggage | ❌ | ✅ Up to €1,000-2,000 |
| Delayed baggage (> 4-6 hours) | ❌ | ✅ €100-300 for essential items |
| Trip cancellation (personal illness/family) | ❌ | ✅ Reimbursement of prepaid costs |
| Personal liability (you cause damage on holiday) | ❌ | ✅ Up to €50,000-100,000 |
| Legal assistance abroad | ❌ | ✅ Legal support |

### 5. Dental care — minimal coverage

The EHIC covers emergency dental care in the public system only — and only "immediate medical necessity" (antibiotics, emergency extraction). A temporary crown, an implant, or root canal treatment is not considered an emergency. And private dental clinics (which are the majority in most EU countries) don't accept the EHIC.

**Cost example:** dental emergency (abscess) on holiday: €150-500 at a private clinic. With EHIC, at a public hospital: €0-30 co-payment. But finding a public dentist who takes emergencies at 10pm on a Saturday in Santorini isn't realistic.

## Common EHIC mistakes that cost travellers money

### 1. "I have the EHIC, so I'm fully covered"

The most dangerous myth. The EHIC covers at most 30-40% of situations you might face on holiday. Repatriation, private clinics, and mountain rescue — the most expensive ones — are completely excluded.

### 2. Not checking the expiry date

The EHIC is valid for 2 years (in most countries). If it's expired, it's as if you don't have one — the hospital won't accept it. Check **before every trip**, not at the airport.

### 3. Travelling outside the EU with only the EHIC

The EHIC doesn't work at all in Turkey, Egypt, the USA, or Asia. If you go to these countries with only the EHIC, you effectively have zero medical protection.

### 4. Assuming "necessary" means everything

"Medically necessary care" doesn't mean any visit to a doctor. Routine consultations, check-ups, and treatments that can be postponed until you return home are NOT considered "necessary" and can be refused.

### 5. Not carrying the physical card

Some hospitals don't accept photos of the card or screenshots. Carry the physical card in your wallet, not just on your phone.

### 6. Not knowing the co-payment system

If France charges a 20% co-payment on consultations, you pay 20% — even with the EHIC. Research the co-payment system of your destination country before you travel.

### 7. Applying too late

The card is delivered by post in 7-15 working days (varies by country). If you apply 3 days before departure, it won't arrive in time. You can request a Provisional Replacement Certificate (PRC), but it's an emergency solution, not a comfortable one.

## How to avoid a rejected insurance claim

Travel insurance claim rejection rates run at approximately 30-35%. The most common reasons and how to avoid them:

### Rejection reasons and prevention

| Rejection reason | Frequency | How to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Missing documentation** | ~40% of rejections | Keep ALL original receipts, prescriptions, medical reports. Photograph everything. |
| **Undeclared pre-existing conditions** | ~25% of rejections | Declare ANY chronic condition when buying the policy — even if well-controlled. |
| **Activity not covered** | ~15% of rejections | Check whether your activity (skiing, diving) is included or needs an add-on. |
| **Exceeded deadline** | ~10% of rejections | File the claim within the specified period (usually 30-90 days from the incident). |
| **Didn't contact insurer in time** | ~10% of rejections | Call the 24/7 helpline BEFORE going to the doctor (if the situation allows). |

### Successful claim checklist

1. **Call the insurer's 24/7 helpline** — first thing, if possible. They can direct you to a partner hospital (they pay directly, no advance from your pocket)
2. **Keep EVERYTHING:** original receipts, prescriptions, medical reports, flight tickets, hotel receipts, taxi/ambulance receipts
3. **Photograph** all documents — digital backup on phone and cloud
4. **Police report** — for theft, road accidents, or lost baggage. Without a police report, baggage claims are almost automatically rejected
5. **Detailed medical certificate** from the treating doctor — must state the diagnosis, treatment provided, and that it was an emergency
6. **File the claim** within the deadline (30-90 days) — with ALL documentation

## The verdict: 3 rules for complete protection

**Rule 1: Get your EHIC now.** It's free, takes 5 minutes online, and is valid for 2 years. Even if you have no holiday planned, have the card ready for any EU trip.

**Rule 2: Buy travel insurance for every trip.** €9-15 per week is nothing compared to a potential €5,000-30,000 bill. And it's your only protection outside the EU.

**Rule 3: Read the policy before you leave.** Check: does it cover your activities (skiing, diving)? Does it include repatriation? What's the medical coverage limit? What are the exclusions? 5 minutes of reading can save you from a rejected claim.

**The protection formula:** EHIC (free) + private insurance (€9-15/week) = complete protection for under €15. Without the EHIC, you lose the free basic coverage. Without private insurance, you're exposed on repatriation, private clinics, mountain rescue, and trip cancellation — exactly where the costs are catastrophic.
