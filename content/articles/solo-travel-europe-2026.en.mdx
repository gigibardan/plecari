## The truth about solo travel (that nobody tells you)

Here's what the Instagram solo travel accounts don't show: the first evening alone in a new city is awkward. You sit at a restaurant table for one, you're not sure where to look while eating, and you wonder if this was a mistake.

Then something shifts. By day two, you eat wherever you want without negotiating. You change your plans on a whim. You spend 3 hours in a museum because YOU wanted to, not because someone else suggested it. You start conversations with strangers because you have nothing to lose. And by day three, you realise this is the most liberated you've ever felt.

Solo travel isn't about being alone. It's about being free.

## Is Europe safe for solo travellers?

**Yes.** Europe is among the safest regions in the world. Cities like [Vienna](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-viena), Copenhagen, Tokyo, and [Lisbon](/en/city-breaks/cheapest-european-destinations-2026) consistently rank in the top 10 safest cities globally. Even cities with "reputation" issues ([Barcelona](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-barcelona) pickpockets, [Paris](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-paris) scams, [Rome](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-roma) taxi tricks) are SAFE — the risks are financial (scams, overcharging), not physical.

### Safety essentials for all solo travellers

**Share your itinerary.** Send your accommodation details, flight numbers, and rough daily plans to someone at home. Update them when plans change.

**Use Bolt/Uber at night.** GPS-tracked, recorded, accountable. Safer than any street taxi. [Full transport safety guide](/en/asigurari/travel-scams-europe-2026).

**Keep valuables in front pockets.** Phone, wallet, passport — always in front pockets or a crossbody bag. Never in a backpack in crowded areas.

**Trust your gut.** If a situation feels wrong, leave. You don't owe anyone politeness at the expense of your safety.

**Get [travel insurance](https://ektatraveling.tpo.mx/uU7jZ5jK).** Solo travellers have nobody to help navigate a medical emergency in a foreign language. Insurance provides a 24/7 helpline and arranges everything. [Complete guide](/en/asigurari/travel-insurance-europe-guide).

**Have an [eSIM](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3) active.** Google Maps, Google Translate, WhatsApp emergency calls — all require data. An eSIM means you're never truly disconnected. [Comparison guide](/en/esim-roaming/best-esim-europe-2026).

### Additional tips for women travelling solo

Everything above, plus:

**Book the first night in a well-reviewed hotel/hostel.** Arriving in a new city at night and searching for your Airbnb in an unfamiliar neighbourhood is stressful. A well-lit, well-reviewed place for the first night removes anxiety.

**Trust your instincts over politeness.** An overly friendly stranger, a persistent vendor, an invitation that feels off — you are never obligated to engage. A firm "no" and walking away is always acceptable.

**Dress for the culture.** Not about what's "right" — about minimising unwanted attention. In [Istanbul](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-istanbul) and [Athens](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-atena), conservative clothing at religious sites is expected.

## Best cities for solo travellers

### Lisbon — warm, walkable, social

Why it works solo: the hostel scene is Europe's best (many hostels have bars, communal dinners, and organised pub crawls). The city is compact and walkable. Portuguese people are genuinely warm. The food is incredible and cheap (€8-12 for a full meal with wine).

**Solo budget:** €55-80/day (hostel €20-30, food €15-25, transport €5, activities €10-15).

### [Bucharest](/en/city-breaks/bucharest-city-break-guide) — cheapest and most surprising

Why it works solo: ridiculously affordable (beer €2.50, full meal €10-15, Bolt across the city €3-4), excellent nightlife for meeting people (the Old Town has 120+ bars), English widely spoken by younger generations, and genuinely surprising for first-time visitors.

**Solo budget:** €40-55/day. [Is Romania expensive? Full cost guide](/en/city-breaks/is-romania-expensive).

Add a [day trip to Transylvania](/en/experiente/transylvania-day-trips-bucharest) — Peleș Castle is 90 minutes by train.

### [Budapest](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-budapesta) — thermal baths and ruin bars

Why it works solo: the thermal baths (Széchenyi, Rudas) are inherently social — you're in warm water surrounded by friendly strangers. The ruin bars (Szimpla Kert) are perfect for solo evening drinks without feeling awkward. And the Danube promenade at night is stunning alone.

**Solo budget:** €45-65/day.

### [Barcelona](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-barcelona) — beach + culture + bar hopping

Why it works solo: Barceloneta beach (you're never alone on a beach), tapas bars (sit at the bar, order plate by plate — the most social way to eat), and walking tours / food tours that attract other solo travellers. Sagrada Familia is equally breathtaking alone.

**Solo budget:** €60-85/day. Watch for [pickpockets](/en/asigurari/travel-scams-europe-2026).

### [Prague](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-praga) — compact, cheap, legendary beer

Why it works solo: the city is so compact that you never feel lost (everything is within 30 minutes' walk). Czech beer culture is inherently communal — sit at a pub table and you'll be in conversation within 20 minutes. And at €2-3 per excellent beer, the barrier to socialising is zero.

**Solo budget:** €45-65/day.

### [Istanbul](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-istanbul) — sensory overload in the best way

Why it works solo: every interaction is a potential conversation (Turkish hospitality is genuine — most "friendly locals" are just being Turkish, though [watch for the bar scam](/en/asigurari/travel-scams-europe-2026)). The food is designed for solo eating (grab a gyro, sit at a counter, sip tea). And the Bosphorus ferry (€1) is a spectacular solo experience.

**Solo budget:** €40-60/day.

## How to meet people

### Stay in social hostels

The single best investment for solo travellers. Look for hostels with: a common area (not just bedrooms), a bar or café, organised activities (walking tours, pub crawls, cooking classes), and good reviews from solo travellers specifically.

**Private rooms in hostels** are the sweet spot: your own space for sleeping, communal areas for socialising. €25-50/night in most European cities.

### Join walking tours and food tours

[GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/?partner_id=EKAA80H) walking tours (€15-30) and food tours (€30-60) are 70%+ solo travellers. You spend 2-3 hours with a small group, have a shared experience, and often continue to dinner or drinks together afterward. It's the most natural way to make temporary travel friends.

### Eat at the bar, not at a table

In [Barcelona](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-barcelona), [Rome](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-roma), and [Istanbul](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-istanbul), sitting at the bar counter puts you next to other people — couples, locals, other solo travellers. It's inherently social. A table for one is inherently isolated.

## The loneliness question — let's be honest

Solo travel has lonely moments. Sunset over the Danube in [Budapest](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-budapesta) would be nicer shared. The couples at the next table in [Paris](/en/experiente/ce-sa-faci-in-paris) are annoyingly romantic. The evening between activities and sleep can feel empty.

This is normal. It doesn't mean solo travel was wrong — it means you're human. The solution: have a routine. Call someone at home during that evening gap. Write in a journal. Go to a hostel bar. Or simply sit with the feeling — it passes, and the freedom on the other side is worth it.

## Solo travel packing (less is more)

One backpack (40-50L) or one carry-on suitcase. That's it. You're carrying everything yourself — on stairs, on cobblestones, on trains. Every extra kilo is regret.

**Essentials:** 3-4 tops, 2 bottoms, underwear for 4 days (wash in sink), one warm layer, comfortable walking shoes (worn, not packed), universal power adapter, [eSIM](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3) installed, power bank (10,000 mAh), and a packable day bag.

**Security:** keep passport, cash reserve, and backup card in a hidden pouch or at the bottom of your pack — not in the day bag you carry around the city.

## The mindset shift

The biggest barrier to solo travel isn't safety or budget — it's permission. The idea that you "need" someone to travel with is a social construct, not a practical requirement. Museums don't require a plus-one. Restaurants serve tables of one. Planes don't charge extra for solo seats.
