If you landed here because an advertisement told you to get your ETIAS "before the new rules hit", stop. As of 18 November 2026, **ETIAS does not exist as a working service**. You cannot apply, no border asks for it, and nobody on earth holds a valid one.

That will change — probably in 2027. This guide covers what ETIAS actually is, exactly where it stands, what you will need to do when it launches, and the part most articles skip: what is *already* running at Europe's borders, because that part has genuinely changed.

## Where ETIAS stands in November 2026

| Question | Answer on 18 November 2026 |
|---|---|
| Can you apply? | **No.** The official form does not exist yet |
| Is it checked at borders? | **No.** No authority asks for it |
| Is there a launch date? | **No.** The Q4 2026 target was withdrawn in July 2026 |
| When is it expected? | 2027, unconfirmed |
| What will it cost? | 20 EUR (up from 7 EUR, decided 17 July 2025) |
| Validity | 3 years, or until your passport expires |
| What is already live? | **EES**, fully operational since 10 April 2026 |

In July 2026 the European Commission removed the "last quarter of 2026" launch window from the official ETIAS website, leaving the system without a date. The EU has said it will announce the start several months ahead so carriers and travellers can prepare. Until that announcement, any timeline you see on a private website is guesswork.

## Who will need one

Citizens of roughly 60 visa-exempt countries and territories. The largest groups:

**North and South America:** United States, Canada, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Paraguay.

**Asia and the Pacific:** Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Malaysia, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Australia, New Zealand, Brunei.

**Europe outside the EU and the Middle East:** United Kingdom, Ukraine, Serbia, Georgia, Moldova, Albania, North Macedonia, Montenegro, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Kosovo, Israel, United Arab Emirates.

ETIAS will be required to enter **30 European countries** — the 29 Schengen states plus Cyprus. Romania and Bulgaria are included.

### Who never needs one

- Citizens of the EU, EEA and Switzerland
- Citizens of the 30 countries that require ETIAS from others
- Holders of a residence permit or residence card issued by any of those 30 countries
- Holders of a long-stay national visa
- Family members of EU citizens who hold a residence card under EU law
- UK nationals and their family members who are beneficiaries of the Withdrawal Agreement, as long as they hold documents proving that status
- Dual nationals holding an EU or Schengen passport — travel on that passport instead

Air and sea crew requirements differ from country to country, so crew should check individually before travelling.

### The biometric passport catch

For several countries, visa-free access depends on the type of passport. **Only biometric passport holders** fall under the visa-free regime and therefore under ETIAS. Holders of non-biometric passports are outside ETIAS entirely — they still need a Schengen visa, exactly as before.

This applies to Moldova, Georgia, Serbia, Albania, North Macedonia and Montenegro. It is a distinction most guides omit, and it decides which process you are in.

## The fee, and who does not pay

**20 EUR per person.**

Regulation (EU) 2018/1240 originally set the fee at 7 EUR. On 17 July 2025 the European Commission announced the increase to 20 EUR, citing inflation since 2018, operating costs and functionality added during the years of delay. European travel industry bodies publicly objected that the rise was disproportionate, but the decision stood.

**Exempt from the fee:** applicants under 18 and over 70. Note the exemption is from payment, not from the requirement — a four-year-old still needs their own ETIAS, free of charge.

## How the application will work

The process is designed to be entirely online, with no appointment and no biometrics at the application stage.

1. **The official site.** One website only: **europa.eu/etias**. The form will appear there when the system launches. Nowhere else.
2. **The form.** Passport details, email address, contact information, travel history, security and health questions. Allow 10 to 20 minutes.
3. **What you need.** A passport valid for more than 3 months beyond your departure date and issued within the last 10 years.
4. **Payment.** 20 EUR by card.
5. **The decision.** Most applications are expected to be decided within minutes. Where additional checks are triggered, it can take considerably longer.
6. **The result.** The authorisation is linked digitally to your passport. There is no physical document to show — it is verified electronically at boarding.

No embassy visit, no interview, no fingerprints while applying. Fingerprints come later, at the border, through EES — a separate process.

## What an ETIAS actually gives you

**Three years, or until your passport expires**, whichever comes first, with unlimited trips inside that window.

The limit stays **90 days in any 180-day period**, across all 30 countries combined. ETIAS does not extend your right to stay and does not change it in any way. It only lets you board.

Change your passport within those three years and the authorisation dies with the old document. You need a new one.

**It does not guarantee entry.** ETIAS is a pre-check, not a right. A border officer can still ask for proof of accommodation, a return ticket or sufficient funds, and can refuse entry — exactly as with the US ESTA.

## The 90/180 rule, with examples

This rule confuses almost everyone, including people who have been living with it for years.

It does not mean "90 days, then a 90-day break". It means that **in any rolling 180-day window looking backwards from today**, you cannot have more than 90 days of presence in the area.

A worked example: you spend 30 days in France in January, 30 days in Italy in March and 30 days in Spain in May. You have used your 90 days. You cannot re-enter until the January days fall out of the back of the 180-day window.

The second trap: **the 30 countries count as one.** You do not get 90 days for Germany and another 90 for France. You get 90 in total.

The third: leaving does not reset the counter. A trip to the UK or Turkey in the middle does not erase the days you have already spent.

## EES — the system that is already running

If ETIAS is an uncertain future, EES is the present.

The **Entry/Exit System** records entries and exits of non-EU nationals at Schengen external borders. The phased rollout began on **12 October 2025**, and the system became **fully operational on 10 April 2026**.

What it does:

- Registers your fingerprints and facial image on first entry
- Records the date of every entry and exit
- Flags overstays automatically
- Replaces passport stamping for non-EU nationals

On your first crossing, biometric enrolment adds a few minutes at the desk. On later crossings it is a check rather than an enrolment, and faster than stamping used to be.

**The practical effect:** the first summer under fully operational EES, in 2026, produced significant queues at major airports. If you are flying into Schengen in peak season on a non-EU passport, allow more time at the border than you used to.

**The structural effect:** the 90/180 rule is now automatically verifiable. It used to rest on stamps that were applied inconsistently, sometimes misread, sometimes skipped. Now there is a complete digital record. Overstays show up.

## ETIAS scams

A system that has been announced, delayed six times and heavily covered in the press is perfect ground for fraud. The mechanism is always the same: an official-looking site with EU flags and a form takes 60 to 90 EUR to "fast-track" a document that is not being issued.

**How to recognise one:**

- It asks for money now. The system does not work, so it has nothing to sell you.
- The domain is not `europa.eu`. The only official site is **europa.eu/etias**.
- It promises "guaranteed approval" or "processing in 24 hours". Nobody can guarantee that, least of all for a system that does not exist.
- The price is not 20 EUR. Intermediaries add fees worth several times the real one.
- It asks for card details for an "eligibility check".

When the system does launch, you will apply directly on the official site in 10 to 20 minutes. There is no practical advantage to paying someone to fill in a form you can complete yourself.

## What to do right now

**Nothing about ETIAS.** There is no early application, no waiting list and no advantage to acting now.

What is worth doing before a European trip in 2027:

- **Check your passport.** It must be valid for more than 3 months beyond your departure and issued within the last 10 years. If it is close to ten years old, renew it before applying for an ETIAS — otherwise you will pay twice.
- **Count your days.** If you have been travelling in and out of Schengen, work out where you stand on 90/180 before booking. EES now counts for you.
- **Allow time at the border.** First EES entry adds minutes; peak season adds more.
- **Bookmark `europa.eu/etias`** and check it before you book anything for 2027. It is the only source that matters.
