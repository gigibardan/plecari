Budapest doesn't try to be Paris. It doesn't compete with Rome. What it does is combine thermal bath culture (120+ natural hot springs beneath the city), the most photogenic riverfront in Europe (the Danube between Buda and Pest at night), a ruin bar scene that invented its own genre of nightlife, and prices that make you feel like you've hacked travel.

Every person who visits Budapest says the same thing: "I didn't expect it to be THIS good."

This guide has prices updated August 2026, verified against official attraction websites. One important change from previous years: **Gellért Bath is closed for major renovation until 2028**, and the **Citadella on Gellért Hill reopened in spring 2026** after a complete 4-year restoration.

## Why Budapest is different from any other city break

Budapest isn't just "another beautiful European capital." It's a city built literally on thermal water — 120+ natural springs beneath the surface, feeding a bathing culture that runs from Roman times, through 150 years of Ottoman rule, and continues today as part of everyday local life.

And it's not just about baths. The ruin bars (romkocsma) are a concept invented here in the early 2000s — bars opened in abandoned buildings in the former Jewish ghetto, transformed into artistic spaces with mismatched furniture, street art, and cheap drinks. Nobody has successfully copied them because you can't replicate organically what happened in District VII.

The Parliament building on the Danube embankment is the third largest in the world and arguably the most beautiful. Buda Castle and the Fisherman's Bastion offer panoramic views that rival anything in Europe. And you get all of this at prices that feel like a travel hack — an authentic goulash for €4-5, a craft beer for €2.50-4, a metro ticket for 500 HUF (~€1.25).

## Thermal baths — Budapest's defining experience

Budapest sits on a geological fault with over 120 natural thermal springs. Bathing culture here dates from Roman times, peaked under the Ottomans (1541-1686), and is now UNESCO-recognized as intangible heritage. The baths aren't "swimming pools" — they're monumental buildings with centuries of history, where locals come daily, not occasionally.

### Széchenyi Baths — the essential experience

Europe's largest medicinal bath complex. 18 pools (3 outdoor, 15 indoor), temperatures from 26°C to 38°C, slides, and the uniquely Hungarian tradition of playing chess while soaking. The neo-Baroque building (1913) is spectacular, and the outdoor pools are the ones you've seen on Instagram — warm water with steam in winter, surrounded by yellow architecture.

Go early at opening (6:00 AM) or after 4:00 PM — between 10:00 AM and 3:00 PM it's busiest. Saturday is madness, avoid if possible. Bring flip-flops (mandatory), swimsuit, and towel (or rent one for ~€5). The outdoor pools are through the main building, past the indoor thermal pools. Warm water + steam in winter is one of the most memorable experiences in the city. The Good Morning ticket is the best value if you're an early riser. ### Rudas Baths — Ottoman atmosphere

Built in 1550, Rudas is Budapest's Ottoman jewel. The main octagonal pool under a dome pierced with star-shaped skylights is probably the most atmospheric spot in the city. But the real star is the **rooftop pool** — 40°C water with panoramic views of the Danube, Parliament, and Chain Bridge, all in one frame. Friday and Saturday nights (10:00 PM - 3:00 AM) it also operates as a night bath.

### Gellért Baths — closed until 2028

Architecturally the most beautiful bath (Art Nouveau, 1918, mosaics, marble columns, wave pool) is **closed since October 2025 for major renovation**, with reopening planned for 2028. If you see online offers for Gellért — they're misleading.

### Sparty — the pool party

Sparty (Spa Party) organizes parties in the thermal baths — usually at Széchenyi or Lukács — with DJs, lights, and club atmosphere. Tickets are sold separately (15,000-20,000 HUF / ~€38-50) and typically include a drink. Check [spartybooking.com](https://spartybooking.com) for dates. Not for everyone, but a unique experience.

## Parliament — the most impressive building in the city

The third-largest parliament building in the world (after Buenos Aires and Bucharest, ironically), and arguably the most beautiful. The interior matches the exterior — 40 kg of gold on ceilings and walls, massive chandeliers, a main staircase that makes you feel tiny. Visits are by guided tour only (45 min, including 15 min security screening).

## Buda side — the hills, castle, and views

Buda is the historic side, on hills, with the Castle, Fisherman's Bastion, and now the newly renovated Citadella. Pest is the energetic, flat side with Parliament, markets, and nightlife.

### Fisherman's Bastion (Halászbástya)

Neo-Gothic terrace with the most photographed view in Budapest: Parliament across the Danube with bridges in the foreground. It's more than just a photogenic terrace — it has a fascinating medieval history tied to the fishermen's guild that defended this section of the city wall.

**2026 prices:**

| Access | Price |
|---|---|
| Lower terraces + balconies | **Free** (all hours) |
| Upper tower | 1,700 HUF (~€4) adult |
| Reduced (students, children 6-14, EU seniors) | 850 HUF (~€2) |
| Children 0-5 | Free |

**Free access mornings before 9:00 AM and evenings after 9:00 PM (June-Sept) / 7:00 PM (Oct-May).** Also free on Christmas (Dec 24 - Jan 1), March 15, August 20, October 23.

**Insider tip:** Go at sunrise (5:30-6:30 AM in summer) — terraces are empty, light is golden, and the upper tower is free. The view from the paid upper tower isn't dramatically different from the free lower terraces — save the €4.

### Matthias Church (Mátyás-templom)

800-year-old church with a psychedelic roof of Zsolnay ceramic tiles (a Hungarian speciality) — probably the most colourful church roof in Europe. The interior is surprising: geometric patterns painted on every surface, in a style mixing Gothic with Oriental influences.

**Price:** ~2,200-3,500 HUF (~€6-9) adult. Hours: Mon-Sat 9:00 AM-5:00 PM, Sunday 1:00-5:00 PM (morning mass). Check the notice board at the entrance — schedule changes for religious events.

### Buda Castle + National Gallery

The Castle complex houses the Hungarian National Gallery (visual arts from medieval to 20th century) and the Budapest History Museum. The building itself is worth visiting even without museum entry — the interior courtyards and Danube-view terraces are free.

**Indicative prices:** National Gallery ~3,200 HUF (~€8), History Museum ~2,400 HUF (~€6).

**Getting up the hill:**

| Option | Price | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Funicular (Budavári Sikló) | 4,500 HUF (~€11) one-way, 5,500 HUF (~€14) return | 2 minutes |
| Walk (from Clark Ádám tér) | Free | 15-20 minutes |
| Bus 16 / 16A | 500 HUF (regular ticket) | 10 minutes |

**Tip:** The funicular is scenic but expensive. Walking up is pleasant, through gardens, and only takes 15 minutes. Take the funicular down if you want the experience, or bus 16.

### Citadella — reopened in 2026

Excellent news: the Citadella on Gellért Hill **reopened in spring 2026** after a complete 4-year renovation. The 1851 Habsburg fortress is now a public park with gardens, wall-top promenades, and 360° panoramic views over the entire city.

**Access:**

| Area | Price |
|---|---|
| Public park, gardens, wall promenades | **Free** |
| Liberty Statue and plaza | **Free** |
| "Bastion of Liberty" exhibition (western tower) | ~4,900 HUF (~€12) |
| Roof Garden 360° viewpoint | Included in exhibition ticket |

The renovation added 3 separate entrances (previously just one), elevators, a hilltop café, and food vendors. **Getting there:** Bus 27 from Móricz Zsigmond körtér to Búsuló Juhász (Citadella) stop, then a flat 6-minute walk. Alternatively, walk up from Szent Gellért tér — 20-30 minutes, steep but scenic. The view from the Liberty Statue at sunset is one of the best free experiences in Budapest.

### Gellért Hill — more than the Citadella

On the same hill: **Cave Church (Sziklatemplom)** — a cave church in the southern face of the hill, hidden behind Jubilee Park. Free (optional donation). The story is fascinating: built in 1926 after the Lourdes grotto model, closed by communists in 1951, literally walled shut, and reopened in 1989. The walk along Gellért Hill from Elisabeth Bridge to the Citadella is one of the most beautiful urban trails in Europe.

## Pest side — the energy, food, and nightlife

### St. Stephen's Basilica (Szent István Bazilika)

Budapest's second-tallest building (96 m, tied with Parliament — a symbolic limit). The interior is impressive, but the real attraction is the **panoramic terrace on the dome** — 360° over all of Budapest.

**2026 prices:**

| Ticket | Price |
|---|---|
| Basilica only | 2,600 HUF (~€6) |
| Panorama + Treasury (combined) | 5,000 HUF (~€12) |
| All-inclusive (basilica + panorama + treasury) | 6,800 HUF (~€16) |
| Wednesday organ demonstration | 9,900 HUF (~€25) |

Hours: Mon-Sat 9:00 AM-6:00 PM, Sunday 1:00-6:00 PM. Panorama: daily 9:00 AM-7:00 PM.

**Tip:** The panorama ticket is the one worth getting — the dome view rivals Fisherman's Bastion but with fewer crowds. In the evening, attend one of the organ concerts — the acoustics are exceptional.

### Central Market Hall (Nagy Vásárcsarnok)

Budapest's largest covered market, in a spectacular neo-Gothic building (1897). Ground floor: fresh produce, cheeses, sausages (kolbász), spices, paprika in every form imaginable. Upper floor: cooked food, lángos (€4-6), gulyás, and souvenirs.

**Entry:** Free. Hours: Mon 6:00 AM-5:00 PM, Tue-Fri 6:00 AM-6:00 PM, Sat 6:00 AM-3:00 PM, Sunday closed.

**What to buy:** sweet/hot paprika (from 500 HUF per bag), mangalica sausages, acacia honey, Tokaji Aszú (if you're not going to Eger), artisanal pálinka. Skip the first floor for eating if you can — the lángos at the entrance is touristy and overpriced (€6). Better to eat at vendéglő restaurants near Kálvin tér, 200m away.

### Shoes on the Danube Bank (Cipők a Duna-parton)

Holocaust memorial: 60 pairs of iron shoes on the Danube bank, facing Parliament. Jewish Budapestians were taken to the bank, made to remove their shoes (which were valuable), and shot into the Danube by Arrow Cross militias in 1944-45. It's a memorial that works through simplicity — free and always accessible. Located on the Pest promenade, 10 minutes south of Parliament.

### Andrássy Avenue and Heroes' Square

Andrássy Boulevard (UNESCO) is Budapest's "Champs-Élysées" — 2.3 km from St. Stephen's Basilica to Heroes' Square, lined with neo-Renaissance buildings, shops, and the State Opera along the route. Underneath runs **Metro Line M1 (the yellow line)** — the continent's first underground railway (1896), itself a UNESCO site.

**Heroes' Square (Hősök tere):** Millennium Monument with the 7 statues of the Magyar tribal chieftains. Impressive, free. On either side: Museum of Fine Arts (European art collection) and Kunsthalle (temporary exhibitions).

**Hungarian State Opera:** Neo-Renaissance building (1884) that rivals Vienna's Opera. Guided tours daily at 1:30, 3:00, and 4:30 PM — **12,000 HUF (~€30)** in English, including a mini-concert with Opera soloists. If you prefer a performance: opera tickets start from ~3,000-5,000 HUF (~€8-12) for upper seats, with 50% student discounts.

### House of Terror (Terror Háza)

Museum of totalitarian regimes (fascist and communist) in Hungary, housed in the former secret police headquarters at Andrássy 60. The interior is a masterclass in museum design — each floor recreates a historical period. The basement with torture cells is emotionally powerful.

**Price:** ~3,000-5,000 HUF (~€8-12). Hours: Tue-Sun 10:00 AM-6:00 PM, Mon closed. No photography inside.

### Városliget (City Park)

Budapest's largest park, adjacent to Heroes' Square. Here you'll find:

- **Vajdahunyad Castle** — a romantic replica mixing multiple architectural styles (Gothic, Baroque, Romanesque), originally built from cardboard for the 1896 Millennial Exhibition, then reconstructed permanently. Houses the Agricultural Museum (~2,000-3,500 HUF).
- **House of Hungarian Music** — striking new building with interactive music exhibitions (~2,500-4,000 HUF).
- **Városliget Lake** — boats in summer (600-1,200 HUF/hour), ice rink in winter (~1,500-2,500 HUF with skate rental).
- **Budapest Zoo** — surprisingly good, with original Art Nouveau buildings (~3,500-5,500 HUF).

## Ruin bars — the concept Budapest invented

The nightlife genre that doesn't exist anywhere else. In the early 2000s, creative entrepreneurs opened bars in abandoned buildings in the former Jewish Quarter (District VII, Erzsébetváros). The result: spaces with second-hand furniture (school benches, bathtubs as seats, old cars), street art, plants, and prices that make any western "craft cocktail bar" look ridiculous.

### Szimpla Kert — the original

The world's most famous ruin bar, open since 2004. A labyrinth of multiple rooms, floors, and an inner courtyard covered with objects — bicycle frames, CRT monitors, globes, plants. Beer: 1,000-1,500 HUF (~€2.50-4), cocktails: 2,000-3,500 HUF (~€5-9). Gets crowded after 10:00 PM — go at 7:00-8:00 PM for a more relaxed experience. **Sunday mornings:** farmers' market inside the bar — one of the coolest experiences in the city.

### Instant-Fogas

The largest ruin bar complex: 26 rooms across two buildings, with more of a club orientation than Szimpla. DJs, dancing, different energy. Similar prices.

### Élesztő

For craft beer lovers: 20+ taps, mostly Hungarian artisanal beers. Quieter, more local, superior beer quality. Perfect for a relaxed evening.

### Mazel Tov

Open-air restaurant-bar with Mediterranean-Middle Eastern food (Israeli influence), lanterns, and a secret garden atmosphere. Food is surprisingly good (hummus, falafel, kebab — 2,500-5,000 HUF / €6-12). Less "ruin bar" classic, more restaurant with vibe.

**Tip for a ruin bar crawl:** You can do an organized tour with pálinka tastings (€25-45, 2-3 hours), but also on your own — they're all walking distance in District VII, along Kazinczy utca, Akácfa utca, and Klauzál utca.

Hungarian cuisine is one of the most underrated in Europe — hearty, flavourful, with Turkish, Austrian, and Slavic influences, built on paprika, sour cream, and slow-cooked meat.

### Where to eat — by price category

**Mid-range (3,000-8,000 HUF / €8-20 per meal):**

- **Hungarikum Bisztró** — touristy but good, serves all Hungarian specialities.
- **Pozsonyi Kisvendéglő** — in District XIII, away from tourist areas, local prices, exceptional food.

**Fine dining (over 15,000 HUF / €38):**
- **Onyx** — 2 Michelin stars, reinvented Hungarian cuisine, tasting menu from ~35,000 HUF (~€88).
- **Costes** — Hungary's first Michelin restaurant (2010), creative cuisine.
- **Babel** — farm-to-table with local ingredients, relaxed atmosphere for fine dining.

### Food tours

A guided food tour (€40-65, 3-4 hours) gets you out of the "goulash at a tourist restaurant" trap and takes you to the right places — étkezde canteens, markets, wine bars, pálinka producers. Usually includes 6-8 tastings, enough for a full meal.

**Typically covers:** Central Market (tastings on the ground floor, not upstairs), a local étkezde, pálinka tasting, Hungarian cheeses and sausages, and a Jewish Quarter street food stop.

## Budapest neighbourhoods — where to stay and explore

### District V — Belváros (Centre)

The tourist heart. Parliament, St. Stephen's Basilica, Váci utca (pedestrian shopping street — avoid restaurants here, they're expensive and mediocre). Perfect for first visits, but accommodation prices are highest.

### District VII — Erzsébetváros (Jewish Quarter)

The most vibrant neighbourhood. Ruin bars, street art, restaurants, the Great Synagogue (Europe's largest, world's second-largest), galleries. Transforms completely in the evening — Kazinczy utca and Akácfa utca become a permanent festival. Best for nightlife. Accommodation: mid-range.

### District VI — Terézváros

Andrássy Boulevard, the Opera, House of Terror. More elegant than VII, calmer in the evenings. Good compromise between centre and local atmosphere.

### District VIII — Józsefváros (Corvin quarter)

A neighbourhood in rapid transformation. Corvin quarter (around the Corvin cinema) has new bars and restaurants, cheaper than VII. The Hungarian National Museum is here. Artsy vibe, better prices.

### District IX — Ferencváros

The Central Market is on the V/IX border. Ferencváros has local atmosphere and MÜPA (Palace of Arts concert hall). Fewer tourists, excellent prices.

### District I — Buda (Castle Hill)

The Castle, Bastion, Matthias. Beautiful but quiet in the evening. Accommodation is less convenient for nightlife (you need to cross bridges). Ideal if you prefer tranquility.

### District II — Buda (Rózsadomb, Margit)

Residential, with Rudas and Lukács baths. Margaret Island is accessible from here. The Mechwart liget area is becoming increasingly popular with locals — cafés, small markets.

### District XI — Buda (Gellért)

Gellért Hill, renovated Citadella, universities. Student neighbourhood with good accommodation prices. Great starting point for climbing Gellért Hill in the morning.

## Margaret Island (Margitsziget)

A 2.5 km island on the Danube, between Margaret and Árpád bridges — Budapest's public park. No cars, just bicycles and pedestrians. Includes: Olympic swimming pool, Japanese garden, medieval convent ruins, musical fountain (light show in summer evenings), 5.3 km running track around the perimeter, and grass areas for picnics.

**Entry:** Free. Bicycle rental: ~2,000-3,000 HUF/hour. Accessible by bus 26 or from both bridges.

**Tip:** Perfect for a recovery morning after a ruin bar evening — fresh air, jogging, and relaxing on the grass.

## Great Synagogue (Dohány utcai Zsinagóga)

Europe's largest synagogue and the world's second-largest (after Temple Emanu-El in New York). The Moorish style with onion-dome towers is iconic. Includes the Cemetery and the Raoul Wallenberg Memorial (Tree of Life — a metal tree with each leaf inscribed with a victim's name).

**Price:** ~5,000-8,000 HUF (~€12-20) adult, includes guided tour (45 min). Hours: Sun-Thu 10:00 AM-4:00/6:00 PM (seasonal), Fri 10:00 AM-2:00 PM, Sat closed.

### Metro — 4 lines

- **M1 (yellow)** — continental Europe's oldest (1896), UNESCO site. Runs under Andrássy from Vörösmarty tér to Heroes' Square. Stations are small and vintage.
- **M2 (red)** — crosses the Danube. Batthyány tér (Buda) – Blaha Lujza tér (Pest) – Keleti pu. (railway station).
- **M3 (blue)** — north-south, through Kálvin tér and Deák Ferenc tér. Recently renovated.
- **M4 (green)** — newest (2014), modern, connects Buda (Kelenföldi pu.) with Pest (Keleti pu.).

### Tram 2 — Europe's most scenic tram

Runs along the Pest embankment from Jászai Mari tér to the Central Market Hall. Views of Parliament, Castle, and all the bridges. Regular BKK ticket. It's both transport and tourist attraction — better than any hop-on hop-off bus.

## Day trips from Budapest

### Danube Bend (Dunakanyar) — Szentendre, Visegrád, Esztergom

The most popular day trip, and rightly so. Three medieval towns along the Danube, 25-70 km north of Budapest.

**Szentendre** (~25 km, 40 min by HÉV suburban train):
Artists' town on the Danube bank — galleries, museums, colourful streets, Serbian churches. HÉV departs from Batthyány tér every 20 min, ~900 HUF return. 4-5 hours is enough.

**Visegrád** (~50 km):
Medieval citadel on a hilltop with views of the Danube Bend — one of the most beautiful panoramas in Hungary. Accessible by bus from Árpád híd. Citadel: ~2,000-3,000 HUF.

**Esztergom** (~70 km):
Hungary's (and Central Europe's) largest basilica, with a dome visible from Slovakia. Train from Nyugati pu. (~1,100 HUF, 1.5h). Or an organized tour combining all three: ~€30-50 on [GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/budapest-l29/?partner_id=EKAA80H).

**By boat on the Danube:** Mahart Passnave operates seasonal services (April-September). One-way to Szentendre: ~2,500 HUF, to Esztergom: ~2,500 HUF, 2-2.5 hours. Return cruise to Esztergom: ~€30 adult, ~€15 child.

### Eger (~130 km, 2h)

Baroque town with a medieval fortress, beautiful churches, and — most importantly — the **Egri Bikavér** (Bull's Blood) wine region. Cellars in the "Valley of the Beautiful Women" (Szépasszony-völgy) offer wine tastings from 2,000-5,000 HUF. Eger has its own thermal baths too. Full-day trip (8-10 hours) with tasting: ~€60-90 organized, or independently by train (2,500-4,000 HUF return).

### Lake Balaton (~130 km, 1.5-2h)

Central Europe's largest lake — "the Hungarian Sea." Tihany (peninsula with Benedictine abbey), Siófok (parties, beach), Balatonfüred (elegant, promenade). Ideal for a beach day in summer. Train from Déli pu. or Keleti pu., 2,000-4,000 HUF return.

### Memento Park (~25 min by bus)

Communist statue park — all the Lenin, Marx, and communist hero statues moved here after 1989. Fascinating as a memory exercise. ~1,800-2,500 HUF entry. Direct bus from Deák Ferenc tér.

## Tourist tax (IFA)

Hungary charges a local tourist tax (IFA — Idegenforgalmi Adó) of **4% of the accommodation rate per night**. Paid at check-in, per person, per night. Children under 18 are exempt. At a €50/night hotel, the tax is ~€2/night — negligible compared to flat-rate taxes in other cities.

## 10 insider tips for Budapest

1. **Go to Rudas on Friday night (night bathing)** — the Ottoman pool with coloured lights under the 1550 dome + the rooftop pool with the illuminated city below. More atmospheric than Széchenyi and less touristy. Tickets online only.

2. **Eat at an étkezde, not a restaurant** — workers' canteens (Kádár, Kisfaludy) serve authentic Hungarian food at €6-11 for a full meal. Look for "napi menü" (daily menu).

3. **Do a pálinka course, not just a shot** — at the Palinka Museum on Rákóczi út or at cellars in the Jewish Quarter you can do structured tastings (3,000-5,000 HUF) with explanations of the distillation process. The difference between supermarket pálinka and artisanal is night and day.

4. **Tram 2 is the cheapest "cruise"** — along the Pest embankment with views of Parliament, Castle, and bridges. 500 HUF ticket. Go at sunset.

5. **Avoid Váci utca for food** — the main pedestrian street has restaurants with no-price menus, aggressive waiters, and mediocre food at double the price. Move one street over (to Irányi utca or Duna utca) and prices drop 40%.

6. **Fisherman's Bastion is free in the morning** — before 9:00 AM, access to the upper terraces is free. Plus: you're nearly alone, the light is perfect for photos.

7. **Don't miss Sunday morning at Szimpla Kert** — farmers' market with local produce, honey, cheese, preserves, artisan bread. Completely different atmosphere from the nighttime bar.

8. **Get a 72h pass if you're staying 3 days** — at 5,750 HUF (~€14.50) it covers all public transport and avoids the risk of a fine (16,000 HUF!). Pays for itself on day one.

9. **Citadella at sunset is the best viewpoint in the city** — recently reopened, with free access to the Liberty Statue plateau. The 360° view at golden hour is probably the most beautiful in Budapest.

10. **Buy Tokaji wine at DiVino** — on Basilica Square, premium Hungarian wines at producer prices. A Tokaji Aszú 5 Puttonyos (exceptional sweet wine) at 3,000-6,000 HUF is a perfect gift or personal treat.

## Seasonal guide — when to go

### Spring (March-May) ☀️
Pleasant temperatures (12-22°C), few tourists in March-April, gardens in bloom. Spring Festival in April. Lower accommodation prices. **Best for walking and cultural visits.**

### Summer (June-August) ☀️☀️
Warm (25-35°C), peak season, Sziget in August. Terraces, outdoor thermal pools, Danube walks. Palatinus Aquapark on Margaret Island. Crowded and more expensive. **Best for nightlife and outdoor activities.**

### Autumn (September-November) 🍂
Perfect temperatures (10-20°C), beautiful autumn colours, wine festival in September, few tourists. Accommodation prices drop significantly. **Best overall season — good weather, good prices, few tourists.**

### Winter (December-February) ❄️
Cold (−2 to 5°C), but Christmas markets are magical (December), thermal baths with steam are memorable, and prices are the lowest of the year. Széchenyi in winter (warm water + steam + snow) is an iconic experience. **Best for Christmas + thermal baths on a budget.**

## Practical tips

**Currency:** Hungarian Forint (HUF). €1 ≈ 395-400 HUF (August 2026). Cards accepted nearly everywhere, including markets and kiosks. Revolut or Wise are ideal for real-rate conversion. ATMs are fine, but always decline "Dynamic Currency Conversion" — choose to be charged in HUF, not EUR.

**Currency exchange:** Avoid "exchange" offices on Váci utca and at the airport — they have hidden 5-15% fees. Best rate: Revolut/Wise, or exchange offices near Blaha Lujza tér and Nyugati.

**Tipping:** 10% at restaurants (check if "service charge" is already included — some restaurants automatically add 12.5-15%). At thermal baths and taxis: round up or 10%.

**[Travel insurance](https://ektatraveling.tpo.mx/uU7jZ5jK):** EU citizens are covered by EHIC for public hospital emergencies. For full coverage (luggage, cancellations, repatriation), dedicated [travel insurance](/en/asigurari/travel-insurance-europe-guide) is recommended.

**[eSIM](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3):** EU residents have free roaming in Hungary. Non-EU: [eSIM comparison guide](/en/esim-roaming/best-esim-europe-2026).

**Power sockets:** Type C/F (same as most of continental Europe) — no adapter needed from most European countries.

**Language:** Hungarian is completely different from every other European language (Finno-Ugric). But English is widely spoken in tourist areas, restaurants, and hotels. Useful words: Köszönöm (thank you), Szia (hi), Igen/Nem (yes/no), Mennyibe kerül? (how much?), Egészségedre (cheers, for toasting).

**Safety:** Very safe. Watch for pickpockets on the metro (especially M1 and M3) and on Váci utca. Don't accept invitations from "beautiful girls" inviting you to a bar — it's a classic scam with an inflated bill.
