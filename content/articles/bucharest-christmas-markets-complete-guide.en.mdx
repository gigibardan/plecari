Bucharest has no medieval square. It has no baroque façades to project light onto and no red roofs with "eyes". What it does have, no other Romanian city can match: **six to nine Christmas markets running at the same time**, the tallest tree in the country, and a 36-metre panoramic wheel planted in front of the largest administrative building in Europe.

The 2026 edition of the **Bucharest Christmas Market** — the 19th — opens on **Friday 27 November at 17:00** in Constitution Square and runs until **27 December**. Entry is free, as always.

This guide covers all of the capital's markets, not just the headline one: confirmed dates, opening hours, real prices from recent editions, where the ice rink actually is (not where everyone assumes), how to arrive by metro, and what to do with the rest of the city.

---

## About the live camera: Bucharest does not have one

Worth saying up front, because it is the first question from anyone who has seen our guides to the other Romanian cities.

**There is no working official live stream from Constitution Square.** Unlike Cluj-Napoca, where the city hall broadcasts continuously from Unirii Square, and Sibiu, where the Grand Square camera runs 24/7, the capital does not operate a permanent public camera pointed at the market. The tourist webcam at the Palace of the Parliament, listed on Romanian webcam directories, has been **offline since August 2026**.

What you can do instead: follow the official Bucharest City Hall and market pages for same-day photos and clips. And if you want a Romanian market you can genuinely check in real time before setting out, we have live-camera guides for [Cluj-Napoca](/en/calatorii/cluj-napoca-christmas-markets-complete-guide) and [Sibiu](/en/calatorii/sibiu-christmas-markets-complete-guide).

## What is in Constitution Square

**The 30-metre tree**, carrying 171,000 LED bulbs. It is the capital's official tree and the tallest in any public square in Romania.

**The 36-metre panoramic wheel.** Taller than the tree, and easily the best view you will get of the floodlit Palace of the Parliament. Worth the 30 lei for that perspective alone.

**A themed double-decker carousel.**

**Santa's House**, with **free access** — children can leave letters and meet the elves.

**133 chalets** of Romanian craftspeople, artisans and producers: decorations, hand-made objects, gifts, plus an expanded food area.

**Concerts and performances** on the square's stage, along with workshops and activities for families.

The site is enormous — around 20,000 square metres — which means that, unlike Sibiu or Cluj, you genuinely have room to move even on a Saturday evening. That is Bucharest's main advantage.

---


## A map of the market and how to move through it

Constitution Square is an enormous open rectangle, with the Palace of the Parliament at one end and Bulevardul Unirii at the other. You cannot get lost, but across 20,000 square metres you can waste time if you walk in without a plan.

**The entrances.** You enter from Bulevardul Unirii and from the sides of the square. The best is the one from Unirii: you get the tree and the Palace in the same frame from your first step, which matters if you want the good photograph before joining the crowd.

**The chalet area** — the 133 craft and producer chalets, grouped along lanes. Crafts and gifts are separated from the food zone, so you do not have to queue past food stalls to reach the decorations.

**The food zone** — expanded, with the longest queues between 18:00 and 20:00.

**The stage** — concerts and performances, with the programme released in stages by the organiser.

**The 30-metre tree** — the central landmark and the default meeting point for anyone who has lost someone in the crowd.

**The panoramic wheel and carousel** — towards the edge of the square, tickets on the spot.

**Santa's House** — free, with a serious queue at weekends after 16:00. If you are with small children, do this first, not last.

A photographer's tip: the best shot is not from the middle of the square but from Bulevardul Unirii, a few dozen metres from the entrance, where the tree, the wheel and the Palace of the Parliament line up in one frame.

## The ice rink myth: it is not in Constitution Square

This is the detail that catches out most visitors, every single year.

**No ice rink is built in Constitution Square.** There never has been one, there is not one now, and the fact that half the articles online imply otherwise changes nothing.

For skating, you have two real options in Bucharest:

**West Side Christmas Market, Drumul Taberei Park** — the largest outdoor rink in the city, around 750 square metres. At the 2024 edition, skating cost 25 lei for 90 minutes.

**A covered rink** — the option that works whatever the weather, which matters in a December when Bucharest alternates between cold rain and 12°C.

If you are heading to Constitution Square with children who have set their hearts on skating, warn them in advance. Or take them straight to Drumul Taberei.

## Bucharest's other Christmas markets

The capital does not have one market, it has a network. Three are confirmed for 2026; the rest are usually announced a week or two ahead.

### Confirmed for 2026

| Market | Where | Dates | Entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bucharest Christmas Market | Constitution Square | 27 November – 27 December | Free |
| West Side Christmas Market | Drumul Taberei Park | 26 November – 27 December | Free |
| „Poveste de Crăciun” | Comic Opera for Children | 20 November – 22 December | Free |

### Markets from previous years

Not confirmed for 2026, but frequently repeated: the **University Square** market (Bucharest Downtown Christmas Market), the one on the **National Opera esplanade**, the **Sector 3** market in Piața Alba Iulia (with a rink, drones and flame throwers), **„Crăciunul Nordului”** at Romexpo (covered rink, panoramic wheel and funfair) and **„Tărâmul de Gheață”** in Lumea Copiilor Park.

Besides Constitution Square, large Christmas trees also go up in University Square, on the National Opera esplanade, in Drumul Taberei Park and in Circus Park.

**How to choose:**

- **The biggest and most spectacular** → Constitution Square
- **Travelling with small children** → West Side in Drumul Taberei, or the Comic Opera for Children
- **You want an ice rink** → Drumul Taberei outdoors, or a covered rink
- **Staying in the centre and want something walkable** → check whether the University Square market returns

## Opening hours

The official 2026 schedule is published at opening. As a benchmark from recent editions:

| Period | Hours |
|---|---|
| Monday – Friday | 12:00 – 22:00 |
| Saturday – Sunday | 10:00 – 22:00 |
| Special days (24, 25, 26 December) | Announced separately |

**Best time:** between 16:30 and 18:00 on a weekday. It gets dark early, the lights are on, and the square has not filled yet.

**Busiest:** Saturdays and Sundays after 17:00, plus the 1 December national holiday weekend and the weekends before Christmas.

**Bucharest's advantage:** even at peak, 20,000 square metres absorb the crowd far better than the small squares of Sibiu or Cluj.

## Getting there

### By metro — the only sensible option

**Izvor** (M1 and M3) and **Piața Unirii** (M1, M2 and M3) are the nearby stations, each about a 10-minute walk. Constitution Square is at the end of Bulevardul Unirii, directly below the Palace of the Parliament.

The metro runs frequently and drops you within strolling distance. On December weekends it is the only option that does not cost you an hour in traffic.

### By car — be prepared

Preparations for the 2026 edition began on **2 November**, with the first traffic restrictions in the area. During the market, traffic is restricted on sections of **Bulevardul Unirii** and **Bulevardul Libertății** near the square.

Parking nearby is limited and fills fast. If you must drive, leave the car in a shopping-centre car park or a paid garage near a metro station and do the last leg underground.

### Arriving from abroad

Henri Coandă Airport (OTP) is Romania's best-connected, with direct flights from most European capitals. From the airport you can take the train to Gara de Nord, the 783 express bus to Piața Unirii, or Bolt and Uber. See our [airport transfer guide](/en/city-breaks/airport-transfer-guide), and the usual routes and fares in our piece on [popular flights from Bucharest](/en/zboruri/popular-flights-from-bucharest).

Bucharest has a far larger hotel stock than Sibiu or Cluj, so it **does not sell out** on December weekends and prices do not spike. It is the only city on this list where you can book a few days ahead without paying double.

One useful quirk: Bucharest's business hotels are cheaper at weekends than midweek. Exactly the opposite of Sibiu.

## Where to eat

The Old Town is a 10–15 minute walk from Constitution Square and has the highest concentration of restaurants in the country. After a wander through the market, do not eat in the square unless you specifically want the market experience — walk into town.

The areas that matter: the **Old Town** (Lipscani, Smârdan, Covaci) for atmosphere and sheer volume, **Calea Victoriei** for serious restaurants, and **Piața Amzei** and **Dorobanți** for bistros and contemporary cooking.

For the full list with addresses and prices, see our guide to [things to do in Bucharest](/en/experiente/things-to-do-bucharest).

**Seasonal food to try:** sarmale with polenta, ciorbă de burtă tripe soup, cozonac sweet bread, artisan charcuterie from the market producers, mulled wine, and hot țuică with pepper.

## What to see in Bucharest in winter

**The Palace of the Parliament** — right next to the market. The guided tour takes about an hour and is the best indoor activity in the city on a cold day. Book ahead.

**The Old Town** — Manuc's Inn, the Old Princely Court, the Macca-Vilacrosse passage, the Central University Library.

**The National Museum of Art of Romania**, on Calea Victoriei, in the former Royal Palace.

**The „Dimitrie Gusti” Village Museum** — an open-air museum on the shore of Herăstrău lake, with traditional houses from across the country. Empty and photogenic in winter, but dress warmly.

**The Romanian Athenaeum** — if you can catch a December concert, it is the most beautiful hall in Romania.

**Calea Victoriei and Bulevardul Magheru** under the festive lighting — which stays on until after St John's Day, so you can see it even once the market has closed.

## Itineraries

### One evening

17:00 — walk into Constitution Square in the blue hour. A circuit of the chalets, mulled wine, one turn on the 36-metre wheel for the view over the Palace of the Parliament. 19:00 — metro from Izvor to Piața Unirii, dinner in the Old Town.

### A weekend

**Saturday.** Morning: tour of the Palace of the Parliament. Lunch in the Old Town. Afternoon: the National Museum of Art or the Village Museum. 17:00: Constitution Square. Evening: dinner and a bar in the Old Town.

**Sunday.** Morning: walk Calea Victoriei with stops. Afternoon: the West Side Christmas Market in Drumul Taberei, for the ice rink and a second market with a completely different feel.

### With children

Morning at the Comic Opera for Children if you can catch a „Poveste de Crăciun” performance. Afternoon at West Side, for the rink and space to run. Evening at Santa's House in Constitution Square, which is free.

## What to buy from the 133 chalets

Unlike the food, where Bucharest is cheap but unremarkable, the craft section genuinely rewards the time. The city selects Romanian craftspeople, artisans and producers, and the range covers hand-made decorations, woodwork and ceramics, textiles, candles, honey and preserves, cheeses and smoked meats, plus țuică and wines from small producers.

As everywhere, some chalets resell bought-in stock. The test is the same: ask who made the piece. Whoever made it will tell you about it; whoever bought it wholesale changes the subject.

If you are gift-hunting and only have one stop to make, the craft section in Constitution Square is the richest in the country by sheer volume — simply because 133 chalets is more than anywhere else in Romania.

## With children in Bucharest

Bucharest offers something no other Romanian city can: you can do **three completely different markets in one weekend** without leaving town.

**Constitution Square** — Santa's House (free), the double-decker carousel, the panoramic wheel. The weak point: no ice rink, and the square is open and windy.

**West Side, Drumul Taberei** — the roughly 750 sqm ice rink, the largest outdoor one in the city, with parkland around it. The best option for children between 4 and 12.

**The Comic Opera for Children** — „Poveste de Crăciun”, 20 November to 22 December. A show in the warm rather than a walk in the cold. The option for cold, rainy days.

**The best window** in Constitution Square is 16:00–18:30: it gets dark, the lights are on, and the Santa's House queue is still reasonable.

## Accessibility and practical details

Constitution Square is flat, paved and very spacious — by far the most accessible Christmas market in Romania with a pushchair or a wheelchair. There are no cobblestones as in Sibiu and no slopes as in Brașov. Izvor metro station has step-free access.

Portable toilets are set up in the square; at weekends the queues are long after 18:00.

Most chalets take cards, but cash moves faster at peak hours. 100–150 lei in cash covers an evening for two.

Because the square is completely open, **wind is the deciding factor**. When the crivăț blows in off the plain, Constitution Square is the least pleasant place in the city. Check the wind forecast, not just the temperature.

## New Year and the period between the holidays

A detail few visitors know: **the market closes on 27 December, but the capital's festive lighting stays on** until after St John's Day, in the second half of January.

That means if you come to Bucharest between Christmas and mid-January you get the city fully lit — Calea Victoriei, Bulevardul Magheru, University Square — without the market crowds and at lower hotel rates.

In some years Constitution Square also hosts the city's New Year's Eve event. Nothing had been announced for 2026–2027 at the time of writing; check the municipality's announcements in December.

---

## Weather

December in Bucharest is unpredictable: it can be +12°C and raining, or −8°C with a cutting wind off the Bărăgan plain. Snow is not guaranteed and, when it falls, it melts quickly in the city.

Because the market sits in an open square with no buildings to break the wind, **Constitution Square feels colder than the rest of the city**. Wear one more layer than you think you need.

| **Sibiu** | The most beautiful setting, projections on historic buildings, rink in the square, European award | Small square, expensive weekend accommodation |
| **Cluj-Napoca** | A 43-day season, festival-grade production, a good city around it | The rink is elsewhere |
| **Craiova** | The largest by area, pure spectacle | High prices, less to do in the city |
| **Brașov** | Mountain setting | Small market |

The honest verdict on Christmas in Bucharest: it is not a destination you travel to specifically, but it is the best **base**. You get the largest airport, the largest hotel stock, the best value accommodation, six to nine markets in one city, and direct trains to Sinaia and Brașov.

---

## Practical tips

**Do not look for an ice rink in Constitution Square.** There isn't one. Drumul Taberei, or a covered rink.

**Take the metro.** Izvor or Piața Unirii. The traffic restrictions from 2 November onwards make driving a bad idea.

**Ride the panoramic wheel at dusk.** Thirty-six metres above the floodlit Palace of the Parliament is an image you cannot get in any other Romanian city.

**Eat in the Old Town, not at the market.** Ten minutes on foot, three times the choice, comparable prices.

**Do two markets, not one.** Constitution Square and Drumul Taberei are completely different experiences, and the second is far more relaxed.

**Book accommodation without panic.** Bucharest is the only Romanian city where the hotel stock is large enough that last-minute booking does not cost you double.

**The lights stay on after the market closes.** Visit between Christmas and St John's Day and the city is still lit.

---

## Is it worth it?

If you live in Bucharest or within two hours of it: obviously, and do two markets rather than one.

If you are travelling from elsewhere in Romania purely for Christmas atmosphere, Sibiu and Cluj deliver better, because they have a compact historic centre wrapped around the market and Bucharest simply cannot compete on that.

But if you are coming from abroad with a weekend in Romania, Bucharest is the logical entry point: direct flights from almost anywhere, cheap beds, the tallest tree in the country seen from 36 metres up, and Sinaia and Brașov two hours away by train. The market is not the reason for the trip. It is the bonus.
