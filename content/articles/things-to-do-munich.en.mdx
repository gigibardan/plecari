Munich knows exactly what it is — and makes no apology for it. It is the capital of Bavaria, it is wealthy, it is proud, and it serves the world's best weisswurst before noon, with wheat beer, in a wooden hall with painted ceilings. Hofbräuhaus is not a tourist trap — it is a 400-year-old institution that has served people from every social stratum under the same roof. The English Garden is larger than Central Park and has surfers riding an artificial wave in the middle of an urban river. Neuschwanstein Castle 130 km to the south inspired Disney's Cinderella castle and is visible from a mountain ridge against a complete Alpine backdrop.

Munich is expensive. It is among Europe's priciest cities, with hotels starting at €100 a night and beer costing €9–10 at a local beer garden and €12–13 at Hofbräuhaus. But it has dozens of free attractions, world-class museums at €1 on Sundays, and a quality of experience that justifies every euro.

## Marienplatz and the Old Town — the Heart of Munich

**Marienplatz** is the city's central square — built in 1158, it is as much a functioning civic space as a tourist attraction. The Frauenkirche with its twin onion-domed towers is the skyline symbol, while the Neues Rathaus (New Town Hall) with its **Glockenspiel** provides the daily spectacle.

**The Glockenspiel:** Daily at 11:00 AM (and noon + 5:00 PM in season), 43 bells and 32 mechanical figures re-enact two stories from Munich's history — the 1568 wedding of Duke Wilhelm V and the Schäfflertanz (coopers' dance) from 1517. **Free** — stand in the square and watch. Duration: 12–15 minutes. Crowds form — arrive 10 minutes early for a good spot.

**Viktualienmarkt** — Munich's food market, in operation since 1807, 2 minutes from Marienplatz. Fresh pretzels, Bavarian sausages, cheeses, honey, exotic fruit, and a **beer garden** at the center. Entry is free; food and drink are paid.

**Asamkirche** — Munich's most spectacular Baroque church, built in 1746 by the Asam brothers as a private chapel. The small dimensions (28m length) concentrate a density of ornaments, marble, gilded stucco, and frescoes that completely overwhelm. **Free entry.**

**St. Peter's Tower (Alter Peter):** Munich's oldest tower, 306 steps, complete panorama over Marienplatz and the Frauenkirche. ~€4 adults — one of the best accessible views in the center.

## English Garden — Bigger Than Central Park, With Surfers

<cite index="34-1">The English Garden is freely accessible</cite> and is one of the world's largest urban parks — 370 hectares (versus 341 for Central Park in New York), with lakes, a Japanese garden, Chinese Tower (a wooden pagoda surrounded by a beer garden), and vast meadows where thousands of Munich residents come to read, walk, or swim in the Eisbach.

**Eisbach Surfers** — the artificial wave created by the fast-flowing Eisbach river current is the world's only permanent urban surf spot. One surfer rides the wave at a time, 30–60 seconds each. Spectators gather on the bridge above. **Free, permanent** (more animated in summer). Location: southern entrance of the English Garden, near Haus der Kunst.

**Chinese Tower Beer Garden** — the beer garden around the Chinese pagoda at the center of the park. <cite index="39-1">A litre of beer (Masskrug): €8–10, giant pretzels €5–7, traditional meals €8–15.</cite> You can bring your own food — it is completely normal and accepted.

**Insider tip:** <cite index="40-1">One of Munich's best-known money-saving traditions: you are legally allowed to bring your own food to beer gardens, as long as you buy your drinks from the venue. This is widely observed by locals and completely accepted.</cite> A supermarket picnic eaten at a beer garden table, with a €9 Masskrug of Augustiner, is one of the best value experiences in Munich.

## Nymphenburg Palace — the Wittelsbachs' Summer Residence

6 km west of the center (tram 17 direct), Nymphenburg Palace is the Wittelsbach royal family's summer residence — a vast Baroque construction from the 17th century, with a central canal, 490-hectare park, and impressive satellite pavilions.

**What not to miss:**
- **Gallery of Beauties** — 36 portraits of women who fascinated King Ludwig I, including Helene Sedlmayr (a cobbler's daughter) alongside nobility and actress Lola Montez (who also brought about his abdication)
- **Amalienburg** — hunting pavilion with a Hall of Mirrors, considered the gem of German Rococo
- **The Park** — free strolling through canals, gardens, and pavilions

**2026 Prices:**

| Option | Price |
|---|---|
| Main palace (interior) | €8 |
| Full ticket (palace + pavilions) | ~€15 |
| Park | **Free** |
| Under 18s | **Free** |

**Hours:** 9:00–18:00 (April–October); 10:00–16:00 (November–March). Pavilions open April–15 October only.

## Deutsches Museum — the World's Largest Science and Technology Museum

On an island in the Isar river, <cite index="34-1">the Deutsches Museum is the world's largest science and technology museum</cite> — with a real submarine (U-Boot), Otto Lilienthal's pioneering glider, a full-scale underground mine reconstruction, the original 1965 Apollo lunar lander mockup, and a working Tesla coil. <cite index="34-1">€15 entry, allow at least 3 hours.</cite>

**Note:** The museum is undergoing partial renovation — check deutsches-museum.de for which sections are open at your visit date.

## Munich Residenz — the Wittelsbach Palace in the City Center

The Residenz is the palace complex in central Munich, the principal residence of the Bavarian royal family from 1508 to 1918 — 130 rooms, the Treasury collection of decorative arts (one of the world's richest), and the Cuvilliés-Theater, one of Europe's most beautiful Rococo theater spaces.

**Prices:** ~€13 (palace + treasury).

**Tip:** Cuvilliés-Theater has opera and chamber music performances — check the program at residenz-muenchen.de for the complete experience.

## Beer Gardens — the Authentic Bavarian Experience

Munich has 30+ beer gardens (Biergarten) — a tradition dating from the 19th century when brewers dug cellars under chestnut trees to keep beer cold in summer. Locals sat down in the shade and asked to drink there. The Bavarian government legalised the tradition in 1812 and added the right to bring your own food — a law that still stands today.

**Recommended beer gardens:**

**Hirschgarten** — the world's largest beer garden (8,000 seats), outside the tourist center. <cite index="40-1">A litre of beer: €8–10 versus €12–13 at Hofbräuhaus.</cite> Authentic local atmosphere.

**Chinese Tower (English Garden)** — central and accessible, inside the park. Lively and touristy but pleasant.

**Augustiner Keller** — near the central station, one of Munich's oldest breweries, with Augustiner beer served directly from wooden barrels.

**Biergarten am Viktualienmarkt** — central, in the heart of the market, perfect for a lunch break.

### Hofbräuhaus — the 400-Year-Old Institution

<cite index="40-1">Visit Hofbräuhaus once for the atmosphere</cite> — long benches, brass band music, tourists from everywhere, and beer at €12–13 a litre. It is an experience in itself, but not where you drink every evening. Go once, stay 2 hours, drink one Masskrug. Ticked off.

## Bavarian Food — What to Order

### Essential dishes

**Weisswurst** (white sausage) — white veal sausage seasoned with parsley and lemon, always boiled (never fried), served with sweet Bavarian mustard and a Brezn (pretzel). <cite index="41-1">Munich tradition: eaten before noon, with wheat beer (Weissbier).</cite> **€3–5 each** at any traditional brewery. Local rule: you don't eat weisswurst after noon — it's a Bavarian breakfast or brunch.

**Schweinshaxe** (pork knuckle) — roasted in beer, with crispy skin and juicy meat, served with red sauerkraut and Knödel (bread dumplings). <cite index="42-1">€22–32</cite> at a good restaurant — a complete meal for one or a large portion for two.

**Obatzda** — camembert cheese paste with butter, beer, and spices, served with Brezn. The definitive beer garden appetizer. **€4–6.**

**Brezn** (Bavarian pretzels) — large, soft, with chunky salt, completely different from industrial pretzels. <cite index="42-1">€3–5 at a street stand, €5–8 at a restaurant.</cite>

**Leberkäsesemmel** — sandwich with leberkäse (baked meatloaf, thick slices), mustard, in a bread roll. <cite index="43-1">~€4.50</cite> — the best quick lunch in Munich, from any butcher (Metzgerei).

**Apfelstrudel** — apple strudel with icing sugar and cream or vanilla ice cream. **€5–8.** The definitive Bavarian dessert.

### Approximate food budget

| Meal type | Cost per person |
|---|---|
| Weisswurst + Brezn + Weissbier (breakfast) | €10–15 |
| Leberkäsesemmel at a butcher (quick lunch) | €4–6 |
| Full meal at a traditional brewery | €20–35 |
| Complete Schweinshaxe | €22–32 |
| Litre of beer at a local beer garden | €8–10 |
| Litre of beer at Hofbräuhaus | €12–13 |

**Golden rule:** <cite index="40-1">Two streets away from Marienplatz, Hofbräuhaus, and the central shopping streets, the same meal costs 30–40% less.</cite>

## Oktoberfest — the 6-Million-Visitor Phenomenon

Oktoberfest is the world's largest folk festival — <cite index="42-1">September 19 – October 4, 2026</cite>, on the Theresienwiese, 10 minutes on foot from Munich's center.

**Entry to the grounds: FREE.** You pay for food and drink.

**2026 prices inside the tents:**

| Item | Price |
|---|---|
| Masskrug (1 litre beer) | <cite index="42-1">€13.60–15.80</cite> |
| Rotisserie chicken (Halbes Hendl) | <cite index="42-1">€16–20</cite> |
| Pork knuckle (Schweinshaxe) | <cite index="42-1">€22–32</cite> |
| Pretzel (Brezn) | <cite index="42-1">€5–8</cite> |
| Bratwurst on the street (cheaper) | <cite index="42-1">€5–8</cite> |

**The tents (Zelte):** 14 major tents (Spaten, Augustiner, Hofbräu, Paulaner, Löwenbräu etc.) + smaller tents. Each has a distinct personality. **Table reservations** for the tents are mandatory for weekends and evenings — they generally open in spring on the tents' official websites.

**Essential Oktoberfest tips:**
- **Arrive at 9:00 AM** for unreserved seats (tents open at 9:00–10:00 AM, free tables disappear fast)
- **Accommodation:** <cite index="42-1">Munich hotels during Oktoberfest cost 3–5x the normal rate</cite> — book at least 6–12 months ahead or stay in S-Bahn accessible suburbs
- **Tracht:** Lederhosen or Dirndl are not compulsory, but 80% of attendees wear them — if you want to fit in, buy in advance (€50–150 for decent versions, €300–500+ for authentic leather)
- **Avoid the scam:** <cite index="43-1">At busy beer gardens, people may offer to return your heavy glass mug — and pocket the €2–3 deposit (Pfand) that is rightfully yours.</cite>

## Neuschwanstein Castle — the Alpine Fairy Tale

130 km south of Munich in the Bavarian Alps, Neuschwanstein Castle is Germany's most visited monument and the direct inspiration for the Disney Cinderella castle. Built by King Ludwig II of Bavaria (nicknamed the "Mad King") between 1869–1886, it was never completed — Ludwig died mysteriously in 1886 at age 40.

**How to get there:**

| Option | Price | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Train Munich–Füssen (Bayern Ticket, group) | €29/group (1–5 people) | ~2h |
| Train Munich–Füssen (individual return) | ~€21 | ~2h |
| Bus Füssen–castle (73/78) | €4–6 | 10 min |
| Organised tour from Munich | €50–70/person | Full day |

**Castle ticket: €21 adults.** <cite index="32-1">Online booking mandatory 2–3 months ahead in summer — daily visitor quotas fill completely.</cite>

**Tip:** The smart visitor walks or takes a carriage to the castle (not the crowded bus) and also visits **Hohenschwangau Castle** (Ludwig's childhood home, €21) in the valley — the combination is richer than Neuschwanstein alone.

## Other Day Trips from Munich

**Salzburg (Austria)** — 1h30 by train, <cite index="39-1">covered by Bayern Ticket</cite> (yes, Bayern Ticket works into northern Austria). Mozart, Hohensalzburg Fortress, UNESCO Baroque center. A complete day.

**Dachau Concentration Camp Memorial** — 30 minutes by S-Bahn + bus. The first Nazi concentration camp (1933), with a documented permanent exhibition. **Free entry**, audio guide €6. Allow 3–4 hours.

**Lake Chiemsee** — "Bavaria's Sea," 1h by train, with Herrenchiemsee Castle on an island (Ludwig II's copy of Versailles). Bayern Ticket valid.

## Insider Tips — What Locals Know

**1. Weisswurst is not eaten after noon.** <cite index="41-1">Bavarian tradition says weisswurst is breakfast or brunch — eaten before noon with wheat beer.</cite> Ordering it at dinner makes locals raise an eyebrow.

**2. You can bring your own food to beer gardens.** <cite index="40-1">As long as you buy drinks from the venue, you can eat your own picnic at the table — a Bavarian law from 1812, still valid today.</cite> An Edeka or Rewe supermarket has everything you need.

**3. The Pfand deposit is yours.** The beer mug has a deposit (Pfand) of €2–3 — when you return it to the bar, you get the money back. <cite index="43-1">Don't let anyone "helpfully" return it for you.</cite>

**4. Supermarkets are closed on Sundays.** <cite index="40-1">Buy everything you need on Saturday</cite> — German supermarkets don't open on Sundays. Petrol stations and small bakeries are open.

**5. Always validate your transport ticket.** Germany has no turnstiles on the metro, but inspectors (in plain clothes) appear frequently. A fine costs €60. Always stamp your ticket at the validator machine.

**6. Bayern Ticket works after 9:00 AM on weekdays.** Bayern Ticket is valid Monday–Friday **after 9:00 AM** (to avoid competing with commuters). Saturday and Sunday — valid **from the first train of the day.** If you catch a train before 9:00 AM on a weekday, you need a different ticket.

**7. Pinakotheken on Sundays = €1 each.** <cite index="39-1">The major state museums cost €1 on Sundays</cite> — Alte Pinakothek (old European masters: Rubens, Rembrandt, Dürer), Neue Pinakothek (19th century), Pinakothek der Moderne (contemporary + design + architecture). All three on the same street — a full day of art for €3 total.
