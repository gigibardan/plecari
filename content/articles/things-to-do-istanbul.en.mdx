## Why Istanbul is unlike anywhere

Istanbul is the only city in the world that spans two continents. Europe on one side, Asia on the other, connected by ferries crossing the Bosphorus every 10 minutes. Layer on top: 2,500 years of history (Roman, Byzantine, Ottoman), the best street food in the Mediterranean basin, and prices that make London look like a different planet.

A Turkish breakfast spread costs €5. A ferry across the Bosphorus costs €1. Turkish tea: €0.50. You can eat like royalty for €15/day.

## Sultanahmet — where empires meet

### Hagia Sophia

For 1,000 years it was the world's largest cathedral (Byzantine, 537 AD). Then the Ottomans converted it to a mosque. Then Atatürk made it a museum. In 2020, it became a mosque again — and it's free to enter.

**Practical info:** open daily (closed briefly during prayer times — check schedule). Remove shoes, women cover heads (scarves available at entrance). Go at 8:00 AM to avoid crowds. The interior — 55 metres of dome, gold mosaics, Islamic calligraphy — is overwhelming in the best way.

**[Guided tour](https://www.getyourguide.com/istanbul-l56/hagia-sophia-blue-mosque-t597339/?partner_id=EKAA80H) recommended** (€20-35) — without context, you're looking at a big room. With a guide, you're walking through 1,500 years of civilisational shift.

### Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed)

Six minarets, 20,000 hand-painted blue Iznik tiles, and 200 stained glass windows. Free entry. Closed during prayer times (5 times daily, ~30 minutes each). Dress modestly (coverings provided at entrance).

**Tip:** the interior is most impressive at midday when light streams through the stained glass.

### Topkapi Palace

The Ottoman sultans' residence for 400 years. The Harem (€7 extra) is the highlight — opulent tiled rooms where the sultan's family lived. The Treasury has the 86-carat Spoonmaker's Diamond.

**Entry:** €20 (palace) + €7 (Harem), or **Museum Pass Istanbul: €55** (covers 12 museums over 5 days — worth it if you visit 3+). [Book guided tour on GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/istanbul-l56/?partner_id=EKAA80H) for skip-the-line.

### Basilica Cistern

Underground Byzantine water reservoir (6th century) with 336 marble columns, atmospheric lighting, and two mysterious Medusa head bases. €15 entry. 20-minute visit but unforgettable.

## Grand Bazaar — the world's oldest shopping mall

4,000+ shops in a covered labyrinth that's been operating since 1461. Leather, ceramics, textiles, jewellery, Turkish lamps, spices, and everything in between.

**Key rules:**
- Bargain. Always. Starting price is 2-3x the final price
- Start at 50% of the asking price and settle at 60-70%
- Walk away — they'll call you back with a lower price
- Don't buy at the first shop — compare
- The deeper inside the bazaar, the better the prices

**Alternative:** Arasta Bazaar (behind the Blue Mosque) is smaller, calmer, and fixed-price — less stress, slightly higher prices.

**[Guided bazaar tour](https://www.getyourguide.com/istanbul-l56/grand-bazaar-t643510/?partner_id=EKAA80H)** (€25-40) — a local guide shows you the hidden passages, workshops, and historic hans (caravanserais) that most tourists never see.

## Bosphorus — the strait between continents

### Public ferry (the budget option)

The Şehir Hatları commuter ferry from Eminönü to Kadıköy costs €1 and gives you a 20-minute Bosphorus crossing with views of Maiden's Tower, Galata Tower, and the Asian shore. It's the best €1 you'll spend in Istanbul.

The full Bosphorus cruise (Eminönü → Anadolu Kavağı, the northern end of the strait) costs €7 on the public ferry, takes 90 minutes each way, and passes under both Bosphorus bridges, past Ottoman waterfront palaces, and through fishing villages.

### Dinner cruise (the splurge)

[Evening Bosphorus dinner cruise](https://www.getyourguide.com/istanbul-l56/bosphorus-dinner-cruise-t415437/?partner_id=EKAA80H) (€40-60): 3 hours, Turkish dinner, live music, illuminated shoreline. Touristy? Yes. Worth it? Also yes — the illuminated mosques and palaces from the water at night are spectacular.

## The neighbourhoods tourists miss

### Kadıköy (Asian side)

Take the ferry from Eminönü to Kadıköy (€1, 20 minutes). The food market here is where Istanbulites actually shop — it's half the price of Sultanahmet and twice the quality. Walk through the Moda neighbourhood for waterfront cafés, vintage shops, and the best ice cream in Istanbul.

### Balat

The colourful, hilly neighbourhood that's taken over Instagram. Genuinely photogenic — painted wooden houses stacked on steep cobblestone streets. But it's also a real residential area with traditional Turkish life. Go in the morning for authentic atmosphere before the influencers arrive.

### Karaköy & Galata

Galata Tower (€12, panoramic view) anchors this neighbourhood of contemporary art galleries, hip coffee shops, and Karaköy's revitalised waterfront. Walk from Galata Bridge (where fishermen line the railings) up the steep streets to the tower.

## Where to eat

**Turkish breakfast** (kahvaltı) — the full spread: cheese varieties, olives, honey, kaymak (clotted cream), eggs, bread, tomatoes, cucumbers, and endless tea. €5-8/person at a breakfast spot. **Van Kahvaltı Evi** (Cihangir) is the classic.

**Kebabs** — not just döner. Adana kebab (spicy minced meat on a skewer), İskender (over bread with tomato sauce and yogurt), and beyti (rolled kebab). Full meal: €5-8 at local spots.

**Street food** — simit (sesame bread ring, €0.30), balık ekmek (fish sandwich from Eminönü waterfront, €3-4), kumpir (loaded baked potato in Ortaköy, €4-5), kokoreç (grilled lamb intestine — sounds wrong, tastes incredible, €3-4).

**Tea and coffee** — Turkish tea: €0.50 everywhere. Turkish coffee: €2-3. Accept every offer of tea — it's how Turkey socialises.

## Getting around

**Istanbul Card:** rechargeable transit card, works on metro, tram, bus, and ferries. €1 per ride (vs €2+ cash). Buy at kiosks near metro stations.

**Metro + tram:** extensive network. Tram T1 runs through Sultanahmet to Kabataş (for the funicular to Taksim).

**Ferries:** the best transport in Istanbul. Eminönü → Kadıköy (20 min), Eminönü → Üsküdar (15 min). €1 with Istanbul Card.

**Bolt:** works in Istanbul, cheaper and safer than yellow taxis. [Avoid taxi scams](/en/asigurari/travel-scams-europe-2026) — the "friendly stranger" bar scam is Istanbul's signature con.

**Airport:** Istanbul Airport (IST) is 40+ km from the centre. Havaist bus to Taksim: €5, 60-90 minutes. Taxi/Bolt: €25-40.

## Practical tips

**Passport required.** Turkey is not in the EU. Most nationalities: visa-free 90 days, passport valid 6+ months.

**Currency:** Turkish Lira (TRY). €1 ≈ 36-38 TRY. Cards accepted at hotels and restaurants. Cash essential at bazaars and street vendors. [Revolut/Wise](/en/zboruri/budget-travel-tips-europe-2026) for zero-fee conversion.

**[Travel insurance](https://ektatraveling.tpo.mx/uU7jZ5jK):** ESSENTIAL. EHIC does not work in Turkey. [Full guide](/en/asigurari/travel-insurance-europe-guide).

**[eSIM](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3):** Turkey = no free EU roaming. An eSIM saves €3-15/day. [Comparison](/en/esim-roaming/best-esim-europe-2026).

**Mosque etiquette:** remove shoes, cover shoulders and knees, women cover hair. Scarves and shoe bags provided at major mosques.

**[Travel scams](/en/asigurari/travel-scams-europe-2026):** the "friendly local who invites you to his bar" is Istanbul's #1 scam. The bill arrives at €200+. Politely decline.
