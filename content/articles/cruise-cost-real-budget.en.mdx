The advertisement says "7-night Mediterranean cruise from €449." You look at your credit card statement after the cruise and the number is considerably larger. This guide is the honest version — what a cruise actually costs, broken down line by line, in three realistic scenarios. No asterisks.

## What the cabin price does and does not include

Before the scenarios, the critical distinction:

**Included in the cabin price:**
- Your cabin for the voyage duration
- Breakfast, lunch and dinner at the main buffet and included dining room
- Onboard entertainment: theatre shows, live music, pools, gym, deck activities
- Access to all ports on the itinerary

**NOT included:**
- Flights to the embarkation port and back
- Hotel the night before departure (strongly recommended — see [first cruise guide](/en/croaziere/first-cruise-guide-beginners))
- Transfer from airport to port
- **Mandatory service charge/gratuity:** €10–15 per person per day, added automatically to your onboard account
- Alcoholic drinks (beer €4–7, cocktails €8–14, wine €6–10/glass)
- Drinks package (€40–65/person/day if you want all-inclusive beverages)
- Shore excursions (€40–120/person per excursion)
- Specialty restaurants (€20–50/person, beyond included dining)
- Wi-Fi (€20–35/day, or €80–150 for the voyage)
- Spa and beauty treatments
- Professional photography packages (€30–150)
- Casino
- Travel insurance

The gap between advertised and actual cost is real and consistent across all cruise lines. The scenarios below show what this adds up to in practice.

## The five costs that surprise people most

### 1. The gratuity — €140–210 for 7 nights (2 people)

This is consistently the biggest surprise on a first cruise. Most cruise lines add the service charge automatically to your onboard account daily, at €10–15 per person. You see it appear each evening when you check your account balance. At the end of the cruise, it is settled with your credit card.

It is removable at Guest Services — but removing it is broadly considered poor practice and directly affects the wages of the cabin steward who tidies your room twice daily, the waiter who remembers your coffee order from day one, and the restaurant staff who serve 2,000 passengers three times a day. Budget for it from the beginning.

### 2. Wi-Fi — €80–150 for 7 nights

Ship Wi-Fi uses satellite connectivity shared among thousands of passengers. Speed is inconsistent. Price is €20–35/day or a package for the voyage.

**The better solution:** an eSIM with a European data plan covers you at every port (where you actually need internet — maps, restaurant searches, translations, social media), costs €10–15 for the entire cruise period, and gives you 4G speeds rather than slow satellite. In open sea, you don't need it. See the full comparison: [best eSIM by destination 2026](/en/esim-roaming/best-esim-by-destination).

### 3. Shore excursions — €40–120/person per port

Ship excursions are the most significant optional cost and also the most avoidable. For the same content:

| Port | Ship excursion | Independent |
|---|---|---|
| Rome (from Civitavecchia) | €85–120/person | €20–30/person (train + Colosseum entry) |
| Athens (from Piraeus) | €65–90/person | €12–18/person (metro + Acropolis entry) |
| Pompeii (from Naples) | €75–110/person | €22–28/person (train + Pompeii entry) |
| Dubrovnik | €45–70/person | Free walk off the ship + €35 for city walls |
| Ephesus (from Kusadasi) | €55–90/person | €25–40/person (taxi + site entry) |

For 4 ports, independently: €79–136/person. By ship: €260–390/person. The saving for two people: €300–500.

**When ship excursions are worth it:** complex archaeological sites where a guide genuinely adds context (Pompeii, Ephesus), or ports where the ship's departure time is tight and any delay risk is unacceptable (the ship's tour is guaranteed to return before departure; your independent timing is not).

For pre-booking independent excursions with smaller groups and local guides, [GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/?partner_id=EKAA80H&utm_medium=online_publisher) consistently undercuts ship prices by 40–60% for equivalent tours.

### 4. Professional photography — €30–150 per package

Photographers are positioned at embarkation, at the captain's dinner, at every port gangway, and at the formal night — everywhere. The individual photos are priced at €15–25 each; packages for the voyage run €100–200. They take good photos in good light with professional equipment.

The simplest alternative: someone in your group photographs embarkation and port arrivals on a phone. The quality gap has narrowed considerably; most modern phones handle low-light ship dining rooms adequately.

### 5. Specialty restaurant dinners — €25–50/person

The included dining room and buffet are genuinely good across most cruise lines. Specialty restaurants (Italian, steakhouse, Asian, seafood) are in a different category — better ingredients, smaller portions, a completely different atmosphere. At €25–50/person, one specialty dinner for two costs €50–100.

Most first-time cruisers book one specialty dinner as a mid-voyage treat. This is a reasonable approach — experience the included dining first, decide if you want the upgrade.

## The cruise vs comparable land holiday: honest comparison

People ask whether a cruise is good value. The right comparison is not the cabin price against a budget hotel — it is the all-in cost against a comparable all-in land holiday.

| Component | 7-night land holiday, Italy (2 people) | 7-night Mediterranean cruise (2 people, Scenario 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Accommodation | €700–1,000 (3-star hotel, 1 destination) | Included |
| Main meals (3/day, 7 days) | €700–1,000 | Included |
| Local transport | €150–250 | Included |
| Activities / sites | €100–200 | Partially included |
| Flights | €200–300 | €200–350 |
| **Total** | **€1,850–2,750** | **€1,510–2,265** |

On this comparison, the budget cruise wins on price and adds 4–5 destinations versus 1. The land holiday wins on depth — you spend 7 days in one place and understand it much better than 8 hours in each of 5 ports.

The honest answer: they are different experiences, not directly comparable. A cruise is the better choice for people who want variety, who have already visited most of the destinations individually, or who are combining destinations that would otherwise require multiple flights (e.g. Rome, Athens, Dubrovnik, and a Greek island in one trip).

## Where to save the most money on a cruise

If your budget is limited, here is where the real savings are, ranked by impact:

**1. Choose interior over balcony cabin — save €300–600**
The interior cabin costs 40–60% less and is perfectly comfortable. You spend 90% of your time outside the cabin.

**2. Skip the drinks package if you drink moderately — save €400–700**
Calculate honestly: how many drinks per day? Under 2.5 drinks per person per day, buying individually is cheaper.

**3. Go independent at most ports — save €200–500**
Use trains, metros, local buses and taxis. Research each port for 20 minutes before sailing. GetYourGuide for the one or two ports where a guided excursion genuinely adds value.

**4. Book early — save €200–500 on the cabin**
Early booking discounts of 15–30% are standard across all major cruise lines, applied 6–10 months before sailing. Early bookers also have the best cabin selection.

**5. eSIM instead of ship Wi-Fi — save €60–130**
€10–15 for a European data eSIM versus €80–150 for the ship's package, with better speeds at every port.

**6. Fly from a secondary airport or use budget airlines for the embarkation flight**
The cheapest flight to Rome can be 40–50% less than the most expensive on the same day. Search with date flexibility (see [how to find cheap flights](/en/zboruri/how-to-find-cheap-flights)) and book independently rather than through the cruise line's flight package, which is almost always more expensive.

## When last-minute makes sense

Last-minute cruise deals (under 4 weeks before sailing) genuinely exist. Cruise lines discount heavily on unsold cabins, particularly:
- Interior and oceanview cabins (easier to fill; balcony cabins sell earlier)
- Shoulder season sailings (May, June, September, October)
- Longer voyages (10–14 nights, which have a smaller audience)
- Less popular itineraries or embarkation ports

The catch: you cannot choose your specific cabin or deck, the itinerary is fixed, and the dates are not your choice. For flexible couples without children, last-minute can produce genuine savings of 30–50% on the cabin price.

## Travel insurance: non-negotiable for a cruise

A cruise concentrates several travel risks:
- The toughest cancellation policies in travel (up to 100% forfeiture within 14 days of sailing)
- Medical emergencies far from shore, where helicopter evacuation can cost €15,000–50,000
- Missed port departures (you miss the ship in a port — your problem to fix)
- Itinerary changes without compensation under standard insurance

A standard travel insurance policy may not cover all cruise-specific scenarios. Check explicitly that your policy covers: medical evacuation at sea, missed port departure, and cruise cancellation. See the full breakdown: [travel insurance guide 2026](/en/asigurari/travel-insurance-guide).
