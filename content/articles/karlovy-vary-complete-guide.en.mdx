There are places in Europe that take your breath away before you even arrive — through reputation alone, through photographs, through the way their name sounds when you say it out loud. Karlovy Vary is one of those places.

I arrived there the first time without high expectations and left convinced it's one of the most beautiful spa resorts on the continent. Not because it's extravagant or noisy — quite the opposite. It's a quiet, elegant town with Belle Époque architecture that makes you feel you've accidentally stepped into another century. The Teplá River flows through the middle of the town, colonnades arch over the thermal springs, steam rises from vents in the pavement, and people stroll with ceramic cups in hand, sipping hot mineral water with a seriousness that first amuses you, then wins you over.

Since 2021, Karlovy Vary has been officially recognised on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the "Great Spa Towns of Europe" project, alongside 10 other resorts across seven European countries.

This guide tells you everything you need to know before you pack your bags.

## Karlovy Vary at a Glance — What to Know First

**Location:** West Bohemia, Czech Republic, 120 km west of Prague (1.5 hours by car or coach).

**Population:** approximately 49,000 residents — a small town you can explore entirely on foot.

**Altitude:** 380 m, in a deep valley flanked by wooded hills.

**Currency:** Czech Crown (CZK). 1 EUR ≈ 25 CZK in 2026. Cards are accepted almost everywhere, but it's useful to have a few crowns for markets and small shops.

**Language:** Czech is the official language. English is well understood in the tourist area. German is also common — the town was called Carlsbad for centuries and was a favourite destination of German and Austro-Hungarian aristocracy.

**UNESCO:** Inscribed in 2021 as part of "Great Spa Towns of Europe", alongside Baden-Baden, Bath, Spa (Belgium), Vichy and 6 other resorts.

**Best for:** couples, lovers of architecture and history, active travellers (hiking), those wanting an elegant alternative to Prague.

## How to Get from Romania to Karlovy Vary

### Option 1 — Fly Bucharest → Prague + Transfer (Recommended)

There are no direct flights from Bucharest to Karlovy Vary. The simplest route is:

1. **Flight OTP → PRG** (Otopeni → Prague Václav Havel): 1h45 flight time. Operators: Wizz Air, Ryanair, TAROM. Prices: 50–150 EUR return if booked 4–6 weeks in advance.
2. **Transfer Prague → Karlovy Vary**: RegioJet or FlixBus coach (~2 hours, ~8–12 EUR), train (~3 hours, ~15 EUR) or private taxi (~80–100 EUR, 1.5 hours).

If you want to skip the logistics, there are **guided day tours from Prague** to Karlovy Vary — an excellent option if you're based in Prague and want to include KV as an excursion.

## Day Trip from Prague to Karlovy Vary — The Hassle-Free Option

### Option 2 — By Car (from Romania)

Distance Bucharest → Karlovy Vary: approximately 1,600 km, 15–17 hours of driving. The route passes through Hungary, Austria or Germany. Not practical for a short city break, but fits well into a Central European road trip.

### Option 3 — By Coach

There are direct coaches from Bucharest to Prague (FlixBus, RegioJet: ~16–18 hours, 40–70 EUR), from where it's another 2 hours to Karlovy Vary. The budget option, less comfortable.

## What to See in Karlovy Vary

### The 5 Main Colonnades

The colonnades are the heart of the town — elegant covered galleries built between the 18th and 20th centuries, housing the thermal springs. Access is free, but you need a special spa cup (3–15 EUR from shops) to sip the mineral water.

**1. Mill Colonnade (Mlýnská kolonáda)** — the most impressive, with neoclassical columns and 5 springs. Built 1871–1881 to plans by architect Josef Zítek. Don't miss the allegorical statues on the roof.

**2. Hot Spring Colonnade (Vřídelní kolonáda)** — modern structure (1975), housing the most powerful spring — Vřídlo. This natural geyser shoots water at 72°C to a height of 12 metres. Impressive to watch, impossible to drink.

**3. Market Colonnade (Tržní kolonáda)** — a light wooden structure in Swiss style, two springs, more intimate atmosphere. Located right in the town centre.

**4. Castle Colonnade (Zámecká kolonáda)** — built in 1913, containing two Castle springs with different properties. Slightly away from the crowds.

**5. Garden Colonnade (Sadová kolonáda)** — the most elegant architecturally, built in 1880, at the southern end of the spa centre. Springs here are cooler and easier to drink.

## Guided Walking Tour Through the Colonnades — The Best Introduction to the City

### The Vřídlo Geyser — The Main Spectacle

If there's one thing you must not miss in Karlovy Vary, it's the Vřídlo geyser. Visible from inside the Hot Spring Colonnade, it shoots mineral water 12 metres into the air at 72°C with a flow rate of 2,000 litres per minute. It runs non-stop, without interruption — it's a natural phenomenon, not an artificial attraction.

### Diana Tower and the Funiculars

For a bird's eye view of the town, head up to **Diana Lookout Tower** — an observation tower built in 1914 on the hill dominating the town's valleys. You can reach it on foot (20 minutes from the centre), by **funicular** (2 funiculars in sequence, ~3–5 EUR) or on a longer hiking trail through the forest.

From the top, Karlovy Vary looks exactly as it does in photographs — colonnades, Belle Époque hotels, the Teplá River and wooded hills stretching in every direction.

### Moser Glass Factory

Founded in 1857 by Ludwig Moser, the factory produces internationally renowned art glassware, lead-free — a unique innovation at the time. **Daily guided tours** (1–2 hours, ~10 EUR/person) let you watch craftsmen blow and shape glass. The factory shop has slightly lower prices than the town centre stores. A Moser spa cup starts from 30–40 EUR.

### Jan Becher Museum — The Origin of Becherovka

**Becherovka** is not just a liqueur — it's a town institution. Created in 1807 by pharmacist Josef Vitus Becher and refined by his son Jan, the recipe contains 20 herbs and spices and remains secret to this day.

The Jan Becher Museum (entry: ~7 EUR, 45–60 minutes) explains the product's history with a tasting included at the end. Becherovka is traditionally drunk cold, neat or in the local combination called **Beton** (Becherovka + tonic water + lime).

### Loket Castle — Half-Day Excursion

Just 12 km from Karlovy Vary lies **Loket** — a medieval castle on a granite rock surrounded by a bend in the Ohře River. It's one of the most impressive landscapes in the Czech Republic and served as a filming location for **Casino Royale** (2006).

**How to get there:** local bus (~30 minutes, 2 EUR) or taxi (~15 EUR). Castle entry: 8–10 EUR. Full visit: 2–3 hours.

There's also a **12 km hiking trail** along the Ohře River from KV to Loket, passing through the Svatošské Rocks — spectacular rock formations. Duration: 3–4 hours. Return by bus.

### The Teplá River Promenade

The simplest and most pleasant thing to do in Karlovy Vary is a stroll along the Teplá River, from the colonnades to the Grand Hotel Pupp — about 1.5 km lined with Art Nouveau hotels, restaurant terraces and souvenir shops.

**Grand Hotel Pupp** — built in 1701, its white façade dominates the southern end of the town centre. It appeared in the film **Last Holiday** with Queen Latifah (2006). Even if you're not staying here, the lobby and salon restaurant are worth stepping into.

## Thermal Springs — A Practical Tasting Guide

There are **15 springs accessible to the public** in central Karlovy Vary, with temperatures between 30°C and 72°C. Each spring has a slightly different mineral composition.

| Spring | Temperature | Taste | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spring no. 3 (Market Col.) | 58°C | Moderate, slightly salty | Easy |
| Spring no. 5 (Mill Col.) | 60°C | More pronounced, sulphurous | Medium |
| Vřídlo Spring | 72°C | Extremely intense, not recommended for drinking | Hard |
| Sadová Spring | 35°C | The mildest, almost neutral | Easy |
| Castle Spring | 55°C | Pronounced mineral taste | Medium |

**Practical tip:** buy a spa cup with a spout (traditional porcelain models with a colonnade image, 3–8 EUR). This is the only suitable vessel for sipping hot water without burning yourself. Avoid plastic or glass — mineralised water affects their taste.

The thermal water is not recommended in large quantities for people with uncontrolled kidney or heart conditions. A few sips from a moderate spring pose no risk for healthy visitors.

## Food and Drink — What to Taste in Karlovy Vary

### Spa Wafers (Oplatky)

The number one edible souvenir from Karlovy Vary. Large round wafers filled with vanilla, chocolate or walnut cream, produced locally since 1788. The warm, freshly made version — available in shops that make them on the spot — is the best. Price: 0.50–1 EUR each.

### Recommended Restaurants for Different Budgets

**Mid-range (10–20 EUR/person for lunch):**
- **Restaurant Dvořák** — traditional Czech food, generous portions, town centre
- **Café Pupp** — brunch or light lunch in Grand Hotel Pupp, garden views
- **Embassy Restaurant** — Czech and international menu, good wine

**Budget (5–10 EUR/person):**
- Local bakeries and cafés in the centre
- Albert supermarket for a picnic by the Teplá River

**For something special:** restaurant **Promenáda** has one of the best menus in town, with reinvented Czech classics. Reservation recommended in season.

### What to Drink

- **Becherovka** — mandatory, "the 13th spring"
- **Beton** — Becherovka with tonic water, served cold with lime
- **Pilsner Urquell or Budvar** — excellent, cheap Czech beer (2–4 EUR/pint)
- **Spring water** — from your spa cup, directly at the colonnade, free

## Practical Tips from Direct Experience

**The spa cup — buy it in the first hour.** Without it you cannot properly taste the springs. Avoid plastic or glass models — porcelain or ceramic are the only ones that maintain the right temperature. Budget: 3–8 EUR for a decent decorative model.

**Walk early in the morning.** The colonnades are far more beautiful and peaceful between 7:00–9:00, before the guided tours arrive. You'll have them almost to yourself.

**Shoes matter.** The town centre has slopes and cobblestone paving. Comfortable trainers or light hiking shoes — not sandals or heels.

**Don't miss the golden hour.** When the sun sets behind the hills and golden light hits the Art Nouveau façades — it's a rare photographic moment.

**Avoid restaurants directly next to the colonnades.** Inflated prices and mediocre quality. Walk two streets further and you'll find much better food at half the price.

## Karlovy Vary vs. Mariánské Lázně — Which is Worth More?

Both are part of the West Bohemia spa triangle, recognised by UNESCO.

| Criterion | Karlovy Vary | Mariánské Lázně |
|---|---|---|
| **Size** | Larger, more lively | Smaller, more intimate |
| **Fame** | Global (Bond, Last Holiday) | Regional, less touristy |
| **Springs** | 15 springs, warmer water | 40+ springs, more varied |
| **Prices** | Medium-high | ~15–20% lower |
| **Tourism** | High, especially in summer | Moderate, quieter |
| **Best for** | First-timers, classic city break | Those who've already seen KV |
