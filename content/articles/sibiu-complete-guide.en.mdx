If you had to choose one Romanian city to show a European tourist who had never been to the country, it would be Sibiu.

Not because it's the largest or most famous. But because Sibiu concentrates, within a few square kilometres, everything Romania has of greatest architectural and cultural value: three interconnected medieval squares with Saxon houses bearing "eyes", a Baroque palace with one of the best art collections in Central Europe, the continent's largest open-air museum, and direct access to the Transfăgărășan — the road BBC declared "the world's greatest."

<cite index="95-1">Sibiu was European Capital of Culture in 2007 — the first Romanian city to receive this distinction.</cite>

It was founded in the 12th century by Saxon (German) colonists brought by Hungarian kings to defend Transylvania. For centuries it was called Hermannstadt and spoke German. <cite index="98-1">Piața Mare was first documented in 1411 as a grain market</cite> — and has remained the heart of the city ever since, a silent witness to five hundred years of commerce, public executions, festivals and medieval life.

## Sibiu at a Glance — Essential Facts

**County:** Sibiu, central Transylvania.

**Population:** approximately 148,000.

**Currency:** Romanian Leu (RON). 1 EUR ≈ 4.97 RON (rate fluctuates slightly).

**Language:** Romanian. German is understood by the remaining Saxon community and many Sibiu residents with German education. English is well spoken in the tourist area.

**Flights:** Blue Air and TAROM, OTP → SBZ, ~1 hour.

**Distance from Bucharest:** 280 km, ~3 hours by car on the A1 motorway.

**Average budget:** 150–250 RON/day (~30–50 EUR) — more affordable than Cluj or Brașov.

## How to Get from Bucharest to Sibiu

### Direct Flight OTP → SBZ

Blue Air and TAROM operate direct flights Bucharest (OTP) → Sibiu (SBZ). Duration: ~1 hour. 2026 prices: 50–120 EUR return.

**Airport → centre transfer:** taxi/Bolt, 15 minutes, 25–40 RON (~5–8 EUR).

### By Car

Bucharest → Sibiu: ~280 km, ~3 hours on the A1 motorway (Bucharest–Pitești) + DN1 via Râmnicu Vâlcea and Cozia Pass. **The spectacular mountain alternative:** via the Transfăgărășan (longer, 4–5 hours, but phenomenal scenery — open July–October).

### By Train

InterCity București Nord → Sibiu: ~4–5 hours, 80–120 RON (~16–24 EUR).

### By Coach

Flixbus and local operators: ~5–6 hours, 50–80 RON (~10–16 EUR). The cheapest option.

## What to See in Sibiu

### Piața Mare — Sibiu's Medieval Heart

<cite index="96-1">Piața Mare is the largest of Sibiu's three main squares and the city's heart for centuries — surrounded by remarkable medieval and Baroque buildings.</cite>

**Brukenthal Palace** — <cite index="96-1">one of Romania's most important art museums, housed in an 18th-century Baroque palace</cite> built by Samuel von Brukenthal, Governor of Transylvania. The collection includes Dutch, Flemish and Italian paintings from the 15th–18th centuries, plus Romanian art. Ticket: ~30 RON (~6 EUR).

**Council Tower (Turnul Sfatului)** — the 13th-century tower that guarded the fortress entrance. <cite index="97-1">Climb to the upper level for an exceptional panoramic view over Piața Mare and the red rooftops of the Old Town.</cite> Ticket: ~10–15 RON (~2–3 EUR).

**Blue House and Baroque buildings** — the coloured façades with eye-like windows (Sibiu's distinctive mansards) line the square. Exterior access: free.

### Piața Mică and the Bridge of Lies

<cite index="96-1">Piața Mică is the old merchants' centre, connected to Piața Mare via the Council Tower and to Piața Huet via the Bridge of Lies.</cite>

**Bridge of Lies (1859)** — <cite index="96-1">Romania's first cast-iron bridge, with the legend that it creaked when someone told a lie on it.</cite> Free crossing; view toward the Lower Town (Suburbiul de Jos).

### Piața Huet and the Evangelical Cathedral

<cite index="97-1">Piața Huet is dominated by the Lutheran Evangelical Cathedral — Sibiu's largest and most imposing church, built in the 14th–15th century in Gothic style.</cite>

Cathedral tower: <cite index="98-1">ticket ~5 RON (~1 EUR) — one of Romania's cheapest panoramic views and one of the best.</cite>

### ASTRA Museum — Europe's Largest Open-Air Museum

<cite index="96-1">The Traditional Popular Civilisation ASTRA Museum in Dumbrava Forest is Romania's largest open-air museum and one of Europe's largest — 96 hectares with 400+ traditional structures brought from across the country.</cite>

**What it contains:** water and wind mills, houses from all Romania's regions, wooden churches, craftsmen's workshops, traditional technical installations (oil presses, sawmills) and traditional domestic animals.

Adult ticket: 35 RON (~7 EUR). <cite index="97-1">Free on Mondays.</cite> Duration: 2–4 hours. Location: 4 km from the centre — bus or taxi.

### The Houses' Eyes — A Unique Architectural Detail

<cite index="97-1">The old houses in Sibiu's Old Town have attic windows with outward-opening shutters resembling eyes looking down at you from the rooftops — "the eyes of Sibiu."</cite>

This is a unique architectural feature in Europe — you'll only see them in Sibiu. Look for them on any street in the old centre.

## The Transfăgărășan — The World's Most Spectacular Road

<cite index="96-1">The Transfăgărășan (DN7C) crosses the Făgăraș Mountains from north to south, reaching a maximum altitude of 2,042 m at Bâlea Lake.</cite>

BBC Top Gear declared it in 2009 "the world's greatest road" and presented it in a special episode with a Ferrari 458, Aston Martin DB9 and Lamborghini Gallardo.

**Why it's spectacular:**
- Bâlea Lake — glacial lake at 2,034 m, surrounded by alpine peaks
- The Bâlea Tunnel — 887 m, crossing with views into both valleys
- Bâlea Waterfall — sought by hikers and photographers
- Poenari Fortress — the "real" Vlad Dracula's castle, on a cliff, 1,480 steps

**When it's open:** fully (including the tunnel) from July to October–November. The rest of the year, accessible by cable car from Bâlea Cascadă.

**Getting there from Sibiu:** 30 km to the Transfăgărășan entry point (Cartișoara or Avrig). By car: 1 hour. Private guided tour from Sibiu: 200–350 RON per person.

### Day Trips from Sibiu

**Sighișoara (90 km, 1.5 hours)** — the UNESCO medieval citadel, Clock Tower, Vlad Dracula's birthplace. Half-day or full-day excursion.

**Corvin Castle (90 km, 1.5 hours)** — <cite index="100-1">Transylvania's largest Gothic castle</cite> — in Hunedoara. Ticket: 45 RON (~9 EUR).

**Alba Iulia (75 km, 1 hour)** — the 18th-century Baroque fortress (Habsburg), Coronation Cathedral. Direct excursion or combined with Sighișoara.

**Păltiniș (32 km, 45 min)** — Romania's oldest mountain resort (1894), hiking trails.

## Food and Drink in Sibiu

### What You Must Eat

**Mici** — Romanian grilled meat rolls, obligatory. At a local terrace: 2–3 RON each (0.40–0.60 EUR).

**Ciorbă de burtă** — tripe soup with sour cream and garlic. Romanian rustic gastronomy's totem. 15–25 RON at a traditional restaurant.

**Sarmale cu mămăligă** — cabbage rolls with minced meat, served with polenta and sour cream. 25–40 RON a portion.

**Sibiu covrigi** — warm pretzels with poppy seeds or sesame sold on the street in Piața Mică and Piața Mare. 2–4 RON each.

### Transylvanian Wine

<cite index="97-1">The Târnave vineyard region (north of Sibiu) produces excellent aromatic white wines — Feteasca Regală, Muscat Ottonel, Italian Riesling.</cite> A glass at a restaurant: 15–25 RON (~3–5 EUR).

### Where to Eat

**Budget (20–50 RON/meal):**
- Restaurants near the squares (not directly on Piața Mare)
- Bakeries and covrigi shops in the centre

**Mid-range (50–120 RON/person):**
- **Crama Sibiu** — specifically Romanian, wine cellar with local wine
- **Hermania** — modern Romanian cuisine, great atmosphere
- Terraces on Piața Mică and Piața Huet

**Caution:** <cite index="97-1">restaurants directly on Piața Mare charge 30–50% more than those on adjacent streets.</cite>

## Recommended Itinerary — 3 Days in Sibiu

### Day 1 — Old Town: Three Squares

- **Morning:** Piața Mare — Brukenthal Palace, Council Tower (panorama), houses with eyes
- **Lunch:** covrigi in Piața Mică (2 RON) + soup at a local restaurant
- **Afternoon:** Piața Mică → Bridge of Lies → Piața Huet → Evangelical Cathedral (tower)

- **Morning:** ASTRA Museum (3–4 hours, 7 EUR or free on Mondays)
- **Lunch:** restaurant near Dumbrava area
- **Afternoon:** Orthodox Cathedral (Romania's second largest), Sibiu Zoo (optional)

- **Option A:** Transfăgărășan by car or guided tour — Bâlea Lake, Bâlea Waterfall (5–6 hours)
- **Option B:** Sighișoara (1.5 hours) — UNESCO citadel, Dracula's birthplace
- **Option C:** Corvin Castle + Alba Iulia circuit (full day)

## Practical Tips

**The Old Town is pedestrianised** — no cars inside. Park at the edge of the centre and walk.

**Free on Mondays at ASTRA** — if you're in Sibiu on a Monday, don't miss it.

**Transfăgărășan** — check online if the road is open before setting off (closes with first autumn snows). Official site: drumuri.ro.

**International Theatre Festival (FITS)** — held in May–June, transforming Sibiu's centre into a continuous stage for 10 days. If you love theatre and street art, plan your visit during this period.

**Christmas Market** — book accommodation 2–3 months ahead for November–December. Sibiu is fully booked during advent weekends.
