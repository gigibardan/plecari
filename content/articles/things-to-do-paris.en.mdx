## Why Paris is still Paris

Every first-time visitor has the same worry: "Is Paris overhyped?" And every first-time visitor has the same reaction when they turn a corner and see the Eiffel Tower framed between Haussmann buildings at dusk: "No. No it is not."

Paris earns its reputation not through any single attraction but through accumulation: the way light falls on limestone, the smell of fresh bread at 7 AM, the scale of the Louvre's galleries, the casual perfection of a €1.20 croissant, and the Seine flowing through all of it. It's a city that rewards both planning and wandering.

## The icons

### Eiffel Tower

**Tickets:** €18.80 (stairs to 2nd floor), €29.40 (elevator to summit). **[Book on GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/paris-l16/eiffel-tower-t1008808/?partner_id=EKAA80H) 60 days ahead** — summit tickets sell out completely. Without advance booking, the queue is 2-3 hours.

**Tips:** sunset tickets (7-8 PM in summer) are the most coveted — the city turns golden, then the tower lights sparkle on the hour after dark. The 2nd floor is arguably better than the summit (you can see the tower's structure around you, not just from above).

**Free alternative:** Trocadéro esplanade (across the Seine) has the classic front-on Eiffel Tower view. The Champ de Mars is perfect for a wine + cheese picnic with the tower as backdrop.

### Louvre

The world's largest art museum. 380,000 objects, 35,000 on display, 8 miles of galleries. You cannot see everything. Don't try.

**Strategy:** pick 2-3 highlights (Mona Lisa, Venus de Milo, Winged Victory of Samothrace, Crown Jewels) and spend 2-3 hours. Or [book a guided tour](https://www.getyourguide.com/paris-l16/?partner_id=EKAA80H) that curates the route for you.

**Tickets:** €22. **Free first Sunday of the month (October-March).** Closed Tuesdays.

### Notre-Dame

Reopened in December 2024 after the 2019 fire restoration. Free entry. The restored interior is spectacular — cleaner and brighter than pre-fire. Queues can be long; go early.

### Sacré-Cœur + Montmartre

The white basilica on the hilltop. Free entry. The view from the steps covers all of Paris. Behind Sacré-Cœur, Montmartre's cobblestone streets, artist squares (Place du Tertre), and vineyard are worth 2-3 hours of wandering.

**Warning:** [bracelet scammers](/en/asigurari/travel-scams-europe-2026) operate at the base of Sacré-Cœur. Hands in pockets, firm "no," keep walking.

## The neighbourhoods

### Le Marais — the best all-round quarter

Medieval streets, gay village, Jewish quarter (best falafel in Paris at L'As du Fallafel, €8), galleries, vintage shops, and Place des Vosges (Paris's oldest planned square, perfect for sitting). Le Marais is where Parisians go on Sunday.

### Latin Quarter — student energy

Around the Sorbonne and the Panthéon. Shakespeare & Company bookshop (free to browse, the most literary shop in the world), Jardin du Luxembourg (free, the most beautiful park in Paris), and cheap restaurants and crêperies on Rue Mouffetard.

### Saint-Germain — literary Left Bank

Where Sartre and de Beauvoir held court at Café de Flore (still there, €6 for a coffee). Elegant, expensive, and effortlessly Parisian. The bookshops, galleries, and pastry shops are worth the premium.

## Where to eat

**Croissant** — from any boulangerie (bakery): €1.20. The quality floor in Paris is higher than the ceiling in most cities. Look for "boulangerie artisanale" (they make everything on site).

**Crêpe** — street stands in Montmartre and the Latin Quarter: €4-6 for a Nutella+banana crêpe. Savoury galettes (buckwheat crêpes with ham, cheese, egg): €8-12 at sit-down crêperies.

**Brasserie meal** — steak frites, duck confit, or croque-monsieur with a glass of wine: €15-20. **Le Bouillon Chartier** (9th arr.) has served classic French food at workers' prices since 1896 — full 3-course meal for €15-20 in a stunning Belle Époque dining room.

**Wine + cheese + baguette** — buy at any fromagerie + cave à vin + boulangerie = a €10-12 picnic that rivals any restaurant.

## Getting around

**Metro:** 16 lines, covers everywhere. Single ticket: €2.15. Navigo Easy (rechargeable card, same price). Day pass: €16.90 (zones 1-5, includes airport).

**Walking:** central Paris is surprisingly walkable. Notre-Dame to Louvre: 15 minutes. Louvre to Eiffel Tower: 30 minutes.

**Airport:** CDG: RER B to central Paris (€11.45, 35 min). Orly: Orlyval + RER B (€14.10, 40 min). Beauvais (budget airlines): bus to Porte Maillot (€17, 75 min — [plan for this](/en/zboruri/budget-travel-tips-europe-2026)).

## Day trips

### Versailles
**[Book on GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/paris-l16/versailles-t59565/?partner_id=EKAA80H)** (€30-50, includes transport + skip-the-line + guide) or DIY: RER C to Versailles-Château (€7.50 return, 40 min) + palace entry €21. Go Tuesday-Friday (Monday closed, weekends packed). The gardens are free except on Musical Fountain Show days (€10).

### Giverny (Monet's garden)
April-October only. Train to Vernon (45 min, €15 return) + shuttle bus. Monet's water lily garden in real life. [GYG tours](https://www.getyourguide.com/paris-l16/?partner_id=EKAA80H) combine Giverny + Versailles in one day.
