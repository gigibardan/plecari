Romania is one of Europe's most rewarding and least visited countries for short breaks. While most Western tourists fly past it on the way to Prague or Budapest, Romanians who travel within their country know it has something genuinely rare: destinations with the visual quality of Western European highlights — medieval citadels, painted monastery frescoes, glacier lakes, one of the world's great river deltas — at prices that feel like a different era.

A weekend for two in Brașov costs €150–300 all-in. The same quality experience in Salzburg or Bruges would cost three times that. This guide covers 10 of the best getaways from Bucharest — with distances, real costs, and exactly what to do.

## Getting Around Romania — Your Options

A car is the most flexible option for most getaways on this list, particularly Maramureș, Bucovina, the Danube Delta, and Cheile Nerei. [GetRentacar](https://getrentacar.tpo.mx/C6z4XHxU) aggregates Romanian rental companies for competitive rates; major international companies also operate from Bucharest Otopeni airport.

For Brașov, Sinaia, and Sibiu, trains are excellent:
- Bucharest–Brașov: InterCity train, 2.5 hours, €12–18 each way (book at cfrcalatori.ro)
- Bucharest–Sinaia: 1.5 hours, €8–12
- Bucharest–Sibiu: 4.5–5 hours, €15–20 (or 2.5h by car on the A1 motorway)

For a multi-stop Transylvania itinerary (Brașov + Sighișoara + Sibiu), a car gives you freedom to stop at castles, villages, and viewpoints that trains bypass.

## 1. Brașov + Bran Castle + Sinaia (2.5h, €150–300/couple)

**The summary:** Romania's most accessible and most rewarding short break. A beautifully preserved medieval city in the foothills of the Carpathians, with Bran Castle 30 minutes away and Peleș Castle 45 minutes in the other direction.

**Brașov itself:**

The old town centres on Council Square (*Piața Sfatului*) — one of Central Europe's most beautiful medieval squares, surrounded by pastel facades, the 14th-century Council House, and narrow streets leading to fortified towers. The **Black Church** (largest Gothic church between Vienna and Istanbul, built 1383–1477, €5) houses remarkable Oriental rugs collected over centuries. The **Strada Sforii** is one of the narrowest streets in Europe — 1.1–1.35 metres wide, connecting two districts through a gap between medieval houses.

The **Tâmpa Mountain** cable car (€10 return) gives a panoramic view of the city and surrounding valleys. The hiking path up takes 45 minutes if you prefer it.

**Bran Castle (30 min):**

The "Dracula's Castle" association is mostly marketing — Vlad the Impaler's connection to Bran was limited. But the 14th-century fortress on a cliff is genuinely dramatic and well-preserved, housing a good museum of Romanian royal history. Entry: €12. Buy tickets online to avoid summer queues. The castle was actually a favourite residence of Queen Marie of Romania (early 20th century) — her story is more interesting than the Dracula connection.

**Peleș Castle, Sinaia (45 min from Brașov):**

If Bran is Romania's most famous castle, Peleș is its most beautiful. A Neo-Renaissance masterpiece completed in 1883 for King Carol I of Romania, with 160 rooms, Murano glass, hand-carved woodwork, and Venetian mirrors. Standard tour: €10 (ground floor + exterior), extended tour: €20 (all accessible rooms). Closed Mondays and Tuesdays — plan accordingly.

**Where to eat in Brașov:**
- Sergiana: enormous portions of traditional Transylvanian food, tourist-popular but genuinely good. Full meal: €8–12.
- Restaurant Ceaun: the best traditional Romanian menu in the old town. Try the ciorbă de burtă (tripe soup, better than it sounds) and sarmale (stuffed cabbage rolls).
- Curtea Brașovului: courtyard restaurant in a medieval building, good wine selection.

**Accommodation:** Brașov old town has excellent 3-4 star hotels at €40–80/night. The Vila Borsec and numerous boutique options inside the city walls are particularly good.

## 2. Sibiu + Transfăgărășan Highway (4–5h, €200–400/couple)

**The summary:** Romania's most elegant city combined with, in season, the most spectacular mountain drive in Europe.

**Sibiu:**

Sibiu was European Capital of Culture in 2007 and still carries the investment made for it. The **Grand Square** (*Piața Mare*) is lined with Baroque facades, the **Brukenthal Palace and Museum** (€5, excellent collection of Flemish masters and Romanian art), and the extraordinary **Passage of Stairs** — a baroque staircase connecting the Upper and Lower towns. The "eyes of Sibiu" — dormer windows on rooftops designed to look like watching eyes — appear on buildings throughout the old town.

The **Astra Open-Air Museum** (5 km from centre, €8) recreates rural Romanian life with 300+ authentic structures moved from across the country — an extraordinary portrait of pre-industrial Romanian civilization.

**Crama Sibiu Vechi** is often cited as the best traditional restaurant in Romania. Order *bulz* (polenta filled with sheep's cheese and sour cream, baked), grilled mici, and local Jidvei wines. Arrive early in peak season.

**Transfăgărășan (open approximately June–October):**

The road climbs 90 km from Curtea de Argeș through the Făgăraș Mountains to 2,042 metres, then descends to Lake Vidraru. The highlights:

- **Bâlea Waterfall** (on the ascent): marked viewpoint, 15-minute walk to a dramatic 60-metre cascade
- **Bâlea Lake** (at the summit): a glacial lake at 2,034 metres. The mountain chalet serves traditional food. In season, the lake has paragliders launching from the ridgeline above.
- The hairpin descent to Curtea de Argeș on the southern side, with views of the reservoir and valley below

**Check road status before going:** transfagarasan.ro updates the opening dates and conditions. The road often opens mid-June; severe autumn weather can close it early.

The full drive takes 3–4 hours with stops. Most visitors approach from Sibiu (north entrance) and exit toward Curtea de Argeș (south), then continue to Bucharest on the A1 motorway.

## 3. Sighișoara — Medieval Citadel Still Alive (4h, €150–250/couple)

**The summary:** The best-preserved inhabited medieval citadel in Europe. Not a museum — people live inside the 12th-century walls.

**What makes it special:** Most European medieval towns have been partially or fully restored — authentic-looking but modern inside. Sighișoara's citadel quarter is genuinely original, with centuries-old houses still occupied. The **Clock Tower** (€5, museum + panoramic view), the **Covered Stairway** (176 wooden steps, built 1642, leads to the citadel church), and the **Church on the Hill** with its painted gothic interior are all intact and authentic.

**Vlad the Impaler:** The house where Vlad III (the historical inspiration for Dracula) was born in 1431 is now a restaurant — **Casa Vlad Dracul**. The food is average but the setting is fun. Order a local wine and pretend you're in 15th-century Transylvania.

**Practical:** Sighișoara is best experienced in the afternoon and evening, when the day-trippers leave and the citadel becomes quieter. Book accommodation **inside the citadel walls** — sleeping in a 12th-century medieval fortress is a genuinely unusual experience. Several small guesthouses operate within the walls, €30–50/night.

**Combining with Brașov and Sibiu:** Sighișoara sits on the Brașov–Sibiu axis (about 100 km from each), making it natural to include in a 3-day Transylvania circuit: Brașov (night 1) → Sighișoara (night 2) → Sibiu (night 3), returning to Bucharest on day 4.

## 4. Danube Delta — Europe's Largest Wetland (4–5h, €200–500/couple/night)

**The summary:** The Danube reaches the Black Sea through a delta of 5,800 km² of channels, lakes, reed beds, and forest — the largest and least disturbed wetland in Europe, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1991. This is not a city break. It's something different entirely.

**The experience:** From Tulcea (the gateway city, 4–5 hours from Bucharest by road), you take a boat into the delta. The main channels are wide and calm; the secondary channels and lakes are silent except for birdsong. In May–June, the delta hosts 320+ bird species including pelicans, herons, cormorants, spoonbills, and — famously — the largest colony of white pelicans in Europe (approximately 2,500 breeding pairs).

The delta villages (Sfântu Gheorghe, Mila 23, Crișan, Letea) are accessible only by boat — no roads, no cars, no supermarkets. Village guesthouses (*pensiuni*) provide accommodation and full board: fresh fish caught that day (carp, pike-perch, catfish, sturgeon in season), local wine, and complete quiet.

**Best for:** nature lovers, birdwatchers, couples who want genuine escape, photographers, those seeking the antithesis of city tourism.

**Practical:**
- From Tulcea, the public ferry to main delta villages runs twice daily (slow, cheap, local)
- Private speedboat from Tulcea: €60–100 for a half-day trip
- Most pensiuni in delta villages include boat trips in their packages
- Season: May–June for birds (pelican nesting, maximum species), July–August for reed flowers, September for quieter crowds and autumn light

**Budget:** Accommodation at a delta guesthouse with full board: €100–200 per couple per night. Includes room, all meals, and often a boat trip. Tulcea hotel + speedboat day trips: €80–120/night accommodation + €60–100/day boat.

## 5. Maramureș — Authentic Rural Romania (8h or 1h flight, €250–400/couple)

**The summary:** Maramureș is what Romania looked like 100 years ago, and — remarkably — still largely looks like. Horse-drawn carts on the roads, villagers in traditional costume on Sundays, wooden churches older than the printed book, and a hospitality that feels entirely unperformed.

**The UNESCO wooden churches:** Eight Maramureș wooden churches are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Built in the 17th–18th century from oak, with distinctive tall spires reaching 50–60 metres, they represent a remarkable vernacular architectural tradition. Bârsana, Ieud, Desești, Șurdești — each slightly different. Entry: free or €1–2 donation.

**The Merry Cemetery of Săpânța** (*Cimitirul Vesel*): Artist Stan Ioan Pătraș (1909–1977) began in 1935 carving colourful wooden grave markers with naive paintings and humorous rhyming epitaphs describing each person's life and the manner of their death. Now 800+ graves, each a tiny painted biography. Uniquely Romanian, unexpectedly moving. Entry: €3.

**The Vișeu Valley steam railway** (*Mocănița*): A narrow-gauge steam train (dating from 1932) originally built to transport timber from the forest. Now operates as a tourist attraction up the Vaser Valley — 3–4 hours through forest and mountain, with a traditional lunch at the end of the line. €30–40/person. Book ahead in summer.

**Getting there:** Maramureș is the furthest destination on this list — 8 hours from Bucharest by car. A direct flight to Baia Mare (the regional capital) takes 1 hour from Bucharest (€50–100 return) and is the practical option. Rent a car in Baia Mare for the weekend.

## 6. Bucovina — The Painted Monasteries (6h, €200–350/couple)

**The summary:** Five 15th–16th century monasteries with exterior frescoes painted in deep blue, green, red, and ochre — the entire exterior walls covered in biblical scenes, apocalyptic visions, and Byzantine saints. Nothing else in European art looks quite like them.

**The monasteries:**
- **Voroneț** (1488): the most famous, known for "Voroneț blue" — an extraordinary cobalt achieved by a technique still not fully understood. The Last Judgment on the western facade is extraordinary. Called the "Sistine Chapel of the East."
- **Sucevița** (1582): largest, with the most complete frescoes; the Ladder of Virtue on the northern wall is the most dramatic single image in Romanian sacred art
- **Moldovița** (1532): the Siege of Constantinople fresco on the southern wall
- **Humor** (1530): warm reddish tones, intimate atmosphere
- **Arbore** (1502): understated, least visited, genuinely moving

**Practical:** The monasteries are spread across a 60-km radius in Suceava County. A car is essential for visiting more than one per day. Entry: €3–5 each. Most are still active convents — dress modestly (cover shoulders and knees).

**Accommodation:** Suceava city (the regional centre) has good hotels at €40–70/night. Village guesthouses near Voroneț or Moldovița offer the most atmospheric stay, €30–50/night including breakfast. The fresh cheese, smoked meats, and forest mushrooms of Bucovina's cuisine are excellent.

## 7. Sinaia — Mountain Escape (1.5h, €100–200/couple)

**The summary:** The fastest escape from Bucharest into mountain scenery — Sinaia is 1.5 hours on a direct train, and Peleș Castle alone justifies the trip.

**Peleș Castle** is covered above under Brașov. If you only have a day, Sinaia is the option — train from Bucharest's Gara de Nord (hourly, 1.5h, €8–12), walk up to the castle (20 minutes from the station), spend 2–3 hours at Peleș and Pelișor Castle, and return in the evening.

**Bușteni** (10 minutes further by train): the Babele and Sphinx rock formations (€8 cable car + 1-hour walk from the top) and the cross on Caraiman Peak (2,291 m, visible from the valley, remarkable in mist). The Bucegi plateau hike from Bușteni is one of Romania's classic mountain walks.

**Budget:** Day trip from Bucharest: €30–50/person (train + cable car + lunch). Weekend stay: €80–120/couple (accommodation + meals).

## 8. Timișoara — Romania's Most European City (5.5h or 1h flight, €200–350/couple)

**The summary:** Timișoara was European Capital of Culture in 2023 and carries the transformation. The city (which ignited the 1989 Romanian Revolution) has an extraordinary architectural heritage from its Habsburg period — three connected squares in the centre, Secessionist buildings, one of Europe's first electric tram systems — and a genuinely lively café and cultural scene.

**What to see:**
- **Piața Victoriei, Unirii, and Libertății:** three connected squares with Habsburg-era buildings, Orthodox and Catholic cathedrals, and the Romanian National Opera
- **Bastion Museum:** the 18th-century Habsburg military bastion, now converted into a cultural centre
- **Fabric and Iosefin districts:** the most interesting neighbourhoods for architecture and café culture, both undergoing gentrification

**Timișoara day trip:** Less practical as a day trip from Bucharest (5.5h each way by car, or 5h by train). A 2-night weekend works well. Direct 1-hour flights from Bucharest are frequent (€30–60 one-way).

## 9. Cheile Nerei + Ochiul Beiului Lake (6h, €150–300/couple)

**The summary:** Romania's most beautiful lake and one of its most spectacular gorges — barely on the tourist map even inside Romania.

**Ochiul Beiului** (Eye of the Beiu): a spring-fed lake in a forest clearing with turquoise water so clear the bottom is visible at 5 metres. The colour is extraordinary — closer to Slovenian lakes than anything you'd expect in Romania. 15-minute walk from a parking area.

**Cheile Nerei** (Nera Gorges): 22 km of canyon carved by the Nera River through limestone, with cliffs rising 300 metres, caves, waterfalls, and a walking trail through the gorge. The full gorge takes 6–7 hours one-way; most visitors do 2–3 hours from each end.

**Getting there:** Cheile Nerei is in the Banat region, near the Serbian border, 6 hours from Bucharest. Base at the village of Sasca Montană (simple accommodation, €20–40/night) or camping.

**Best for:** hikers, nature photographers, those who want remote Romania with no other tourists.

## 10. Romanian Black Sea Coast — Vama Veche & 2 Mai (3.5h, €150–400/couple)

**The summary:** Romania's bohemian answer to the overcrowded Mamaia resort strip. Vama Veche is a village that became a counter-culture refuge in communist times and retained its character — naturist beaches, live folk music at waterfront bars until 4 AM, camping on the beach, and an atmosphere that feels nothing like a typical beach resort. 2 Mai (next village south) is quieter and more family-oriented.

**What it's not:** It's not Sunny Beach. It's not all-inclusive. The accommodation is simple (camping, basic rooms), the beaches are natural (no sunloungers, no beach clubs), and the vibe is decidedly independent-traveller. This is the choice for those who find conventional beach resorts claustrophobic.

**What it is:** Genuine, cheap, and atmospheric. Full board at a Vama Veche guesthouse: €40–70/night/couple. Beach shack food: €5–10 per meal.
