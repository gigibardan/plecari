There are places in Europe where history is preserved behind glass barriers, with entry tickets and audio guides. And there are places where history simply continues — where laundry dries on 400-year-old walls, children run on the same cobblestones Ottoman soldiers walked, and dinner is served in a salon with carved wooden ceilings where a pasha slept three centuries ago.

Berat and Gjirokastër are the second kind. Albania's two UNESCO cities are not open-air museums — they're living communities that happen to inhabit the best-preserved Ottoman heritage in Europe. And they cost almost nothing to visit.

## Berat — City of a Thousand Windows

120 km south of Tirana, Berat is a town built on both banks of the Osum river, with a steep hill dominated by the Kalaja fortress where people have lived continuously for 2,400 years. What made it famous: the Ottoman house façades in the Mangalem quarter, with rows of large windows side by side, that seem to watch you from the hillside — hence the name.

### The Castle (Kalaja)

The hilltop castle is the oldest quarter — and the most fascinating thing is that people STILL live here. It's not a ruin with an entry fee — it's a stone village on a hill, with houses, churches, mosques, grape-vine gardens, cats on walls, and a silence that contrasts violently with the traffic below.

**What you see:** Free entry to the castle grounds. 4th-century walls (Illyrian foundation, rebuilt Byzantine and Ottoman). The inner town with ~300 permanent residents. The Church of St Mary Vllaherna (13th century) with excellent Byzantine frescoes. Panoramic views over the Osum valley.

**The Onufri Museum** (in the Church of the Dormition, inside the castle): The icon collection of Onufri — a 16th-century icon painter considered the greatest artist of the period in the Balkans. His icons use a carmine red that nobody has managed to reproduce. Entry: €3. Absolutely worth it.

### Mangalem and Gorica

The two historic quarters — Mangalem on the north bank (Ottoman houses with the thousand windows, the King's Mosque, the hammam) and Gorica on the south bank (stone houses on the slope, small churches) — are separated by Gorica Bridge (18th-century Ottoman bridge, reconstructed, pedestrian). Walking from Mangalem across the bridge to Gorica and back is the classic circuit — 1.5–2 hours, with café stops along the way.

### Wine in Berat

Berat is Albania's wine centre. Mediterranean climate, limestone soil, and the indigenous Shesh i Bardhë (white) and Shesh i Zi (red) grapes produce surprisingly good wines.

**Nurellari Winery (Ura Vajgurore, 10 km from Berat):** Albania's best-known winery — tasting on a terrace with hillside views. €8–15 (4–5 wines + local snacks). Their Shesh i Zi red is probably Albania's best red wine.

**Çobo Winery (Ura Vajgurore):** Smaller, more artisanal, run by a young family. Tasting €5–10. Their Puls (traditional sparkling wine) is a surprise.

## Gjirokastër — The Stone City

130 km south of Berat (2.5 hours' drive), Gjirokastër is a town built from grey stone on a steep hill beneath a massive castle — one of the largest in the Balkans. It's also the birthplace of Ismail Kadare (Albania's most important writer, repeatedly nominated for the Nobel) and dictator Enver Hoxha — two opposite faces of Albania from the same street.

### The Castle (Kalaja e Gjirokastrës)

Massive — one of the largest fortresses in Southeast Europe. 12th-century foundation, Ottoman expansion, used by Italians in WWII and communists during the Cold War. Today it houses:

**The Arms Museum:** Cannons, weapons, military vehicles — including an American aircraft intercepted in 1957 during the Cold War (Albania was allied with communist China, not the USSR). Entry: included in castle ticket (€4).

**The Cold War Tunnel:** An anti-nuclear tunnel built beneath the castle in the 1970s by the Hoxha regime, intended for the elite in case of nuclear attack. 800 m long, decontamination chambers, storage rooms, and a genuinely claustrophobic atmosphere. Recently opened for visits. Entry: €2. Fascinating and mildly disturbing.

**The panorama:** From the castle towers, you see all of Gjirokastër — grey stone rooftops, minarets, the Drinos valley, and the Nemërçkë mountains beyond. One of the best urban panoramas in the Balkans.

### The Old Town and Bazaar

The main street (Rruga Sokaku i Gjatë) descends from the castle through the Ottoman bazaar — cobblestone paving, shops in stone houses, craftspeople (wool, silver, wood). It's small but authentic — not manufactured for tourists.

**Visitable Ottoman houses:**

**Skënduli House:** An impeccably preserved 18th-century Ottoman house — 3 floors, reception salon with carved wooden ceilings, private hammam, interior courtyard. The owner (or his granddaughter) guides you personally. €2. The best Ottoman house experience in Albania.

**Zekate House:** Gjirokastër's largest Ottoman house — twin towers, matching salons on the top floor with valley views. €2. Impressive in scale.

## Food — Rural Albania on the Table

**Tavë kosi:** Lamb baked with yoghurt — at Gjirokastër this is claimed as a local speciality. Best version: Taverna Antigoni (Gjirokastër), €4.

**Qofte (meatballs):** Veal with herbs, fried or grilled. €3–4/portion. Simple, addictive.

**Mish në saç (meat under metal dome):** The Albanian version of Dalmatian peka. Lamb or veal with potatoes and peppers, cooked 2–3 hours. Order in advance. €5–8/portion.

**Where to eat:**

**Taverna Antigoni (Gjirokastër, bazaar):** Ottoman house with garden, traditional cuisine. Perfect tavë kosi. €5–8/person.

**Taverna Mangalemi (Berat, Mangalem):** Osum river views, traditional food, enormous portions. €6–10/person.

**Onufri (Berat, castle):** Small restaurant inside the castle, terrace on the walls. Qofte and salads with views. €5–8/person.

## The Road Trip Route: Tirana → Berat → Gjirokastër → Riviera

This is the classic 7–10 day Albania itinerary — and one of Europe's best road trips at unbelievable prices.

| Day | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tirana | Arrival, Bunk'Art, Skanderbeg, nightlife |
| 2 | Tirana → Berat (2 hrs) | Drive, Mangalem, Gorica, bridge, evening boulevard |
| 3 | Berat | Castle, Onufri, wine tasting at Nurellari |
| 4 | Berat → Gjirokastër (2.5 hrs) | Drive (stop at Përmet or Osumi Canyon), evening in bazaar |
| 5 | Gjirokastër | Castle, Ottoman houses, Cold War tunnel |
| 6 | Gjirokastër → Blue Eye → Saranda (1.5 hrs) | Blue Eye stop, settle on the Riviera |
| 7–10 | [Albanian Riviera](/en/city-breaks/albanian-riviera-complete-guide-2026) | Ksamil, Dhermi, Gjipe, Himarë, Corfu ferry |

**Total distance Tirana → Saranda:** ~330 km, ~6 hours driving (without stops).

## Essential Tips

**Cash is king.** Cards work at hotels and larger restaurants, but bakeries, small tavernas, taxis, and museum entries require cash (Albanian lek). ATMs work in both towns.

**eSIM is essential.** Albania is NOT in the EU. [Airalo eSIM](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3) or a local Vodafone Albania SIM (€5 from any shop).

**The Berat–Gjirokastër road via Përmet** is longer but spectacular — it passes through the Vjosa canyon (Europe's last wild river), the thermal springs at Bënjë (natural warm-water pools in a canyon — 1–2 hour stop, free), and the Ottoman Katiu bridge. One of Albania's most beautiful drives.

**Ottoman houses as accommodation** are the definitive experience. New hotels are comfortable but generic — an 18th-century Ottoman house with carved ceilings, creaking wooden floors, and breakfast of honey, cheese, and byrek served by your host in the interior courtyard is irreplaceable.

**Ismail Kadare.** Read at least "The General of the Dead Army" before your visit — it's a short, powerful novel that completely transforms how you see Albania.
