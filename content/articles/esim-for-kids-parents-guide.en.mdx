Family holidays mean connectivity questions multiply: Does the 9-year-old need data on the iPad? Can the teenager be trusted with their own SIM? Does everyone need separate plans? And is there a cheaper way than buying data for 4 devices?

This guide answers all of it — practically, without jargon — so you can set up your whole family's connectivity before you leave the airport car park.

## The Short Answer: One eSIM Per Adult, Hotspot for Everyone Else

Before getting into the detail, here's the practical answer that covers 90% of families travelling with children:

**Buy one eSIM per adult. Enable hotspot on each adult's phone. Connect children's phones, tablets, and other devices to the hotspot.**

That's it. No separate purchases for the kids, no fiddling with their devices, no age restrictions to navigate. Children connect to Mum's or Dad's hotspot the same way they connect to your home Wi-Fi. The data comes from your eSIM plan. You pay once per adult, not per device.

A 10 GB eSIM plan covers approximately: your navigation and messaging (2–3 GB), your children's tablets and casual use via hotspot (3–5 GB), plus a comfortable buffer. Total cost for a family of four: two eSIM plans at €8–16 each = €16–32 total for a week, versus four separate plans at €8–16 each = €32–64.

The exception — when a child genuinely needs their own eSIM — is covered further below.

## What Is an eSIM and Why Does It Matter for Family Travel?

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a digital SIM card built into the phone or tablet. Instead of buying a physical SIM card at the airport or paying expensive roaming fees to your home carrier, you:

1. Buy a data plan online before you travel (from Airalo, Saily, or Yesim)
2. Receive a QR code by email
3. Scan the QR code on the device — takes 2 minutes
4. Activate roaming data — the phone connects to local networks at the destination

No physical SIM to lose, no visit to a phone shop at the airport, no roaming bill surprise at the end of the trip. The family's home SIM cards stay in the phones for calls and messages — eSIM handles data only.

**Why not just use carrier roaming?**

If you're travelling within the EU from Romania, EU roaming is technically free — but with fair use limits that vary by carrier. Outside the EU (UK, Turkey, Egypt, USA, Thailand), your Romanian carrier charges €3–15 per device per day. For a family of four spending a week in London: €84–420 in roaming charges, versus €32–50 for two adult eSIMs covering everyone.

## Setting Up the Family Hotspot — Step by Step

The hotspot is the key to covering the whole family with one eSIM purchase per adult. Here's exactly how to set it up:

### On iPhone (any model with Personal Hotspot)

1. Go to **Settings → Personal Hotspot**
2. Toggle **Allow Others to Join** to ON
3. Note the Wi-Fi password shown on screen (or change it to something simpler)
4. On the child's device: go to Wi-Fi settings, find your phone's name, enter the password
5. Connected devices appear in your hotspot settings

**Battery note:** Running a hotspot uses more battery than normal browsing — expect 20–30% faster battery drain. Bring a portable charger (power bank) if you're doing full days out with the hotspot active.

**Data note:** All data used by connected devices counts against YOUR eSIM plan. A child streaming YouTube for 2 hours uses 1–2 GB of your data. Download videos on hotel Wi-Fi before heading out.

### On Android (Samsung, Pixel, OnePlus, etc.)

1. Go to **Settings → Connections** (or **Network & Internet**)
2. Find **Mobile Hotspot** or **Tethering**
3. Toggle **Mobile Hotspot** ON
4. Tap the hotspot name to see or change the Wi-Fi password
5. On connected devices: join as a regular Wi-Fi network

**Note:** Some Android phones limit hotspot to 5–8 devices simultaneously. More than enough for a family.

### Hotspot Range and Speed

Hotspot works within approximately 5–10 metres of the hosting phone. In practice: everyone at the same restaurant table, walking together on a tour, or in the same hotel room — all covered. If the family splits up during the day (one parent with younger children, another with a teenager), activate hotspot on both adults' phones.

Speed depends on your eSIM plan and local network. In most European cities and tourist destinations, expect 20–50 Mbps download — more than sufficient for navigation, video calls, and light streaming.

## When Does a Child Actually Need Their Own eSIM?

The hotspot covers most situations. A child needs an independent eSIM in these specific circumstances:

**1. Teenager travelling semi-independently**
If your 16+ year-old is exploring a city on their own for part of the day (meeting friends, going to a concert, exploring a neighbourhood separately), they need their own data connection — not dependence on a parent's hotspot from 2 km away.

**2. Child staying with different family members**
Extended family trip where grandparents or relatives are in a separate accommodation or touring separately from the parents. Own eSIM = own connectivity.

**3. Multiple-device families where hotspot battery is a concern**
If you have 3+ children each with a device and both parents are heavy data users, the hotspot drain on two phones may be unsustainable for a full day of touring. Two eSIM plans plus two dedicated "children's" eSIM plans on the most data-heavy child devices may be more practical.

**4. Child with a medical app requiring constant connectivity**
Some medical devices (glucose monitors, specific apps for health management) require persistent data connectivity — not reliable through an intermittent hotspot.

## eSIM for Tablets — iPad, Samsung Tab, and Others

Many families travel with a children's tablet (iPad, Samsung Galaxy Tab, Amazon Fire) for entertainment. Important distinction:

**eSIM-compatible tablets (can have their own eSIM):**
- iPad Air (5th gen 2022+), iPad Pro (all models from 2018+), iPad mini (6th gen 2021+)
- Samsung Galaxy Tab S7+, S8, S9 series (cellular versions)
- Microsoft Surface Pro with LTE

**Non-eSIM tablets (hotspot only):**
- Amazon Fire tablets (no eSIM support)
- Most budget Android tablets
- Wi-Fi-only versions of iPads and Samsung tablets

For an iPad with eSIM capability, you can install a children's eSIM directly on the iPad — no need to go through a parent's hotspot. But the hotspot approach is simpler and cheaper for most families.

**Recommended approach for tablets:** Connect to parent's hotspot. Download content (Netflix, YouTube, Disney+, games) on home or hotel Wi-Fi before going out. Children's tablets rarely need live data outside of maps and messaging.

## How Much Data Does a Family Need?

Planning your data purchase requires estimating consumption. Here's a realistic breakdown:

### Per adult per day (typical holiday use)
- Google Maps navigation: 50–150 MB
- WhatsApp (messages + photos, no video calls): 30–80 MB
- Social media browsing (Instagram, Facebook): 100–300 MB
- Video calls (WhatsApp, FaceTime — 30 min): 200–400 MB
- News and web browsing: 50–150 MB
- **Total typical adult: 400 MB–1 GB/day**

### Per child per day (connected via hotspot, typical holiday use)
- YouTube (with offline downloads done at hotel): 50–200 MB (for clips only)
- Gaming (most mobile games): 20–100 MB
- WhatsApp/iMessage with friends back home: 20–80 MB
- Maps if navigating: 30–100 MB
- **Total typical child: 100–400 MB/day**

### Family of 4 (2 adults + 2 children, 7 days)
- Adults: 2 × 7 GB = 14 GB
- Children via hotspot on adult plans: 2 × 2 GB = 4 GB distributed across both parents' plans
- **Total family need: ~14–18 GB across two adult eSIM plans**
- **Recommended purchase: 2 × 10 GB plans** (one per adult, each child connects to nearest parent's hotspot)

If your family does more video calling (grandparents back home, video updates for relatives), add 2–3 GB per adult.

## Practical Tips for Family eSIM Setup

**Install at home, not at the airport.** Installation requires Wi-Fi. Do it the evening before departure at home, not in the departure lounge on airport Wi-Fi. If there's a problem (incompatible device, QR code issue), you have time to fix it.

**Check eSIM compatibility before buying.** Not all phones support eSIM. Check: iPhone Settings → General → About → look for IMEI2 or EID. Android: Settings → About Phone → SIM card status → check for eSIM. If a phone lacks eSIM, it can still connect via the parent's hotspot — it just can't have its own eSIM.

**Set data limits on children's connected devices.** On iPhone: Settings → Screen Time → Content & Privacy → App Limits. On Android: Settings → Digital Wellbeing. You can cap daily data usage so a child doesn't drain your hotspot allowance watching YouTube.

**Download before you go.** On the hotel/home Wi-Fi each evening, download:
- Netflix/Disney+/YouTube episodes for the next day (offline mode)
- Google Maps for your destination (offline maps)
- Any large game updates

This dramatically reduces daytime data consumption via hotspot.

**Keep your home SIM active for emergencies.** Your Romanian SIM stays in your phone alongside the eSIM. If the eSIM fails or has no signal in a specific area, you can switch to your home SIM for an emergency call or message.

**Bring a power bank.** Running hotspot accelerates battery drain by 20–30%. A 10,000 mAh power bank is essential for a day of touring with children connected to your hotspot.
