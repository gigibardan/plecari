There is a detail about Sighișoara that few guides emphasise sufficiently: <cite index="96-1">it is the only permanently inhabited medieval citadel in Europe.</cite>

Not an open-air museum. Not a reconstruction. Real people — approximately 2,000 — live today in the medieval houses of the hilltop Citadel. Children go to school on the covered Scholars' Staircase built in 1642. Grandparents sit on the porches of Gothic-façaded houses. Cats sleep on rough stone steps.

<cite index="98-1">The Sighișoara citadel was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1999 as a fine example of a small fortified medieval town that played an important strategic and commercial role on the fringes of Central Europe for several centuries.</cite>

Added to this is a documented historical connection to Transylvania's most famous character: <cite index="99-1">Vlad Drăculea, known as Vlad the Impaler, ruler of Wallachia from 1456 to 1462 who inspired Bram Stoker's character Dracula, was born in Sighișoara around 1431.</cite>

## Sighișoara at a Glance — Essential Facts

**County:** Mureș, central Transylvania.

**Population:** approximately 24,000 in the whole town, ~2,000 in the citadel.

**UNESCO:** Historic Centre of Sighișoara — since 1999.

**Uniqueness:** <cite index="96-1">the only permanently inhabited medieval citadel in Europe.</cite>

**Distance:** Brașov 130 km (1.5 hours), Sibiu 90 km (1 hour), Cluj 110 km (1.5 hours).

**Visit budget:** minimal (mostly free) — 50–100 RON for museums + a meal.

---

## How to Get to Sighișoara

### From Brașov (Most Practical)

**Train:** direct Brașov → Sighișoara, 1–1.5 hours, <cite index="95-1">20–40 RON (~4–8 EUR)</cite>. Most comfortable. Sighișoara station is 15 minutes' walk from the citadel.

**By car:** 130 km on DN13, 1.5 hours. Parking available at the base of the citadel.

### From Sibiu

90 km, 1 hour by car or 1.5 hours by train. A natural day trip from Sibiu.

## The Citadel — What to See

### Citadel Square (Piața Cetății)

The heart of the citadel — a small square surrounded by colourful medieval houses, with restaurant terraces. <cite index="99-1">The first houses built in Sighișoara were raised around this small central square</cite> — essentially unchanged for centuries.

**Vlad Drăculea's House (no. 5)** — <cite index="99-1">the birthplace of Vlad the Impaler (b. ~1431), today a themed restaurant and museum.</cite> The "Dracula's room" includes a costumed actor and a kitsch-historical exhibition. Ticket: 15–20 RON (~3–4 EUR). A more amusing than academic experience — but the place itself is authentic.

### The Clock Tower (Turnul Sfatului)

<cite index="99-1">The Clock Tower, 64 metres high, is Sighișoara's emblem — built in the 13th century, with a clock mechanism that sets figurines in motion at the change of hours.</cite>

Today it houses the History Museum with medieval artefacts, jewellery, weapons and documents. <cite index="96-1">Climb to the upper level for a complete panorama of the citadel and the Lower Town.</cite>

Museum ticket: 20–25 RON (~4–5 EUR). Hours: Tuesday–Sunday 10:00–17:30.

### Scholars' Staircase (Covered Staircase)

<cite index="97-1">The Scholars' Staircase is a covered wooden staircase with 175 steps, built in 1642 to protect students on their daily journey to the School on the Hill and the Church on the Hill, particularly in winter.</cite>

It is unique in Romania and one of the few remaining structures of this type in Europe. Access is free. The staircase itself is a remarkable photographic subject — old wood, light filtering through gaps in the ceiling, perspective toward the citadel.

### Church on the Hill (Evangelical Church)

<cite index="98-1">The Church on the Hill is one of Romania's most significant Gothic monuments, with 500-year-old medieval frescoes and an interior with Anatolian rugs, a carved Renaissance altarpiece, a Baroque pulpit and a 17th-century organ.</cite>

Ticket: ~15 RON (~3 EUR). Accessible via the Scholars' Staircase.

### The Citadel's Towers

Sighișoara originally had 14 towers — 9 survive. Each tower was in the care of a craft guild:

- **Tinsmiths' Tower** — best preserved after the Clock Tower
- **Tailors' Tower**
- **Furriers' Tower**
- **Tanners' Tower** — with views toward the Lower Town

Walking the citadel walls (partially open, free) provides spectacular views.

## Sighișoara Medieval Festival

<cite index="96-1">Sighișoara's Medieval Festival takes place in the last week of July</cite> (2026: 24–27 July) and transforms the citadel into a living medieval stage — with:
- Knight tournaments and combat demonstrations
- Period musicians and jugglers
- Craftsmen in period costumes
- Historical processions
- Pyrotechnics and night-time performances

It is Romania's largest medieval festival and one of the most spectacular in Eastern Europe. Book accommodation 3–4 months ahead — the citadel and the whole town fill up.

---

## The Lower Town — Beyond the Citadel

Sighișoara's Lower Town is less visited but has its own charm — with the Gothic Holy Trinity Church, colourful-façaded houses and the promenade along the Târnava Mare River.

**German Cemetery** — a quiet space with old stone crosses and monuments of influential Saxon families. Free, a place of reflection away from tourist traffic.

---

## Sighișoara + Biertan UNESCO — Two Fortresses in One Day

### Biertan — The UNESCO Site 30 km Away

<cite index="74-1">Biertan is a Saxon village 30 km from Sighișoara with an imposing fortified church — with three rings of defensive walls, nine towers and Gothic architecture.</cite> Inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List independently of Sighișoara.

Ticket: ~15 RON (~3 EUR). Visit duration: 45–60 minutes. Accessible by car.

**Classic circuit:** Sighișoara in the morning (Clock Tower + Scholars' Staircase + Vlad Dracula's House) → lunch in Citadel Square → Biertan in the afternoon → return.

## Food and Drink in Sighișoara

**Citadel restaurants** — several restaurants with authentic medieval atmosphere on Citadel Square and adjacent streets. <cite index="96-1">Prices are mid-range — 50–100 RON per person</cite> for a full meal with local wine.

**Kürtőskalács** — sold at several stalls in the citadel: 15–20 RON.

**Transylvanian pálinka** — local plum or cherry brandy, obligatory to end a meal: 8–15 RON a shot.

**Târnave wine** — the Târnave vineyard region, neighbouring Sighișoara, produces excellent Feteasca Regală and Muscat Ottonel. A glass at a restaurant: 15–20 RON.

## Recommended Itinerary — One Day in Sighișoara

### 10:00 — Arrival

- Train from Brașov or by car
- Park at the base of the citadel, walk up (5–10 minutes)

### 10:30–12:00 — The Citadel

- Citadel Square + Vlad Dracula's House (~15 RON)
- Clock Tower + museum (~20 RON, climb for panorama)
- Walk along the walls

### 12:00–13:30 — Lunch

- Restaurant in Citadel Square

### 13:30–15:00 — Scholars' Staircase and Church on the Hill

- Scholars' Staircase (free, 175 steps)
- Church on the Hill (~15 RON, medieval frescoes)
- Panorama from the hill toward the citadel and Târnava Mare

### 15:00–16:30 — Towers and Lower Town

- Walk past the citadel towers
- Descent to the Lower Town — Holy Trinity Church, Târnava promenade

### 16:30+ — Departure or Evening in the Citadel

- If staying overnight: the citadel after 18:00 is completely different — quiet, almost eerie, magical
- If leaving: train to Brașov or Sibiu

## Practical Tips

**Evening** — <cite index="96-1">if not staying in the citadel, remain until dusk. After 18:00, the day visitors leave and Sighișoara becomes authentic — almost eerie on the narrow lanes in lamplight.</cite> One of Transylvania's best experiences.

**Footwear** — the limestone cobblestones in the citadel are beautiful but slippery after rain. Sturdy soles, not heels.

**Medieval Festival** — book accommodation 3–4 months ahead for the last week of July.

**Biertan** — if you have a car, absolutely combine with Biertan (30 km, 30 minutes). Two UNESCO sites in one day.
