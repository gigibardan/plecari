## The Big Story of 2026: The Champs-Élysées Christmas Market Returns

### Ten Years of Absence

For decades, the **Marché de Noël des Champs-Élysées** was one of the most photographed Christmas scenes anywhere in Europe. Wooden chalets lining nearly 2 kilometres of the world's most famous avenue, with the Arc de Triomphe at one end and Place de la Concorde at the other — a postcard image known globally.

Then, in the mid-2010s, Parisian authorities began restricting street trading on the Champs-Élysées, and the traditional market gradually disappeared, leaving a significant void in Paris's Christmas landscape. For nearly a decade, visitors who came specifically for this market left disappointed.

### The Triumphant Return in 2026

Paris has listened. In 2026, **the Marché de Noël des Champs-Élysées is back**, with a new concept, higher artisan quality, and an organization befitting the world's most famous street.

**What we know about the new market:**
- **~100 carefully selected artisan chalets** — emphasis on quality craftsmanship rather than tourist trinkets
- Premium French gastronomic products: foie gras, artisan cheeses, regional wines, handmade macarons
- Regional French craft from Alsace, Provence, Brittany, Normandy
- Spectacular lighting along the entire length of the avenue
- Children's activities and seasonal attractions

**Location:** Avenue des Champs-Élysées, from Rond-Point des Champs-Élysées toward Concorde
**Estimated 2026 dates:** Approximately November 20 — December 31, 2026
**Opening hours:** Mon-Thu 11:00-22:00, Fri-Sun 11:00-23:00

### Why the Champs-Élysées is Like Nowhere Else

Even without the market, the Champs-Élysées in winter is a unique spectacle. The **Christmas illumination** along the avenue — hundreds of thousands of white and golden lights on the trees for the full 1.9 km — is switched on in November and transforms the most famous street in the world into a tunnel of magic light.

With the market active, the experience becomes complete: strolling between chalets, mulled wine in hand, watching the Arc de Triomphe at one end and, on clear days, the Eiffel Tower glittering at the horizon.

## Complete Map of Paris Christmas Markets 2026

Paris doesn't have one Christmas market — it has **a network of themed markets** scattered across the city, each with a distinct personality and iconic location.

### 1. Marché de Noël des Tuileries (Jardins des Tuileries)

**Location:** Jardins des Tuileries, near the Louvre and Place de la Concorde (1st arrondissement)
**Estimated dates:** Mid-November — mid-January 2027
**Access:** Métro Tuileries (Line 1) or Concorde (Lines 1, 8, 12)

The most **central and most gastronomic** of all the Parisian Christmas markets. The Tuileries Gardens transform every winter into a festival of tastes with emphasis on luxury French products.

**Main attractions:**
- **60-metre panoramic Ferris wheel** — one of the emblematic attractions of the Parisian winter; the view includes the Eiffel Tower, the Louvre and all of Paris at sunset
- **Traditional carousel** — perfect for children
- Chalets showcasing specialties from every region of France
- Alsatian, Bordeaux and Burgundy wines served warm
- Black truffles from Périgord, foie gras from Gascony, artisan cheeses
- Creative workshops for children

**The gastronomic signature:** Tuileries is the place to taste **the best French Christmas food** without leaving Paris. Each French region has a stall.

**Hours:** 11:00-23:00 on weekends; 11:00-22:00 on weekdays

### 2. Marché de Noël de la Défense

**Location:** Esplanade La Grande Arche, La Défense (business district)
**Estimated dates:** Late November — December 26, 2026
**Access:** RER A or Métro Line 1 (La Défense station)

The largest Christmas market in Paris by number of stalls — over **400 chalets** arranged across the vast esplanade of the Grande Arche. It's a completely different experience from the city-centre markets: larger, more frequented by locals, and offering a unique perspective — the modern architecture of La Défense draped in Christmas decoration.

**Notable features:**
- Outdoor ice rink
- Daily concerts and entertainment
- Christmas products from across France and Europe
- Slightly lower prices than Tuileries or Champs-Élysées
- The illuminated Grande Arche as backdrop — spectacular after dark

**Hours:** 11:00-22:00 Mon-Fri, 10:00-23:00 Sat-Sun

### 3. Village de Noël de Montmartre

**Location:** Place du Tertre and surroundings, Montmartre (18th arrondissement)
**Estimated dates:** Late November — December 26, 2026
**Access:** Métro Abbesses (Line 12) or the Funiculaire from Place Saint-Pierre

Montmartre in winter has a charm that no other Parisian neighborhood can match. The little artists' village on the hill below Sacré-Cœur transforms every Christmas season into an authentic **village de Noël** — a provincial French Christmas village atmosphere in the heart of Paris.

**What Montmartre offers at Christmas:**
- Small chalets with quality artisan products (jewelry, paintings, decorative objects)
- The authentic atmosphere of a Christmas village — not commercial, but intimate and genuine
- Sacré-Cœur illuminated in the background — one of the most photographed images of Christmas Paris
- Local restaurants with French specialties at more reasonable prices than the center
- Street musicians performing carols and Christmas music outdoors
- Boulevard de Rochechouart — spectacular lighting

**The Montmartre advantage:** Less crowded than central Paris and more authentic. Locals come here too, which gives it a special quality.

### 4. Marché de Noël Saint-Germain-des-Prés

**Location:** Place Saint-Sulpice or Boulevard Saint-Germain (6th arrondissement)
**Estimated dates:** Mid-November — December 24, 2026
**Access:** Métro Saint-Sulpice (Line 4) or Saint-Germain-des-Prés (Line 4)

The most **elegant and select** Christmas market in Paris. Saint-Germain is known for fashion boutiques, art galleries and intellectuals — and the Christmas market reflects this character perfectly.

**The Saint-Germain signature:**
- Luxury craftsmanship and contemporary French design
- Jewelers, fashion designers, interior designers
- Rare and illustrated book stalls
- Collectible wines and premium gastronomic specialties
- A quieter atmosphere than other markets — perfect for serious shopping

**While you're here:** Église Saint-Sulpice (second largest in Paris, featured in The Da Vinci Code), Luxembourg Gardens, historic literary cafés (Café de Flore, Les Deux Magots).

### 5. Marché de Noël de Notre-Dame (Île de la Cité)

**Location:** Parvis Notre-Dame de Paris, Île de la Cité (4th arrondissement)
**Estimated dates:** Mid-November — December 26, 2026
**Access:** Métro Cité (Line 4) or Saint-Michel Notre-Dame (RER B/C)

**The absolute unmissable of the 2026 season:** Notre-Dame de Paris, the cathedral that partially burned in April 2019, **reopened its doors** at the end of 2024, after five years of extraordinary restoration. Christmas 2026 is only the second season in which Christmas can be celebrated in front of the fully restored cathedral — an emotional and historic experience.

**What the Notre-Dame market offers:**
- Artisan chalets on the forecourt of the restored cathedral
- Traditional Christmas products and crafts
- Christmas lights on the historic façade of Notre-Dame — not to be missed
- Proximity to Sainte-Chapelle (impressive medieval stained glass)

**Visit the interior of restored Notre-Dame** — book tickets online at least 2-3 weeks in advance.

### 6. Marché de Noël de Versailles

**Location:** Château de Versailles, Versailles (Île-de-France)
**Estimated dates:** November 29 — December 24, 2026
**Access:** RER C from Paris (45 minutes)

Versailles's Christmas market offers an experience unique in the world: artisan chalets in the **Royal Gardens of Europe's largest palace**, with the illuminated Palace of Versailles as a backdrop.

Not many tourists know there's a Christmas market at Versailles — which is precisely why it's one of the most authentic and least crowded Christmas experiences in the Paris region.

**The Versailles Christmas programme includes:**
- Market in the gardens
- Light shows on the palace façade
- Classical music concerts in the palace rooms
- Palace visit with special Christmas decorations

### 7. Village de Noël Disneyland Paris

**Location:** Disneyland Paris, Marne-la-Vallée (30 km east of Paris)
**Period:** November — January 2, 2027
**Access:** RER A from Paris (45 minutes), direct to Marne-la-Vallée/Chessy station

Disneyland Paris in the Christmas season is **a show in itself** — one of the largest and most impressive themed Christmas programmes in Europe.

**The Christmas programme includes:**
- Christmas parades with Disney characters
- Christmas show on Main Street USA
- Scheduled artificial snowfall at fixed times
- Christmas decorations throughout the park
- Special nocturnal Christmas parade
- Meetings with Santa Claus and Disney characters in special Christmas costumes

Disneyland Paris at Christmas isn't cheap — tickets are 80-120€/person — but for families with children it's an unforgettable experience.

## Christmas Attractions Beyond the Markets

Paris at Christmas is not just about markets — it's an entire city transformed into the most beautiful holiday set imaginable.

### The Christmas Illuminations — An Essential Evening Walk

Paris invests enormous sums annually in Christmas lighting, and the results are spectacular.

**The most beautiful illuminations:**

**Champs-Élysées** — already described, but worth repeating: 1.9 km of white and golden light tunnel along the trees of the avenue. The illumination ceremony (generally November 20-25) is an event in itself, with concerts and ceremonies.

**Galeries Lafayette** — the great department store on Boulevard Haussmann transforms its windows into **animated Christmas spectacles** requiring months of production. Galeries Lafayette's Christmas windows are among the most photographed in the world. Inside, **the main dome** is decorated with a giant Christmas tree and spectacular ornaments — entry is free.

**Printemps** — Galeries Lafayette's neighbor has equally extraordinary animated Christmas windows, with a different concept every year.

**Montmartre** — the Sacré-Cœur hill with the basilica illuminated white against the sky — an image you don't forget.

**The Eiffel Tower** — while it has no specific Christmas programme, the Eiffel Tower sparkles gold every hour for 5 minutes from nightfall until 1:00 AM. At Christmas, the light show sometimes includes special variations.

### Notre-Dame de Paris — Restored and More Magnificent Than Ever

If you followed the news of April 15, 2019, you know that the Notre-Dame fire shocked the entire world. The flames that consumed the cathedral's roof and central spire became perhaps the most dramatic cultural moment of the 2010s.

But the restoration — one of the most ambitious in modern architectural history — is complete, and **Notre-Dame reopened in December 2024** — more magnificent than before. The medieval stained glass, sculptures, altars and Gothic vaulting have been restored to the highest standard, with a few discreetly integrated improvements.

**Visiting Notre-Dame in 2026:**
- **Book your ticket online** — at minimum 2-3 weeks in advance; queues are enormous
- Entrance to the cathedral interior: free
- The towers (with the view over Paris): separate ticket, ~15€, booking mandatory
- The candlelit Christmas Midnight Mass (December 24) — one of the most moving experiences in Paris

### The Louvre in Winter — Without the Summer Crowds

**The Louvre in winter** is an entirely different experience from summer. Queues are 10 times smaller, the atmosphere is calmer, and you can contemplate the Mona Lisa or Venus de Milo without being crushed by crowds.

**Tips for visiting the Louvre at Christmas:**
- Buy tickets online in advance — avoid queues entirely
- Choose a Tuesday or Thursday (least crowded)
- The Louvre is closed on Tuesdays — verify exact hours
- The glass Pyramid in winter rain or snow — extraordinary photographs

### A Seine River Cruise at Christmas

**Bateaux Mouches** and other Seine cruise operators run year-round, sometimes with special Christmas programmes featuring mulled wine and music on board. Seeing illuminated Paris from the Seine is one of the unmissable experiences.

### The Eiffel Tower at Night

The Eiffel Tower has been sparkling its nightly light show (the hourly 5-minute sparkle) since 2003 and shows no signs of stopping. For the ultimate Paris Christmas photograph, position yourself on **Trocadéro** at dusk and wait for the sparkle — the view across the Seine with the tower framed by the wings of the Palais de Chaillot is iconic.

## Getting to Paris

Paris is one of Europe's best-connected cities, with direct flights from virtually every major European hub and excellent ground transport within France and across borders.

### By Air — From Bucharest and Eastern Europe

**Direct flights Bucharest (OTP) — Paris:**
- **Wizz Air:** Bucharest Otopeni (OTP) → Paris Beauvais (BVA) — ~2h45m flight; prices from **30-80€** one-way, depending on timing and how far in advance you book
- **Tarom:** Bucharest (OTP) → Paris Charles de Gaulle (CDG) — direct, ~3 hours; prices 100-250€ one-way
- **Air France:** Bucharest (OTP) → Paris CDG — prices 150-350€ one-way
- **Ryanair:** Sometimes operates routes from Bucharest Băneasa (BBU) to Paris Beauvais

**Paris airports:**
- **Charles de Gaulle (CDG)** — largest airport, north of Paris; RER B direct to city center (45 min, ~11€)
- **Orly (ORY)** — closer to center; OrlyBus or RER B+C
- **Beauvais (BVA)** — 85 km north of Paris; Beauvais shuttle bus (90 min, ~17€). **Important:** Beauvais is far — allow 2 hours from airport to central Paris

**Tips for cheap flights:**
- Book **2-4 months in advance** for minimum prices
- December is peak season — prices rise significantly versus the rest of the year
- Compare all operators simultaneously on **Skyscanner** or **Google Flights**
- Check also flights from regional airports (sometimes cheaper routes exist)

### By Rail — The Eurostar and TGV Network

**Eurostar** connects Paris to London in 2h15m from St Pancras; **Thalys** (now part of Eurostar) connects to Brussels (1h20m), Amsterdam (3h20m), Cologne. **TGV** covers the French domestic network at high speed.

For visitors already in France or neighboring countries, the train is often the most convenient option — arriving at Gare du Nord, Gare de Lyon or Gare de l'Est directly in the city center.

## French Christmas Food — What to Eat and Where

French Christmas cuisine has an extraordinary tradition. At markets and restaurants, the season brings specialties you won't find the rest of the year.

### At the Markets

**Vin chaud (mulled wine)** — French mulled wine differs from German or Romanian versions: typically Beaujolais or Bordeaux wine with star anise, cinnamon, orange peel and discreet spices. Price: 4-8€/glass (more expensive than elsewhere, but Paris is Paris).

**Crêpes** — French crêpes at Christmas markets come in special winter versions: dark chocolate, salted butter caramel (caramel au beurre salé de Bretagne), chestnut jam, or flambéed banana.

**Raclette** — the Swiss-French cheese melted and scraped hot over boiled potatoes and charcuterie. One of the great winter specialties, warming and substantial.

**Tartiflette** — a traditional Savoyard gratin with potatoes, lardons (smoked bacon), onions and Reblochon cheese. Hearty and warming, perfect in the cold.

**Marrons chauds (roasted chestnuts)** — street vendors roasting chestnuts over open fires are a symbol of the Parisian winter; you'll find one at virtually every street corner with a chestnut fire. 2-4€ per cone.

**Foie gras** — duck or goose liver is the quintessential French Christmas specialty. At markets, producers from Gascony or Périgord sell foie gras in jars or blocks, terrines and pâtés of superior quality. Prices: 15-50€/jar depending on weight and quality.

**Huîtres (oysters)** — in France, oysters are the Christmas food. Around the holidays in Paris, a unique phenomenon appears: **oyster stalls on the street**, with lemon and mignonette, open in the cold air. Paris is probably the only place in the world where you can eat fresh oysters on the street in winter cold, with a glass of Muscadet, and have it be completely normal.

**Bûche de Noël** — the traditional French Christmas log cake (a yule log dessert) is available in every patisserie. Each Paris patisserie has its version, from the classic chocolate to culinary art creations with berries, pistachio or salted caramel.

**Christmas macarons** — Ladurée and Pierre Hermé, the two great macaron houses of Paris, annually launch special Christmas collections with seasonal flavors (cinnamon, ginger, chestnut, dark chocolate).

### Restaurant Recommendations

**Bouillon Chartier** (9th arrondissement) — the traditional French brasserie restaurant, open since 1896, with accessible prices and classic dishes. The queue outside is long but worth it — the Belle Époque atmosphere is unique.

**Café de Flore and Les Deux Magots** (Saint-Germain) — the historic literary cafés frequented by Sartre, Simone de Beauvoir and Hemingway. Prices are Parisian (10-20€ for an espresso and croissant), but the cultural experience is priceless.

**Le Bouillon Pigalle** (Montmartre/Pigalle) — the more accessible version of classic brasseries, opened relatively recently and with surprisingly reasonable prices for Paris.

**Breton crêperies** — in winter Paris, Breton crêperies (buckwheat galettes with ham, cheese and egg, accompanied by Breton cider) are one of the most authentic and affordable meals.

## 3-Day Paris Christmas Itinerary

### Day 1 — Champs-Élysées, Tuileries and the Louvre

**09:00-10:00** — Breakfast at a local boulangerie. Espresso, butter croissant or pain au chocolat.

**10:00-12:00** — The Louvre (book tickets online). Mona Lisa, Venus de Milo, Winged Victory of Samothrace — don't try to see everything in 2 hours; choose 2-3 wings and appreciate the space.

**12:00-13:30** — Lunch in Jardins des Tuileries or at a bistrot in the 1st.

**13:30-15:30** — Walk through Jardins des Tuileries and first contact with the Marché de Noël des Tuileries: mulled wine, foie gras tasting, first Ferris wheel ride.

**15:30-20:00** — Champs-Élysées at nightfall. Walk the full length of the illuminated avenue, the chalets of the new Christmas market (the first time in a decade!), the Arc de Triomphe up close.

**20:00-22:00** — Dinner at a traditional bistrot in the 8th or 1st. Steak tartare or entrecôte with pommes frites.

### Day 2 — Montmartre, Notre-Dame and the Seine

**09:00-10:30** — Breakfast in Montmartre — one of the most beautiful possible mornings in Paris. Coffee while looking out over the city from the hill.

**10:30-13:00** — Walk through Montmartre: Sacré-Cœur (interior + panoramic view), Place du Tertre with the artists, Village de Noël de Montmartre.

**13:00-14:30** — Lunch at a traditional restaurant in Montmartre or down toward Pigalle.

**15:00-17:00** — Notre-Dame de Paris (booking mandatory) — the emotion of seeing the restored cathedral after the fire. Sainte-Chapelle next door (impressive medieval stained glass).

**17:00-19:00** — Seine cruise with Bateaux Parisiens or Bateaux Mouches — Paris illuminated for Christmas, seen from the water.

**19:00-21:00** — Walk through Île Saint-Louis and the Marais — the most authentic neighborhoods in central Paris.

**21:00** — Galeries Lafayette for the animated window displays and spectacular interior decorations.

### Day 3 — Versailles, Eiffel Tower and Saint-Germain

**08:30-10:00** — RER C to Versailles. Arrive early (before the crowds appear).

**10:00-13:30** — Palace and Gardens of Versailles. Christmas market at Versailles. Lunch on-site or at a Versailles restaurant.

**15:00-17:00** — The Eiffel Tower (book tickets online!). Ascend to the second floor or top for the view over illuminated Paris. At 17:00 in December it's already getting dark — perfect for the photograph with Paris in artificial light.

**17:00-19:00** — Saint-Germain-des-Prés: Marché de Noël Saint-Germain, historic cafés, art gallery.

**19:00** — Farewell dinner in Paris — choose a French restaurant with seasonal specialties: game meat, winter cheeses, Bûche de Noël for dessert.

## Practical Tips for the Perfect Paris Christmas Visit

### Getting Around

**The Métro** is the ideal transport in Paris — the network covers the entire city, tickets are affordable and frequency is excellent. Buy a **carnet of 10 tickets** (12-15€) for a day or two, or a **Paris Visite Pass** for multiple days (25-45€ for 2-3 days, includes RER to CDG and Versailles).

**Taxis and Uber** — available, but in peak hours (evenings, weekends) can be slow and expensive.

**On foot** — Paris is a walking city. The distance from the Louvre to the Eiffel Tower is ~3 km — 40 minutes of walking, completely pleasant when it's not raining.

### What to Buy as Souvenirs

**From the markets:**
- Foie gras in a jar or gift tin (ideal for transport)
- Macarons from Ladurée or Pierre Hermé (buy fresh on the last day)
- Dijon mustard
- Lavender from Provence (cosmetics, perfumes)
- Breton butter caramels (caramels au beurre salé)

**From shops:**
- An écharpe (scarf) — from affordable French H&M to artisan brands
- Art and illustrations from Montmartre artists
- French perfumes (Marché du Livre Ancien, Fragonard, L'Artisan Parfumeur)

### Weather and What to Wear

**Paris in December:**
- Average temperature: 3-10°C
- Frequent rain and grey skies — always carry an umbrella or waterproof jacket
- Possibility of frost, rarely snow (the city is not cold enough for regular snowfall)

**Clothing recommendations:**
- Layered clothing for variable temperatures
- Good waterproof jacket — essential
- Comfortable walking shoes — you'll walk 10-15 km per day
- Scarves and gloves for evening market visits
