On 1 December 1918, in the Assembly Hall in Alba Iulia, over 100,000 delegates from all Romanian provinces voted for union with the Kingdom of Romania. It was the birth moment of Greater Romania — and has been celebrated as Romania's National Day ever since.

But Alba Iulia is not important solely for 1918. <cite index="153-1">It is the place where the Dacians built fortresses, where the Romans constructed Apulum (the largest Roman settlement in Dacia), where Mihai Viteazul first united the three principalities in 1600, and where King Ferdinand and Queen Maria were crowned in 1922.</cite>

And above all these layers of history stands Alba Carolina Fortress — <cite index="157-1">the most representative Vauban-style bastion fortress in Romania and South-Eastern Europe, built at the initiative of Emperor Charles VI of Habsburg, under the supervision of Marshal Eugene of Savoy, between 1715 and 1738.</cite>

<cite index="155-1">The fortress covers 120 hectares, has 7 bastions and entry is completely free</cite> — one of the most valuable places to visit in Romania without any ticket cost.

## Alba Iulia at a Glance — Essential Facts

**County:** Alba, central Transylvania.

**Population:** approximately 63,000.

**Symbolic importance:** Romania's symbolic capital — site of the Great Union (1 December 1918) and royal coronations (1922).

**Currency:** Romanian Leu (RON).

**Distances:** Sibiu 75 km (1 hour), Cluj-Napoca 75 km (1 hour), Hunedoara (Corvin Castle) 45 km (45 min).

**Visit budget:** practically free for the fortress + cathedrals. With museums + guide: 50–100 RON per person.

## How to Get to Alba Iulia

### From Sibiu (Most Practical)

75 km, 1 hour by car on DN1. Coach: frequent services, ~2 hours, 15–20 RON. **Best circuit:** Sibiu morning + Alba Iulia midday–evening (changing of the guard at 12:00).

### From Cluj-Napoca

75 km, 1 hour by car. Coach or train: ~1.5–2 hours.

### From Bucharest

~350 km, ~4 hours by car on A1 + DN1. By train: 5–6 hours. Alternative: flight OTP → CLJ (Cluj, ~1 hour, 50–120 EUR) + car/coach to Alba Iulia (1 hour).

## Alba Carolina Fortress — The Seven-Bastion Stronghold

<cite index="157-1">Alba Carolina Fortress is the largest Vauban-style bastion fortress in Romania and South-Eastern Europe — built on the defensive system devised by French military engineer Vauban, the same system used at Versailles and in Louis XIV's fortresses.</cite>

**Technical facts:**
- Area: 120 hectares
- 7 bastions and 6 monumental gates
- Wall length: ~12 km
- Construction: 1715–1738, initiative of Emperor Charles VI of Habsburg
- Overseen by: Eugene of Savoy (who stopped Ottoman expansion in Europe)

### Entry — Free 9:00–23:00

<cite index="155-1">Fortress entry is completely free, daily 9:00–23:00. There is no entry ticket for the enclosure, walls, bastions or interior squares.</cite> Fees only apply to specific museums inside the fortress.

### The 7 Monumental Gates

The fortress has 6 monumental Baroque gates, ornamented with Habsburg reliefs. <cite index="153-1">Gate III (the Main Gate) is the most impressive — with mythological sculptures and imperial coats of arms.</cite> It is also the site of the changing of the guard.

### Underground Galleries

<cite index="155-1">Beneath the fortress stretches a system of defensive underground galleries accessible during certain periods</cite> — communication and storage tunnels from the 18th century.

## The Changing of the Guard — The Free Daily Spectacle

<cite index="148-1">The changing of the guard takes place daily at 12:00 during tourist season (May–September), at Gate III of the fortress. The guard consists of soldiers in 18th-century costumes — infantry, cavalry, artillery and commanders — in uniforms specific to the Habsburg army.</cite>

The spectacle lasts 20–30 minutes and includes:
- Guard parade in front of the fortress
- The gate exchange ceremony
- Cannon salute (on certain occasions)

**At weekends:** the changing of the guard may be doubled — check the schedule at the fortress entrance.

### Medieval Combat Demonstrations

<cite index="148-1">Every Friday from 19:00, during April–October, there are combat demonstrations with Roman soldiers, Dacian warriors and medieval knights in the fortress centre.</cite> The show is free and spectacular — re-enacted with authentic period weapons and costumes.

**1 December** — Romania's National Day: Alba Iulia is the official venue of the national celebration, with military parade, solemn ceremonies and hundreds of thousands of people. A unique spectacle but maximally crowded.

## What to See Inside the Fortress

### Coronation Cathedral (Cathedral of the Reunification)

<cite index="153-1">The Cathedral of the Reunification was built specifically for the coronation of King Ferdinand I and Queen Maria on 15 October 1922 — the first coronation in Greater Romania.</cite>

In neo-Brâncovenesc style (inspired by 17th–18th century Romanian princely architecture), with spectacular interior frescoes and the royal crypt (tombs of King Ferdinand and Queen Maria). Free entry.

### Roman Catholic Cathedral of St Michael

<cite index="154-1">Transylvania's oldest cathedral, with sections dating from the 11th–12th century, with overlapping Romanesque, Gothic and Baroque elements.</cite> Inside lies the tomb of John of Hunedoara (Iancu de Hunedoara) — father of Matthias Corvinus, King of Hungary. Free entry.

### Union Hall

<cite index="153-1">Union Hall is the room where, on 1 December 1918, the delegates gathered from all Romanian provinces voted the Resolution of Union.</cite> Voted enthusiastically by thousands of delegates — the birth certificate of Greater Romania.

Entry to Union Hall: free. Located in the former Military Casino (Palatul Apor).

### National Museum of the Union

Museum with 200,000+ exhibits covering the region's history from the Stone Age to modernity — with Roman, medieval artefacts and Union documents. Ticket: ~15–20 RON (~3–4 EUR).

## Corvin Castle and Alba Iulia Circuit from Sibiu or Cluj

### Corvin Castle in Hunedoara — Transylvania's Largest Gothic Castle

45 km west of Alba Iulia (45 minutes), <cite index="153-1">Corvin Castle is Transylvania's largest Gothic castle</cite> — the Huniade/Corvin family residence, construction from the 14th–15th century, with impressive towers, Gothic inner courtyard and the legend that Vlad Dracula was held prisoner here for three years (disputed by historians).

Ticket: 45 RON (~9 EUR). Hours: daily 9:00–17:00 (18:00 in season).

**Classic circuit from Sibiu:** Sibiu (depart 9:00) → Alba Iulia (10:00–12:00, changing of the guard) → Corvin Castle (14:00–17:00) → return to Sibiu (19:00). A complete day, ~3 hours driving total.

## Food and Drink in Alba Iulia

**Restaurants in the fortress** — several medieval-themed restaurants installed in the fortress's historic buildings. Fantastic atmosphere, mid-range prices (50–100 RON per person).

**City centre restaurants** — cheaper than the fortress, traditional Romanian cuisine (soup, sarmale, mici).

**Wine from the Aiud vineyard region** — Alba Iulia is in the heart of the Aiud-Crișana wine zone. Local white wine: 15–25 RON a glass at a restaurant.

- Gate III, 20–30 minute free spectacle

### 14:00 — Lunch

- Medieval restaurant in the fortress or local place in the city

- Walk along the walls, explore the galleries

- Optional: Corvin Castle, Hunedoara (45 km)

## Practical Tips

**Parking** — free or with a small fee at the fortress gates. Don't drive inside the fortress — pedestrian zone.

**Changing of the guard at 12:00** — if planning a single visit, arrive at the fortress 15 minutes before noon and position yourself near Gate III.

**1 December** — if going on National Day, book accommodation 3–6 months ahead. Hotels in Alba Iulia and Sibiu sell out during this period.

**AR App** — the fortress has an augmented reality app that lets you visualise virtual soldiers on the walls — download "Cetatea Alba Carolina AR" before visiting.

**Evening** — the fortress is spectacularly illuminated after dark. An evening walk inside the fortress is one of Transylvania's most beautiful experiences.
