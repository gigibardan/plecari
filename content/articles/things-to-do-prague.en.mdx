Prague is the city that makes you believe in fairy tales — except the beer costs €2 and the food is hearty enough to skip the gym. The old town survived World War II virtually untouched (one of the few European capitals not bombed), and the result is an extraordinary concentration of Gothic, Baroque, Art Nouveau, and Cubist architecture, all within a walkable area you can cross in 30 minutes.

Czechs drink more beer per capita than anyone else on Earth (140 litres per year), and that's not just a statistic — it's an entire culture you feel in every pub in the city. Add prices 30-40% lower than Western European capitals, and you have a city that's remarkably hard to leave.

This guide has all prices updated for 2026 (in CZK and EUR at the rate of 1 EUR ≈ 24.2 CZK), concrete itineraries, tested beer halls, and the tips you'd only learn from living there.

## Prague Castle — the world's largest castle complex

Prague Castle (Pražský hrad) is Guinness-recognized as the world's largest castle complex: 70,000 m² of palaces, churches, gardens, and towers on a hill above the Vltava river. It has been the seat of Czech rulers for 1,100 years and remains the official residence of the president.

### What's inside the Castle

**St. Vitus Cathedral (Katedrála sv. Víta)** — the Gothic masterpiece that took 600 years to build (1344-1929). Alfons Mucha's Art Nouveau stained glass window (in the third chapel on the left) is worth the visit alone. Climb the south tower (287 steps) for the best panoramic view of Prague — 150 CZK (~€6) separately.

**Old Royal Palace (Starý královský palác)** — the Vladislav Hall (1502) is so large that jousting tournaments were held inside — the horses climbed a special staircase that still exists.

**St. George's Basilica (Bazilika sv. Jiří)** — the oldest church in the Castle (920 AD), with a surprisingly vivid red Romanesque façade.

**Golden Lane (Zlatá ulička)** — tiny colourful houses built into the castle wall, once home to archers and later goldsmiths. Franz Kafka lived at No. 22 (now a bookshop). The houses are furnished as they were — armour displays, alchemists' workshops, artisan studios.

**Royal Garden (Královská zahrada)** — the gardens on the north side are free and stunning in spring when the tulips bloom. Includes the Queen Anne's Summer Palace, the purest Renaissance structure north of the Alps.

### Practical tips for Prague Castle

**When to go:** enter at 9:00 AM when interiors open — by 10:30 the tour buses arrive and queues triple. The courtyard and gardens open at 6:00 AM and are magical in the early hours, virtually empty.

**Changing of the Guard:** every hour, but the most elaborate is at noon (with a brass band). Photogenic but extremely crowded — if you don't insist, the 11:00 AM ceremony is nearly identical but with far fewer people.

**Entrances:** there are two — the First Courtyard (from Hradčanské náměstí, the main entrance with security check) and the east gate (from Golden Lane side, much less crowded). Pro tip: enter from the east and visit in reverse order.

**Duration:** 2-3 hours self-guided, 3-4 hours with a guided tour. Add gardens + lunch at a terrace with a view and it's a half-day.

**The descent:** don't take the metro back — walk down through Malá Strana (15-20 min) via Nerudova street or through Seminářská park (lesser-known route, fewer tourists, spectacular views). You arrive directly at Charles Bridge.

## Old Town (Staré Město) — Prague's medieval heart

### Old Town Square (Staroměstské náměstí)

Prague's centre for 1,000 years. The square is surrounded by Gothic, Baroque, and Renaissance architecture, dominated by the Church of Our Lady before Týn (the two Gothic spires that define Prague's skyline) and St. Nicholas Church (Czech Baroque at its most exuberant).

**Astronomical Clock (Orloj)** — the world's oldest functioning astronomical clock (1410). Every hour (9:00 AM-11:00 PM), the apostles appear in small windows above the dial. The show lasts 45 seconds — everyone watches, then disperses. The dial simultaneously shows three things: time, the position of the sun and moon, and the calendar. Legend: master clockmaker Hanuš was blinded by the city council to prevent him from reproducing the clock elsewhere.

**Old Town Hall Tower** — the best viewpoint in the Old Town. Lift + stairs, 250 CZK (~€10). The 360° view from 65m covers the entire square, the Týn towers, the Castle on its hill, and red rooftops as far as the eye can see.

### Clementinum — the library the internet envies

The second-largest architectural complex in Prague (after the Castle), a former Jesuit college turned National Library. The Baroque Library Hall (1722) is one of the most beautiful rooms in the world — painted ceiling, historical terrestrial globes, leather-bound books on walnut shelves.

Mandatory guided tour (45 min): ~300 CZK (~€12). Includes the Library Hall, the Mirror Chapel (classical music concerts in the evening — 500-800 CZK), and the Astronomical Tower with views.

**Insider:** the classical music concerts in the Mirror Chapel (evening, 8:00 PM) are an extraordinary experience — perfect acoustics, Baroque setting, 45-60 minutes. Tickets 500-800 CZK (€20-33) on GetYourGuide or at the door.

### Charles Bridge (Karlův most) — 600 years of history on 621 metres

30 Baroque statues lining a 621-metre stone bridge across the Vltava. It's Prague's signature image — and its most crowded spot. In summer, 30,000 people cross daily.

**When to cross:** at **6:00 AM**. The bridge is empty, morning mist rises from the river, the light on the Old Town towers is golden, and you'll take photos that look like they belong in another century. By 8:00 AM, street artists and the first tourists arrive. By 10:00 AM, it's shoulder-to-shoulder.

**Alternative:** see the bridge FROM the river. Rent a paddleboard or pedal boat on the Vltava (250-400 CZK/hour from Žluté Lázně or Slovanka) — unique perspective, zero crowds.

**Bridge towers:** you can climb both. The Old Town Bridge Tower (250-step ascent) offers a view straight down the length of the bridge toward the Castle — one of the most iconic perspectives in Europe. ~100 CZK (~€4).

**St. Nepomuk statue** — the 8th sculptural group on the right (coming from Old Town). Legend: touch the bronze plaque at the base and your wish will come true. The plaque is polished smooth by millions of hands. It's kitschy, but it's tradition.

### Powder Tower (Prašná brána)

The Gothic gate from 1475 marking the entrance to Old Town. 186 steps, 100 CZK (~€4). A different view from the Town Hall Tower — here you see along Celetná street toward the square.

## Jewish Quarter (Josefov) — Europe's best preserved

Prague's Jewish Quarter is unique in Europe — it survived for a grim reason: Hitler deliberately preserved it as a "museum of an extinct race." The result: Gothic and Renaissance synagogues, the Old Cemetery (tombstones stacked on 12 levels), and an extraordinary collection of Jewish artefacts.

## Czech beer — the national religion

Czechia invented the pilsner (in Plzeň, 1842). Czechs drink more beer per capita than anyone else in the world — 140 litres per year (roughly a glass a day, weekends included). Czech beer isn't just good — it's an art form, and Prague is the main gallery.

### Essential Prague beer halls

**U Fleků** — brewing since 1499 (yes, over 500 years). They make a single beer — a dark lager available nowhere else on Earth. Served in a medieval hall with long wooden benches. Touristy but historically essential. ~130 CZK/beer (~€5.40). Waiters bring beer to your table without asking — if you don't want another, put a coaster on your glass.

**Lokál** — modern Czech pub chain with Pilsner Urquell tankové pivo (unpasteurised beer served from pressurised tanks delivered weekly from Plzeň). ~65 CZK/half-litre (~€2.70). The food is equally excellent — svíčková, vepřo-knedlo-zelo at 180-280 CZK. Three locations: Lokál Dlouhááá (Old Town), Lokál U Bílé kuželky (Malá Strana), Lokál Hamburk (Karlín). The Dlouhá one is the most atmospheric.

**Letná Beer Garden** — open-air terrace in Letná Park with THE best panoramic view of Prague. Bridges, river, Old Town, Castle — everything from one spot. 60-80 CZK/beer (~€2.50-3.30). Best at sunset. Fun fact: the terrace sits where the world's largest Stalin statue once stood (demolished in 1962 with 800 kg of dynamite), and the current metronome marks the spot.

**U Sudu** — underground maze of rooms, each with a different atmosphere. Student prices (55-70 CZK/beer). Comes alive after 10 PM. You enter through an unremarkable door on Vodičkova — you'd never guess what's inside.

**Strahov Monastery Brewery (Klášterní pivovar Strahov)** — monastic beer brewed by Premonstratensian monks since 1628. Terrace with views of the Castle and city. Sv. Norbert beer (their own) — amber, dark, IPA. 80-110 CZK/beer (~€3.30-4.50). Visit after Prague Castle — it's a 5-minute walk.

**BeerGeek** — Prague's craft beer bar. 32 rotating taps, Czech and international craft beers. For connoisseurs. 80-150 CZK/beer. Two locations (Vinohradská and Seifertova).

### Czech beers to know

|---|---|---|---|
| Pilsner Urquell | Pilsner (the original!) | Plzeň | The first pilsner in history (1842), every pilsner derives from it |
| Budějovický Budvar | Lager | České Budějovice | The real Czech Budweiser — 100+ year legal dispute with the Americans |

**Tankové pivo (tank beer)** — this is the secret you need to know. Tank beer is unpasteurised and unfiltered, delivered in pressurised tanks directly from the brewery. It has a freshness and complexity that bottled beer will never match. Lokál, Kozlovna, and most local hospodas serve tankové pivo. Ask "Máte tankové?" — if the answer is yes, order that.

## Czech food — hearty, authentic, surprisingly varied

Czech cuisine is winter food, hearty and based on meat, potatoes, cabbage, and knedlíky (bread dumplings that soak up the sauce). It's comfort food at maximum level — the kind of meal after which you want to lie down on a park bench.

### Essential Czech dishes

**Svíčková na smetaně** — the national dish. Slow-braised beef sirloin in creamy vegetable sauce, served with bread dumplings (knedlíky) and cranberry compote. 180-280 CZK (€7.50-11.50). The ultimate test of any hospoda — if the svíčková is good, everything is good.

**Vepřo-knedlo-zelo** — roast pork + dumplings + sauerkraut. The Czech holy trinity. 160-250 CZK (€6.60-10.30). Simple, but when done right, overwhelmingly satisfying.

**Smažený sýr** — deep-fried Eidam cheese, served with fries and tartar sauce. Maximum comfort food, accidentally vegetarian. 130-200 CZK (€5.40-8.30). Street version (in bread, takeaway): 70-100 CZK.

**Guláš** — the Czech version is thicker than the Hungarian one, with more meat and less broth, served in a bread bowl (chlebový koláč) at street stands. 120-200 CZK at a restaurant, 80-130 CZK at a stand.

**Svíčková vs. guláš:** both are must-tries, but svíčková is more refined, guláš is more rustic. If you only have time for one, choose svíčková — it's the most Czech thing you can eat.

**Trdelník** — the cylindrical pastry you see at every corner in the centre. The uncomfortable truth: trdelník is NOT traditional Czech — it's Slovak/Hungarian and only became popular in Prague in the 2000s as a "tourist tradition." That doesn't mean it's not delicious — but locals don't eat it. Price: 70-90 CZK plain, 130-210 CZK with ice cream/Nutella/fruit.

**Chlebíčky** — open-faced mini-sandwiches from traditional delis. Egg, salami, cheese, fish paste, vegetables — endless combinations. 30-60 CZK each (€1.20-2.50). The most authentic Czech snack. Sisters Bistro (near Old Town Square) does excellent modern versions.

**Palacinky** — Czech pancakes (thicker than French crêpes), with jam, chocolate, fruit, or sweet curd cheese (tvaroh). 80-140 CZK. The traditional dessert.

### Havelská Market — Prague's food market

The medieval market (since 1232!) that still operates — fruit, vegetables, honey, oven-baked trdelník (not microwaved like in the tourist centre), flowers, and souvenirs. Mornings it's a real food market; by lunchtime it becomes more touristy. Address: Havelská, Old Town.

**Naplavka (the Vltava riverbank market)** — Saturday mornings (8:00 AM-2:00 PM), locals flock to the Farmer's Market on the Rašínovo nábřeží embankment. Organic produce, artisan cheeses, bread, honey, Moravian wine, craft beer. Excellent atmosphere, Castle views. Probably the most authentic food experience in Prague.

## Underground Prague — the city beneath the city

Beneath present-day Prague lies another city — medieval tunnels, Gothic cellars, WWII air-raid shelters, and catacombs. Medieval Prague was 3-4 metres lower than the current street level — when they raised the level to prevent flooding, ground floors became basements.

**Underground tours:** 500-850 CZK (€20-35), 1-1.5 hours. The most interesting are the historical tours showing what medieval Prague looked like at the original level — Romanesque cellars, connecting tunnels between houses, and the chambers where Rudolf II's alchemists hid.

The ghost/horror version (evening) is popular but more entertainment than history. Recommendation: the daytime historical tour showing the real underground, with explanations of life at the original level.

## Petřín Hill — Prague's green lung

Petřín is the park-hill dominating the left bank of the Vltava, and one of Prague's most underrated experiences. Most tourists head for the Castle but skip Petřín — a mistake.

**Petřín Tower (Petřínská rozhledna)** — a smaller-scale replica of the Eiffel Tower (1891), built for Prague's Jubilee Exhibition. 63.5m tall, 299 steps. The view from the top is arguably Prague's finest — the entire city at your feet, plus the Castle at eye level. 150 CZK (~€6).

**Mirror Maze (Zrcadlové bludiště)** — next to Petřín Tower. 75 CZK (~€3). Fun with kids, but also without — includes a diorama of Czech students fighting the Swedes on Charles Bridge in 1648.

**Rose Garden (Růžový sad)** — over 12,000 roses, free, most beautiful in May-June.

**Funicular (Lanová dráha)** — takes you up the hill from Malá Strana. Works with a regular public transport ticket (36 CZK). Departs from Újezd every 10-15 minutes.

**Insider:** go to Petřín at sunset, climb the tower, then walk down through Seminářská park toward Malá Strana — the roundabout route passes Strahov Monastery (stop at the monks' brewery!).

## Vyšehrad — the other castle, without the tourists

Vyšehrad is the "other castle" — older than Prague Castle (10th century), but incomparably less visited. It's a fortress on a steep hill above the Vltava, with spectacular views, a Romanesque rotunda, and the National Cemetery where Dvořák, Smetana, Mucha, and Čapek are buried.

**Free.** The fortifications, park, views — all free. Only the Casemates (underground tunnels) have a ticket (50 CZK).

**Basilica of St. Peter and St. Paul** — dramatically neo-Gothic, with Art Nouveau interiors.

**Insider:** go to Vyšehrad toward evening with a beer from the park kiosk and watch the sunset over the Vltava. It's the moment locals treasure most in Prague — complete quiet, no tourists, 270° views over the city. Accessible by metro C (Vyšehrad station).

## Local neighbourhoods — where Prague gets real

### Malá Strana (Lesser Town)

Between the Castle and Charles Bridge. Baroque palaces, embassy gardens, quiet squares, and the John Lennon Wall (continuously graffitied since the 1980s, a symbol of anti-communist protest — it's constantly repainted). Nerudova Street — the most atmospheric route to the Castle, with medieval houses bearing trade signs (at the two suns, at the golden horse, at the red crayfish).

**Vrtba Garden (Vrtbovská zahrada)** — one of Europe's most beautiful Baroque gardens, hidden in a courtyard on Karmelitská. 150 CZK (~€6). A secret Castle view that 95% of tourists miss.

**Kampa Island** — the little island under Charles Bridge, with a park, Kampa Museum (modern art — David Černý, the crawling baby sculptures from the TV Tower), and restaurants along "Čertovka" (the Devil's Channel — Prague's Venice).

### Vinohrady — where Praguers actually live

The residential neighbourhood with Prague's finest Art Nouveau architecture. Riegrovy sady park has a beer terrace with a Castle view that rivals Letná. Mánesova street and náměstí Míru square (with St. Ludmila Church) are the neighbourhood's centre.

**Why go:** excellent restaurants and cafés at local prices (20-30% less than the centre), no tourists, the feeling of a real city. Vinohradský pivovar (neighbourhood brewery with their own beer).

### Žižkov — the rebel quarter

The highest density of pubs per capita in Prague (and possibly the world). The Žižkov TV Tower with David Černý's giant crawling baby sculptures (2000) — possibly Europe's most bizarre monument. You can go up to the bar at the 66m level for cocktails with a view.

**U Sadu**, **Sedm vlků**, and dozens of pubs where beer is 50-65 CZK and you might be the only non-local.

### Holešovice — contemporary art

The transformed industrial quarter. DOX Centre for Contemporary Art (200 CZK) — provocative exhibitions in a former warehouse. Veletržní palác (National Gallery, 300 CZK) — Czechia's largest modern art collection (Mucha, Kupka, Toyen). Manifesto Market Holešovice — food truck market with atmosphere.

### Karlín — reborn after the floods

Karlín was devastated by the 2002 floods and completely rebuilt. Now it's the neighbourhood with Prague's best restaurants (Eska — fermentation, baking, and reinvention of Czech cuisine; Můj šálek kávy — excellent third-wave coffee), post-industrial architecture repurposed, and a young energy.

### Smíchov — Staropramen's neighbourhood

The Staropramen brewery is here and can be visited (factory tour: ~300 CZK, includes tasting of 3 beers). Nový Smíchov (modern mall) and new riverside developments.

## Street art and David Černý — Prague as an open-air gallery

David Černý is the Czech artist who turned Prague into a museum of the absurd. His works are everywhere:

**Crawling Babies (Miminka)** — the sculptures on the Žižkov TV Tower. 10 giant faceless babies climbing the tower. Bizarre, unforgettable, free to see.

**Inverted Horse (Kůň)** — in the Lucerna Passage (off Wenceslas Square). Saint Wenceslas riding a dead horse, hanging upside-down. Caustic political commentary.

**Kafka's Head (Hlava Franze Kafky)** — a kinetic sculpture of 11 metres made of 42 independently rotating stainless steel layers that form and deform Kafka's face. Near the Quadrio mall, Národní třída metro station.

**Piss (Čůrající postavy)** — two statues urinating onto a map of Czechia, near the Kafka Museum. You can send an SMS and they'll spell your message in water. Yes, seriously.

## Day trips from Prague

### Český Krumlov — the fairytale medieval town (UNESCO)

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Distance | ~180 km south (2.5-3h by bus, 3h by train) |
| Trip duration | 10-12 hours |
| Transport | RegioJet/FlixBus coach (150-250 CZK), ČD train, or organised tour |
| GYG organised tour price | 1,200-2,000 CZK (€50-80) with transport + guide |

A perfectly preserved medieval town on a meander of the Vltava, with Czechia's second-largest castle (after Prague), colourful 13th-14th century houses, and an atmosphere that feels frozen in time. UNESCO World Heritage since 1992.

**What to see:** the Castle with its painted tower (250 CZK), the central square, the view from Plášťový most, the Vltava riverside walk. In summer you can raft the Vltava through town (300-500 CZK, 2-3h) — a unique experience.

**Insider:** the town is small — 4-5 hours is enough for a visit. If going by public transport (RegioJet is fastest), leave early morning (7:00-8:00 AM) and return in the evening. The organised tour is more convenient but more rigid.

### Kutná Hora — the Bone Chapel

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Distance | ~80 km east (1h by train) |
| Trip duration | 5-7 hours |
| Transport | Direct ČD train from Praha hl.n. (100-160 CZK) |
| Ossuary + Cathedral ticket | ~290 CZK (~€12) |

**Sedlec Ossuary (Kostnice v Sedlci)** — a chapel decorated with the bones of 40,000-70,000 people. Chandeliers made of bones, coats of arms made of bones, pyramids of skulls. Fascinating and macabre in equal measure. The reason: in 1278, an abbot brought soil from Golgotha and the cemetery became so popular that old bones were exhumed to make room.

**St. Barbara's Cathedral** — a late Gothic masterpiece rivalling St. Vitus Cathedral. The star-ribbed vault ceiling is spectacular.

**Insider:** take the train (not the organised tour) — it's cheaper, more flexible, and Kutná Hora is small enough to explore on your own. Kutná Hora hlavní nádraží station → local bus/15-min walk to the Ossuary → walk through town to St. Barbara's Cathedral. Total: 5-6 hours with leisurely walking.

### Karlovy Vary — the emperors' spa town

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Distance | ~130 km west (2-2.5h by bus) |
| Trip duration | 8-10 hours |
| Transport | RegioJet/FlixBus (150-250 CZK), ČD train |

A spa town since the 14th century where emperors, tsars, and Beethoven came to take the waters. 12 thermal springs (30-72°C) — you drink the water directly from the springs using traditional ceramic cups (oplatky). Spectacular colonnades, Belle Époque architecture, and becherovka — the traditional herbal liqueur originating from Karlovy Vary.

**Insider:** take a RegioJet coach (fastest and most comfortable). Karlovy Vary is surprisingly cinematic — James Bond was filmed here (Casino Royale), and the international film festival (July) draws major stars.

### Plzeň (Pilsen) — the beer pilgrimage

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Distance | ~90 km west (1-1.5h by train/bus) |
| Trip duration | 6-8 hours |
| Transport | ČD train, RegioJet (100-200 CZK) |
| Pilsner Urquell tour | ~350 CZK (~€14) |

The city where pilsner was invented in 1842. The Pilsner Urquell factory tour (Plzeňský Prazdroj) is the ultimate pilgrimage for beer lovers — see the brewing process, visit the medieval cellars where beer matures at 0°C, and taste unfiltered beer drawn directly from barrels in the cellar. It's a completely different experience from anything you've ever drunk from a bottle.

**Insider:** the English tour usually runs at 12:45 PM, 2:15 PM, and 4:15 PM — book online. The factory is a 10-minute walk from the main train station.

## Getting around Prague

### Public transport (DPP)

Prague has one of Europe's most efficient public transport systems — metro, trams, buses, and funicular, all on the same ticket.

| Ticket | Price (on app) | Price (paper) | Validity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 minutes | 36 CZK (~€1.50) | 39 CZK (~€1.60) | Any transport, 30 min |
| 90 minutes | 46 CZK (~€1.90) | 50 CZK (~€2.10) | Any transport, 90 min |
| 24 hours | 140 CZK (~€5.80) | 150 CZK (~€6.20) | Unlimited |
| 72 hours (3 days) | 340 CZK (~€14) | 350 CZK (~€14.50) | Unlimited |

**PID Lítačka app** — buy tickets on your phone (~10% cheaper than paper tickets), scan at inspection. Works perfectly.

**Metro** — 3 lines (A green, B yellow, C red). Covers everything. Runs 5:00 AM-midnight. Frequency: 2-3 minutes during rush hour, 5-10 minutes in the evening.

**Trams** — the most beautiful way to get around. 30 lines, excellent frequency. **Tram 22** is a sight in itself — runs through the centre, Malá Strana, and past the Castle. **Tram 23** runs a similar route but is less crowded. Night trams (midnight-5:00 AM): numbers 91-99 + night buses — Prague runs 24/7.

### Airport to city centre

Václav Havel Airport (PRG) — 17 km west of the centre.

| Option | Duration | Price | Details |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus 119 + Metro A | 45-50 min total | 46-50 CZK (~€2) | Bus 119 → Nádraží Veleslavín (terminus) → Metro A to centre |
| Airport Express (AE) | 35-40 min | 100 CZK (~€4) | Direct bus to Praha hlavní nádraží (central station) |
| Bolt/Uber | 25-35 min | 500-650 CZK (€20-27) | Most comfortable, 24/7 |
| Official taxi | 25-35 min | 700-900 CZK (€29-37) | More expensive, use Bolt only |

**Insider:** Bus 119 + Metro A is the most efficient option — runs every 5-15 minutes, metro runs like clockwork. Never take a taxi from the airport queues — prices are predatory. Order Bolt from the terminal.

### Taxi and ride-sharing

**Bolt** is the go-to app in Prague — fixed prices, GPS, no negotiation. Uber works equally well. NEVER take a taxi from the street or from the "official" queues at stations/airport — Prague taxi drivers have an (earned) reputation for scamming tourists with rigged metres or roundabout routes. Bolt solves everything.

Indicative Bolt fares: Airport → Centre: 500-650 CZK. Old Town → Castle: 120-180 CZK. Centre → Vyšehrad: 100-150 CZK.

## Practical tips 2026

### Currency and payment

**Currency:** Czech Koruna (CZK). 1 EUR ≈ 24.2 CZK (2026). Czechia has NOT adopted the euro (and doesn't plan to anytime soon).

**Card payment:** accepted almost universally — restaurants, shops, transport. Revolut, Wise, and no-fee cards work perfectly.

**WATCH OUT for DCC (Dynamic Currency Conversion):** when paying by card, the POS terminal often offers conversion to EUR/GBP/USD. **ALWAYS REFUSE** and choose to pay in CZK. DCC adds a hidden 3-7% fee that goes to the card processor, not to you. Same at ATMs — refuse conversion, withdraw in CZK.

**Cash:** withdraw from bank ATMs (Komerční banka, ČSOB, Česká spořitelna, Raiffeisenbank). **NEVER exchange at tourist-street exchange offices** — those on Karlova, Na Příkopě, and Old Town Square display attractive rates but hide 15-20% commissions in the fine print. Some are technically legal but morally unconscionable.

### Safety

Prague is one of Europe's safest capitals. The main risks:

**Pickpockets** — active on Charles Bridge, in Metro A (the tourist line), at the Astronomical Clock, and on Tram 22. Solution: inside pockets, backpack in front, no wallet in back pockets. Not paranoia — realistic, especially in season.

**Taxi drivers** — earned reputation. Use ONLY Bolt/Uber. Never from the street or "official" station/airport queues.

**Exchange offices** — already covered. They're essentially legal scams. Pay by card or withdraw from bank ATMs.

**Clubs on Dlouhá** — the party street in Old Town. Quality varies enormously. Avoid clubs with "promoters" on the street offering free entry or "free drinks" — they're traps for an inflated bill at the end.

### Language

Czechs speak Czech, but English is widely spoken in the centre, restaurants, and the tourism industry. A few Czech words are enormously appreciated:

## 10 local secrets that guidebooks skip

1. **Tank beer (tankové pivo) is on another level.** Ask "Máte tankové?" at any hospoda — if the answer is yes, you've struck liquid gold. Lokál, Kozlovna, and most local pubs serve from tanks.

2. **Letná Park at sunset > any expensive cruise.** Walk up to the beer garden, grab a half-litre for 65 CZK, and watch Prague from above. Locals come here, not to the restaurants on Charles Bridge.

3. **Tram 22 is Prague's free sightseeing tour.** It passes through the centre, Malá Strana, and past the Castle — without the hop-on-hop-off "tour" fee.

4. **Vrtba Garden is Malá Strana's best-kept secret.** A Baroque Castle view that 95% of tourists miss. 150 CZK, entered from Karmelitská.

5. **Don't eat on Charles Bridge or Karlova.** Triple prices, half the quality. Detour 2 streets and prices drop by 50%.

6. **Naplavka market (Saturday mornings)** is Prague's most authentic food experience — locals flock to the Farmer's Market on the Vltava riverbank.

7. **Únětický pivovar (15 min from Prague)** — the award-winning microbrewery where Praguers go at weekends. Fresh beer at 40-55 CZK, garden seating.

8. **Classical music concerts at Clementinum** (Mirror Chapel) are an experience that costs as much as dinner (500-800 CZK) but is worth as much as an opera performance.

9. **The Astronomical Clock is impressive as an object, not as a show.** The hourly spectacle lasts 45 seconds and is anticlimactic. Go for the 1410 dial and the guide's explanation, not the animation.

10. **Prague at night is something else.** Night trams (91-99) run midnight-5:00 AM and the illuminated city is spectacular. Take tram 91 from Národní třída through the centre at 2 AM — that's the Prague locals remember fondly.

## Unique experiences you won't find everywhere

**The pay-as-you-drink hospoda tradition** — in many local pubs, the waiter places beers on your table without you ordering, and at the end you count how many you've had. Authentic Czech tradition. If you don't want another beer, put a coaster on your glass.

**Staropramen brewery tour (Smíchov)** — ~300 CZK, includes tasting of 3 different beers and 150+ years of brewery history. Less touristy than Pilsner Urquell (which is in Plzeň), but equally interesting.

**Concerts in the Spanish Synagogue** — classical music in Prague's most beautiful interior. 500-900 CZK. Extraordinary acoustics, unique Moorish setting.

**The Letná Metronome** — where the world's largest Stalin statue once stood (demolished in 1962), now a giant metronome. Skaters and young people gather here in the evenings — a symbolic spot of post-communist Prague.

**Czech Beer Museum (Český pivní muzeum)** — tasting of 30 beers in mini-glasses with explanations. ~450 CZK with full tasting. On Husova street, Old Town. Different from beer tours (which are on the street) — this one is interactive and educational.

## Common mistakes to avoid

**Don't exchange money at tourist-centre exchange offices.** Use bank ATMs or pay by card.

**Don't eat at restaurants with laminated photo menus.** They're tourist traps with triple prices and minimum quality.

**Don't cross Charles Bridge at midday.** From noon to 4:00 PM it's a sea of people. Early morning (6:00-8:00 AM) or evening after 9:00 PM.

**Don't confuse trdelník with Czech tradition.** It's delicious, but it's a Slovak/Hungarian import. Locals don't eat it.

**Don't skip Vyšehrad.** It's free, it's spectacular, and it has zero tourists compared to the Castle.

**Don't take street taxis.** Bolt or Uber, period.

**Don't buy souvenirs on Karlova or Charles Bridge.** Prices are 3-5x higher. Manufaktura and Botanicus (on Na Perštýně or in malls) have authentic Czech products at fair prices.

**Don't stay only in Old Town.** Vinohrady, Žižkov, Karlín, Holešovice — that's where real Prague lives.
