There are islands that look good in photos. And then there's Santorini — which hits you physically when you see it for the first time with your own eyes. The ferry enters the caldera through the gap between Thirasia and Aspronisi, and suddenly you're facing an arc of 300-meter cliffs, white with houses, dropping vertically into the bluest sea you've ever seen. It's as if someone cut a mountain in half and poured ocean into the wound.

The island is what remained after the volcano exploded 3,600 years ago — an explosion four times more powerful than Krakatoa, generating a tsunami that devastated Minoan Crete and which some historians link to the legend of Atlantis. The caldera you admire from a restaurant terrace in Oia is actually a flooded crater. The small island in the middle, Nea Kameni, is the volcano that still smokes. Santorini isn't just beautiful — it's geologically alive.

This guide is written for travelers who want to see the real Santorini — not the Instagram version with filters and hidden costs, but the island with all its charm, challenges, and secrets.

## Oia, Fira, Imerovigli — Where You Stay Matters Enormously

Santorini is not a large island — 18 km long, 5 km at its widest — but the differences between villages are dramatic in terms of experience, price, and logistics.

**Fira (Θήρα):** The island's capital and the most practical base for a first visit. The central bus station is here, most restaurants and shops are here, and the caldera panorama is extraordinary — without Oia's overcrowded glamour. A decent room with caldera view in Fira: €120–250/night in season. In Oia, the same room: €250–500. Fira is also the best starting point for the caldera rim walking trail — 10 km from Fira to Oia, the finest way to see the island.

**Oia (Οία):** The most photographed place in Greece. The white houses, blue domes, windmill, and sunset that turns everything to gold and orange — they're all here. But here's what you should know: the Oia sunset is famous, and consequently 500–1,000 people crowd onto the Byzantine Castle walls every evening to watch it. The experience is sometimes more claustrophobic than romantic. Better alternatives: watch from Imerovigli (equally beautiful, a quarter of the crowd), from Pyrgos (360° panorama, almost nobody), or from a boat in the caldera (zero crowds, 360° sunset).

**Imerovigli:** 2 km north of Fira, on the caldera rim, reachable on foot in 25 minutes via a spectacular paved path. The highest point on the caldera (nicknamed "The Balcony of the Aegean"). Quiet, panoramic, excellent hotels — and prices 20–30% below Oia. Skaros Rock — a 13th-century Venetian fortress on a dramatic headland — is a 10-minute walk and magical at sunset.

**Kamari and Perissa** (east coast): If beach and budget matter more than caldera views. Both have long black volcanic sand beaches, affordable hotels (€60–120/night), tavernas with feet in the sand, and a relaxed atmosphere. Kamari has an organized promenade and an open-air cinema at the foot of Mesa Vouno cliff. Perissa is younger, with beach bars and music. Red Beach is 15 minutes by car.

**Pyrgos:** The best-preserved medieval village on Santorini, on a hill in the island's interior, with Venetian houses and a castle at the top offering 360° views of the entire island. Zero tourists compared to Oia. Franco's Café at the summit offers arguably the best sunset on the island — without the crush. Seriously underrated.

## The Caldera — The Experience That Defines Santorini

The caldera is everything. It's what makes Santorini different from any other Greek island. And the best way to experience it is from the water.

**Catamaran tour (5–6 hours):** The most popular option. You depart from Vlychada port or Ammoudi Bay (below Oia), sail along the caldera, stop at the hot springs (warm sulfurous water — it's not crystal clear, it's rusty-orange and smells of sulfur, but the sensation is unique), hike up Nea Kameni (the active crater — 20-minute walk on lava with fumaroles), snorkel in clear water, and often enjoy dinner on board at sunset. Prices: €80–120 for group catamaran (max 20 people), €250–400 for private.

**Traditional boat tour (kaiki):** Cheaper (€40–60), more authentic, simpler. Wooden boats running the Fira–Nea Kameni–hot springs–Thirasia–return route. Duration: 3–4 hours. Without the catamaran's luxury, but with a charm modern boats can't match.

**Caldera kayaking:** For active travelers. A 3–4 hour guided route paddling along the base of the caldera cliffs, with stops at sea caves and beaches accessible only from the water. €60–90/person. Early morning departures (07:00) are best — calm water, perfect light, zero tour boats.

## Volcanic Beaches — What to Expect

If you're coming to Santorini expecting beaches like Zakynthos or Lefkada, adjust your expectations. Santorini's beaches are volcanic — black sand, red sand, or pebble, with water that deepens quickly. They're visually spectacular, but different.

**Red Beach (Kokkini Paralia):** 10 minutes' walk from Akrotiri. Dramatic red cliff plunging into a small beach of reddish pebble. Extremely photogenic. But: small, crowded from 11:00, and the access path has an unstable section on the cliff (watch for rockfalls — this is not rhetorical, it genuinely happens). Best visited early morning or from the water (boat from Akrotiri, €5 return).

**Perissa and Perivolos:** The longest beaches (3 km continuous black sand). Perivolos is quieter at its southern end; Perissa is more organized with beach bars. The black sand gets extremely hot in the sun — water shoes are essential. Sunbed prices: €8–15/day with umbrella. Beachfront restaurants serve decent food at €10–18/main course.

**Vlychada:** Lunar landscape — white cliffs sculpted by wind and erosion that look like frozen waves. The beach is small, pebbly, and much quieter than Red Beach or Perissa. Vlychada port is the departure point for catamaran tours. An excellent spot for dramatic photography without tourists.

**Kamari:** Organized with a promenade, restaurants, shops. Ideal for families. In the evening, the Open Air Cinema Kamari on the beach projects films with Mesa Vouno cliff as a backdrop — one of the most surprisingly pleasant experiences in Santorini.

## Akrotiri — The Pompeii of the Aegean

At the island's southern tip, the Akrotiri archaeological site is a Minoan settlement buried by the ~1600 BC eruption — preserved under volcanic ash for 3,600 years. Unlike Pompeii (where mainly stone structures survive), Akrotiri has preserved complex frescoes, intact ceramic vessels, furniture, and even drainage systems — one of the most sophisticated Bronze Age civilizations.

The boxer fresco, the blue monkeys fresco, the spring fresco with lilies and swallows — are masterpieces of ancient art, now displayed at the Museum of Prehistoric Thera in Fira (entry: €6, combined with Akrotiri: €14). The site itself is covered by a modern protective structure and visited on walkways suspended above the ruins.

Entry to Akrotiri: €12. Open 08:00–20:00 (summer). Audio guide: €5 — worth it, otherwise the ruins look like stone walls without context.

**Useful comparison:** If you've already visited Pompeii or Herculaneum on the [Amalfi Coast](/en/city-breaks/amalfi-coast-complete-guide-2026) or in [Naples](/en/city-breaks/naples-complete-guide-2026), Akrotiri is smaller in scale but of comparable historical importance — it's 1,500 years older than Pompeii.

## Santorini Wines — Why They Matter

Santorini isn't just an island with a view — it's one of the world's oldest wine regions. Vines on volcanic soil, without irrigation, with the Assyrtiko grape, produce wines that have conquered the wine world over the past 20 years.

**What makes the wine special here:** The soil is pumice, volcanic ash, and lava — zero organic matter. Grapes grow practically on rock. Roots descend 20+ meters to find water. The result: a white wine with intense minerality, vibrant acidity, and notes of citrus, salt, and wet stone that doesn't resemble anything from other regions.

**The traditional "kouloura" shape:** Vines aren't on trellises but trained into a circular basket (kouloura) close to ground level — protecting grapes from the meltemi wind that blows at 50–80 km/h in summer.

**Wineries to visit:**

**Santo Wines (Pyrgos):** The most famous. Tasting on the caldera-view terrace. Popular — gets crowded after 17:00. Tasting: €15–25 (4–6 wines). Go at 11:00–12:00 for peace.

**Venetsanos Winery (Megalochori):** In a 1947 cave winery carved into volcanic rock, with a direct caldera view. More intimate than Santo. Tasting: €12–20.

**Gavalas Winery (Megalochori):** The oldest winemaking family in Santorini, since 1888. Relaxed tasting, no tourist glamour — just wine and conversation. €10–15.

**Vinsanto:** Not the same as Tuscan "vin santo." It's a local dessert wine from Assyrtiko and Aidani grapes sun-dried for 12–14 days, then fermented and aged in oak barrels. With dessert, aged feta cheese, or on its own — exceptional.

## Food — What to Seek, What to Skip

**The golden rule in Santorini:** Avoid the first-line caldera restaurants in Fira and Oia for main meals — they're expensive (€25–50/dish) and quality is mediocre, they survive on views. Go there for a sunset cocktail (€12–15), not for dinner. The real food is in the villages.

**What's specific to Santorini:**

**Fava:** A creamy yellow split pea paste (Lathyrus clymenum), grown on volcanic soil. It's not hummus, it's not a lentil paste — it's something unique, with silky texture and an earthy-sweet taste. Served with caramelized onion and capers. Santorini fava has Protected Designation of Origin.

**Santorini cherry tomatoes (tomataki):** Tiny, intensely sweet, grown without irrigation on volcanic soil — containing 3x more sugar and lycopene than regular tomatoes. Tomatokeftedes (fried tomato fritters) are the island's signature dish.

**Kapari (capers) with caper leaves:** Salads with capers, caper leaves, and local cherry tomatoes — simple and unforgettable.

**Where to eat:**

**Metaxy Mas (Exo Gonia):** Probably the best restaurant in Santorini — Cretan-Santorinian cuisine, ingredients from their own farm. Reservation essential 3–5 days ahead. €20–30/person.

**To Psaraki (Vlychada):** Fresh fish at the port. No pretension, fair prices (€12–20/main course).

**Lucky's Souvlaki (Fira):** The best gyros on the island. €4–5. The queue is long — worth it.

**Argo (Fira):** Family restaurant on the caldera, prices below area average (€15–22/dish), spectacular view. Where locals eat with visiting friends.

## Getting Around

**KTEL bus:** Cheap and functional. All routes depart from Fira's central station. Main routes: Fira–Oia (€1.80, 20 min), Fira–Kamari (€1.80, 15 min), Fira–Perissa (€2.40, 20 min), Fira–Akrotiri (€1.80, 20 min). Summer frequency: every 20–40 minutes on popular routes. Tickets bought on the bus. Last bus to Oia: ~23:30 — check schedules at ktel-santorini.gr.

**ATV/Quadbike:** Popular with young tourists, but frankly — dangerous. Santorini's roads are steep, with volcanic sand on asphalt, and traffic includes large buses. Santorini has one of Greece's highest ATV accident rates. If you insist: wear a helmet (mandatory but unenforced), avoid night riding, don't drive after drinking.

**Rental car:** Much safer than ATV. €35–60/day in season. Parking is limited in Oia (official car park at village entrance, 10-minute walk) and Fira (peripheral parking). Kamari, Perissa, Akrotiri — parking easily found.

**Taxi:** Fewer than 30 taxis on the entire island. In season, waits of 30–60 minutes. Book via phone or through your hotel. Fira to Oia: ~€20.

## Practical Tips

**The Oia sunset is overrated as a live experience.** It's beautiful — extraordinary even — but the experience of being crammed with hundreds of people on the Byzantine Castle walls, elbows in ribs and phones raised, isn't romantic. Better alternatives: Imerovigli (equally beautiful, 80% fewer people), Pyrgos (360° panorama, almost nobody), or from a catamaran in the caldera (zero crowds, 360° sunset).

**Cruise ship calls transform the island.** When 3–4 cruise ships anchor in the caldera simultaneously (which happens frequently May–October), Fira and Oia receive 10,000–15,000 additional visitors in a single day. Check cruisemapper.com for the call schedule and plan Oia/Fira visits on ship-free days.

**Don't walk down from Fira to Old Port with the donkeys.** There's a 580-step path descending from Fira to Old Port (where cruise tenders and some local ferries depart). The path is shared with donkeys carrying tourists. The donkeys leave "traces" on the steps, it's slippery, and the smell is... memorable. Take the cable car (€6 each way) or walk only if you have good shoes and a strong nose.

**The meltemi wind is real.** From July through September, the meltemi blows from the north at 30–60 km/h, sometimes more. Upside: it cools the 35°C temperatures. Downside: cancelled ferries (especially fast ones), beach bars with sand in your eyes, and dresses fluttering in photos. Hat with a chin strap — not without.

**Internet:** EU roaming applies. If you need extra data, an [Airalo eSIM](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3) costs a few euros and covers everything.

**Insurance:** EU health card works, but Santorini's hospital is small. Serious cases are airlifted to Athens. A [comprehensive policy](https://ektatraveling.tpo.mx/uU7jZ5jK) covering medical evacuation is a small investment (€10–15/week) with enormous potential value.

## How to Get There

**Direct flight:** Seasonal flights (May–October) from many European cities to Santorini Thira (JTR). Flight duration from most European capitals: 2–3.5 hours. Thira airport is small — 10 minutes by bus or taxi to Fira.

**Via Athens:** Fly to Athens (year-round, budget airlines from €40–80 return) + ferry from Piraeus. Conventional ferry (Blue Star Ferries): 5–8 hours, €35–45. Speed ferry (SeaJets, Golden Star): 2–2.5 hours, €60–80. The overnight ferry (departing ~19:00–21:00, arriving ~05:00–07:00) is an excellent option — you save one night's accommodation, sleep on the ferry (cabin: €50–80 extra, or free deck seating), and arrive at sunrise in the caldera.

**From the airport to accommodation:** KTEL bus from airport to Fira: €1.80, departing 30 minutes after each flight. Taxi to Fira: €15–20. Taxi to Oia: €30–35. Most hotels arrange transfers on request.
