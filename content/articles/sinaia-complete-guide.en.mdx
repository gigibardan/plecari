There is an axiom that all castle architects know: grandeur is won in the details. Peleș Castle won it 168 times over — one for each room, each decorated in the style of a different European culture. The Turkish room. The Spanish armour hall. The Florentine salon. The ebony-panelled library. The Moorish hallway.

<cite index="153-1">Peleș was commissioned by King Carol I and built between 1873 and 1914. It is a remarkable achievement of Neo-Renaissance architecture, blending elements typical of a Bavarian fairy-tale castle and an Austro-Hungarian palace.</cite>

<cite index="151-1">Peleș is considered one of the world's most beautiful castles — travellers who have explored castles across Europe, from France's Loire Valley to the Czech Republic's gems, confidently rank Peleș among the very best.</cite>

But Sinaia is not only Peleș. It is also one of Romania's most elegant mountain resorts ever built — with belle époque villas scattered among pine forests, the 1695 Monastery founded by Prince Cantacuzino, a cable car to the Bucegi plateau at 2,000 metres and a royal atmosphere that makes you feel you've landed in another era.

## Sinaia at a Glance — Essential Facts

**County:** Prahova, Bucegi Mountains (southern Carpathians).

**Population:** approximately 10,000 permanent residents — much more in summer and winter.

**Altitude:** 860–900 m (resort centre).

**Nickname:** "Pearl of the Carpathians."

**Distance from Bucharest:** ~130 km, ~1h45 by train.

**Distance from Brașov:** ~45 km, ~40 minutes by train.

---

## How to Get to Sinaia

### By Train (Most Recommended)

**From Bucharest Nord:** InterRegio/Rapid direct, ~1h45–2h, 30–60 RON (~6–12 EUR). Most practical — Sinaia station is central, 15 minutes' walk from the Monastery and 25–30 minutes from Peleș Castle (or direct taxi, ~20 RON).

**From Brașov:** direct train, ~40 minutes, 15–25 RON. Perfect for a day trip from Brașov.

### By Car

**From Bucharest:** ~130 km on A3 + DN1, ~1h30–2h. **Critical warning:** <cite index="159-1">weekend traffic on DN1 can turn 2 hours into 3.5–4 hours.</cite> Avoid Friday evenings and Sunday evenings.

## Peleș Castle — Tickets, Hours, How to Book

### The Golden Rule: Mandatory Online Booking

<cite index="155-1">Peleș Castle tickets are purchased EXCLUSIVELY online, in advance, for a specific time slot. No walk-up tickets exist.</cite>

**Available slots:**
- Wednesday: 10:00–12:45
- Thursday–Sunday: 9:15–11:00
- Monday–Tuesday: closed

**Capacity:** 500 people per slot — sells out quickly in season.

**Prices:** 60–100 RON (~12–20 EUR) depending on tour type chosen.

**Where to book:** peles.ro or GetYourGuide (with skip-the-line included).

**How far ahead:** at least 1–2 weeks in season (July–August); 3–5 days in shoulder season.

### What You'll See Inside Peleș Castle

<cite index="151-1">Peleș Castle has 168 rooms, each decorated in the style of a different European culture — the Turkish room, the Spanish armour hall, the Florentine salon, the Moorish hallway, the ebony-panelled library.</cite>

The standard guided tour covers the ground and first floors — approximately 35–40 rooms. The guide explains the history of each room and the collections (tapestries, paintings, weapons, period furniture).

**Peleș Gardens** — access to the castle park is free. The terraced gardens with statues, fountains and views of the Bucegi Mountains are spectacular.

## Pelișor Castle — The Art Nouveau Gem

<cite index="152-1">Pelișor is the smaller castle built next to Peleș for Crown Prince Ferdinand and his wife Queen Maria, in Art Nouveau style as opposed to Peleș's grand Neo-Renaissance.</cite>

**Why it deserves a separate visit:**
- Queen Maria's Gold Room — an interior completely decorated in gold leaf with Byzantine symbolism and modern European art
- The original Art Nouveau furniture is intact — far better preserved than in many other European royal houses
- Much less visited than Peleș — you often have the rooms to yourself

Ticket: ~30 RON (~6 EUR). 5 minutes' walk from Peleș.

---

## Sinaia Monastery — Where the Town Gets Its Name

<cite index="155-1">Sinaia Monastery was founded in 1695 by Prince Mihail Cantacuzino and named after the Saint Catherine's Monastery on Mount Sinai in Egypt.</cite> The town itself was named after the monastery.

The complex has two courtyards with two churches:
- **Old Church (1695)** — the oldest structure, with Brâncovenesc-style frescoes
- **Great Church (1846)** — larger, more solemn, with 19th-century frescoes

**The Monastery Museum** houses 17th-century icons and — most precious — <cite index="155-1">the first Bible printed in the Romanian language, from 1688.</cite> Museum ticket: ~10 RON.

Monastery entry is free. Located on the forest footpath between the station and Peleș — perfect as a stop on the route.

### Sinaia Casino and Dimitrie Ghica Park

<cite index="151-1">Sinaia Casino (1912) is an impressive building in eclectic French-Romanian style in Dimitrie Ghica Park</cite> — today a cultural centre, not a gaming casino. Hosts exhibitions, concerts and cultural events.

<cite index="154-1">Dimitrie Ghica Park, with fountains, shaded alleys and the eclectic villa architecture surrounding it, makes for an elegant stroll.</cite> Free.

## The Cable Car and the Bucegi Mountains

### Sinaia Cable Car → Cota 1400 → Cota 2000

**The cable car system** in Sinaia is in two stages:
- **Stage 1:** Sinaia (860 m) → Cota 1400 — old cable car from the centre or newer one further up
- **Stage 2:** Cota 1400 → Cota 2000 — a second cable car

Return ticket: ~50–80 RON (~10–16 EUR) depending on stages.

**Practical warning:** <cite index="153-1">the cable car may stop for wind or technical faults — check its status on the day of your visit before making your way there.</cite>

### From Cota 2000 — Bucegi Trails

<cite index="151-1">From Cota 2000, trails lead across the Bucegi plateau with spectacular alpine scenery:</cite>

**Babele** (~2 hours, easy) — the wind-eroded rock formations resembling "old women", an iconic Bucegi image.

**Bucegi Sphinx** (~2.5 hours, easy) — the rock formation that, from a specific angle, resembles a human sphinx.

**Heroes' Cross on Caraiman** (~3.5 hours, moderate) — the 8-metre metal cross on Caraiman Rock (2,291 m), erected in memory of World War I heroes.

**Ialomița Cave** (~4 hours, moderate) — the cave and cliff monastery inside the mountain.

## Practical Tips

**Peleș online booking mandatory** — no tickets on the day. Check slot availability at peles.ro 1–2 weeks ahead. If no slots available, GetYourGuide with skip-the-line may be a solution.

**Cable car — check status** on the morning of your visit. It can stop without notice.

**Forest path from station to Peleș** — 25–30 minutes on the footpath through the forest (passing the Monastery). Beautiful, recommended. Alternative: taxi direct, ~20 RON.

**Sinaia in winter** — the resort operates at full capacity December–March. Book accommodation in advance. Peleș is less crowded in winter (but verify it's open — Tuesday hours may change).
