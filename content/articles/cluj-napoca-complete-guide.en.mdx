Cluj-Napoca is the hardest Romanian city to explain to someone who hasn't been there. It has no iconic castle (Peleș is in Sinaia, Bran is in Bran). No complete medieval centre like Sibiu. No sea or Danube.

What Cluj has is something else — an energy.

<cite index="149-1">Home to Babeș-Bolyai University, one of Romania's largest universities, Cluj-Napoca has grown by assuming that culture, parks and public space belong to everyone.</cite> With 100,000+ students, a startup scene and a booming tech industry, Cluj is Transylvania's most cosmopolitan and most dynamic city — a blend of Gothic and Baroque architecture and Art Nouveau stained glass on the same street as research labs and specialty coffee shops.

<cite index="148-1">World-class festivals: Untold (one of the world's largest electronic music festivals), Electric Castle (in a ruined Baroque castle) and TIFF (International Film Festival).</cite>

And 30 km away: Turda Salt Mine — a medieval salt mine transformed into the most unexpected amusement park on the planet.

## Cluj-Napoca at a Glance — Essential Facts

**County:** Cluj, north-western Transylvania.

**Population:** approximately 325,000 — Romania's second largest city.

**University:** Babeș-Bolyai — Romania's largest university, with 45,000+ students.

**Bilingualism:** Cluj has a significant Hungarian community — bilingual signage, dual Romanian-Hungarian culture.

**Airport:** Cluj Avram Iancu (CLJ) — international, with direct flights across Europe.

**Average budget:** <cite index="150-1">180–320 RON/day (~36–64 EUR) — pricier than Sibiu or Brașov, cheaper than Bucharest.</cite>

## How to Get to Cluj-Napoca

### Direct Flight OTP → CLJ (Recommended)

Wizz Air, Blue Air, TAROM, Ryanair operate flights from Bucharest and several European capitals. Duration OTP → CLJ: ~1 hour. 2026 prices: 50–120 EUR return.

**Airport → centre transfer:**
- **A1E bus** → Unirii Square: 25 minutes, 6.18 RON (~1.25 EUR) — ultra-cheap
- **Bolt/Uber**: 35–50 RON (~7–10 EUR), 15 minutes

## What to See in Cluj-Napoca

### Unirii Square — The Medieval Heart

<cite index="148-1">Unirii Square is the city's centre, with the Gothic St Michael's Church dominating it and the equestrian statue of Matthias Corvinus — King of Hungary, born in Cluj in 1443 — standing in front.</cite>

**St Michael's Church** — one of Transylvania's largest and most impressive Gothic churches, built in the 14th–15th centuries. <cite index="148-1">The 72-metre tower is visible from across the city centre.</cite> Free entry.

**Matthias Corvinus Statue** — one of Romania's most beautiful equestrian statues. Matthias Corvinus, King of Hungary, was born in Cluj (Kolozsvár) in 1443 and remains a symbol of the city's dual Romanian-Hungarian identity.

**Matthias Corvinus House** — a Gothic building near the square where the king is believed to have been born. Viewable from outside, with an accessible inner courtyard.

### Cetățuia — The Hilltop Panorama

<cite index="149-1">Cetățuia is a hill above Cluj with the ruins of a 17th-century Habsburg fortress and a complete panoramic view of the centre, surrounding hills and on clear days toward the Apuseni Mountains.</cite>

Climb: 20–25 minutes on a path from the centre (behind Hotel Continental) or via steps. Free access. Best moment: sunset.

### Central Park (Parcul Iuliu Hațieganu)

<cite index="149-1">Central Park is an elegant Victorian park along the Someșul Mic River, with rowing boats for hire, the casino mirrored in the lake and old chestnut trees.</cite>

A perfect relaxation spot — locals play chess, families come with children, students read. Free.

### Tailors' Bastion and Medieval Walls

<cite index="150-1">The Tailors' Bastion is one of the few remaining segments of Cluj's medieval city wall</cite> — an architectural surprise hidden on Strada Baba Novac, a few minutes from Unirii Square. Touristically overlooked. Free access.

## Day Trip from Cluj: Turda Salt Mine, Corvin Castle and Alba Carolina Fortress

### Turda Salt Mine — The World's Most Unexpected Amusement Park

<cite index="153-1">Turda Salt Mine is a medieval salt mine 30 km from Cluj, exploited since the Middle Ages, transformed in recent decades into a completely unique underground amusement park.</cite>

At 120 metres depth, the enormous salt galleries (appearing white-pinkish in artificial light) house:
- **Panoramic Ferris Wheel** — an 8-metre wheel inside the salt mountain
- **Underground lake rowing boats** — canoeing inside the earth
- **Underground minigolf**
- **Bowling alley**
- **Amphitheatre** for concerts and shows
- **Panoramic lift and stairs** between levels

Constant temperature: 12°C — bring an extra layer regardless of season.

**Ticket:** <cite index="153-1">85 RON (~17 EUR) adult.</cite> Hours: daily 9:00–17:00 (18:00 in season). Online booking recommended at weekends.

**Getting there:** taxi from Cluj (~150–200 RON return with waiting) or guided tour with transport.

### Untold Festival — Cluj Arena, 6–10 August 2026

<cite index="147-1">Untold Festival 2026 returns to Cluj Arena for its 11th edition. It is one of the world's largest electronic music festivals, with 400,000+ attendees from 100+ countries.</cite>

**Ticket:** 165–450 USD (~150–410 EUR) for a 4-day pass, depending on access type.

**Accommodation during Untold:** <cite index="148-1">prices triple during the festival period. Book 3–6 months ahead.</cite> If nothing available in Cluj, Turda or nearby towns are alternatives.

### Electric Castle — Baroque Castle and Music (July)

Electric Castle is a multi-genre festival held at **Bánffy Castle in Bonțida** (15 km from Cluj) — a magnificently ruined Baroque castle as the main stage. More intimate and diverse than Untold.

**Ticket:** ~100–200 EUR for a 5-day pass. Date: third week of July.

## Café Scene and Gastronomy

<cite index="149-1">Cluj-Napoca rivals Vienna for the number of cafés per capita</cite> — whether you prefer specialty coffee, premium teas or concept cafés, Cluj has Romania's densest and most creative scene.

### What You Must Eat

**Varza à la Cluj (Cabbage Cluj-style)** — gratin of pickled cabbage with pork, sour cream and chilli. Cluj's traditional dish. <cite index="149-1">Most authentic address: Vărzărie, Unirii Square — cheap, local, original.</cite> 30–50 RON.

**Kürtőskalács** — present in Cluj thanks to the Hungarian community. 15–25 RON.

**Lángos** — Hungarian fried dough with sour cream or cheese. Classic street food. 10–20 RON.

## Recommended Itinerary — 3 Days in Cluj-Napoca

### Day 1 — Old Town

- **Morning:** Unirii Square, St Michael's Church, Matthias Corvinus Statue and House
- **Lunch:** Vărzărie — Varza à la Cluj (~35 RON)
- **Afternoon:** Tailors' Bastion, Museum Square, National Theatre

- **Day:** tour to Turda Salt Mine (30 km, ~4–5 hours with transport, 85 RON ticket)

## Practical Tips

**A1E bus from the airport** — 6.18 RON (~1.25 EUR) versus a taxi at ~50–80 RON. Goes directly to Unirii Square through the centre.

**Bilingualism** — in Cluj you'll see signs in Romanian and Hungarian. Both communities are present and friendly. Avoid political comments about Hungarian-Romanian relations.

**Tech scene** — Cluj has the highest number of IT companies per capita in Romania. Excellent coworking spaces if you're a digital nomad.

**Untold accommodation** — if going to Untold (6–10 August), book accommodation at least 3–4 months ahead. Prices triple and availability drops fast.
