## What travel insurance covers — in detail

### Standard coverage (present in most policies)

**1. Emergency medical expenses**
- Doctor consultations, ER visits, hospitalization
- Emergency surgical procedures
- Prescribed medications
- Emergency dental treatment (usually with a separate, lower cap — €200-500)
- Typical cap: €30,000-500,000 (depends on plan)

**2. Medical repatriation**
- Air ambulance transport to your home country if your medical condition requires it
- The most expensive covered risk — without insurance it costs €15,000-30,000 from Western Europe, €30,000-80,000 from the USA
- Usually included without a sub-limit (fully covered up to the policy cap)

**3. Repatriation in case of death**
- Transport of mortal remains to the country of origin
- Real cost: €3,000-10,000
- Included in most policies

**4. 24/7 assistance**
- Emergency phone line
- Coordination with local hospitals
- Sending medication or equipment if not available locally
- Administrative support (contacting embassy, family)

### Additional coverage (depends on plan)

**5. Trip cancellation**
- Reimbursement of pre-paid costs (flights, hotels, excursions) if you cancel for covered reasons: illness, accident, death in the family, court summons
- Usually 80-100% of costs, up to a cap (€1,000-5,000)
- **CFAR (Cancel For Any Reason):** the most flexible option — cancel for any reason, get 50-75% back. Costs 40-60% more than the standard policy.

**6. Trip delay**
- Covers accommodation and meals if your flight is delayed over 4-12 hours
- Usually €100-300 per incident
- Check: some policies kick in after 4 hours, others after 12 — huge difference

**7. Lost or delayed baggage**
- Permanent loss: reimbursement up to a cap (€500-2,000)
- Delay: covers first-necessity items purchased (€100-300)
- Important: keep proof of value (receipts, photos)

**8. Third-party liability**
- If you accidentally cause damage to other people or their property
- Usually €50,000-500,000
- Example: you accidentally hit someone with a rented bicycle — insurance covers the damages

**9. Legal assistance**
- Coverage for legal costs abroad
- Usually €3,000-10,000
- Useful if you have a car accident or a legal dispute

**10. Sports and activities**
- "Standard" sports (on-piste skiing, snorkeling, cycling) are usually covered
- "Extreme" sports (parasailing, scuba >18m, bungee, mountaineering) require an additional rider
- Professional sports are never covered

### What insurance does NOT cover — critical exclusions

This is where people lose money. 33% of travel insurance claims are denied. The main exclusions:

| Exclusion | Why it matters | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-existing medical conditions | Diabetes, hypertension, asthma — if not declared, any related claim is denied | Declare EVERYTHING at purchase, request a waiver |
| Extreme sports without rider | Parasailing, scuba >18m, kite, technical climbing | Buy a sports rider for your policy |
| Activities under the influence of alcohol | Accident after a night of drinking = claim denied | If you've been drinking, any medical incident becomes an exclusion |
| Planned/elective treatments | Scheduled surgery, cosmetic procedures, routine dental | Insurance is for EMERGENCIES, not treatments |
| Pregnancy (routine) | Prenatal consultations are not covered | Unexpected complications may be covered (check the policy) |
| Events already known | Named storm before purchase, announced strike | Buy insurance BEFORE the risk appears |
| Conflict/war zones | Travel to areas with travel warnings | Check the geographic exclusion list |
| Illegal activities | Driving without a license, drugs | Obvious, but worth mentioning |
| Undocumented losses | Theft without a police report, luggage without proof | Document EVERYTHING (see claim section) |

**The golden rule:** read the exclusions BEFORE buying the policy. Not AFTER you need it.

## EHIC (European Health Insurance Card) — what it REALLY covers

### What is the EHIC

The European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) is a free document issued by your country's national health service. It gives you the right to medically necessary healthcare during a temporary stay in the EU, EEA (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway), Switzerland, and the UK — under the same conditions as local residents.

### How to get your EHIC

1. Apply online through your national health insurance portal (or in person at your local health insurance office)
2. Free of charge
3. Validity: usually 2-5 years (varies by country)
4. You must be enrolled in your country's public health insurance system
5. Apply at least 2 weeks before departure

### What the EHIC covers — and what it does NOT

| Covered by EHIC | NOT covered by EHIC |
|---|---|
| Emergencies at public hospitals in EU/EEA | Private clinics and doctors |
| Medically necessary consultations in the public system | Medical repatriation (€15,000-30,000) |
| Treatment as locals (including local co-pays) | Repatriation in case of death |
| Prescribed medications in the public system | Mountain rescue / helicopter (€3,500+) |
| | Planned/elective treatments |
| | Trip cancellation, lost luggage |
| | Third-party liability |
| | ANYTHING outside EU/EEA (Turkey, USA, Asia, Egypt) |

### Co-pays you still pay EVEN with the EHIC

"Same as locals" does not mean "free." Each country has its own co-payment system:

| Country | What you pay with EHIC |
|---|---|
| Germany | ~€10/day hospitalization (standard co-pay) |
| Italy | €50-60 for minor emergencies (White/Green code — "ticket di pronto soccorso") |
| France | 20% of hospital costs (ticket modérateur) |
| Spain | €0 for public emergencies (the system is free for emergencies) |
| Austria | Small co-pays on prescriptions |
| Greece | 0-15% co-pay on prescriptions |

### Where the EHIC does NOT work

**The EHIC does not work in:** Turkey, USA, Canada, Mexico, Egypt, Tunisia, Morocco, Thailand, Japan, Australia, and any other country outside the EU/EEA/Switzerland/UK. For these destinations, private travel insurance is your ONLY protection.

### The correct strategy: EHIC + private insurance

It's not "either-or" — it's "both." They complement each other:

- **EHIC** (free) → covers emergencies at public hospitals in the EU, without complicated administrative procedures
- **Private insurance** (€9-20/week) → covers everything EHIC doesn't: repatriation, private clinics, mountain rescue, cancellation, luggage, and works ANYWHERE in the world

With both, you're fully covered. With only EHIC, you're partially covered. Without either, you're on your own.

## Special types of insurance

### Ski and winter sports insurance

Skiing is one of the highest-risk vacation activities for injuries. Without special insurance:

- **On-slope injury + mountain rescue:** €1,000-5,000
- **Helicopter rescue (Austria/Switzerland):** €3,500-18,000
- **Hospitalization + knee surgery:** €5,000-20,000

**What your ski policy must include:**
- On-piste accident coverage
- Off-piste coverage (if you plan to — some policies exclude off-piste)
- Mountain rescue / search and rescue / helicopter
- Lost/stolen ski equipment (usually €500-1,500)
- On-slope third-party liability (if you hit someone)

**Additional cost:** a sports rider typically adds 20-50% to the base policy price.

### Insurance during pregnancy

Routine pregnancy (prenatal consultations, routine ultrasounds) is NOT covered by travel insurance. However:

- **Unexpected complications** (premature birth, spontaneous miscarriage) CAN be covered, depending on the policy
- **Week limit:** most policies cover complications up to 24-28 weeks of pregnancy (EKTA covers up to 27 weeks)
- **Check explicitly:** "complications of pregnancy" must be mentioned in the policy

**Tip for pregnant travelers:** if traveling at >20 weeks, verify that the policy covers pregnancy complications, that you have a letter from your doctor confirming you can travel, and that your destination has access to a maternity ward.

### Insurance for pre-existing conditions

Pre-existing conditions (diabetes, hypertension, asthma, cancer in remission, heart problems) are the most common cause of denied claims.

**How it works:**
1. When purchasing the policy, **declare all existing medical conditions**
2. The insurer evaluates them and either accepts them (with or without an additional premium), or explicitly excludes them
3. If you do NOT declare and have an incident related to a pre-existing condition, the claim is **automatically denied**

**Pre-existing conditions waiver:** some insurers offer a waiver if you purchase the policy within 10-21 days of your first trip payment. EKTA and other international providers have explicit options for pre-existing conditions.

**Tip:** declare EVERYTHING, even if you think it's not relevant. It's better to pay a slightly higher premium than to have a €20,000 claim denied.

### Insurance for travelers over 65

- Premiums increase significantly with age (2-3x compared to 30-year-olds)
- Some insurers don't cover people over 75-80
- Pre-existing conditions are more common, making declaration even more important
- EKTA insurance: covers up to advanced ages, check the conditions

### Insurance for long trips (backpacking, digital nomads)

If you're traveling for more than 30-45 days:
- Single trip policies usually cover a maximum of 90-180 days
- Annual policies have a per-trip limit of 25-45 days — not suitable!
- **Solution:** long-stay policy or dedicated travel medical insurance (EKTA, SafetyWing, World Nomads)

Family packages (2 adults + 2-3 children) are usually 15-20% cheaper than separate individual policies.

What to check additionally:
- Are children under 2 included? (usually yes, free or very cheap)
- Age limit for children (16, 18, or 21 depends on the insurer)

## How to file a claim — step by step (without getting denied)

33% of travel insurance claims are denied. The main reason: lack of documentation. Here's how to avoid that:

### Before an incident: preparation

1. **Save the insurer's emergency number** in your phone, in Notes, and on paper. Not just in contacts — if your phone is lost, you need the number.
2. **Save a copy of the policy** in email, cloud (Google Drive, iCloud), and printed.
3. **Photograph important documents:** passport, insurance card, flight tickets, hotel confirmations. You want these accessible even if your phone disappears.

### During an incident: what to do

**Step 1: Call the insurer IMMEDIATELY**
- The 24/7 emergency line, BEFORE going to the doctor if possible
- The insurer can direct you to a partner hospital/clinic (direct payment between insurer and hospital, no advance from your pocket)
- If it's an absolute emergency, go to the nearest hospital — call the insurer after

**Step 2: Document EVERYTHING**
- Keep ALL original invoices (hospital, medications, taxi, extra hotel)
- Request the detailed medical report (diagnosis, treatment, prescriptions)
- Photograph everything (invoices, prescriptions, reports, ER wristbands)
- For theft: file a police report (MANDATORY — without a police report, the theft claim is automatically denied)
- For lost luggage: PIR report from the airline + proof of contents

**Step 3: Don't throw anything away**
- No invoice, no prescription, no receipt
- Even the taxi receipt from the airport to the hospital is reimbursable

### After an incident: filing the claim

1. **Complete the claim form** on the insurer's website (deadline: usually 30-90 days from the incident)
2. **Attach documents:** original invoices (or quality scans), medical report, police report (if theft), proof of payment
3. **Translations:** some insurers require certified translations of medical documents
4. **Payment:** usually within 14-30 business days after claim acceptance

### Most common reasons for denial

| Reason | % of denials | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Missing documentation | ~35% | Keep EVERYTHING, photograph, file police report |
| Undeclared pre-existing condition | ~25% | Declare ALL conditions at purchase |
| Uncovered activity (extreme sport) | ~15% | Check exclusions, buy sports rider |
| Incident under influence of alcohol | ~10% | Obvious — but many underestimate |
| Policy expired/wasn't active | ~8% | Check dates precisely — include transit days |
| Late claim filing | ~7% | File within the deadline (30-90 days) |

## Schengen visa — insurance requirements

If you're a non-EU citizen and need a Schengen visa (short-stay), travel insurance is **mandatory**. Requirements (EU Regulation 810/2009):

- **Minimum medical coverage:** €30,000
- **Validity:** entire duration of stay + a few extra days (buffer)
- **Geographic coverage:** all Schengen countries visited
- **Must include:** medical emergencies, hospitalization, medical repatriation, repatriation in case of death
- **No deductible** at some embassies (check specific requirements)

## 10 common mistakes (and how to avoid them)

1. **Traveling without insurance because you have an EHIC.** The EHIC doesn't cover repatriation, private clinics, and doesn't work outside the EU. A few euros per day covers you completely.

2. **Not declaring pre-existing conditions.** Any claim related to an undeclared condition is automatically denied. Declare EVERYTHING.

3. **Not reading the exclusions.** You buy a policy, go parasailing, get injured — and discover that extreme sports aren't covered. Read BEFORE.

4. **Not calling the insurer before going to the doctor.** If you call first, the insurer can send you to a partner hospital (no advance payment). If you go directly, you pay out of pocket and file a claim later (more complicated, slower).

5. **Not keeping original invoices.** Without invoices, the claim is denied. Photograph EVERYTHING, keep EVERYTHING.

6. **Buying the CHEAPEST policy without checking the deductible.** A 25% deductible on a €10,000 bill = you pay €2,500 out of pocket. The price difference for a 0% deductible is a few euros per day.

7. **Not filing a police report for theft.** Without a report, the theft/loss claim is automatically denied. For airport baggage theft: PIR report from the airline + police report.

8. **Buying insurance AFTER booking everything.** Some cancellation policies have a deadline — they must be purchased within 10-21 days of the first trip payment. Buy early.

9. **Assuming your policy covers scooter/motorcycle.** Most policies do NOT cover scooter accidents if you don't have a valid motorcycle license. In Southeast Asia, this is a very common trap.

10. **Ignoring transit days.** Your policy must cover ALL days, including transit days. If your flight is on the 15th and the policy starts on the 16th, the flight day is not covered.

## Travel insurance vs. bank card insurance

Many premium cards (Revolut Metal, N26 Metal, Visa Gold/Platinum cards) include some form of travel insurance. But be careful:

| Aspect | Dedicated travel insurance | Premium card insurance |
|---|---|---|
| Medical coverage | €30,000-500,000 | Usually €10,000-50,000 |
| Repatriation | Yes, usually without sub-limit | Sometimes included, sometimes not |
| Trip cancellation | Optional, €1,000-5,000 | Usually €1,000-2,500 |
| Luggage | €500-2,000 | €500-1,000 |
| Sports | With additional rider | Usually only "standard" sports |
| Conditions | Dedicated policy, clear documentation | Automatic activation if you paid for the trip with the card |
| Claims | Direct with insurer, clear deadline | Through the bank, may be slower |

**The bottom line:** card insurance is a nice-to-have bonus, but rarely replaces a dedicated policy. Check what your card actually covers BEFORE relying on it.

## Questions people Google (and direct answers)

**Can I buy insurance AFTER I've departed?**
Yes, from some providers (EKTA allows purchase in the first days of travel). But it's more expensive and you may have a waiting period (24-72h during which the policy isn't active). Ideal: buy BEFORE.

**Does insurance cover COVID?**
Yes, with most providers in 2026 (EKTA explicitly covers COVID — treatment + isolation up to 15 days). Check the specific policy.

**Does insurance cover incidents if I've been drinking?**
No. Incidents under the influence of alcohol are excluded by virtually all insurers. If the medical report mentions alcohol, the claim is denied.

**What happens if the hospital requires an advance payment?**
Call the insurer — they can usually arrange direct payment with the hospital (guarantee of payment). If you pay out of pocket, keep the invoices and file a claim afterward.

**Does insurance cover a companion?**
Some premium policies cover the accommodation and transport of a companion if you're hospitalized (e.g., a parent staying with a child). Check explicitly for "accommodation of accompanying person."

**Can I file a claim WITHOUT original invoices?**
Very difficult. Most insurers require originals or certified copies. Quality scans/photos are accepted by some. Rule: keep EVERYTHING.

**Does insurance work if I travel to a country with a travel warning?**
It depends. Many policies exclude areas with a level 4 warning (Do Not Travel). Check the exclusion list and your country's travel advisories before departure.

## When you DON'T need travel insurance

Let's be fair — there are situations where insurance is less critical:

- **Domestic travel (within your own country):** you have national health coverage, ambulance is free, hospitalization is free
- **Short city break (2-3 days) in an EU country with EHIC:** the financial risk is limited if you're not doing sports
- **You already have verified premium card insurance:** if the coverage is sufficient and includes repatriation

But even in these cases, the price of a basic policy (€3-5/day) is so small that it's hard to justify not having one. It's the peace of mind you buy for the price of a coffee.
