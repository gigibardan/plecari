There is a moment on the Budva Riviera — usually in May or September, when the water temperature is 22°C, the beaches aren't yet crowded and the sun descends behind the mountains as the Old Town lights up — when you understand why Montenegrins say they have created the best place in the world to live.

Budva is not subtle. It is one of the most direct tourist destinations on the Mediterranean: spectacular beaches a few minutes from a Venetian medieval town, hotels in all categories, seafood restaurants at half the price of Dubrovnik, and a nightlife that puts many European cities in the shade.

<cite index="120-1">Budva is Montenegro's top coastal destination — a 2,500-year-old walled town backed by Adriatic beaches, with a Riviera stretching 35 km south toward the Albanian border.</cite>

What makes it different from any other Mediterranean resort: 30 minutes north lies UNESCO Kotor, 6 km south lies Sveti Stefan — the fairytale island — and the entire Montenegrin coast has the accessibility and prices of a place that hasn't yet reached full tourist maturity.

## Budva at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Montenegro — not in the EU, not in Schengen. Uses the euro.

**Population:** approximately 20,000 permanent residents — 200,000+ at peak season (July–August).

**Currency:** Euro (EUR) — no currency exchange needed.

**Nearest airport:** Tivat (TIV) — 20 km, 20–25 minutes. Second: Podgorica (TGD) — 65 km, 1 hour. Third: Dubrovnik (DBV, Croatia) — 90 km, 1.5–2 hours.

**Visa:** EU citizens do not need a visa for Montenegro — entry with a valid ID card.

**Main season:** May–October. Budva outside season (November–April) is a quiet small town — some hotels and restaurants are closed.

## How to Get from Romania to Budva

### Option 1 — Flight OTP → Tivat (Direct, Seasonal)

Wizz Air operates OTP → TIV flights in season (May–September). Duration: ~1h30. Transfer Tivat → Budva: taxi (~15 EUR, 20 min) or local minibus (~5 EUR, 30 min).

### Option 2 — Flight OTP → Dubrovnik + Transfer (Year-Round)

<cite index="120-1">Budva is 90 km from Dubrovnik — 1.5–2 hours by car or minibus.</cite> Flight OTP → DBV (Wizz Air/Ryanair): year-round, from 50 EUR return. Transfer Dubrovnik → Budva: minibus (~15–20 EUR, 2h), private taxi (~80–100 EUR). Advantage: you can combine Dubrovnik + Kotor + Budva in a single circuit.

### Option 3 — By Car

Bucharest → Budva: ~1,000 km, 10–11 hours (via Serbia or Bulgaria). Good for a Balkans road trip: Bucharest → Belgrade → Kotor → Budva → Dubrovnik.

## Budva's Beaches — Complete Guide

### Mogren I and II — The Most Photogenic

<cite index="119-1">Mogren consists of two sand and pebble beaches connected by a short tunnel, approximately 100 metres west of the Old Town.</cite> They are surrounded by white-gold limestone cliffs, with water of an intense turquoise.

Mogren II (accessible only through Mogren I) is smaller and more secluded — one of the most beautiful small beaches on the Adriatic. Access: free, 10 minutes' walk from the Old Town. <cite index="124-1">Sunbeds: 10–20 EUR per pair.</cite>

### Slovenska Plaža — The Main Beach

The 1.5 km long beach in front of the town — the classic zone for beach bars, terraces and water activities. <cite index="125-1">Access is free, with a free section along the entire length.</cite> <cite index="124-1">Sunbeds: 15–25 EUR per pair in the organised area.</cite>

### Bečići — The Most Awarded

3 km south of Budva, Bečići is a 2 km fine-sand beach, awarded the Blue Flag and considered one of the best beaches in the Mediterranean. <cite index="124-1">Sunbeds: 10–15 EUR per pair — the best value in the region.</cite> Bus: 1.50–2 EUR.

### Jaz Beach — For Quiet and Festivals

2 km west of Budva, Jaz is a larger, quieter beach outside festival season. Host of the Sea Dance Festival in July–August. Access by bus or on foot (~25 minutes).

### Sveti Stefan Beach — The Postcard Image

<cite index="126-1">6 km south of Budva, the public beach on the southern coast of Sveti Stefan island is free — sunbeds on the organised section at ~15 EUR per person.</cite> The combination of the island's pink rock behind you and the turquoise Adriatic ahead is one of Montenegro's most photographed images.

## The Old Town (Stari Grad) — The Medieval Heart

<cite index="118-1">Budva's Stari Grad is surrounded by medieval walls, with narrow cobblestone streets and Venetian houses with red-tiled roofs.</cite> Its history goes back to antiquity — an Illyrian and Greek centre that became a Roman, Venetian, Ottoman and Austrian fortress.

**The Citadel** — the 15th-century fortress at the top of the Old Town, with spectacular views over the beaches and sea. Entry: 5 EUR.

**The Ballerina Monument** — the bronze statue of a ballerina on the stone at the end of the walls, with Mogren beach behind — Budva's most photographed spot. Free.

**Main Square** — in the centre of the Old Town, with three churches built side by side: the Roman Catholic Cathedral of Saint John (7th century), the Orthodox Church of the Holy Trinity (1804) and the Chapel of Santa Maria in Punta (840 AD). Free.

**The seawall promenade** — the pedestrian path along the Old Town walls toward the small Ričardova Glava cove — a direct water access point directly below the walls.

## Sveti Stefan — The Luxury Destination You Can See for Free

<cite index="116-1">Sveti Stefan is a small village on an island connected to the mainland by a narrow isthmus, 6 km south of Budva.</cite> The island belongs to the Aman Resort — one of the world's most expensive hotel groups (rooms from 1,000 EUR per night).

But you don't need to be an Aman guest to enjoy Sveti Stefan. Three ways to experience it free or cheaply:

**1. The road viewpoint** — the best viewpoint is from the main road above the island. 10–15 minute stop, possible parking, perfect photograph. <cite index="126-1">Best time: sunset (19:00–20:00 in summer) for golden hour light.</cite>

**2. The southern public beach** — <cite index="116-1">the Sveti Stefan beach on the south side normalised access in 2026, with the public sector available from July.</cite>

**3. The bus** — <cite index="126-1">the Budva–Sveti Stefan bus costs 1.50–2.50 EUR and takes 25–40 minutes.</cite> You can go, photograph the viewpoint and return in half a day.

## Nightlife — Budva After Dark

Budva's nightlife is layered into three distinct levels:

**Level 1 — Bars and lounges in Stari Grad** — the most atmospheric. Terraces on the medieval walls, live music, cocktails with sea views. Prices: cocktail 8–15 EUR.

**Level 2 — Beach bars on Slovenska Plaža** — <cite index="129-1">more relaxed, natural transition from beach to bar without cover charge. Daytime lounges that become social spots in the evening.</cite> Beer: 3–5 EUR.

**Level 3 — Open-air clubs** — <cite index="129-1">Top Hill is Budva's largest summer club, with international DJs, open May–September, entry 15–30 EUR, running until 6 AM.</cite> A shuttle bus runs from the main seafront on club nights.

**Sea Dance Festival** (Jaz Beach, July–August): <cite index="128-1">one of the Balkans' largest electronic festivals, with 20,000+ daily attendees and international acts.</cite>

## Food and Drink

### What to Eat

**Fresh fish** — at any konoba by the sea. Sea bass, dorada, branzino on the grill with lemon and olive oil. <cite index="123-1">A seafood dinner for two at a good restaurant rarely tops 30–35 EUR.</cite> Always ask the price per kg before ordering.

**Shrimp buzara** — shrimps in garlic and white wine sauce. 12–18 EUR.

**Black risotto (crni rižoto)** — with cuttlefish ink. Adriatic speciality. 10–15 EUR.

**Njeguški cheese and pršut** — smoked cheese and ham from the Montenegrin mountains. Classic aperitif with local wine. 6–10 EUR.

### Where to Eat

**Essential tip:** <cite index="127-1">some restaurants in Stari Grad place unrequested bread or appetisers on the table and charge for them on the bill. Ask first or send them back immediately.</cite>

**Better value** — go to restaurants on the promenade between Old Town and Bečići or to konobes in the peripheral neighbourhoods (Partizanski put) for the same menu at 20–30% less.

**Local beer** — Nikšićko pivo is Montenegro's national beer. At a bar: 3–4.50 EUR a pint.

**Local wine** — Vranac (robust red) and Krstač (white). 3–6 EUR a glass.

## Day Trips from Budva

### Kotor (30 minutes, essential)

<cite index="120-1">Kotor is a UNESCO World Heritage bay town, 30 minutes by car from Budva.</cite> The fortress walls with 1,350 steps, baroque Perast and Our Lady of the Rocks — all accessible in a single day. Read our complete Kotor guide.

### Dubrovnik, Croatia (90 km, 1.5–2h)

<cite index="137-1">The Budva–Dubrovnik road passes through the Verige ferry crossing and continues along the Croatian coast.</cite> A full day, 90 km, Montenegro–Croatia border. Guided tours from Budva: 25–40 EUR.

### Lovćen National Park and Cetinje (1 hour)

Cetinje is Montenegro's former royal capital and Lovćen National Park holds the Njegoš Mausoleum — Montenegro's most spectacular viewpoint at 1,660 m.

### Skadar Lake (1.5 hours)

The Balkans' largest lake, with pelicans, fishing villages and a landscape unlike anything on the coast. Boat tours on the lake: 20–40 EUR.

## Recommended Itinerary — 3 Days in Budva

### Day 1 — Stari Grad and Central Beaches

- **Morning:** Stari Grad — Citadel (5 EUR), square with three churches, Ballerina Monument
- **10:30–13:00:** Mogren I and II beaches (10 minutes' walk from Old Town)
- **Lunch:** konoba in the Old Town or on the promenade
- **Afternoon:** Slovenska Plaža, water activities or relaxation
- **Evening:** bars in Stari Grad, seafood dinner

### Day 2 — Sveti Stefan and Bečići

- **Morning:** bus to Sveti Stefan (~2 EUR, 30 min) — viewpoint, public beach

- **Option A:** excursion to Kotor (bus 3–5 EUR, 35 min) — fortress with 1,350 steps, Perast, Our Lady of the Rocks
- **Option B:** boat tour along the Budva coast (3–5 hours, 25–45 EUR) — sea caves, inaccessible beaches, Sveti Nikola Island
- **Evening:** farewell dinner in the Old Town

## Practical Tips

**Parking** in peak season is a serious problem. <cite index="125-1">Paid parking in Budva: 2–4 EUR per hour. Free parking near Hotel Mediteran fills before 10:00 on peak days.</cite> If possible, arrive without a car and use local buses.

**The Mediteran Express bus** runs every 15–30 minutes along the Riviera (Budva–Bečići–Przno–Sveti Stefan). <cite index="124-1">Price: ~1–2 EUR per trip.</cite> The most convenient way to explore the coastline.

**Unlicensed taxis** — <cite index="127-1">taxis without official roof numbers or visible meters frequently overcharge tourists. Verify the meter starts from the 1 EUR base fare.</cite> Use Bolt where available or taxis with displayed rates.

**Unrequested bread and appetisers** — <cite index="127-1">some restaurants in Stari Grad place unordered items on the table and charge them on the bill. Send them back if you don't want them, or ask the price first.</cite>

**Insurance** — Montenegro is not in the EU. EHIC is not valid. You need insurance that explicitly covers Montenegro.

## Budva vs. Kotor — Which Do You Choose?

| Criterion | Budva | Kotor |
|---|---|---|
| **Beaches** | Excellent, varied | Limited (bay, not sand beaches) |
| **Nightlife** | Legendary, large clubs | Moderate, bars in Stari Grad |
| **Medieval authenticity** | Present but more touristy | Superior, UNESCO |
| **Prices** | Similar | Similar |
| **Scenery** | Adriatic Riviera | Fjord-like bay |
| **Excursions** | Sveti Stefan, Dubrovnik | Perast, Submarine Base, Lovćen |
| **Best for** | Beaches + evenings + party | Medieval + nature + quiet |

**The perfect combination:** stay in Budva (2–3 nights) and spend a day in Kotor. Or the reverse.
