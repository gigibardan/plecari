If Tallinn is the Baltic capital of perfectly preserved medieval architecture and Riga is the Art Nouveau capital, Vilnius is something entirely different — and in the opinion of many travellers who have seen all three, it is the most interesting of them.

Not because it's the largest or most famous. But because Vilnius has the largest Baroque Old Town in Eastern Europe (UNESCO-listed since 1994), an artistic quarter that declared independence from the rest of the world, a museum installed in the former KGB building with original detention cells, a medieval castle on an island in the middle of a lake 45 minutes away, and national dishes that keep you lingering longer than planned.

Add to this prices that make Vilnius one of Europe's most affordable capitals, and an atmosphere that remains genuinely unrushed — despite growing tourist numbers.

---

## Vilnius at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Lithuania, EU member since 2004, Schengen Area, Eurozone (since 2015).

**Population:** approximately 580,000 — the largest Baltic capital after Riga.

**Currency:** Euro (EUR) — no exchange needed.

**Language:** Lithuanian is the official language. English is spoken well in the tourist area, especially by younger generations. Russian is understood by many older residents.

**UNESCO:** Old Town (Senamiestis) — listed since 1994, the largest Baroque ensemble in Eastern Europe.

**Direct flights from Bucharest:** Wizz Air, OTP→VNO, ~2h, from 35 EUR.

**Average budget:** 55–75 EUR/day (mid-range) — among Europe's cheapest capitals.

### Direct Flight (Recommended)

Wizz Air operates direct flights Bucharest (OTP) → Vilnius (VNO) several times a week. Duration: approximately 2 hours.

**Indicative prices 2026:**
- Early booking (6–8 weeks ahead): 35–55 EUR return
- Mid-range (2–4 weeks): 65–95 EUR return
- Peak season (July–August): 100–160 EUR return

**Airport → city centre transfer:**
- **Bus no. 1** → Cathedral Square: 20 minutes, 1 EUR — cheapest option
- **Bolt/Uber**: 8–12 EUR, 15–20 minutes
- **Airport taxis**: acceptable in Vilnius (no Riga-style scam), but verify it's metered or use an app

## What to See in Vilnius

### The Old Town (Senamiestis) — The Largest Baroque Old Town in Eastern Europe

Senamiestis covers 3.6 km² — enormous compared to the Old Towns of Tallinn or Riga. It was built primarily in the Baroque style, under the influence of Jesuit religious orders who transformed Vilnius into a major Catholic centre of Northern Europe in the 17th and 18th centuries.

**Pilies gatvė (Castle Street)** is the main artery — the most animated street in the Old Town, with amber shops, cafés, restaurants and galleries. It runs from Cathedral Square up to Gediminas Tower.

**Cathedral Square (Katedros aikštė)** — the city's centre, with the Cathedral of St Stanislav and St Vladislav (neoclassical exterior, Baroque interior), a separate Bell Tower, and a lucky tile "Stebuklas" (Miracle) set into the pavement. According to tradition, spin on the spot while making a wish and it will come true. Whether you do this is entirely up to you.

**Vilnius University (Vilniaus universitetas)** — one of the oldest universities in Northern Europe, founded in 1579 by Jesuits. The campus in the city centre conceals 13 interconnected courtyards, each with distinct architecture and atmosphere. Enter through the main gate on Pilies gatvė. Access to the courtyards: free.

**Gate of Dawn (Aušros Vartai)** — the only medieval gateway remaining from the city walls. It houses the icon of Our Lady of the Gate of Dawn, considered miraculous, which attracts pilgrims from across Central and Eastern Europe. The inner chapel can be visited for free.

**St Anne's Church (Šv. Onos bažnyčia)** — the Gothic church that captivated Napoleon when he saw it in 1812. It is said he wanted to carry it back to Paris in his palm. It is built from 33 different types of red brick — the exterior is free to admire.

### Gediminas Tower — Vilnius's Classic Panorama

Gediminas Tower is Lithuania's symbol — a 14th-century brick fortress on the hill dominating the Old Town and the confluence of the Neris and Vilnia rivers. From the top, the panorama takes in the Baroque rooftops of Senamiestis, church spires, and in the distance, the Soviet-era apartment blocks of the outer neighbourhoods — a visual contradiction that says everything about Lithuania's 20th-century history.

**How to get there:** on foot (15-minute mountain trail) or by **funicular** (3 EUR return). Tower entry: 5 EUR adults, 2.50 EUR students.

**Tip:** go early morning (at opening, 10:00) or in the evening before closing (20:00 in summer) for the best light and fewer visitors.

### The Republic of Užupis — Vilnius's Montmartre

Užupis is the quarter that makes Vilnius completely special. Separated from the rest of the city by a small river (Vilnia) and accessible mainly through a single bridge, Užupis symbolically declared independence on **1 April 1997**.

It has:
- **Its own constitution** displayed on a wall in 25 languages. Notable articles: "A person has the right to be happy", "A person has the right to be unhappy", "A dog has the right to be a dog"
- **Its own president** — artist Romas Lileikis
- **Its own anthem** and **its own passport** (available for a fee at a café)
- **Border guards** checking passports on 1 April

Užupis today is a neighbourhood of artists' studios, galleries, independent cafés and a genuinely bohemian atmosphere. It's not touristy in the negative sense — locals actually live and work there.

**Don't miss:** the bronze angel on the central column (the Republic's symbol), the riverside studios and Bernardinų sodas (Bernardine Garden) bordering the district.

Entry: free at all times.

### Palace of the Grand Dukes of Lithuania

Rebuilt and reopened in 2018, the Palace of the Grand Dukes was the centre of power of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania — one of the largest powers in medieval Europe (at its height, the Grand Duchy stretched from the Baltic Sea to the Black Sea). The palace had been demolished by the Russians in 1801; the reconstruction was based on historical documents, archaeological excavations and comparisons with contemporary palaces.

Entry: ~10 EUR. Hours: Tuesday–Sunday 10:00–18:00.

### Museum of Occupations and Freedom Fights (the KGB Museum)

Informally known as the **KGB Museum**, this is installed in the very building where the Soviet KGB conducted its operations between 1940 and 1991. The basement preserves the original detention cells, the soundproofed isolation cell, and the execution chamber.

It is one of the most unsettling and important cultural experiences in the Baltic states — less visited than the Occupation Museum in Riga, but equally powerful. Allow at least 1h30.

**Entry:** 6 EUR adults. Free on Sundays.

**Note:** emotionally intense content — not recommended for young children.

## Day Trip to Trakai Castle — Vilnius's Most Popular Excursion

Trakai Castle is Lithuania's postcard image. Built in red brick on an island in the middle of Lake Galvė, 28 km from Vilnius, it is one of the most photogenic medieval castles in Northern Europe.

Grand Duke Vytautas the Great expanded it to its current dimensions in the 15th century — and died inside it in 1430. Today it houses the Trakai History Museum, with exhibitions on the Grand Duchy.

**Practical details:**
- **Getting there:** bus from Vilnius (~45 minutes, 2 EUR return) or guided minibus tour (~35–50 EUR including guided tour)
- **Castle entry:** 12 EUR adults (May–September) / 10 EUR (October–April)
- **Hours:** daily 10:00–19:00 (summer) / 10:00–17:00 (winter)
- **Recommended time:** 2–3 hours for castle + village

**Don't leave without kibinai.** Kibinai are crescent-shaped pastries filled with meat (lamb or pork), the speciality of the Karaim community — a Turkic-Mongol people brought to Lithuania by the Grand Duke in the 14th century. Find them at traditional shops in the village: 2–3 EUR each. They are remarkably good.

## Food and Drink — Vilnius Gastronomy Guide

### What You Must Eat

**Cepelinai** — the national dumpling, made from potato dough, filled with pork or cottage cheese, served with sour cream and bacon. A portion of 2: 4–8 EUR. Filling, hearty, delicious. Do not leave Vilnius without eating them.

**Kibinai** — Karaim crescent pastries with meat, classic in Trakai, also available in Vilnius. 2–3 EUR each.

**Šaltibarščiai** — cold beetroot soup, served in summer with boiled potatoes or boiled eggs. The bright pink colour surprises you; the refreshing taste wins you over. 1.50–4 EUR.

**Keptas duona** — fried black rye bread with garlic and cheese — the classic bar snack with a local beer. 3–5 EUR.

**Kugelis** — baked potato pudding, served with sour cream. Rustic comfort food. 4–7 EUR.

### Where to Eat

**Budget (6–12 EUR/meal):**
- **Šnekutis** — local pub with traditional Lithuanian food, frequented by locals, large portions, authentic atmosphere. At Užupio g. 9
- **Halės Turgus** (Hales Market) — the city's covered market, with food stalls, local cheeses and smoked meats

**Mid-range (12–25 EUR/person):**
- **Etno Dvaras** — traditional Lithuanian food in elegant rustic décor, in the Old Town
- **Bernelių Užeiga** — medieval inn atmosphere, complete Lithuanian menu, live music at weekends

**Fine dining:** Vilnius has Michelin-recognised restaurants — Džiaugsmas, Nineteen18, Sweet Root. Book 2–3 weeks ahead.

### What to Drink

**Lithuanian beer** — one of the most diverse in Eastern Europe. Brands: Švyturys, Utenos, Kalnapilis. At a bar: 2.50–3.50 EUR for a 0.5L pint.

**Midus (Mead)** — Lithuanian traditional fermented honey drink. Find it at traditional restaurants and souvenir shops. 3–6 EUR a glass.

**Coffee** — Vilnius has a surprisingly good third-wave coffee scene. Flat white: 3–4 EUR.

## Practical Tips for Vilnius

**Baltic amber** — Vilnius is actually a better place to buy authentic amber than Riga. Go to shops behind Pilies gatvė, not street vendors. Fair price: 5–30 EUR for simple jewellery, 50–200 EUR for pieces with inclusions (fossil insects in amber).

**The Užupis Constitution** — read it in full. It is a work of philosophical art — funny and profound at the same time. The English version is on the wall.

**Public transport** is good and cheap. Ticket: 1 EUR by card, 0.65 EUR if paid electronically in advance. Most useful lines: bus 1 (airport-centre), trams through the centre.

**Language** — try at least "ačiū" (thank you, pronounced "ačiu") — Lithuanians appreciate any effort. English works well everywhere in the tourist area.

**Safety** — Vilnius is safe. There are no significant scams like those in Riga. Watch out for unlicensed taxis at the airport (use Bolt) and pickpockets in crowds.
