There are well-preserved medieval cities in Europe — and then there is Tallinn. The difference is that Tallinn doesn't feel preserved; it simply feels intact. As if someone built a fairytale city in the 15th century and left it functioning without touching anything essential.

Gothic towers rise above medieval walls. The limestone cobblestones bear the marks of cart wheels. Merchant houses with coloured façades line the central square unchanged for centuries. Cats sleep on warm stone windowsills. <cite index="75-1">The entire medieval centre covers just 113 hectares — you can walk Vanalinn from one end to the other in 20 minutes.</cite>

But Tallinn is not a frozen museum. It is also the capital of a state that reinvented itself after 1991 — the first country in the world to vote electronically, the global leader in e-governance, the birthplace of Skype. Below the medieval walls operates a start-up ecosystem that has produced more unicorns per capita than any other European country.

And two hours by ferry lies Helsinki — a combination not to be missed if you're in the area.

## Tallinn at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Estonia — EU member since 2004, Schengen Area, Eurozone (since 2011).

**Population:** approximately 430,000 — the smallest Baltic capital by area but the most tourist-dense.

**Currency:** Euro (EUR). Estonia is one of the world's most cashless societies — cards are accepted almost everywhere.

**Language:** Estonian is the official language (a Finno-Ugric language, related to Finnish and Hungarian, unrelated to Slavic or Germanic languages). English is spoken excellently, especially by younger generations. Russian is understood by many (30% of the population has Russian roots).

**UNESCO:** Old Town (Vanalinn) — World Heritage Site since 1997.

**Direct flights from Bucharest:** Wizz Air, OTP→TLL, ~2h30, from 40 EUR.

**Average budget:** <cite index="83-1">50–80 EUR/day (mid-range) — 30–50% cheaper than Nordic capitals, similar to Berlin.</cite>

## What to See in Tallinn

### The Old Town (Vanalinn) — Perfect Medieval

<cite index="78-1">Vanalinn is one of Europe's best-preserved medieval city centres and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.</cite> It divides into two distinct zones:

**Lower Town (Allminn)** — the merchants' quarter, with Town Hall Square (Raekoja plats), Hanseatic houses, Gothic churches and narrow lanes.

**Toompea (Cathedral Hill)** — the aristocracy and power zone, with Toompea Castle (today Estonia's Parliament), Alexander Nevsky Cathedral and the most spectacular panoramas of the city.

**Raekoja plats (Town Hall Square)** — the centre of medieval Tallinn, flanked by 15th-century Hanseatic houses. <cite index="74-1">Tallinn Town Hall — standard ticket 7 EUR, open June–August.</cite> In December, this square hosts one of Europe's most beautiful Christmas markets.

**St Olaf's Church Tower (Oleviste kirik)** — at 124 metres, it was once the world's tallest building (16th century). <cite index="74-1">Climb the tower for exceptional panoramic views: ticket 8 EUR, open seasonally.</cite>

**Kiek in de Kök and Bastion Tunnels** — a 15th-century defensive tower with museum and a system of underground tunnels dug into the limestone bedrock, used in the city's defence. <cite index="75-1">Combined ticket: ~14 EUR, 60–90 minutes of exploration — one of Tallinn's best-value paid experiences.</cite>

**St Catherine's Passage (Katariina käik)** — a narrow medieval alley between two streets, with craftsmen's workshops (ceramics, jewellery, textiles) and cats sleeping on workshop doorsteps. One of Tallinn's most photographed spots, free.

### Toompea — The Hill with Views of All Tallinn

Toompea is the limestone hill dominating the Old Town, with three panoramic observation platforms and a completely different architecture from the Lower Town.

**Kohtuotsa vaateplats** and **Patkuli vaateplats** — the two main platforms. <cite index="77-1">Both are free and open around the clock. Early morning (before 8:00) or at sunset are the perfect moments — soft light over the red rooftops of the Lower Town.</cite>

**Alexander Nevsky Cathedral** — the Russian Orthodox cathedral with onion-shaped domes, built in 1900 as a symbol of Russian dominance. <cite index="74-1">Entry is free; modest dress code required.</cite>

**Toompea Castle** — the seat of Estonia's Parliament (Riigikogu), not accessible to the public, but the exterior with Hermann Tower and the Estonian flag is spectacular.

### The KGB Museum at Hotel Viru — Tallinn's Most Fascinating Museum

Hotel Viru was built in 1972 as the first "Western" hotel in Soviet Estonia — aimed at Finnish tourists and other Western visitors. <cite index="95-1">On the 23rd floor — the secret floor, not listed in the lift — the KGB installed surveillance equipment to monitor guests.</cite>

Today, the 23rd floor is a guided museum: original listening equipment, documents discovered after independence, and the extraordinary story of a hotel that was simultaneously a Soviet showcase and a spy station. <cite index="95-1">Ticket: 15 EUR; guided tour mandatory, duration 45 minutes. Booking recommended 2–3 days ahead in season.</cite>

### Telliskivi — Bohemian and Creative Tallinn

<cite index="80-1">Telliskivi Creative City is a converted industrial district turned cultural space — Tallinn's most vibrant alternative neighbourhood.</cite> A 10-minute walk from Vanalinn, Telliskivi brings together third-wave cafés, concept restaurants, art galleries, vintage shops and creative studios.

**Balti Jaam Market (Balti Jaama turg)** — the covered market next to the old railway station, with street food stalls, second-hand and local products. Open daily.

**Weekend:** Telliskivi Flea Market on Saturdays and Sundays — one of the best in the Baltic states.

<cite index="81-1">Prices in Telliskivi are 20–30% lower than in the Old Town for the same quality.</cite>

### Kadriorg Park and Palace

<cite index="80-1">Kadriorg Park is a Baroque park designed by Peter the Great for Catherine I in 1718,</cite> 2 km from the Old Town. The palace today houses the Kadriorg Art Museum.

**KUMU** — the museum of modern and contemporary Estonian art, winner of the European Museum of the Year. Entry ~15 EUR. <cite index="80-1">The park is free and perfect for a half-day.</cite>

### Lahemaa National Park — Day Trip

<cite index="83-1">Lahemaa National Park is 1 hour from Tallinn and combines bogs, manor houses, fishing villages and coastal trails.</cite> One of the most beautiful national parks in the Baltic states. Guided excursion: 50–80 EUR.

## Food and Drink

### What You Must Eat

**Black rye bread (leib)** — dense, acidic, with a complex aroma, the base of the Estonian diet. Completely different from what you might expect. At a local bakery or market: 2–4 EUR a loaf.

**Elk soup (põdraliha supp)** — <cite index="83-1">elk soup with rye bread at ~4.50 EUR is one of the best cheap lunches in the Old Town.</cite>

**Marinated herring with sour cream and potatoes** — the traditional Estonian coastal dish. 8–14 EUR at a restaurant.

**Tallinn marzipan** — Tallinn has a 600-year tradition of marzipan making. Café Maiasmokk (the oldest café in Tallinn, open since 1864) and specialist shops sell marzipan figures and sweets. The perfect souvenir: 5–15 EUR a box.

### What to Drink

**Vana Tallinn** — the black liqueur from Tallinn, made from rum, cinnamon, citrus and other secret ingredients. 40% ABV. Drunk in coffee (Vana Tallinn Coffee) or neat. A bottle: 8–14 EUR. Estonia's #1 edible souvenir.

**Estonian beer** — brands: Saku, A. Le Coq, Põhjala (craft). At a bar: <cite index="86-1">~6 EUR a pint</cite> — more expensive than Riga or Belgrade, but much cheaper than Helsinki.

**Coffee** — the third-wave coffee scene is excellent in Tallinn, especially in Telliskivi and Kalamaja. Flat white: 3.50–4.50 EUR.

### Where to Eat

**Budget (8–15 EUR/meal):**
- <cite index="81-1">**Leib Resto ja Aed** — the best traditional Estonian restaurant in the Old Town</cite>
- Cafés and restaurants in **Telliskivi** — better value than the Old Town
- **Balti Jaam Market** — street food stalls

**Mid-range (15–28 EUR/person):**
- **Olde Hansa** — authentic medieval restaurant from the 15th century, with period costumes and medieval food. Touristy but good and spectacular. Reservation recommended.
- **III Draakon** — the Town Hall's inn on Raekoja plats, with cauldron soups and medieval atmosphere at surprisingly reasonable prices.

**Caution:** <cite index="86-1">restaurants directly on Raekoja plats charge prices 30–50% higher than those on adjacent streets for the same quality.</cite> One side street and prices normalise immediately.

## Recommended Itinerary — 3 Days in Tallinn

### Day 1 — Old Town: Allminn and Toompea

- **Early morning (8:00):** Toompea — Kohtuotsa vaateplats at sunrise, before tourists
- **9:00–12:30:** Raekoja plats, St Olaf's Church Tower, Katariina käik, medieval walls
- **Lunch:** III Draakon (cheap medieval soup) or Telliskivi
- **Afternoon:** Kiek in de Kök + Bastion Tunnels (14 EUR)
- **Evening:** Olde Hansa with reservation or a Kalamaja restaurant

### Day 2 — KGB Museum, Telliskivi and Kadriorg

- **Morning:** KGB Museum at Hotel Viru (booking required, 15 EUR, 45 min)
- **Lunch:** Telliskivi — Balti Jaam market or a local restaurant
- **Afternoon:** Kadriorg Park and Palace, KUMU if interested in art
- **Evening:** sunset from Toompea, dinner in Kalamaja

### Day 3 — Helsinki or Lahemaa

- **Option A:** ferry to Helsinki (2 hours, 20–45 EUR) — a day in Finland's capital, return in the evening
- **Option B:** guided excursion to Lahemaa National Park (1 hour, 50–80 EUR tour)
- **Option C:** free day — Telliskivi flea market (weekend), shopping (marzipan, Vana Tallinn), public sauna (15 EUR)

## Practical Tips

**Tallinn Card** — <cite index="77-1">28 EUR/24h, 38 EUR/48h — includes free entry to 50+ museums, unlimited public transport and discounts. Worth it if you visit 4+ paid museums.</cite>

**Cashless** — Tallinn is one of the world's most cashless cities. Cards are accepted at kiosks, markets, taxis and, according to local humour, even by buskers. Still, keep 20–30 EUR cash for exceptional situations.

**Trams and buses** — <cite index="83-1">tourists buy day passes via the mobile app.</cite> Trams 1 and 3 connect the Centre, Telliskivi and Kadriorg.

**The "gentlemen's club" scam** — <cite index="86-1">beware of promoters in the Old Town inviting tourists into clubs where "hostess drinks" can result in fraudulent bills of hundreds of euros.</cite> Politely decline any unsolicited invitation.

**Ferry to Helsinki** — Tallink Silja and Viking Line. <cite index="76-1">Morning departure and evening return tickets cost 20–45 EUR return.</cite> Book in advance in peak season.
