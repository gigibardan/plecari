Accommodation is usually the largest single cost in any trip. And it is the category where the difference between a considered choice and an impulsive one is most significant — the same night's sleep in the same city can cost anywhere from €40 to €200 depending on a handful of decisions made before you confirm the booking.

---

## Timing: when you book matters as much as where

### Early booking with free cancellation — the highest-value strategy

The strategy that outperforms all others for popular destinations in season:

1. **Book 2–4 months ahead** with a free cancellation policy
2. **Set a price alert** (Google Travel, Booking, Trivago) for the same property
3. **If the price drops:** cancel and rebook at the lower rate — zero loss
4. **If the price rises:** keep the original booking

**Real example:** 3-star hotel in central Athens, double room, 15 August:
- 5 months ahead: **€65/night** (free cancellation)
- 2 months ahead: **€89/night**
- 2 weeks ahead: **€124/night** (last available rooms)

The same room, on the same date, a €59 difference per night — purely from timing.

### Off-season savings: real numbers

| Destination | Peak season | Off-season | Saving |
|---|---|---|---|
| Santorini | €180–350/night | €110–200/night | 35–45% |
| Barcelona | €120–200/night | €80–130/night | 30–35% |
| Dubrovnik | €150–280/night | €90–160/night | 35–40% |
| Rome | €100–180/night | €70–120/night | 25–35% |
| Lisbon | €120–200/night | €75–130/night | 30–35% |

May–June and September–October deliver the same excellent European weather, dramatically fewer crowds, and these price differences. Off-season is not a compromise — for most destinations it is the better experience.

### Last-minute: when it works

Last-minute (under 2 weeks) produces genuine deals when:
- You are travelling off-season to destinations with variable demand
- The destination has a significant number of unsold rooms
- You have complete flexibility on location within the city

**Does not work for:** any popular Mediterranean destination in July–August, major European cities during known high-demand periods (Christmas markets, New Year, Fashion Weeks), or ski resorts in peak winter weeks.

## Platforms: where to find the best price

### Comparison table

| Platform | Strength | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Booking.com** | Maximum variety, free cancellation, Genius loyalty | Any destination, any type |
| **Agoda** | Asia prices, Insider Deals | Asia, Middle East |
| **Hotels.com** | Rewards programme (10 nights → 1 free) | Frequent travellers |
| **Airbnb** | Apartments with kitchens, unique properties | Groups, families, 5+ nights |
| **Hostelworld** | Hostels and dormitories | Budget, solo travellers |
| **Hotel's own website** | Rate without commission | Independent hotels |

### The 3-minute rule

Before any booking, check in sequence:
1. **Booking.com** — reference price and availability
2. **The hotel's own website** — often 5–15% cheaper at independent properties
3. **Agoda or Hotels.com** — to catch platform-specific promotions

Three minutes. Can save €20–40 per night.

### Direct booking vs Booking.com: the mechanics

Booking.com charges hotels a 15–25% commission. Independent hotels (boutique properties, guesthouses) have the strongest incentive to offer a discount on their direct website — the commission percentage matters more to them than to large chains, which typically maintain rate parity everywhere.

**How to find the direct website:** search the exact hotel name + "official website." Avoid sponsored search results — they may lead to resellers, not the hotel.

## Location: the most underestimated savings strategy

### Zone 2–3: same experience, 30–50% lower price

Hotels and apartments in the historic centre (zone 1) cost 30–50% more than equivalents 15 minutes away by metro or tram. The experience difference: negligible — often better (quieter streets, more authentic neighbourhood).

| City | Central zone | Zone 2–3 | Saving | Transport |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paris | €160–250/night | €90–140/night | 40% | Metro, 15 min |
| London | €180–320/night | €110–190/night | 40% | Tube, 20 min |
| Rome | €120–200/night | €75–120/night | 35% | Metro/tram, 15 min |
| Barcelona | €130–210/night | €85–130/night | 35% | Metro, 15 min |
| Athens | €90–150/night | €60–95/night | 35% | Metro, 15 min |
| Istanbul | €80–140/night | €50–80/night | 35–40% | Tram/ferry, 15 min |

**How to identify zone 2–3:** on Google Maps, draw a 3–5 km circle from the main attraction. Zone 2 is at the edge. Check public transport: if there is a metro or tram stop within 5 minutes' walk, the location works.

## Accommodation types: which to choose and when

### Budget hotels (1–3 stars)

The most predictable category. Appropriate for city breaks of 2–4 nights when you spend little time in the room.

**Price reference:**
- Eastern Europe (Budapest, Prague, Bucharest): €35–65/night
- Mediterranean (Rome, Barcelona, Athens): €70–120/night
- Western Europe (Paris, Amsterdam, London): €100–180/night

### Hostels: private rooms and dormitories

A well-rated social hostel in 2026 is not what the stereotype suggests. Top hostels in Lisbon, Prague, Budapest, and Amsterdam have bars, communal kitchens, and organised activities.

**Private hostel room:** €25–50/night — cheaper than budget hotels, with the option of communal social spaces. Ideal for solo travellers.

**Dormitory beds (€8–20/night):** the cheapest accommodation in Europe. Check: beds per room (4–6 is manageable), locker provision, ventilation. Score threshold: 8.5+ on Hostelworld for consistent quality.

### Apartments: when they make financial sense

| Scenario | Why the apartment wins |
|---|---|
| Group of 3+ | €80/night ÷ 3 = €27/person |
| 5+ night stay | Kitchen saves €15–30/day on meals |
| Family with children | Space, flexible mealtimes, washing machine |
| Remote worker | Dedicated desk, faster Wi-Fi |

**The Airbnb cleaning fee problem:** an apartment listed at "€45/night" with a €60 cleaning fee on a 3-night stay costs effectively €65/night. Booking.com Apartments typically includes cleaning in the nightly rate. Always compare **total** prices, not per-night prices.

## Hidden costs: the price before you confirm

### Tourist tax (city tax)

Charged separately at check-in, not always included in the displayed total on Booking.com before you confirm.

| City | Tourist tax (per person, per night) |
|---|---|
| Venice | €5–10/day (access fee + accommodation) |
| Amsterdam | 16% of the accommodation rate |
| Barcelona | €3.50–6.75 |
| Rome | €3.50–7 (max 10 nights) |
| Florence | €5.50 |
| Paris | €1–5 (by star category) |
| Dubrovnik | €2 |
| Athens | €0.50–4 |

**Practical impact:** 4 nights in Amsterdam at €100/night → tourist tax: €64 extra. Add this to every comparison.

See: [overtourism in Europe 2026](/en/city-breaks/overtourism-europe-2026-tourist-taxes) for the full picture on new 2026 tourist levies.

### Resort and facility fees

€5–25/night at resort and popular tourist hotels. Covers pools, gym, Wi-Fi as a daily charge. Appears at check-in, not in the advertised price. Check "Price information" or "Taxes and fees" on Booking before confirming.

### Breakfast and parking

If breakfast is not included: €10–20/person/day extra. A nearby bakery provides an equivalent or better breakfast at €5–8 for two. If driving: always check parking cost — central hotel parking at €25/night is often avoidable with a nearby public garage at €10–15.

## Reading reviews correctly

**Sort by "Most Recent"** — not "Most Relevant." The relevance algorithm weights higher-scoring older reviews, masking recent quality decline.

**What to look for:**
- Cleanliness mentions in the last 90 days
- Noise: street, neighbours, ongoing construction nearby
- Wi-Fi reliability if you plan to work remotely
- Location accuracy: "further from the metro than the map suggested"
- Staff behaviour patterns: consistent negatives signal management issues

**Review specificity:** reviews that describe specific experiences ("room 203 had a broken shower at 8am") are more reliable than vague superlatives. The more specific the negative detail, the more trustworthy the source.
