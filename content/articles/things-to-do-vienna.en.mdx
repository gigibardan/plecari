## Why Vienna is quietly perfect

Vienna is the city where you walk into a 1,441-room palace in the morning, eat a perfect schnitzel for lunch, drink coffee at a marble table in a café from 1876, then listen to opera at the most prestigious house in the world — all with the precision of a Swiss clock and the elegance of a Viennese waltz. No other European capital combines imperial grandeur with a quality of life ranked #1 globally for over a decade.

Unlike other European capitals, in Vienna everything actually works: public transport is impeccable, the food is consistently excellent, the museums are world-class, and the prices — while not Eastern European — are fair for what you get.

## Schönbrunn Palace — the residence that ruled an empire

Schönbrunn isn't just a palace — it's a universe of its own. The Habsburgs' summer residence has 1,441 rooms (you visit 40 or 22 depending on the ticket), gardens stretching to the horizon, the world's oldest zoo (1752), a maze, a tropical palm house, and spectacular views from the Gloriette.

**Insider:** Schönbrunn Zoo (Tiergarten) — founded in 1752, it's the world's oldest and one of the most beautiful. Giant pandas are the star attraction. Separate ticket: ~€26 adult. Or get the Classic Pass which includes it.

## Belvedere — Klimt, Baroque and Vienna's best panorama

The Belvedere complex has two Baroque palaces connected by magnificent gardens, with the best view of Vienna (including Stephansdom and the Alps on clear days). But the #1 reason people come here: **Gustav Klimt's "The Kiss"** — arguably the most famous painting in Austria.

**Insider:** under 19 enter FREE — one of the best cultural deals in Europe. If you're travelling with teenagers, Belvedere is the attraction where you save the most.

## The historic centre — Stephansdom, Hofburg & Ringstraße

Vienna's centre (Innere Stadt, District 1) is a UNESCO World Heritage site best explored on foot. The Ringstraße — the circular boulevard surrounding the centre — passes the Opera, Parliament, Rathaus (City Hall), Burgtheater, and the University. It's arguably the most elegant boulevard in Europe.

### Stephansdom (St. Stephen's Cathedral)

The heart of Vienna, literally — distances are measured from here. The 12th-century Gothic cathedral dominates the skyline with its 136.4m south tower.

| Experience | Adult | Children 6-14 |
|---|---|---|
| Cathedral entry (main nave) | **FREE** | **FREE** |
| South Tower (343 steps, walk up!) | €5.50 | €2 |
| North Tower (elevator, Pummerin bell view) | €6.50 | €2.50 |
| Catacombs (30-min guided tour) | €6.50 | €2.50 |
| Cathedral Treasury | €6 | €2 |
| **All-Inclusive combo (everything)** | **€18** | **€7** |

**Tip:** the all-inclusive combo ticket (€18) saves ~€15 versus separate tickets. The south tower — 343 steps with no lift — is Vienna's physical challenge, but the view from the top is the best in the city. The catacombs contain the bones of 10,000 Viennese and the organs of the Habsburgs — not for the squeamish.

**Insider:** the cathedral roof has 230,000 coloured tiles forming the coat of arms of Vienna and the double-headed eagle of Austria — best seen from the North Tower or from upper floors in Stephansplatz.

### Hofburg — the Imperial Winter Palace

The Habsburgs' winter residence for over 600 years. The massive complex houses the Sisi Museum, Imperial Apartments, the National Library (one of the most beautiful reading rooms in the world), and the Spanish Riding School.

**Sisi Museum + Imperial Apartments:** €20 adult, €12 children 6-18, €18 students. Includes audioguide. The story of Empress Elisabeth (Sisi) is fascinating — the beauty, the obsession with weight, the assassination, and the complicated relationship with the court.

**Sisi Pass (€60):** combines Schönbrunn + Hofburg + Furniture Museum — economical if you visit all three.

### Spanish Riding School (Spanische Hofreitschule)

The only place in the world where you can see classical dressage of Lipizzaner horses in a Baroque riding hall from 1735. It's spectacle, not sport — horses dancing to classical music is mesmerising.

| Experience | Price |
|---|---|
| Gala Performance | €35-225 (depends on seat) |
| Morning Exercise | €16-26 |
| Stable Tour | €18 |

**Tip:** Morning Exercise (€16-26) is the most accessible way to see the Lipizzaners. Lasts ~1h, you watch the real training of horses to classical music. Gala Performances (€35-225) are more formal but sell out fast — book 2-3 months ahead. The Vienna PASS includes Morning Exercise.

## Kunsthistorisches Museum — one of the world's greatest museums

If you have time for only one museum in Vienna (besides Belvedere), this is it. The Habsburg art collection includes Vermeer, Caravaggio, Raphael, Bruegel (the world's largest collection), Velázquez, and Titian. The building itself — with the monumental staircase painted by Klimt — is a work of art.

**Tickets:** €21 adult (digital) / €23 (at the desk). Under 19: **FREE**. Combo "Treasures of the Habsburgs" (KHM + Imperial Treasury): €30.

**Tip:** go on Tuesday, Thursday or Friday after 3:00 PM — fewest people. Don't try to see everything in one visit — focus on the Bruegel gallery (1st floor, right wing) and Kunstkammer (cabinet of curiosities). The Egyptian and Greek rooms are underrated.

**Insider:** the National Library — the State Hall (Prunksaal) is 200m from KHM. €10 adult. One of the most beautiful rooms in the world — imperial Baroque with 200,000 old books and ceiling frescoes. Worth 20-30 minutes.

## Vienna State Opera (Wiener Staatsoper) — the world's most accessible opera house

Vienna is probably the only place in the world where you can see world-class opera for €13. Seriously.

### Standing room tickets (Stehplatz) — the Viennese trick

| Location | Price |
|---|---|
| Balcony (standing) | €13 |
| Gallery (standing) | €15 |
| Parterre (standing) | €18 |

Standing tickets go on sale online at **10:00 AM Vienna time on performance day** on the official website. Includes a personal subtitle monitor with translations in 5 languages. Duration: 2-4h (operas vary), but you can leave during intermission.

**Seated tickets:** €15-280 (mid-range seats €60-110 in the parterre or balcony offer the best sound/sightline/value ratio).

**Under 27:** at the box office from 2:00 PM on performance day, unsold seats (including parterre!) for just €15 with valid ID.

**Insider:** in summer (July-August) the Opera is closed, but performances are projected on a screen outside the building for free — bring a blanket, sit on the steps, and enjoy opera under the stars. Completely free.

**BundestheaterCard (€20/year):** gives priority access to standing tickets one day early — worth it if you're staying 3+ days and want to catch popular performances.

## Naschmarkt — where Vienna eats

Vienna's largest market: 120+ stalls stretching 1.5 km, from Austrian cheeses to Lebanese falafel, fresh fish to Turkish kebab. It's everything good about Vienna — multicultural, orderly, high-quality.

**Entry:** free. **Hours:** Mon-Sat 6:00 AM-9:00 PM (restaurants stay later). **Closed Sundays.**

**What to eat:** falafel wrap (€5-9), fresh sushi (€8-12), cheese + charcuterie (€5-8 tasting portion), Turkish baklava (€3-5), Austrian wine by the glass (€4-6).

**Tip:** Saturday morning adds the flea market (Flohmarkt) at the Kettenbrückengasse end — antiques, vinyl records, curiosities. Worth an hour of browsing.

**Insider:** don't eat at the front-row stalls (tourist prices). Walk to the secondary aisle or the stalls at the back — same quality, 20-30% cheaper.

## Prater — the amusement park with the movie-famous Ferris wheel

Prater is enormous: 6 million square metres of green park crossed by straight avenues, with the Wurstelprater amusement park in one corner. **Park entry is free** — you pay per ride.

**Wiener Riesenrad (Giant Ferris Wheel):** €13.50 adult, €6 children, under 6 free. The rotation takes 15-20 minutes. It's the wheel from "The Third Man" (1949) and "Before Sunrise" — if you've seen the films, the moment is magical.

**Cabin restaurant:** €100-200/person — private dinner in a rotating cabin. Romantic but pricey.

**Wurstelprater:** ~250 rides, from children's carousels to roller coasters. Pay per ride (€3-8 per attraction). No entry fee.

**Tip:** go in the evening — the illuminated wheel against the sky is a Vienna landmark. Then walk along the Hauptallee (the main avenue through Prater) — 4.3 km of straight avenue lined with chestnut trees, where Viennese jog, cycle, and stroll.

## MuseumsQuartier & Mariahilfer Straße

**MuseumsQuartier (MQ):** one of the world's largest cultural complexes, with the Leopold Museum (Schiele, Klimt — €16), MUMOK (modern art — €14), Kunsthalle (temporary exhibitions — €10-14), and inner courtyards with colourful urban furniture where Viennese sit in the sun with a Spritzer in hand. **The courtyards are free** and perfect for a break.

**Mariahilfer Straße:** Vienna's largest shopping street, 2 km of shops. Mix of international brands, Austrian stores, and cafés. If you need shopping, this is the place.

## Where to eat in Vienna (the real guide)

### Wiener Schnitzel — what you need to know

An authentic Wiener Schnitzel is made from **veal** (not pork — that's "Schnitzel Wiener Art", a cheaper imitation). It must be paper-thin, with a golden crust that puffs slightly away from the meat.

**Where:**

**Figlmüller** (Wollzeile 5) — the most famous, the schnitzel overhangs the plate. €16-19. **Reservation essential** — book online 1-2 weeks ahead. Queue without reservation: 30-60 min.

**Gasthaus Pöschl** (Weihburggasse 17) — less touristy, excellent schnitzel with potato salad. €14-17.

**Schnitzelwirt** (Neubaugürtel 52, District 7) — best price-to-quality ratio. Huge schnitzel, €9-13. Authentic Viennese pub atmosphere, zero pretension.

**Centner** (Josefstädter Str.) — neighbourhood local, authentic veal schnitzel at €12-15. No tourists.

### Würstelstand — Viennese street food

Sausage stands are a Viennese institution. Open until 2-4 AM, they're where Viennese eat after the opera or after a night at the bar.

**What to order:** Käsekrainer (sausage with melted cheese — €5-6), Bosna (sausage with curry and onion in a roll — €4-5), Burenwurst (classic sausage — €4). With bread, mustard, and horseradish.

**Where:** Bitzinger am Albertinaplatz (near the Opera — most famous), Leo's (near Hofburg), any neighbourhood stand.

### Viennese coffee houses — UNESCO Intangible Heritage

Viennese café culture has been on the UNESCO list since 2011 — it's not just coffee, it's a lifestyle. You order one coffee, receive a glass of water, read the newspaper, and nobody rushes you.

**Types of Viennese coffee:**
- **Melange** — the cappuccino equivalent, with whipped cream (€4-6)
- **Kleiner Brauner** — small espresso with a dash of milk (€3-4)
- **Einspänner** — double espresso with whipped cream, served in a glass (€5-7)
- **Wiener Eiskaffee** — iced coffee with vanilla ice cream and whipped cream (€7-9, summer)

**Where:**

**Café Central** (Herrengasse 14) — the most famous. Freud, Trotsky, and Hitler were regulars. The interior is spectacular (neo-Gothic vaults). Coffee + cake: €10-14. Queue at the door: 15-30 min in season.

**Café Sacher** (Philharmonikerstr. 4) — for the original Sachertorte. A slice: €9.50 in the café (€14-18 at the hotel). Dense chocolate, apricot jam, obligatory whipped cream.

**Café Sperl** (Gumpendorfer Str. 11) — the locals' favourite. Authentic atmosphere without the tourist hordes. The Apfelstrudel (€6-8) is Vienna's best.

**Café Hawelka** (Dorotheergasse 6) — tiny, smoke-scented (metaphorically), with Buchteln (sweet dumplings with vanilla sauce — €4-5) served only in the evening from 10:00 PM. Pure Viennese experience.

**Insider:** at any Viennese café, the minimum order is one coffee. You can sit for hours with a single coffee — nobody will ask you to leave. The newspaper is included (on a wooden rack). It's a social contract: you buy the right to space, time, and quiet.

### Heurige — Vienna's urban wineries

Vienna is the only capital city in the world with significant wine production within its city limits. **Heurige** are taverns serving the new wine (this year's vintage) directly from the producer, with cold food (cheeses, charcuterie, salads).

**Where:** the districts of Grinzing, Nussdorf, Stammersdorf, Neustift am Walde — all 20-30 min by tram from the centre.

**Prices:** a quarter litre (Viertel) of wine: €3-5. Cold platter: €8-14. Full evening: €20-30/person.

**Insider:** skip Grinzing (most touristy). Go to Stammersdorf (tram 31 from Schottenring) or Neustift am Walde (bus 35A from Spittelau) — more authentic, cheaper, same quality.

**How it works:** a pine branch hung above the door means the Heuriger is open and serving new wine. No reservations, walk in, order at the counter, sit at communal tables in the garden. Democratic and relaxed.

## Classical music in Vienna — from free to premium

Vienna = the world capital of classical music. Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert, Strauss, Mahler, Brahms — all lived and composed here. Music is everywhere, and you DON'T have to spend a fortune.

### Free options

- **Film Festival on Rathausplatz** (July-August): free screenings of operas and concerts on a giant screen, with food stalls from around the world. Vienna's most popular summer event.
- **Opera screen** (summer): live projections of performances on the Opera House façade.
- **Church concerts:** Stephansdom, Augustinerkirche, Karlskirche — many free or €5-15.

### Paid options

- **Musikverein** (home of the Vienna Philharmonic — the famous New Year's Concert): tickets from €30. The Great Hall (Goldener Saal) has perfect acoustics. Concerts nearly daily.
- **Konzerthaus:** more diverse programming, from classical to jazz. Tickets from €20.
- **Mozart/Strauss concerts** in palaces: €40-80. Touristy but enjoyable — costumed, in historic halls. Book on GYG with free cancellation.

**WARNING:** Avoid the costumed ticket sellers in Stephansplatz and Graben hawking "classical concerts." Quality varies enormously — some are good, others are tourist traps with amateur musicians. Buy directly from the venue websites or on GetYourGuide.

## Day trips from Vienna

### Wachau Valley (UNESCO) — 80 km west

The Danube valley with terraced vineyards, medieval villages, and Melk Abbey (spectacular Baroque). **How:** ÖBB train from Wien Westbahnhof to Melk (1h, €15-20). Visit the abbey (€14.50 adult), then Danube cruise from Melk to Krems (€29, 1.5h). Return by train from Krems (1h, €15).

**Or:** organised tour on [GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/vienna-l7/?partner_id=EKAA80H) (€50-80, includes transport, wine tasting, and Melk Abbey).

### Bratislava — 1h by train

The closest capital to Vienna (65 km!). ÖBB train: €10-15 return with Sparschiene (early bird ticket). The old town is compact, visiteable in 4-5 hours. Lunch is 40-50% cheaper than in Vienna.

### Hallstatt — the Alpine jewel

The village with colourful houses on the lakeside, UNESCO World Heritage. **Warning:** Hallstatt is tiny and overcrowded in summer (800 residents, 10,000 tourists per day). Train from Hauptbahnhof: 3.5h, €25-35 with Sparschiene. Better alternative: [organised tour](https://www.getyourguide.com/vienna-l7/?partner_id=EKAA80H) that includes Salzburg too (€70-100).

## Recommended itinerary — 3 days in Vienna

### Day 1: The imperial centre

**Morning:** Stephansdom (free entry + all-inclusive combo €18 if you want towers and catacombs). Walk along Graben and Kohlmarkt (Vienna's most elegant streets). Coffee + Apfelstrudel at Café Sperl or Café Central.

**Afternoon:** Hofburg — Sisi Museum + Imperial Apartments (€20, 1.5h). Then the National Library — Prunksaal (€10, 20 min). Walk through the Volksgarten (spectacular roses in summer).

**Evening:** standing tickets at the State Opera (€13-18) or concert at the Musikverein. After the show: Würstelstand at Bitzinger (€5-6, near the Opera).

### Day 2: Schönbrunn & culinary culture

**Morning:** Schönbrunn — palace + gardens + Gloriette (Classic Pass €49 for everything, Palace Ticket €42 for the palace alone). Arrive at opening (9:00 AM). Minimum 3-4h.

**Afternoon:** Naschmarkt (lunch with falafel + sushi + wine — €10-15). Then MuseumsQuartier — Leopold Museum (Schiele, €16) or MUMOK (modern art, €14). Relax in the MQ courtyard.

**Evening:** Mariahilfer Straße for shopping. Dinner: schnitzel at Schnitzelwirt (€9-13) or Figlmüller (€16-19, with reservation).

### Day 3: Art, Prater & farewell

**Morning:** Belvedere — Upper Belvedere for Klimt (€23, 1.5h). Belvedere Garden (free, 30 min).

**Afternoon:** Kunsthistorisches Museum (€21, 2h — focus on Bruegel and Kunstkammer). Or Albertina (if you prefer modern art).

**Evening:** Prater — Riesenrad (€13.50) at sunset. Walk along the Hauptallee. Beer and Käsekrainer at a Prater Würstelstand. Or — Heuriger in Stammersdorf (tram 31, 30 min) for wine and charcuterie with panoramic views.

## Vienna with kids — why it's Europe's best city for families

Vienna beats every other European city for families. State museums are free under 19, public transport is free on Sundays under 15, and the attractions are designed for children without being condescending.

### Top family experiences

**Schönbrunn Zoo (Tiergarten):** the world's oldest zoo (1752), but fully modernised. Giant pandas, baby elephants, tropical aquarium. Included in Classic Pass (€49) or separate ticket ~€26 adult, €16 children.

**Haus des Meeres:** aquarium built in a WWII anti-aircraft tower — unique combination of history and marine biology. €13.90 adult, €6.90 children 6-15, under 6 free. Panoramic rooftop terrace.

**ZOOM Children's Museum** (MuseumsQuartier): interactive workshops for ages 0-14. Sessions of 1.5h on themes of art, science, media. €5-7 per session, **booking essential** — fills up fast.

**Prater:** free entry, rides €3-8 each. Riesenrad for everyone, roller coasters for the big kids, carousels for the little ones. Can fill an entire day.

**Technical Museum (Technisches Museum):** €16 adult, under 19 FREE. Fantastic interactive sections — children can experiment with physics, mechanics, electricity. Mini coal mine, flight simulator.

**Schönbrunn Maze & Children's Museum:** the garden maze (fun for ages 4-12) + interactive museum. Combo: €16.

**Insider:** Schweizerhaus restaurant in Prater — Stelze (crispy pork knuckle) served at communal tables in the beer garden. Huge portions, €12-16. Kids run around the garden, parents drink beer. The perfect Viennese family experience.

## Vienna's neighbourhoods — where to stay & what to explore

### Innere Stadt (District 1) — the imperial centre

Everything within walking distance: Stephansdom, Hofburg, Graben, the Opera. Most expensive hotels (€150-300/night) but you save on transport. Nightlife concentrated in the Bermuda Dreieck (Bermuda Triangle — Rabensteig/Seitenstettengasse area).

### Leopoldstadt (District 2) — Prater & cool local

The gentrifying district near Prater and the Donaukanal. More affordable hotels (€80-140), excellent restaurants on Karmelitermarkt (local market without tourists — superb Saturday brunch). Augarten — quiet park with the Augarten porcelain factory.

### Mariahilf (District 6) — Naschmarkt & shopping

The most practical district: Naschmarkt at your feet, Mariahilfer Straße for shopping, close to MuseumsQuartier. Hotel 3★: €90-150/night. Lively atmosphere without being touristy.

### Neubau (District 7) — hipster Vienna

Speciality coffee shops, vintage stores, independent galleries, Spittelberg with cobblestone lanes. The most creative district — Vienna's answer to Brooklyn. Hotels: €90-160. Excellent fusion restaurants on Burggasse and Zieglergasse.

### Josefstadt (District 8) — the discreet elegant

The smallest district, with theatres, second-hand bookshops, and neighbourhood restaurants. Quiet, elegant, tourist-free. Locals love Josefstadt for its village atmosphere in the middle of the city. Hotel: €80-130.

### Wieden (District 4) — Karlskirche & Belvedere

The district between the centre and Belvedere. Karlsplatz, the Freihausviertel square (excellent restaurants), Theresianum. Well-connected, moderate prices. Hotel: €85-140.

## Christmas markets — Vienna in the magic season

Vienna's Christmas markets (Christkindlmärkte) run from **mid-November to December 26** and are among the most beautiful in Europe.

### Top 5 Christmas markets

**Rathausplatz (City Hall Square):** the largest and most famous. City Hall illuminated, 150+ stalls, ice skating rink, miniature train for children. Punch: €5-7 (mug deposit €3-4, refunded). Most crowded — go on weekdays.

**Schönbrunn:** market in the palace courtyard, spectacular festive lighting. Fewer stalls but exclusive atmosphere. Quality Austrian handicrafts.

**Spittelberg:** the most intimate — narrow lanes in District 7, stalls with handmade products, mulled wine (Glühwein, €4-6), candlelit lanterns. The locals' favourite.

**Belvedere:** small market in front of the palace — the most photogenic setting (illuminated palace behind).

**Am Hof:** handicraft market — ideal for authentic Austrian gifts. Ceramics, textiles, wooden ornaments.

**Christmas market budget:** Glühwein €4-6, Punch €5-7, Kartoffelpuffer (potato pancakes) €5-7, Langos €5-6, Maroni (roasted chestnuts) €4-5, Lebkuchen (gingerbread) €3-5. An evening at the market: €15-25/person.

## Events & festivals 2026

**January:** Opera Ball (Opernball) — the world's most famous ball, tickets €300-25,000 (!). The ball season continues with 400+ balls through March.

**April-May:** Vienna City Marathon (April) — 42,000 runners through the historic centre. Easter Music Festival.

**May-June:** Vienna Festival (Wiener Festwochen) — theatre, music, contemporary art. Many free outdoor performances.

## Things nobody tells you about Vienna

**Shops close early.** Saturday at 6:00 PM, Sunday everything is closed (with rare exceptions). If you're coming from a city with 24/7 shopping, adjust. Supermarkets (Billa, Spar) close at 7:30-8:00 PM.

**Viennese are correct, not warm.** Don't confuse efficiency with coldness. Waiters don't smile excessively — that doesn't mean they're unhappy. Say "Grüß Gott" (the Austrian greeting) instead of "Hallo" and you'll get different treatment.

**Tap water is spectacular.** It comes directly from Alpine springs in Steiermark and Niederösterreich through 150 km aqueducts. It's better than any bottled water you could buy.

**Smoking in restaurants has been banned** since 2019, but in traditional bars and cafés you may still smell old tobacco. New venues are completely smoke-free.

**Feet on the metro seat = fine.** Wiener Linien is strict with rules. No valid ticket = €105 fine. Music without headphones = intense disapproving stares from the entire carriage.

**Vienna has a beach.** Yes, on the Donauinsel (Danube Island) and Alte Donau. In summer, Viennese go to the Danube beach for swimming, BBQ, paddleboarding. Completely free, accessible by metro (U1 Donauinsel or U1 Alte Donau). Copa Cagrana — the area with beach bars.

**The best Sachertorte isn't at Hotel Sacher.** Yes, the original is there (€9.50/slice), but Demel — the historic rival — makes an equally good version at half the price. The "original vs. copy" dispute has been running since 1832 and is part of Viennese folklore.

## Don't miss (and don't bother)

### Don't miss

- **Sunset from the Gloriette (Schönbrunn)** — free, imperial panorama
- **Albertina** (€18.90) — Monet, Renoir, Picasso collection in an elegant palace. Less crowded than KHM
- **Karlskirche** (€8) — Baroque church with lift to the dome for an interior panoramic view and up-close frescoes
- **Spittelberg** (District 7) — cobblestone lanes with Biedermeier houses, independent cafés, galleries. Vienna's most photogenic neighbourhood
- **Donauinsel** (Danube Island) — summer: free beach, BBQ zones, cycling. 21 km of artificial island, the Viennese relaxation spot

### Don't bother

- **Madame Tussauds** (€24) — the same wax figures as in every city
- **Mozart's House** (€14) — the exhibits are modest, for hardcore fans only
- **Time Travel Vienna** (€22) — touristy ride with special effects, kitsch
- **Concert tickets sold by costumed street hawkers** — quality risk is very high

## Practical tips

**[Travel insurance](https://ektatraveling.tpo.mx/uU7jZ5jK):** EHIC covers Austrian public hospitals, but a dedicated policy (€15-25/week) adds repatriation, theft, and cancellation cover. [Full guide](/en/asigurari/travel-insurance-europe-guide).

**[eSIM](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3):** EU roaming is free for EU residents. If you're from outside the EU, an eSIM saves €5-15/day. [Comparison guide](/en/esim-roaming/best-esim-europe-2026).

**Water:** Vienna's tap water comes directly from Alpine springs and is among the best in Europe. Don't buy bottled water — bring a reusable bottle and refill anywhere.

**Safety:** Vienna is one of the safest cities in the world. Pickpockets exist (Stephansplatz, the U1/U3/U4 metro during rush hour, Naschmarkt), but at a much lower level than Barcelona, Rome, or Paris.

**Tipping:** 5-10% at restaurants is standard. At cafés: round up the amount. At hotels: €1-2/night for housekeeping. NOT at fast food or Würstelstands.

**Best time to visit:** May-June and September — best weather, reasonable prices. July-August: hot (30°C+), many tourists, but free film festival on Rathausplatz. November-December: magical Christmas markets. January-February: cheapest, but cold (0°C).

**Power socket:** type F (same as most of continental Europe) — no adapter needed for EU/UK travellers (UK needs adapter).

**Language:** German, but English is spoken everywhere in the tourist zone. Viennese are correct but not excessively warm — don't take offence if the waiter seems distant, it's the Viennese style.
