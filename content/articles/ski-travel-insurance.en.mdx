Skiing sits in an unusual insurance category: the activity is extremely popular, the potential costs of something going wrong are very high, and the specific coverage required is almost universally absent from standard travel insurance. Every winter season, thousands of skiers discover this after an injury — when it is too late to purchase the right coverage.

This guide covers everything specific to ski and winter sports insurance: what the key coverage elements mean in practice, the clauses that routinely catch out experienced skiers, and how to choose a policy that actually covers the holiday you are taking.

---

## Why standard travel insurance is not enough

A standard travel insurance policy is designed for mainstream travel risks: illness, trip cancellation, lost luggage, flight delays. Most standard policies explicitly exclude hazardous or extreme activities — and skiing almost universally falls into this category.

The typical wording in a standard policy exclusions section:

*"This policy does not cover claims arising from participation in hazardous activities including but not limited to: skiing, snowboarding, off-piste skiing, ski racing, extreme sports, winter mountaineering..."*

**The consequence:** if you are injured while skiing on a policy that does not include winter sports cover:
- Your medical treatment is not covered
- Your mountain rescue is not covered
- Your extended accommodation due to injury is not covered
- You pay everything out of pocket

The fix is simple: choose a policy that explicitly includes winter sports, or add a ski extension to your existing travel insurance. The additional premium for ski cover: typically €8–20 per person per week on top of base travel insurance — the best-value insurance purchase available.

## The six key coverage elements for ski insurance

### 1. Piste rescue and mountain rescue

This is the most immediate cost when something goes wrong. If you fall and cannot ski down, the ski patrol brings you off the mountain — by sled (pulka), snowmobile, or helicopter depending on the severity and location.

**Helicopter rescue costs:**
| Country | Approximate cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| France | €2,000–8,000 | Some costs offset by PGHM (mountain rescue service) |
| Austria | €3,000–12,000 | Varies by Bundesland |
| Switzerland | €4,000–15,000 | Typically highest in Europe |
| Italy | €2,000–10,000 | Varies by region |
| Romania (Poiana Brașov, Sinaia) | €500–2,000 | SMURD helicopter, lower cost |

In France and Austria, the lift pass levy funds part of the rescue infrastructure — but the helicopter itself and any private medical involvement is billed to the patient. Swiss rescue is typically the most expensive.

**What the insurance covers:** the full cost of rescue, transport to hospital, and any medical stabilisation during transport.

### 2. Emergency medical treatment for ski injuries

The most frequent ski injuries by cost:

| Injury | Typical treatment cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ACL/knee ligament tear | €8,000–25,000 | Surgery + physio + follow-up |
| Fractured wrist | €3,000–8,000 | Very common in beginners |
| Shoulder dislocation/fracture | €4,000–12,000 | Common in falls |
| Head injury (concussion) | €2,000–8,000 | Imaging, monitoring |
| Spinal injury | €20,000–100,000+ | Rare but catastrophic |

**ACL tear is the most consequential common ski injury:** it requires surgery (often unavoidable), 6–9 months of physiotherapy, and potentially multiple follow-up appointments. The total cost can exceed €25,000 in Western European private hospitals.

**What to verify:** does the policy cover only emergency stabilisation, or full surgical and rehabilitation costs? Some budget ski policies cover only emergency treatment — leaving the surgery and rehabilitation to be managed on return. A comprehensive policy covers the complete medical episode including follow-up surgery at home if repatriation is required.

### 3. Medical repatriation

If the injury is serious enough to require treatment at a specialist facility in your home country, medical repatriation is required. This means transport by air ambulance or medically equipped commercial flight, with medical personnel.

Cost: €15,000–60,000 depending on the distance and medical complexity.

A spinal injury in the Alps requiring specialist neurosurgery available only at home: the repatriation alone costs more than most people earn in a year. This is the scenario where insurance exists specifically to protect against.

### 4. Trip cancellation

If injury prevents you from skiing before the trip starts — a broken leg three weeks before departure, for example — trip cancellation cover reimburses the non-refundable costs: flights, lift passes, accommodation, ski hire.

**Important detail:** the injury must occur after you have purchased the policy. If you injure your knee before buying the insurance and subsequently cannot travel, it is a pre-existing condition and not covered.

**Also covered under trip cancellation:** illness of a family member requiring you to cancel, redundancy, jury service, and specific other reasons listed in the policy. Read the cancellation triggers carefully — they vary significantly between policies.

### 5. Ski equipment cover

Equipment theft and accidental damage to owned ski equipment:

**What is typically covered:**
- Theft from a locked vehicle or secured ski storage
- Damage in a collision accident
- Loss of equipment (rare, but covered)

**What is typically excluded:**
- Equipment left unattended on a public ski rack (common exclusion)
- Normal wear and tear
- Damage to hired equipment (covered separately by the hire shop's own damage waiver)

**Cover limits:** typically €500–1,500 for the full equipment set. High-end equipment (custom boots, race skis, performance helmets) may exceed standard limits — declare and pay for additional cover if needed.

**Hired equipment:** if you rent skis, boots, and poles at the resort, and they are stolen or damaged while in your care, your liability depends on the hire shop's own terms. Many hire shops offer their own damage waiver at 3–7 EUR/day — worth taking if your travel insurance does not explicitly cover hired equipment.

### 6. Piste closure and poor snow conditions

Piste closure insurance pays a daily benefit when the ski area reduces operation significantly due to:
- Insufficient snow
- High wind causing lift closures
- Fog reducing visibility below operational limits
- Temperature extremes

**What it pays:** a fixed daily amount (typically €30–50/day) for each day the area is substantially closed. It does not refund your accommodation, flights, or lift pass.

**Is it worth buying?**
- High-altitude resorts with reliable snow (Verbier, Zermatt, Val d'Isère above 2,000m): low risk, probably not worth it
- Mid-altitude resorts susceptible to snow variability (many Balkan resorts, lower Austrian/Italian resorts): worth considering
- Late-season bookings (March/April): higher risk, worth it
- Early-season bookings (November/December): moderate risk

## On-piste vs off-piste: the most misunderstood distinction

**On-piste:** marked and groomed runs within the official ski area boundary, numbered and maintained by the resort. Standard ski insurance covers on-piste skiing.

**Off-piste:** anything outside the marked run boundary — even 5 metres from the piste edge into ungroomed snow. Including:
- Powder fields adjacent to runs but not within the marked boundary
- Trees runs ("tree skiing")
- Ski touring (ascending and descending outside resort boundaries)
- Backcountry skiing
- Heliskiing

**Why the distinction matters:** many intermediate and advanced skiers drift off the edge of marked runs regularly — for a powder turn, to cut through trees to the next run, or simply because the run boundary is not clearly visible in poor visibility. Any injury occurring in this terrain, on a policy without off-piste endorsement, is not covered.

**The off-piste endorsement:** most ski policies offer this as an optional extra at €5–15 additional premium for the week. If you ski at intermediate to advanced level, add it.

**Guided off-piste:** some policies distinguish between unguided and guided off-piste skiing — with a qualified local mountain guide, coverage may be available on some policies without the standard off-piste endorsement. Verify specifically.

## Snowboarding, ski touring, and other winter activities

### Snowboarding

Covered under all ski insurance policies that reference "winter sports" — snowboarding injuries are treated identically to skiing injuries.

### Ski touring (ski mountaineering)

Ascending and descending terrain using touring skis and skins, outside the resort boundary. Requires explicit cover — standard ski insurance and standard off-piste endorsements sometimes exclude touring. Look for "ski touring" or "ski mountaineering" in the policy.

### Cross-country skiing (Nordic)

Lower risk, lower altitudes, lower rescue costs. Most ski policies cover Nordic skiing, but verify — some budget ski policies are designed only for downhill.

### Ice skating, sledging, snowshoeing

These are lower-risk winter activities that many standard travel policies cover without a specific ski endorsement. Check your standard policy before adding ski cover.

## Popular ski destinations and specific considerations

### The Alps (France, Switzerland, Austria, Italy)

The world's highest-density ski infrastructure and the highest rescue costs. French and Austrian resorts have well-organised mountain rescue (PGHM in France, Bergrettung in Austria) — but the helicopter hours are billed. Swiss insurance requirements are strict and costs are high.

**Best approach:** comprehensive ski insurance with off-piste endorsement, minimum €500,000 medical cover, explicit mountain rescue.

### Romania (Poiana Brașov, Sinaia, Predeal)

Rescue costs are lower — SMURD helicopter rescue typically €500–2,000. Medical treatment in private hospitals: much lower than Western Europe. A standard ski policy is appropriate, but do not skip insurance — the mountains are real and the risks are real. An ACL tear in Poiana Brașov still requires surgery.

For car rental to reach Romanian ski resorts: see [hidden fees in car rental](/en/inchirieri-auto/taxe-ascunse-inchiriere-auto) and [Romania road trip guide](/en/inchirieri-auto/romania-road-trip-transylvania).

### Bulgaria (Borovets, Bansko, Pamporovo)

Bansko is the largest ski resort in the Balkans, well-developed, with a good rescue infrastructure. Medical treatment costs are lower than Western Europe. Rescue costs: moderate. Standard ski cover is appropriate.

### Andorra

Zero VAT means cheap lift passes, ski hire, and equipment — but medical costs are standard European private rates if you end up at a clinic. Ski insurance is as necessary as anywhere.

## How to choose: the checklist

Before purchasing ski travel insurance, verify:

**Medical and rescue:**
- [ ] Minimum €500,000 medical cover (€1,000,000+ for Alpine destinations and USA)
- [ ] Mountain/piste rescue explicitly included
- [ ] Helicopter rescue explicitly included
- [ ] Medical repatriation included

**Activity cover:**
- [ ] Skiing on marked pistes confirmed as covered
- [ ] Off-piste endorsement (if you ski off-piste)
- [ ] Snowboarding confirmed (if applicable)
- [ ] Ski school injuries covered

**Financial protection:**
- [ ] Trip cancellation (injury before departure)
- [ ] Trip interruption (injury during trip, cannot return as planned)
- [ ] Piste closure benefit (optional but useful for variable-snow resorts)
- [ ] Equipment cover (owned and/or hired)

**Practical:**
- [ ] 24/7 emergency assistance line (in English or your language)
- [ ] Pre-authorisation process understood (call before hospital where possible)
- [ ] Policy period covers travel days AND skiing days
