Brașov is the city from which you understand why Transylvania has captured Europe's imagination for centuries. Stand in Piața Sfatului — the white limestone paving, the colourful-façaded houses, the Gothic Black Church in the background — and look up. Mount Tâmpa rises abruptly above the rooftops, with the white BRAȘOV letters gleaming in the sunlight, like a declaration that you're somewhere entirely special.

<cite index="165-1">Brașov is a medieval town at the foot of Mount Tâmpa and the gateway to Transylvania — surrounded by the Carpathian Mountains, with an Old Town full of history and character.</cite>

It is also the most practical base for exploring the greatest concentration of castles, fortresses and mountain landscapes in Romania: <cite index="166-1">Bran Castle 30 minutes away, Peleș Castle one hour, the Transfăgărășan 1.5 hours, Sighișoara 1.5 hours, Sibiu 2 hours.</cite>

An important development for 2026: <cite index="163-1">Brașov-Ghimbav Airport (GHV) is now operational, with direct flights from several European cities — you no longer have to land in Bucharest to reach Transylvania.</cite>

## Brașov at a Glance — Essential Facts

**County:** Brașov, southern Transylvania.

**Population:** approximately 290,000 — Romania's sixth largest city.

**Currency:** Romanian Leu (RON). 1 EUR ≈ 4.97 RON.

**Own airport:** Brașov-Ghimbav (GHV) — <cite index="163-1">opened in 2024, with direct flights from several European cities.</cite>

**Distance from Bucharest:** ~170 km, ~2h30 by train or car.

**Average budget:** 160–270 RON/day (~32–54 EUR) — similar to Sibiu, more affordable than Cluj.

## How to Get from Bucharest to Brașov

### By Train (Most Recommended)

<cite index="162-1">InterRegio/Rapid Bucharest Nord → Brașov: ~2h30, 60–100 RON (~12–20 EUR).</cite> Most comfortable — Brașov's station is in the city centre, 15 minutes' walk from Piața Sfatului.

### By Car

~170 km on DN1, ~2h30 under normal conditions. **Warning:** DN1 on Friday evenings and Sunday evenings can be blocked for hours by traffic to/from the mountains. Leave in the morning or check Waze.

### Direct Flight to Brașov-Ghimbav (GHV)

<cite index="163-1">Brașov-Ghimbav Airport is now operational with regular services</cite> — check availability of direct flights from your city.

## What to See in Brașov

### Piața Sfatului — The Medieval Heart

<cite index="162-1">Piața Sfatului is the heart of medieval Brașov — a rectangular square with colourful houses, restaurant terraces and the Council House (former town hall) at the centre.</cite>

**Council House (Casa Sfatului)** — the 15th-century Gothic building with central tower, today a history museum. Ticket: ~10 RON (~2 EUR). Exterior access free.

**Strada Republicii** — Brașov's pedestrian artery, connecting Piața Sfatului to the civic centre, with shops, cafés and a character typical of a university city.

### The Black Church — Romania's Largest

<cite index="166-1">The Black Church (Schwarze Kirche) is Romania's largest Gothic church and the largest between Vienna and Istanbul, built in the 14th–15th centuries.</cite> It received its name after the great fire of 1689, when smoke blackened the exterior walls.

<cite index="162-1">Entry: ~15 RON (~3 EUR). Hours: Monday–Saturday 10:00–17:00.</cite>

Interior: one of the most valuable collections of Anatolian rugs outside Turkey (150+ carpets brought by Saxon merchants), the monumental organ and medieval fresco.

### Mount Tâmpa and the BRAȘOV Sign

<cite index="163-1">Tampa cable car: Tuesday–Sunday 9:30–17:00, return ticket 8 EUR/adult.</cite>

On foot: 40–45 minutes on the Serpentines trail (follow the red marking from the mountain base).

At the top: panoramic observation platform with complete views of the medieval centre, the Bucegi Mountains and the surrounding valleys. The BRAȘOV sign (Hollywood-style) is visible from below and spectacular up close.

### Weavers' Bastion and Medieval Walls

<cite index="162-1">The Weavers' Bastion is the best-preserved medieval bastion in Brașov, today a museum of the city's medieval defences.</cite> Ticket: ~10 RON. Wall trail: partially open, free.

### Schei Quarter — Beyond the Walls

Schei is Brașov's traditionally Romanian neighbourhood, separated from the Saxon zone by Schei Gate. Here stands the First Romanian School (1495) — the first school teaching in Romanian in Transylvania, today a museum. Ticket: ~10 RON.

## Tour: Peleș Castle, Bran Castle and Râșnov Fortress from Brașov

### Bran Castle — Dracula's Castle?

<cite index="166-1">Bran Castle is 30 km from Brașov — an impressive medieval fortress built in the 14th century, above a gorge that marked the border between Transylvania and Wallachia.</cite>

**The Dracula connection:** Vlad the Impaler probably visited Bran, but didn't live there. The link to Bram Stoker's novel is tourism marketing, not history. The castle is worth visiting regardless — the labyrinthine architecture with towers, narrow corridors and medieval atmosphere are spectacular.

**Ticket:** <cite index="166-1">45 RON (~9 EUR) adult. Fast-pass (skip-the-line): 60 RON (~12 EUR). Hours: daily 9:00–18:00 (May–Sept), 9:00–16:00 (Oct–Apr).</cite>

**Essential tip:** <cite index="162-1">arrive at opening (9:00) or after 16:00 to avoid the maximum crowds between 10:00–15:00.</cite>

### Peleș Castle — Romania's Most Beautiful Castle

Peleș Castle in Sinaia (50 km from Brașov) is the former summer residence of the Romanian royal family, built in neo-Renaissance German style between 1873–1914.

<cite index="163-1">Tickets are purchased EXCLUSIVELY online, in advance, for a specific time slot. There are no walk-up tickets. Capacity: 500 people per slot.</cite>

**Online prices:** 60–100 RON (~12–20 EUR) depending on the tour chosen.

**Peleș MUST be booked in advance** — check availability at peles.ro or GetYourGuide with skip-the-line included.

## Day Trips from Brașov — The Transylvania Castle Circuit

### Râșnov Fortress

15 km from Brașov, Râșnov Fortress is a medieval refuge fortress on a hill, with valley panorama. Ticket: ~25 RON (~5 EUR). Accessible by local cable car or on foot (15 minutes). **2026 note:** verify it is open — <cite index="164-1">Râșnov Fortress was temporarily closed for works in 2026.</cite>

### Libearty Bear Sanctuary — Zărnești

<cite index="162-1">The Libearty Sanctuary shelters over 100 brown bears rescued from captivity across 69 hectares of forest — Europe's largest bear sanctuary.</cite>

Ticket: 90 RON (~18 EUR). Online booking mandatory. 20 km from Brașov.

### Viscri and the Saxon Villages

Viscri is Romania's best-preserved Saxon village — with the fortified church, white houses and preserved traditions. Promoted by Prince Charles (who bought a house there). 65 km from Brașov.

### Poiana Brașov — The Ski Resort

13 km from Brașov's centre, Poiana Brașov is Romania's largest ski resort — with 20 km of slopes, ski lifts and active winter nightlife.

## Food and Drink in Brașov

**Kürtőskalács (chimney cake)** — spiral fried dough with sugar and cinnamon, sold by street vendors on Piața Sfatului. 15–25 RON.

**Transylvanian sour soup (Ciorbă ardelenească)** — denser and more acidic than Moldovan versions. 20–30 RON.

**Mici (grilled meat rolls) and chips** — obligatory at a Transylvanian restaurant. 25–40 RON a portion.

### Where to Eat

**Budget (20–50 RON/meal):** restaurants on Strada Republicii or Piața Unirii area.

**Mid-range (50–120 RON/person):** terrace restaurants on Piața Sfatului (pricier but atmospheric), or places in the Schei quarter (more authentic and cheaper).

## Recommended Itinerary — 3 Days in Brașov

### Day 1 — Old Town and Tâmpa

- **Morning:** Piața Sfatului, Council House, Strada Republicii
- **Lunch:** kürtőskalács + Transylvanian sour soup
- **Afternoon:** Tampa cable car (or hike up) + panoramic view
- **Evening:** Black Church (if evening programme), dinner on Piața Sfatului

### Day 2 — Bran Castle and Surroundings

- **Morning:** Bran Castle (arrive at 9:00, online ticket 45 RON)
- **Lunch:** restaurant in Bran or Zărnești
- **Afternoon:** Libearty Bear Sanctuary (mandatory booking, 90 RON) or Râșnov Fortress
- **Evening:** return to Brașov

### Day 3 — Peleș Castle and Sinaia

- **Morning:** Peleș Castle (online ticket mandatory, slot 9:15 or 10:00)
- **Lunch:** Sinaia — resort restaurants
- **Afternoon:** Sinaia Monastery or boulevard walk
- **Evening:** return to Brașov, train to Bucharest

## Practical Tips

**Train Bucharest–Brașov** — the most comfortable and reliable. Brașov station is central.

**Peleș online booking mandatory** — cannot be purchased on the day. Book at peles.ro or GetYourGuide at least 1 week ahead in season.

**Bran crowded** — if going to Bran Castle in season, arrive at 9:00. Between 10:00 and 15:00, queues can be long even with an online ticket.

**DN1 traffic** — Friday evenings and Sunday evenings, the Brașov–Bucharest road is a nightmare. Take the train or leave early.

**Poiana Brașov in winter** — bus 20 from Brașov centre reaches Poiana in 30 minutes, 3 RON. No need for taxi or car.
