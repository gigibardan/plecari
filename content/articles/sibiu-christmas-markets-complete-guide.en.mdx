Sibiu invented the modern Romanian Christmas market. In 2007, when nobody else in the country was doing it, Grand Square received its first wooden chalets built on the Central European model. Nineteen editions later, the result is the one Romanian market that looks as though it has always been there — because the medieval square around it genuinely has.

The 2026 edition carries the theme **„Ai voie să crezi”** — You Are Allowed to Believe — and runs from **13 November to 3 January 2027**, 52 days. It is also the first edition since Sibiu was named an **„Excellent European Christmas Market”** in 2026, one of only 12 markets recognised out of more than 600 evaluated across Europe.

This guide covers the exact hours, the real prices from the edition running right now, whether the 99-lei Golden Ticket is worth buying, how to get here (including why you should not fly from Bucharest), where to park, where to stay and what to do with the daylight hours.

## What is new in 2026

**Story Alley (Aleea Poveștilor).** A trail of four special chalets where visitors listen to recorded stories. It is the most talked-about addition of this edition, aimed mainly at children but also working well as a quiet pause in the middle of the crowd.

**A gate of reclaimed timber.** The entrance to Grand Square now carries a gate built from wood salvaged from old buildings — in keeping with the line Sibiu has held for years: real materials, not plastic.

**An expanded food area** with new chalets, and a **larger stage** for concerts and performances.

**The projections onto the historic buildings are back.** This is the single element that separates Sibiu from every other market in Romania: the baroque façades and the famous "eyes of the houses" around the square become the screen.

## Nineteen editions: how Sibiu invented the Romanian Christmas market

In 2007 Sibiu was European Capital of Culture. That same year, the first wooden chalets built on the Central European model appeared in Grand Square, with Austrian backing and expertise. Nothing comparable existed in Romania at the time — the concept of an urban Christmas market with a programme, a stage and juried craftspeople entered the country through Sibiu.

Nineteen editions later, almost every large Romanian city runs its own. Craiova went for surface area and spectacle, Cluj for production values and season length, Bucharest for several locations at once. Sibiu stayed on the original line: natural timber chalets, real craftspeople, live carols, and a square that needs no set dressing because it is the set.

The organiser is the **Events for Tourism Association, Sibiu**, and artisan registrations for the 2026 edition opened as early as 1 May — a detail that says something about how far ahead the selection is made, and why these chalets do not look like market stalls.

## What the „Excellent European Christmas Market” award means

In 2026 Sibiu was named an **„Excellent European Christmas Market” (EECMA)**, one of just **12 markets awarded out of more than 600 evaluated** across Europe.

For a visitor, that is more than a plaque. Criteria of this kind look at the authenticity of the products, the quality of the craftsmanship, how the market integrates with local heritage, and the overall experience — precisely the things that separate a market with artisans from a market with imported stock. The 2026 edition is the first since the award, and the organisers have pushed further in the same direction: more food chalets, the same strict curation on crafts.

Romania has one other market with comparable international recognition — Craiova, recognised by European Best Destinations for sheer scale. They are two completely different philosophies, and the comparison is in our [Craiova guide](/en/calatorii/craiova-christmas-markets-complete-guide).

## A map of the market: what is where

Grand Square is not large. That is both Sibiu's quality and its limit: you can walk the whole market in twenty minutes, but on a Saturday evening you move slowly.

**The centre of the square** — the tree and the stage. The artistic programme concentrates here, and so does the evening crowd.

**The Brukenthal Palace side** — the craft chalets. Ceramics, textiles, woodwork, icons, candles, hand-made tree decorations. This is the part Sibiu's reputation rests on.

**The food area** — expanded in 2026 with new chalets. Mulled wine, beer, hot dishes, sweets. It also has the longest queues between 18:00 and 20:00.

**The ice rink** — built into the square itself, 600 square metres.

**The panoramic wheel** — around 22 metres. From the top you see the chalets, the tree, the rink and the red roofs of the old town all at once.

**Story Alley** — the 2026 addition, four chalets with recorded tales.

**The Enchanted Elevator** — the themed ride up to Santa's House.

Beyond Grand Square, the atmosphere continues naturally into **Piața Mică**, along **Nicolae Bălcescu street** and towards **Huet Square**, all less than two minutes' walk away. They are not part of the market, but they are lit, full of terraces and — important for the budget — full of restaurants cheaper than those with a direct view of the chalets.

Sibiu International Airport sits about 5 km from the centre, one of the shortest airport-to-city hops in Romania. A taxi or Bolt reaches Grand Square in roughly 15 minutes for 25–40 lei (€5–8).

## Parking

The old town is pedestrianised, so cars cannot enter Grand Square. Park at the edge of the centre — the car parks around Piața Cibin or along the outer boulevards — and walk five to ten minutes.

On December weekends the nearer spaces fill by midday. If your hotel has its own parking, leave the car there for the whole stay: old Sibiu is walkable end to end in twenty minutes.

## Where to eat

The golden rule of Sibiu: **restaurants directly on Grand Square charge 30–50% more than those on the side streets** for the same cooking. Walk ten paces sideways and you save a meal.

**Traditional and Transylvanian:** **Crama Sibiul Vechi** — an authentic cellar setting and the best-known name for local cooking. **Hermania** — a modern menu with local influences, in the old town. **La Turn** — on Grand Square, with a view of the Council Tower.

**What to eat:** ciorbă de burtă, the garlicky tripe soup (15–25 lei at a traditional restaurant), sarmale with polenta (25–40 lei a portion), Cluj-style cabbage (30–45 lei), grilled mici, and the warm Sibiu pretzels with poppy or sesame seeds sold on the street in Piața Mică.

**Typical 2026 prices:** a full lunch at a central restaurant 60–100 lei per person, a half-litre beer 12–18 lei, a coffee 10–15 lei.

## What else to see in Sibiu

The market owns the evenings. The daylight hours belong to the city — and Sibiu has the best-preserved medieval centre in Romania.

**The Council Tower** — the 13th-century tower that once guarded the entrance to the citadel. Climb to the top for the panorama over Grand Square and the red roofs: the best overall photograph of the market is taken from up here. Ticket: around 10–15 lei.

**Brukenthal Palace** — one of Romania's most important art museums, in an 18th-century baroque palace right on Grand Square. Ticket: around 30 lei.

**The Bridge of Lies and Piața Mică** — Romania's first cast-iron bridge (1859), a few steps from the market. Crossing is free and takes ten seconds, but it is compulsory.

**The eyes of the houses** — the attic windows shaped like eyes that watch the street from almost every roof in the old town. They are the reason Sibiu looks the way it does and resembles no other Romanian city.

**Huet Square and the Evangelical Cathedral** — pure Gothic, a few steps from Grand Square.

**The ASTRA Museum** — the largest open-air museum in Europe, on the edge of the city in the Dumbrava forest. It has its own winter charm, but dress warmly: it is all outdoors and involves a lot of walking.

## Day trips

**Alba Iulia (75 km, 1 hour).** The Alba Carolina citadel, one of the best-restored fortifications in Romania, with its own holiday programme. See our [Alba Iulia guide](/en/experiente/alba-iulia-complete-guide).

**Sighișoara (90 km, 1h30).** The inhabited medieval citadel, with snow on the roofs. See our [Sighișoara guide](/en/experiente/sighisoara-complete-guide).

**The Transfăgărășan** is closed on its alpine section in winter. Do not plan it in December; only the Bâlea Cascadă area remains accessible, by cable car.

## Weekend itinerary

**Friday.** Arrive in the afternoon, check in. 16:30 — walk into Grand Square in the blue hour and catch the façade projections. A circuit of the chalets, mulled wine, one turn on the panoramic wheel for the view from above. 19:30 — dinner booked on a side street, not on the square.

**Saturday.** Morning: the Council Tower for the panorama, then Brukenthal. Lunch in the Lower Town. Afternoon: Piața Mică, the Bridge of Lies, Huet Square. 17:00 — one session on the ice rink. Evening: a second pass through the market, this time for the food.

**Sunday.** The ASTRA Museum in the morning, wrapped up warm, or a run out to Alba Iulia, then the road home.

## What to buy

Of all the Romanian markets, Sibiu is the one where it is actually worth shopping rather than only eating. Its roughly 120 chalets gather exhibitors from dozens of counties — the organiser counts 36 — plus traders from Italy and Hungary, and the selection is made on the basis of hand-made goods.

What you will realistically find: Horezu and Corund ceramics, hand-worked textiles and traditional blouses, wooden objects, icons painted on glass and wood, beeswax candles, honey and preserves from small producers, natural soaps, tree decorations that look nothing like supermarket ones, mountain cheeses, smoked meats and țuică plum brandy.

One honest warning: there are chalets here too selling imported goods at a markup. The simple test is to ask who made the piece. Real craftspeople answer immediately, and usually tell you more than you asked.

## With children

Sibiu is probably the most family-friendly Romanian market, because everything sits within fifty metres: the ice rink, the carousel, the children's train, the Enchanted Elevator and Story Alley are all in the same square — unlike Cluj, where the rink is a fifteen-minute walk away.

**The best window is 15:00–18:00.** You get the afternoon light, short queues for the rides, and you can leave before the square becomes hard to cross with a small child.

**The Golden Ticket makes sense here.** At 99 lei for unlimited access for a day to the wheel, carousel, children's train and Enchanted Elevator, a child who wants to repeat anything pays it off by the fourth ride.

**The ice rink** covers 600 square metres and holds around 300 skaters, so even at weekends you will get on. Skates are hired on site for 30 lei.

**Story Alley** is the built-in break: four chalets where a child listens to recorded tales, out of the wind and standing still.

## Accessibility and practical details

Grand Square is flat and fully paved, so it is navigable with a pushchair or a wheelchair, but **the cobblestones are uncomfortable** and turn slippery when they freeze. Chalet counters are high, and at peak hours the gaps between them narrow considerably.

Public toilets are signposted in the square; at weekends, a café on a side street is the faster option.

Most chalets take cards, but cash moves faster in the evening queues. 100–150 lei in cash covers an evening for two.

Dogs are allowed in the square on a lead, though the crowds and the grill embers are worth watching for.

## Three days in Sibiu

If you have a day beyond the weekend itinerary above, the best use of it is a morning excursion and a second evening at the market, this time without a list.

**Option 1 — Alba Iulia.** An hour's drive, the Alba Carolina citadel, the changing of the guard, the cathedral. Back in Sibiu by 16:00, in time for the projections.

**Option 2 — The ASTRA Museum.** A full morning in the Dumbrava forest among houses, mills and wooden churches brought from across the country. In winter it is empty and quiet, but you walk a lot — dress as you would for the mountains.

**Option 3 — Păltiniș.** Forty-five minutes to Romania's oldest mountain resort. When there is no snow in the city, there usually is up there.

**Option 4 — Sighișoara.** An hour and a half to the inhabited medieval UNESCO citadel. The best combination if you want two medieval towns in one trip, and it links naturally to our [Transylvania road trip](/en/inchirieri-auto/romania-road-trip-transylvania).

## Weather

Sibiu is colder than Bucharest and more likely to get snow than Cluj or Craiova, sitting at the foot of the Cindrel Mountains. December highs usually fall between **0 and 7°C**, with sub-zero nights.

Waterproof shoes with good grip — the cobbles in Grand Square turn slippery when they freeze. Layers rather than one thick coat. Touchscreen gloves.

## Sibiu compared with the other Romanian markets

| City | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Sibiu** | Authentic medieval setting, projections on historic buildings, rink in the square, EECMA distinction | Small square, crowds quickly, expensive weekend accommodation |
| **Cluj-Napoca** | 43-day season, festival-grade production, a large city around it, direct flights | The rink is elsewhere, above-average prices |
| **Craiova** | The largest by area, spectacular installations | Surprisingly high prices, less to do in the city |
| **Brașov** | Mountain setting, pairs with Poiana Brașov | Small market |
| **Bucharest** | Easy to reach, several venues | Fragmented experience |

Sibiu wins "most beautiful" without argument. It does not win "most to do" — for that, see [Cluj](/en/calatorii/cluj-napoca-christmas-markets-complete-guide). Nor "cheapest": at 49 lei for a portion of sarmale, Sibiu is no longer a budget destination.

Against a classic Central European market, though, the maths still favours it: see our guides to [Vienna](/en/calatorii/vienna-christmas-markets-complete-guide) and [Budapest](/en/calatorii/budapest-christmas-markets-complete-guide), where mulled wine starts at €5–6 rather than Sibiu's €3.

## Practical tips

**Do not eat on Grand Square if you are watching the budget.** Ten paces sideways, the same cooking, 30–50% cheaper.

**Only buy the Golden Ticket if you have children.** For adults, paying per ride is cheaper.

**Book the Enchanted Elevator online.** Online tickets exist precisely to skip the queue, which is serious on Saturday evenings.

**Come Monday to Thursday.** Sibiu is a small, dense market; the weekend turns a stroll into a scrum.

**Climb the Council Tower before sunset.** The panorama over the lit square from above is the best photograph available here — and most visitors miss it entirely.

**Check the live stream.** The camera above tells you in ten seconds whether to set out now or wait an hour.

---

## Is it worth it?

Sibiu does not have Romania's largest Christmas market, nor its longest, nor its cheapest. It has the only one that does not look built for the occasion.

The chalets stand in a square that looks just as good in March. The projections fall on real baroque façades, not panels. The ice rink is in the middle of the market, not fifteen minutes away. And the 2026 European distinction confirms what anyone who has been already knew: Sibiu is not imitating a Central European Christmas market. It is one.

Come on a Tuesday, stay until Thursday, climb the tower at sunset.
