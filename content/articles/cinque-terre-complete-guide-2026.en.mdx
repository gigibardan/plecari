Cinque Terre doesn't prepare you. You arrive by train from La Spezia or Florence, the carriages go through a series of dark tunnels in the cliff, and then suddenly the train stops and you step out into a village that appears to have grown directly from the vertical rock face — houses in ochre, terracotta, pink, and pale yellow stacked above each other, a harbour where fishing boats still go out in the morning, and behind and below everything, the Ligurian Sea in a shade of blue that has no equivalent name in English.

Five villages. No cars (there are no roads connecting them to each other, only to the world above). Connected by the coastal railway completed in 1874 (before which they were accessible only by sea or by the steep cliff paths), by the ferry in season, and by the famous walking trails. UNESCO World Heritage since 1997, National Park since 1999.

This guide covers everything for 2026: the exact state of every trail, the Cinque Terre Card explained honestly, the ferry system, which village suits which type of visitor, the food that's genuinely local, and the secrets that the crowded main path misses entirely.

## 2026 Trail Status — The Complete, Honest Update

This is the most important section for anyone planning to hike. The trail situation in 2026 is more complex than most guides suggest:

### Via dell'Amore (Riomaggiore → Manarola) — OPEN with Restrictions

The most famous section — the coastal path between Riomaggiore and Manarola. Closed in 2012 after a devastating landslide. Restored over 12 years at a cost of €22 million. Fully reopened 9 August 2024.

**How it works in 2026:**
- One-way only: Riomaggiore to Manarola (not reversible)
- Timed entry: you book a 30-minute slot in advance via the National Park portal
- Maximum 200 people per slot
- Access is now **included in the standard Cinque Terre Card** (previously required an extra €10 supplement, eliminated March 2026)
- The path is flat, paved, approximately 1 km, takes 20 minutes

**The honest assessment:** The Via dell'Amore is beautiful — the engineered path carved into the cliff with the sea directly below, the tunnel sections, the restored benches and railings. But the managed system has changed its character. It is now a 20-minute supervised walk rather than a place to sit and linger. Graffiti (which covered every surface in its previous incarnation) is now strictly prohibited — the walls are pristine.

Local guides who know the area well often recommend the **Via Beccara** instead — the free, unmanaged path over the hill connecting the same two villages. You pick it up above Riomaggiore station (walk up Via Signorini to the junction with Via Tracastello). It takes longer (45–60 minutes vs 20 minutes), climbs more, but gives you unobstructed panoramic views and complete solitude compared to the managed path below.

### Manarola — Corniglia Section — CLOSED until 2028

This section has been closed for years due to ongoing landslide damage and geological instability. Reopening is not anticipated before 2028–2029. This is the section that most guidebooks written before 2022 describe as the easy, flat coastal walk.

**The alternatives:**
- Train: 3 minutes between Manarola and Corniglia
- High inland route via Volastra (trails 506 and 586): adds approximately 600 metres of elevation and 90 minutes compared to the closed coastal route — but gives extraordinary panoramic views of the whole Cinque Terre coast

### Corniglia — Vernazza Section — OPEN

The classic harder coastal section. Approximately 2 hours, genuine ups and downs, the best views of Vernazza's harbour as you descend. Requires a valid Cinque Terre Card.

### Vernazza — Monterosso Section — CLOSED (from February 2026)

A dry-stone wall collapse after heavy rainfall in February 2026 closed this section. No confirmed reopening date as of the time of writing. Check parcnazionale5terre.it for current status before your visit.

**The alternative:** Train (2 minutes) or ferry (20 minutes, in season).

### Alta Via delle Cinque Terre — The Ridge Route (FREE, Always Open)

The high-altitude inland trail that runs along the ridge above all five villages — the most spectacular panoramic hiking in the park, with continuous views of the coastline below, the Ligurian Sea, and on clear days, Corsica. **No Cinque Terre Card required. No charge. Almost no crowds.** This is the alternative that experienced hikers choose when the coastal path is crowded or closed.

The ridge route connects the sanctuaries above each village (Sanctuary of Madonna di Montenero above Riomaggiore, Sanctuary of Madonna di Gazzo above Manarola, Sanctuary of San Bernardino above Vernazza, Sanctuary of Madonna di Soviore above Monterosso). You can combine sections of the Alta Via with village descents for extraordinary half-day or full-day circuits.

## The Five Villages — What Makes Each One Different

### Riomaggiore — The Gateway

The southernmost village, the first you reach arriving from La Spezia (about 8 km north). Built into a narrow valley that drops steeply from the Ligurian hills to a small natural harbour, with tower houses painted in shades of ochre, pink, and burnt sienna rising on both sides of a single main street — Via Colombo — that leads down toward the harbour.

The village name comes from Rio Maggiore, the stream (now channelled under the main street) around which it grew. The harbour is too small for fishing boats of significant size — it was always a village of small-scale fishing, and some families still go out in traditional wooden boats.

**Best for:** Day visitors arriving from La Spezia, starting point for Via dell'Amore, those wanting a lively main street and reasonable accommodation options.

**Don't miss:** The harbour at dusk — small, intimate, with the multicoloured houses reflected in the water. The castle (free to enter, basic, but good views from the promontory). The Church of San Giovanni Battista (14th century, Gothic-Ligurian style).

**Practical:** Most visitors arrive here first. The main street (Via Colombo) becomes crowded between 10:00 and 16:00 — visit the harbour early morning or evening.

### Manarola — The Iconic Image

The village that appears on most Cinque Terre photographs — a cluster of colourful houses stacked against the cliff face, reflected in the small inlet below, with the dry-stone terraced vineyards rising steeply behind. Many photographers consider this the single most photogenic village in Italy.

**The photograph:** The classic shot is taken from the elevated path above the village (Punta Bonfiglio overlook) at golden hour, when the warm light turns the ochre and terracotta facades intense. The viewpoint is reached via a 10-minute walk from the village centre.

**The wine:** Manarola is at the centre of Sciacchetrà production — the rare amber dessert wine of Cinque Terre. The vineyards on the steep terraces above the village are tended partly by monorail (a single-track cable system used to transport equipment and grapes on slopes too steep for any wheeled vehicle). Small cantinas in the village sell Sciacchetrà directly.

**Best for:** Romantic couples, photographers, those wanting the most picturesque village.

**Practical:** Very limited accommodation, books out months ahead in summer. If you can't get a room here, day-trip from La Spezia or Monterosso.

### Corniglia — The Hilltop Village

The only inland Cinque Terre village — sitting on a 100-metre-high promontory sheer above the sea, with no harbour (no ferry stop). It's the smallest and least-visited of the five, and therefore the most peaceful.

Arriving by train, you face a choice: take the shuttle bus to the village entrance (€2.50), or climb the 377 steps of the Lardarina staircase — a long flight of brick steps built in 2001, steep but manageable, with views of the coast below as you climb.

**Character:** Corniglia has a markedly agricultural rather than maritime character — the terraced vineyards are more visible, the main square (Largo Taragio) is a genuine gathering place for local residents, the bars and restaurants are slightly cheaper than in Vernazza or Monterosso.

**The view:** The Santa Maria panoramic terrace at the far end of the village offers one of the best views of the whole Cinque Terre coast — Manarola visible to the south, Vernazza to the north.

**Best for:** Those wanting to escape the crowds, photographers, visitors who value peace over beach access.

### Vernazza — The Perfect Harbour

Consistently voted the most beautiful village in Cinque Terre by visitors who stay long enough to form an opinion rather than just photograph. The harbour is protected by a medieval promontory with the Doria Castle at its tip, the piazzetta (small square) opens directly onto the sea, and the church tower is reflected in the water of the harbour.

Vernazza was damaged by catastrophic flooding in 2011 (as was Monterosso) — a rainfall event that deposited enormous quantities of mud and debris. The village recovered through a years-long effort, and the main street (Via Roma) and harbour piazza are fully restored.

**The gelato:** Gelateria Il Porticciolo on Via Roma has a consistently excellent reputation for house-made gelato — particularly the local flavours (limone, basil, Sciacchetrà).

**Best for:** First-time visitors who want the quintessential Cinque Terre experience, anyone interested in the medieval castle and harbour atmosphere.

**Practical:** The Corniglia-Vernazza trail gives you the dramatic descent into Vernazza with the harbour appearing below you — one of the great walk-arrival moments in Italy. Arrive by trail from Corniglia rather than by train if you can.

### Monterosso al Mare — The Practical Choice

The largest, most touristic, and most practical of the five villages. Split into two halves by a headland: the old town (Vecchio) with its medieval caruggi (narrow alleys) and the church of San Giovanni Battista (Gothic-Ligurian, 14th century), and the newer Fegina quarter with the area's only real sandy beach.

Monterosso is the only Cinque Terre village with a proper sandy beach (300 metres, partly free, partly organised beach clubs at €20–30/day per sunlounger). In July–August, the beach is extremely crowded.

**The anchovy:** Monterosso is the home of the Cinque Terre anchovy (*acciuga di Monterosso*) — local fishermen still salt and pack anchovies in the traditional way, producing a product regarded as among the finest in Italy. You can buy them directly from producers in the old town. The lemon granita (in season) uses local Sfusato Ligure lemons.

**Best for:** Those wanting beach access, families, visitors with most accommodation, the best base for multi-day visits.

**The giant:** On the sea-facing wall of the Fegina quarter, a giant figure emerging from the cliff — Il Gigante, a 14-metre concrete Neptune figure installed in 1910, originally holding a terrace above. The terrace collapsed; the hands are still extended. Storm damage has degraded the figure significantly.

## Getting There — All Routes

### From Florence

Fastest option: **Frecciargento direct train** from Firenze Santa Maria Novella to La Spezia Centrale — approximately 1h30, €30–60 depending on booking advance. From La Spezia, the Cinque Terre train (included in Cinque Terre Card) takes 8 minutes to Riomaggiore, the first village. **Total: approximately 2 hours from central Florence to Cinque Terre.**

### From Pisa

Train from Pisa Centrale to La Spezia Centrale: approximately 1 hour, €10–15. La Spezia to Riomaggiore: 8 minutes. **Total: approximately 1h15.**

Pisa Airport (PSA) is a viable entry point for Cinque Terre visits from Romania — direct flights from Bucharest with Wizz Air from €50 return. From PSA to Pisa Centrale: shuttle bus 10 minutes.

### From Genoa

Train from Genova Piazza Principe or Brignole to La Spezia or directly to Cinque Terre villages: approximately 1.5–2 hours, €10–20.

Genoa Airport (GOA): small airport with limited connections; less practical from Romania.

### From Milan

Train from Milano Centrale to La Spezia: approximately 2.5 hours (Frecciarossa or Intercity), €30–60. Then Cinque Terre train from La Spezia.

### Getting Around Once There

**The train** is the practical backbone. Regional trains stop at all five villages and at La Spezia and Levanto. Journey time between adjacent villages: 2–4 minutes. Frequency in peak season: every 15–30 minutes. Included in Cinque Terre Treno MS Card.

**The ferry** (April–November only) is the scenic option. Operated by Navigazione Golfo dei Poeti. Connects Riomaggiore, Manarola, Vernazza, and Monterosso (Corniglia has no harbour — no ferry stop). Day pass: **€42** (includes Porto Venere). Single ticket La Spezia to Monterosso one-way: approximately €20–25.

**Strategy:** Train for efficiency and getting between villages quickly. Ferry for seeing the coast from the sea — the view of each village from the water is categorically different from the land view. Ideal: train one direction, ferry the other.

**The Cinque Terre Express** (peak season): additional trains specifically for the Cinque Terre route with slightly higher frequency. Check Trenitalia for current schedule.

## The Hike — Sentiero Azzurro (Blue Trail) 2026

The Sentiero Azzurro (SVA) is the legendary coastal path connecting all five villages. In 2026, it cannot be walked as a single continuous route — see the trail status section above. What is walkable:

**Via dell'Amore** (Riomaggiore–Manarola): 1 km, 20 minutes, flat, requires timed booking. Included in Cinque Terre Card.

**Corniglia–Vernazza** (SVA3): approximately 5 km, 2 hours. The most scenic currently open section of the coastal path — begins with a gentle descent from Corniglia, then a dramatic final approach to Vernazza with the harbour visible below. Moderate difficulty.

**For the full coastal experience in 2026:** The only way to traverse all five villages on foot is to combine the walkable sections with train connections for the closed sections.

### The Real Hike — Alta Via delle Cinque Terre

If you're a serious hiker and want an extraordinary experience without the crowds: the **Alta Via delle Cinque Terre** ridge route. Free. Always open. Virtually no crowds even in August.

**Best day circuit from Monterosso:**
- Start at the Sanctuary of Madonna di Soviore (above Monterosso — reach by ATC shuttle bus from Monterosso station, €2)
- Walk the ridge southeast with continuous panoramic views of the entire coast
- Descend into Vernazza (approximately 3 hours)
- Ferry or train back to Monterosso

**Best day circuit from Riomaggiore:**
- Climb to the Sanctuary of Madonna di Montenero (45 minutes, very steep)
- Continue on the ridge to Manarola overlook
- Descend to Manarola
- Via dell'Amore or Via Beccara back to Riomaggiore (or train)

The ridge views show you something the coastal path cannot: the relationship between the villages, the extraordinary extent of the terraced vineyards, and the way the five communities fit into the mountain landscape like characters in a story.

## Food in Cinque Terre

Ligurian cuisine is one of Italy's most distinctive regional traditions — herbs, seafood, and olive oil rather than the butter and cream of the north, with flavours influenced by centuries of Mediterranean trade. In Cinque Terre specifically, four things matter:

### Pesto alla Genovese

The genuine article — basil grown on Ligurian hillsides (smaller leaves, more aromatic than greenhouse basil), extra-virgin Ligurian olive oil, pine nuts (not toasted), Parmigiano Reggiano and Pecorino Sardo, garlic, and coarse salt, ground in a marble mortar with a wooden pestle. The texture is different from the processed versions — slightly coarser, more fragrant, less oil-heavy.

Pesto classes are available in all five villages — a 2-hour class where you make your own with a mortar and pestle, keep the result, and eat pasta dressed with it for lunch. Book via [GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/cinque-terre-l2626/?partner_id=EKAA80H&utm_medium=online_publisher). One of the best value culinary experiences in Italy.

**Trofie al pesto:** The traditional shape — short, twisted pasta made from durum wheat (sometimes with chestnut flour for a nuttier flavour) that holds pesto sauce in its ridges. The combination of trofie and pesto is the definitive Ligurian pasta dish.

### Acciughe (Anchovies of Monterosso)

The anchovies fished in the waters off Monterosso and salted using traditional techniques have DOP (Protected Designation of Origin) status. They're larger, meatier, and less sharp than standard tinned anchovies. Eaten on focaccia, in pasta sauces, or marinated in lemon juice as a crudo. Buy them directly from producers in Monterosso old town.

**Acciughe al verde:** Anchovies marinated with parsley, garlic, and olive oil — a typical Ligurian antipasto.

### Focaccia Ligure

Different from Genovese focaccia (which is thicker) — the Cinque Terre version is thinner, crispier, olive-oil rich, with coarse salt on top. The best version: focaccia col formaggio (stuffed with fresh Stracchino cheese) from a forno (bakery) in any village. Buy it in the morning when it comes out of the oven.

### Sciacchetrà

The rarest wine of Cinque Terre — described above under Manarola. Seek it in cantinas in Manarola and Corniglia. A small glass (75–100 ml) as a dessert wine with local biscuits: €15–25. A bottle: €50–100+ for the genuine article.

### Farinata

Chickpea flour flatbread cooked in a wood-fired oven in a wide copper pan, golden and slightly crispy at the edges. Ligurian street food, available from friggitorie throughout the villages. €3–4 for a slice.

## When to Visit — The Real Seasonal Guide

**April:** Trails fully operational, wildflowers on the cliffsides, wisteria still blooming in some villages, green terraced vineyards. Crowds low. Ferry may not yet be running (usually starts late March/early April). **Excellent.**

**May:** Peak spring. All transport operational. Lemon fragrance from Sfusato Ligure trees. Manageable crowds on weekdays, more crowded weekends. Basil at its most aromatic — best time for pesto. **Excellent.**

**June:** Transition. First beach visitors in Monterosso. Still manageable early in the month; crowding increases after mid-June. Water temperature reaches swimming comfort (21–23°C). **Good to very good.**

**July–August:** The difficult months. 3+ million visitors per year and most arrive in these two months. The Corniglia-Vernazza trail can be so crowded you walk in a single-file queue. Accommodation must be booked 4–6 months ahead. Beach clubs in Monterosso packed. Heat intense on south-facing cliffs (35–40°C at midday). **Possible but stressful without careful planning.**

**September:** The best month for experienced travellers. Water at its warmest (24–26°C). Crowds noticeably thinner after mid-September. Grape harvest on the terraced vineyards begins — you may see the monorail carrying crates of Sciacchetrà grapes down the cliff. Restaurants fully operational. **Excellent.**

**October:** Beautiful light, dramatic autumn colours on the hillsides, very few tourists. Some accommodation and restaurants begin closing. Ferries reduce and eventually stop. Possibility of autumn storms. **Very good for hikers and photographers.**

**November–March:** Quiet to empty. Many restaurants closed, ferries not running, some trails closed for maintenance. The villages have genuine winter character — locals fishing, fog on the cliffs, complete absence of tourists. For those who want to see what these places actually are, this is when to visit.

## Practical Tips — What Most Guides Don't Tell You

**Avoid the train on weekday mornings in July–August.** The 09:00–11:00 trains from La Spezia toward Riomaggiore are packed to the point of structural concern. Go earlier (first train around 06:00) or later (after 14:00 when day-trippers begin returning).

**The villages in order of crowd intensity (highest to lowest):** Vernazza > Monterosso > Riomaggiore > Manarola > Corniglia. If you want peace, walk toward Corniglia.

**Don't eat at the first restaurant you see in any village.** The trattoria on the harbour piazza in Vernazza or the restaurant visible from the ferry pier in Manarola charges 40–60% more than equivalent quality 50 metres inland on any side street.

**The lemon granita** in Monterosso (made from local Sfusato Ligure lemons, completely different from standard lemon sorbet) is one of the specific pleasures of this coast. Find it at any bar in the old town.

**Book Via dell'Amore separately.** Even though it's included in the Cinque Terre Card, you still need to book a timed slot via the National Park portal. Do this when you buy the Card — slots fill up quickly in summer.

**The Corniglia 377 steps:** If you have significant luggage or mobility issues, always take the shuttle bus from Corniglia station to the village. The staircase is fine for hiking; brutal with heavy bags.

**La Spezia as a base:** More honest accommodation availability (book 2–4 weeks ahead instead of months), lower prices (hotels 40% cheaper than Cinque Terre villages), direct train to all five villages in 8–20 minutes. The city itself has excellent restaurants and a Friday-Saturday covered market. A thoroughly underrated base.

**Photography:** The famous Manarola sunset view from Punta Bonfiglio involves waiting in a crowd. Arrive at least 45 minutes before sunset. Alternatively, the same view from the small cemetery steps (5 minutes walk further on the same path) usually has far fewer people.

## Day Trips from Cinque Terre

### Porto Venere

South of La Spezia, connected by ferry or bus. A medieval village with a Romanesque church (San Pietro) dramatically positioned on a cliff above the sea, the Grotta Arpaia (Byron's Cave, where the poet allegedly swam), and the gateway to the Palmaria, Tino, and Tinetto islands. Ferry from La Spezia: approximately 30 minutes, included in day ferry pass.

### Portovenere to the Islands

The three islands (Palmaria, Tino, Tinetto) off Porto Venere form an excursion available from most Cinque Terre ferry operators — boat circuit around all three, €15 supplement on the day pass.

### Portofino and the Ligurian Riviera

Portofino is the most glamorous town on the Ligurian coast — 90 minutes by ferry or bus from La Spezia (via Genoa). Very expensive, very beautiful, worth a half-day excursion if budget allows.

## Practical Information

**Shoes:** Non-negotiable — proper hiking shoes with grip. The coastal paths are rocky, uneven, often damp, and steep. Fashion trainers will cause blisters and possibly a twisted ankle. This is the single most universal piece of advice from everyone who has hiked here.

**Water:** Carry a refillable water bottle. There are drinking fountains in every village. In summer, dehydration on the sun-exposed cliffsides is a real risk.

**No cars:** There are no roads between the five villages. Once you're in the villages, everything is on foot. If you arrive by car, park at La Spezia or Levanto and take the train.

**Mobile signal:** Variable on the trails, consistently good in the villages. EU roaming applies for Romanian SIM cards (Cinque Terre is in Italy). An eSIM for Italy is rarely necessary but available from [Airalo](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3) if your plan has limited EU data.

**Weather changes rapidly.** The Ligurian microclimate creates sudden fog, wind, and rain. A compact rain jacket is worth carrying even in July.

**Trail closures happen without warning.** Always check parcnazionale5terre.it the morning of your hike. A trail that was open yesterday may have a barrier across it today following overnight rain.
