If you've ever used a standard bank card abroad and noticed mysterious fees eating into your budget, you're not alone. **Here's what a two-week trip to Japan costs you in bank fees alone:**

- Traditional bank card (3% FX + €5 ATM fees): **€45–60 lost**
- Revolut Premium: **€0 lost** (plus travel insurance included)
- Wise: **~€6 lost** (at 0.42% mid-market rate)

Over the course of a year with multiple trips, the difference can reach €200–500 — enough for another flight.

This guide covers **15 alternative financial services** for travelers: their real fees, availability across Europe, security features, tax reporting obligations, and exactly which ones work best for which travel scenarios in 2027.

Monese was designed specifically for **expats, immigrants, and people without a permanent local address** in their country of residence. Opening a standard bank account in a new country often requires proof of address — a utility bill, a lease agreement — which you might not have in your first weeks or months. ### Key Advantages of Monese

The ability to open an account in **minutes, using just a passport or ID**, with no address proof required, makes Monese the go-to service for people in transition — newly arrived in a new country, waiting for a lease to start, or working seasonally across multiple countries.

## 11. Crypto Cards — Spending Your Digital Assets

### How Crypto Cards Work

Crypto debit cards link to a cryptocurrency wallet or exchange account. When you make a purchase, your crypto is **instantly converted to fiat currency** at the current market rate, and the merchant receives their local currency. You're essentially spending crypto without the merchant needing to know or accept crypto directly.

### The Tax Implication Most People Miss

In most EU jurisdictions (including Germany, France, Spain, and Romania under proposed crypto tax frameworks), **each conversion of crypto to fiat is a taxable event** — even if it happens automatically at the point of sale. If you bought Bitcoin at €20,000 and spend it when it's worth €60,000, you technically realized a €40,000 capital gain at that moment, even though it was just a coffee purchase.

This is a critical consideration. Some users are unaware that frequent crypto card spending creates dozens or hundreds of small taxable events throughout the year. Check your country's specific crypto tax rules before heavy use.

## 12. CRS (Common Reporting Standard): What Governments Know About Your Fintech Accounts

### The Global Tax Transparency Network

The **Common Reporting Standard (CRS)** is an OECD framework for automatic exchange of financial account information between over 120 countries. All EU member states, the UK, Switzerland, and most developed economies participate.

This means your bank or fintech in another EU country is **legally required to report your account details to their local tax authority**, which then forwards the data to your country of tax residence.

### What Gets Reported to Your Home Tax Authority

For each account that qualifies for reporting, the financial institution sends:

- Full name and address
- Tax identification number (TIN/equivalent)
- Account number
- **Year-end balance** (or closing balance if closed during the year)
- **Gross income** received: interest, dividends, proceeds from asset sales

**What is NOT reported:** Your everyday spending (card payments for groceries, restaurants, hotels). CRS targets investment income and account balances, not consumption.

### CRS Reporting Status by Service (European Users)

| Service | CRS Participant | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Revolut Bank UAB | Yes (Lithuania) | Full EU bank, mandatory CRS |
| Wise | Yes (Belgium) | EMI, subject to CRS |
| N26 | Yes (Germany) | Full EU bank, mandatory CRS |
| Monese | Yes (UK) | EMI, UK-EU CRS exchange |
| Paysera | Yes (Lithuania) | Payment institution, CRS applicable |
| Bunq | Yes (Netherlands) | Full EU bank, mandatory CRS |
| Skrill/Neteller | Yes (UK) | EMI, subject to CRS |
| Bybit Card | Varies | Exchange domicile varies; MiCA increasing obligations |
| WhiteBIT Nova | Varies | Similar to Bybit |

### What This Means in Practice

If you have a Revolut account with a €15,000 year-end balance and have received €2,000 in interest or investment income, **your local tax authority may already know about this**. If you haven't declared this income on your tax return, you're at risk of investigation.

CRS has dramatically reduced the ability to hold undeclared income in foreign fintech accounts. The assumption that "it's outside the country, they can't see it" is no longer accurate for EU residents.

### MiCA and Crypto Reporting (2024-2027)

The EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation, implemented from 2024-2025, progressively increases reporting requirements for crypto exchanges operating in the EU. By 2026-2027:

- EU-licensed crypto exchanges must report customer transactions exceeding €1,000 aggregated per year to tax authorities
- DAC8 (the EU directive on crypto asset information reporting) extends CRS principles to crypto exchanges
- Bybit, Crypto.com, and other exchanges with EU licenses are subject to these reporting obligations

The notion that crypto is "invisible to the taxman" is increasingly outdated for EU residents.

## 15. Security: What Happens When Your Card Is Lost or Stolen

### Instant Card Blocking — The Game-Changer

Every major fintech (Revolut, Wise, N26, Monese, Bunq, Vivid) lets you **freeze your card in seconds from the app**. No hold music, no waiting in a phone queue, no explaining to a customer service agent. One tap, card frozen. Another tap, card unfrozen.

This functionality alone is a compelling reason to switch from a traditional bank card as your primary travel card.

### Steps If Your Card Is Stolen Abroad

1. Open your app → freeze the card immediately (even if you're not 100% sure)
2. Review recent transactions for any unauthorized use
3. Report unauthorized transactions within the app
4. Order a replacement card (3–10 days standard, 1–2 days express on higher plans)
5. For Revolut Premium/Metal users: continue using the virtual card while waiting for the physical replacement

### Deposit Protection Summary

| Service | Type | Deposit Protection |

| N26 | Licensed EU Bank | Up to €100,000 (German DGS) |

| Wise | EMI (Belgium) | Safeguarding (not DGS) |

For everyday travel spending, the distinction between DGS and safeguarding matters little — you're not keeping your life savings in a travel card. For holding larger amounts, prefer licensed banks (Revolut, N26, Bunq).

## 17. 10 Common Mistakes Travelers Make With Fintech Cards

**1. Accepting Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC)**
When an ATM or POS terminal offers to charge you "in your home currency," always decline. DCC adds an invisible 3–8% markup by the terminal operator, on top of your card's own fees.

**2. Making Frequent Small ATM Withdrawals**
Every ATM withdrawal counts against your monthly limit or incurs a fixed fee. Withdraw larger amounts less frequently to minimize per-transaction costs.

**3. Weekend Currency Conversion on Revolut Standard**
The 0.5–1% weekend surcharge is small but adds up. For large conversions, wait until Monday morning or convert Thursday/Friday afternoon.

**4. Using a Non-Bank ATM**
Private ATMs (in convenience stores, tourist areas, bars) often charge additional fees regardless of your card. Always prefer bank-branded ATMs.

**5. Topping Up via Credit Card**
Several fintech apps (including Revolut) treat top-ups from a credit card as a "cash advance" on your credit card, triggering the credit card's cash advance fee (typically 2–5%). Always fund via bank transfer.

**6. Ignoring the Inactivity Fee**
Skrill and Neteller charge monthly inactivity fees after 12 months of no use. If you signed up for one of these for a specific purpose, make a small transaction annually to keep the account active.

**7. Not Enabling 3D Secure**
Most fintech cards offer 3D Secure (additional authentication for online payments). Enable it for all online transactions to protect against unauthorized use.

**8. Assuming Crypto Cards Work Everywhere**
Bybit Card and other crypto cards sometimes face merchant-side restrictions. Always carry a traditional card as backup.

**9. Not Declaring Investment Income**
CRS means your foreign fintech account income may be reported to your local tax authority. Undeclared interest, dividends, or capital gains from investments held in fintech accounts can trigger investigations.

**10. Carrying Only One Card**
Technology fails. Cards get demagnetized. Apps crash during critical moments. Always travel with at least two different cards, ideally from different networks (one Visa, one Mastercard).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Revolut safe for keeping larger amounts of money?

Yes, up to €100,000, which is covered by the Lithuanian Deposit Guarantee Scheme (the EU equivalent of FDIC in the US). Above €100,000, you should diversify across multiple institutions. For everyday travel, this limit is more than sufficient.

### Does Wise or Revolut give better exchange rates?

It depends on the day and currency. Revolut Premium gives 0% FX with a small markup built into the interbank rate. Wise charges a transparent percentage (0.37–2% depending on currency) but uses the pure mid-market rate. For common currency pairs (EUR/USD/GBP), the difference is typically under 0.3%. Wise's weekend consistency (no surcharge) is an advantage for weekend travelers.

### Can I use Revolut as my only bank account?

Technically yes for most day-to-day needs, but not recommended as your ONLY account. Revolut lacks physical branches, may not meet official bank statement requirements of some employers or government agencies, and depends on your smartphone being functional. Keep at least one traditional bank account for formal documentation purposes.

### What happens to my Wise or Revolut account if the company goes bankrupt?

For Revolut (licensed bank): up to €100,000 is protected by deposit guarantee. For Wise (EMI): funds held in safeguarding accounts at tier-1 banks are ring-fenced from Wise's own assets — in bankruptcy, they should be returned to customers. Neither scenario has occurred for these major providers, but the legal protections differ.

### How do I know if I need to pay taxes on my fintech account activity?

If you receive income (interest, dividends, freelance payments, investment gains) in any fintech account, you likely need to report it in your country of tax residence regardless of where the account is held. CRS means your local tax authority may already be aware. Consult a local tax advisor for your specific situation.

### Are crypto cards legal to use in the EU?

Yes, crypto cards are legal in the EU. Spending crypto is legal. The tax treatment (each spend may be a taxable disposal) is what creates complexity, not the legality of the act itself. Ensure you're tracking your crypto spending for tax purposes.

### What's the best card for spending in Japan specifically?

Japan still relies heavily on cash. Carry a card with low ATM fees (Wise for first 2 withdrawals/month free, or Revolut within your free limit) and plan to withdraw larger amounts at once from 7-Eleven ATMs (which are reliable and accept most foreign cards).

### Can I open multiple fintech accounts at the same time?

Yes, entirely legal and common. Many travelers hold 3–5 fintech accounts for different purposes: Revolut for everyday spending, Wise for transfers and mid-market rates, Monese as a European IBAN, Payoneer for client payments. There's no prohibition on having multiple accounts.

### Does Bunq actually plant trees?

Yes, with audited reports. The commitment is real and independently verified — it's not just marketing language. Bunq publishes annual sustainability reports with specific tree-planting numbers and partner organizations.

### What's the best card for long-term travel (6+ months)?

Revolut Premium covers you with continuous travel insurance, 0% FX, generous ATM limits, and investment features — all in one app. For extended stays, supplement with Wise for a local account number if you need to receive local payments, and Payoneer if you're earning remotely.
