Greece in 2026 is not just a destination — it is the destination. Bookings are up sharply across all markets as travellers who tried Dubai or Egypt and found prices and logistics disappointing have redirected to a proven, accessible, spectacular destination that delivers reliably. The result: popular islands are fully booked earlier, and the case for September has never been stronger. Here is everything you need to plan the right trip.

## Why Greece works so well as a holiday destination

The case for Greece is not complicated. Within 2–3 hours from most of Europe, you have:

- Water that is genuinely turquoise — not "travel brochure turquoise," actual turquoise, visible from the beach
- Reliable summer weather from May through October with virtually zero rain
- Food that is among the best in the Mediterranean and meaningfully cheaper than Italy or Spain
- History layered so densely that a beach day and a Bronze Age palace are often on the same road
- Infrastructure that has improved dramatically over the past decade — good roads, reliable ferries, consistent accommodation quality

What Greece cannot offer: city sophistication at the level of Paris or Rome, world-class museums (the National Archaeological Museum in Athens is exceptional, but the islands are not museum destinations), or significant nightlife outside Mykonos and Rhodes Town.

For a beach-centred, culturally enriched, affordable Mediterranean holiday, Greece beats comparable alternatives at almost every price point.

## Island by island: which one is right for you

### Crete — the one that does everything

Crete is Greece's largest island at 260 km long and feels, in many respects, like a country of its own. It has mountains over 2,400 metres, Europe's longest gorge (Samaria Gorge, 16 km), three distinct regions with different characters, Bronze Age palaces (Knossos, the centre of Minoan civilisation 3,700 years ago), some of the most extraordinary beaches in Europe, and a culinary tradition that many food writers argue is the best in Greece.

**The beaches:** Elafonisi (pink-tinted sand, shallow turquoise lagoon — genuinely one of the most beautiful beaches on the continent), Balos (accessed by boat or rough road, three shades of blue in one lagoon), Preveli (palm forest, river, beach), Falassarna (wide, sandy, facing west for spectacular sunsets), Vai (unique palm forest beach in the east).

**Beyond the beach:** the Palace of Knossos (€15 entry, 30 minutes from Heraklion — mandatory), the old Venetian harbours of Chania and Rethymno, the White Mountains, the Archaeological Museum of Heraklion (one of the best in the world), and villages in the inland mountains where little has changed in decades.

**Getting there:** direct flights from most European cities to Heraklion (HER) or Chania (CHQ). Flight time from London: 3.5 hours. From Amsterdam or Paris: 3.5–4 hours.

**Prices (7 nights, 2 people, 2026):**
- Budget (self-catering apartment, own food): €700–1,100 including flights
- Mid-range (3-star B&B hotel, some restaurants): €1,000–1,600 including flights
- All-inclusive 4-star: €1,200–2,000 including flights
- Luxury: €2,500–5,000+

**Best for:** families with children, first-time visitors to Greece, travellers who want beach AND culture, food enthusiasts, anyone who wants the most complete Greece experience.

**Worst time to visit:** the third week of August — extremely crowded and at peak prices. Book early or go September.

### Rhodes — the lively one with history

Rhodes has the best medieval old town in Greece — a UNESCO-listed walled city built by the Knights of St. John in the 14th century, with cobbled streets, the Palace of the Grand Master, and a maze of alleyways that somehow still feels inhabited and alive rather than museified.

Beyond the old town: Lindos (a spectacularly situated village with an acropolis above a white-painted hillside above a turquoise bay — the most photographed scene in Rhodes), excellent beaches on the west coast (Faliraki, Tsambika), and the butterfly valley of Petaloudes.

**Character:** more animated than Crete, with a stronger nightlife scene (Faliraki has one of the liveliest strips in Greece), more package-holiday infrastructure, and a slightly younger crowd on average.

**Getting there:** direct flights from most European airports to Rhodes (RHO). Flight time from London: 4 hours.

**Prices (7 nights, 2 people, 2026):** similar to Crete — €800–2,000 including flights depending on accommodation category.

**Best for:** couples (mix of history and beach), young adults, people who want the Old Town experience alongside beach time.

**Car:** useful for reaching the south of the island (Prasonisi, Gennadi) — less essential than in Crete given better public transport.

### Santorini — the expensive icon

Santorini is the most immediately recognisable Greek island — the caldera, the blue-domed churches of Oia, the white cubic architecture draped over volcanic cliffs 300 metres above the sea. These images are accurate and beautiful. So are the crowds and the prices.

In July and August, Santorini hosts approximately 15,000 cruise passengers per day on an island of 15,000 permanent residents. The main streets of Oia are shoulder-to-shoulder by 4 PM, and the famous sunset spot requires positioning yourself by 5 PM at the latest.

**The honest Santorini assessment:** it is spectacular, unmistakably beautiful, and genuinely worth visiting — in the right conditions. Those conditions are: September or October (crowds drop dramatically, prices fall 30–40%), arriving by ferry rather than tender (cruise passengers have the least pleasant experience), and staying in Imerovigli or Akrotiri rather than Oia (equally beautiful, significantly less crowded).

**What to do:** caldera walk from Fira to Oia (9 km, 3–4 hours, free), boat tour of the caldera including hot springs and the volcano (€30–60), wine tasting at one of the volcanic-soil wineries (Santo Wines, Domaine Sigalas), Akrotiri Bronze Age excavation (€12 — a Minoan city preserved under volcanic ash, extraordinary).

**What to skip:** the cable car in peak season (queue is 45+ minutes — the donkey path or walking path is faster), the "instagrammable" blue dome churches in Oia at any time after 10 AM in summer.

**Prices (7 nights, 2 people, high season):** €2,000–5,000 — the most expensive Greek island by a significant margin. September: €1,400–3,000.

**Best for:** couples, honeymoons, special occasions, people with flexibility to go in September.

### Mykonos — parties and design hotels

Mykonos is Greece's premium party destination and a design-hotel showcase. It is expensive, it is beautiful in an austere, Cycladic way (whitewashed cubes, windmills, narrow lanes), and it has some of the best beach clubs in Europe (Paradise Beach, Super Paradise).

It is also the island where a cocktail costs €15, a beach umbrella €40/day, and dinner for two at a good restaurant starts at €100. The beauty and the experience are genuine — but the value proposition is poor compared to any other Greek island.

**Best for:** affluent travellers who specifically want the Mykonos scene, party-focused visitors, people who want the beach club experience and are happy to pay for it.

**Not for:** families with children, budget travellers, anyone who wants to eat well without spending a fortune.

### Corfu — the greenest island

Corfu is the most European-feeling of the major Greek islands, shaped by centuries of Venetian and British occupation into something closer to the Italian Adriatic than the Aegean. Cypress trees, cricket (the British legacy), Venetian fortresses, a UNESCO old town with arcaded streets, and some of the most beautiful sandy bays in Greece (Paleokastritsa, Glyfada, Issos).

Less raw than Crete, less dramatic than Santorini, Corfu delivers consistent quality at mid-range prices with good infrastructure and shorter distances between attractions.

**Prices (7 nights, 2 people):** €900–1,800 including flights.

**Best for:** couples and families looking for a balanced, beautiful, uncomplicated holiday.

### Zakynthos — the photogenic one

Zakynthos is famous for one image: Navagio Beach (Shipwreck Beach) — a rusting freighter on white pebbles in a turquoise cove, visible from the clifftop above and accessible only by boat. It is genuinely one of the most spectacular natural scenes in Greece and one of the most photographed places on earth.

Beyond Navagio: the Blue Caves sea caves (boat tour, €15–20), Loggerhead turtle nesting beaches (the island is a protected sea turtle habitat — night excursions are organised responsibly), and several excellent beaches on the east and south coasts.

**Prices (7 nights, 2 people):** €900–1,800 including flights.

**Best for:** couples, anyone who specifically wants the Navagio experience, beach-focused holidays.

## When to go — the month-by-month breakdown

| Month | Weather | Crowds | Prices | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| May | 24–27°C, sea cold (20°C) | Low | Low | Excellent for sightseeing, sea still cool |
| June | 27–30°C, sea 22–24°C | Medium | Medium | Sweet spot — warm enough, manageable |
| July | 30–34°C, sea 25°C | High | High | Peak season, book everything in advance |
| August | 30–35°C, sea 26°C | Very high | Very high | Most expensive and crowded — avoid if possible |
| September | 28–32°C, sea 25°C | Medium-low | Medium | **Best overall month — optimal conditions** |
| October | 22–26°C, sea 23°C | Low | Low | Good for sightseeing, some closures |

**September is consistently the best month for Greece** — particularly for Santorini, Mykonos, and Rhodes where August crowds are at their most intense. The sea is at its warmest (a full season of sun has heated it to 25–26°C), the weather is outstanding, and prices are 20–30% lower than August peaks.

## Getting to Greece

**By air:** direct flights from London to Athens (3 hours), Heraklion (3.5 hours), Rhodes (4 hours), Corfu (3 hours), Zakynthos (3.5 hours), and Mykonos (3.5 hours). Ryanair, easyJet, Jet2 and charter operators all serve Greek island airports directly from multiple UK and European airports. For best fares: [how to find cheap flights](/en/zboruri/how-to-find-cheap-flights).

**Between islands:** the Greek ferry network is extensive and efficient. From Athens (Piraeus port), ferries reach Santorini in 5–8 hours, Mykonos in 3–5 hours, Crete in 7–9 hours, and Rhodes in 10–16 hours. High-speed ferries (SeaJets, Hellenic Seaways) cut times by 30–40% but cost more. Book ferries at Ferryscanner or directly with operators.

**Internal flights:** Olympic Air and Sky Express operate domestic flights connecting Athens with island airports. Athens–Heraklion costs €40–80 each way and takes 45 minutes — faster than the ferry for longer routes.

## Renting a car in Greece

On Crete and Rhodes, a rental car transforms the holiday. Without one, you are confined to resort areas and expensive organised tours. With one, you reach beaches that most tourists never see — Elafonisi and Balos in Crete, Gennadi and Prasonisi in Rhodes.

**Prices (2026):** small car, 7 days, summer: €250–500. Book online, 4–6 weeks in advance, for the best prices. Airport collection costs 10–15% more — consider picking up in town.

**Insurance:** CDW (basic damage waiver) is included in most rentals but with an excess of €500–1,500. A third-party excess waiver from a specialist (€3–7/day) reduces this to zero and is far cheaper than the counter's own upgrade.

## Practical Greece: what you actually need to know

**No visa required** for EU/EEA citizens. Enter with a national ID card. Non-EU nationals (including UK citizens post-Brexit) may need to check entry requirements.

**No eSIM needed** for Greece — EU free roaming applies, so your home mobile plan works without extra charges. If your trip extends to Turkey or other non-EU destinations, see [best eSIM by destination 2026](/en/esim-roaming/best-esim-by-destination).

**Currency:** Euro. Cards are widely accepted at hotels, larger restaurants, and shops. Keep some cash for small tavernas, village kiosks, and ferry tickets at smaller ports.

**Health:** the European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) covers emergency treatment at public hospitals. However, it does not cover private clinics (which are common in tourist areas), repatriation, or sports/water activity accidents. A travel insurance policy is recommended — see the comparison: [EHIC vs travel insurance](/en/asigurari/ehic-vs-travel-insurance).

**Language:** English is widely spoken in tourist areas. In villages and local tavernas, learning a few words of Greek (kalimera, efharisto) is genuinely appreciated.

**Tap water:** drinkable in most areas of Crete, Rhodes, and Corfu. On smaller or more arid islands (Santorini, Mykonos), bottled water is recommended — the local water supply is limited and sometimes desalinated.

## What to eat and drink

Greek food at its best is exceptional. The problem in heavily touristed areas is that the food is simplified and homogenised for international audiences.

**Where to eat well:** look for tavernas a few streets back from the main waterfront. In Crete particularly, the inland villages serve the most authentic food at the lowest prices — a full meal with wine costs €12–15/person rather than €25–35.

**What to order:**
- **Saganaki** — pan-fried cheese, served flaming in some places. Excellent appetiser.
- **Fresh fish by the kilo** — always look at what came in that day, priced per kilo. Ask the waiter to see the fresh catch. Grilled fish at a good taverna is one of the great Mediterranean experiences.
- **Lamb slow-cooked in a wood oven** (especially in Crete) — a village specialty that rarely appears on tourist menus. Ask locals where to find it.
- **Greek yoghurt with honey** — for breakfast at any hour, from any kafeneio. The quality in Greece versus exported Greek yoghurt is genuinely different.
- **Local wine** — Greek wine (Assyrtiko from Santorini, Moschofilero from the Peloponnese, Retsina if you are feeling adventurous) is underrated and good value.

**Skip:** anything on a menu with photographs, anything a waiter actively invites you into from the street, and any restaurant directly on the most famous squares. These are overpriced without exception.

## Experiences worth booking in advance

For the best experiences in Greece, advance booking is essential — especially in summer:

- **Akrotiri, Santorini** (Bronze Age excavation, €12) — buy online, queues at peak season
- **Palace of Knossos, Crete** (€15 or €30 combined ticket) — timed entry recommended
- **Boat tours** in any popular port (caldera in Santorini, Blue Caves in Zakynthos, Balos lagoon in Crete) — sell out weeks ahead in July–August
- **Sunset spots in Oia, Santorini** — arrive by 5 PM at the latest in summer
