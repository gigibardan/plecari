There is a tourist prejudice about Novi Sad I hear frequently: "It's like Belgrade, but smaller." Wrong. Novi Sad isn't a miniaturised Belgrade — it's a completely different city in character, atmosphere and identity.

<cite index="33-1">Nicknamed the "Athens of Serbia" for its intellectual and artistic tradition, Novi Sad is the capital of the Vojvodina province and Serbia's second city — a blend of Austro-Hungarian influences, Slavic culture and multinationalism (Vojvodina has 6 official languages and 20 ethnic communities) that makes it unique in the region.</cite>

Above the city, on a 40-metre plateau above the Danube, Petrovaradin Fortress dominates — <cite index="38-1">nicknamed the "Gibraltar of the Danube" because it was never conquered militarily throughout history.</cite> And every July, this fortress hosts the EXIT Festival — <cite index="33-1">one of Europe's largest and most acclaimed music festivals, founded in 2000 as a student movement for peace and democracy, with 200,000+ attendees from 70 countries.</cite>

80 km from Belgrade and 40 minutes by fast train, Novi Sad is perfect as a standalone destination or as an extension of a Belgrade city break.

## Novi Sad at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Serbia — not in the EU, not in Schengen.

**Population:** approximately 280,000 — Serbia's second city.

**Currency:** Serbian Dinar (RSD). <cite index="41-1">1 EUR ≈ 117 RSD in 2026.</cite>

**Language:** Serbian (Cyrillic and Latin). Hungarian is co-official in Vojvodina. English is well spoken by younger generations.

**Visa:** EU and Romanian citizens enter without a visa — valid passport or ID, max 90 days.

**Airport:** Belgrade (BEG), 80 km away — 1h30 by bus or 40 minutes by fast train.

**Average budget:** <cite index="41-1">35–55 EUR/day — 20–30% more affordable than Belgrade.</cite>

## How to Get from Romania to Novi Sad

### Step 1 — Flight OTP → BEG (Belgrade)

Wizz Air, Air Serbia operate direct flights Bucharest (OTP) → Belgrade (BEG). Duration: ~1h15. 2026 prices: 35–90 EUR return.

### Step 2 — Transfer Belgrade → Novi Sad

**Bus** (recommended) — Lasta Bus Station in Belgrade, bus every 30–60 minutes, duration 1h30, <cite index="41-1">600–800 RSD (~5–7 EUR).</cite>

**Fast Intercity Train** — <cite index="41-1">the new Belgrade–Novi Sad Intercity train takes under 40 minutes</cite> — the modernised line is one of Serbia's fastest. 700–900 RSD (~6–8 EUR).

## Petrovaradin Fortress — The Gibraltar of the Danube

<cite index="33-1">Petrovaradin Fortress is Vojvodina's most visited heritage site, drawing over 500,000 visitors annually.</cite> Built by the Habsburgs in the 17th–18th century on a 40-metre rocky spur above the Danube, <cite index="38-1">the fortress was never conquered militarily</cite> — earning it the nickname "Gibraltar of the Danube."

### The Reversed Clock Tower

Petrovaradin's Clock Tower has a unique feature: <cite index="29-1">the minute and hour hands are reversed compared to a normal clock, and the hour hand is larger than the minute hand</cite> — designed specifically so that Danube mariners could read the approximate hour from a distance, more interested in the rough time than the exact minutes.

### The Catacombs (Underground Tunnels)

Beneath the fortress stretch 16 km of underground tunnels built for defence and storage. <cite index="31-1">The catacombs are accessible with a guide</cite> — ~300 RSD (~2.50 EUR). The tour includes a former Orient Express passage that ran through the tunnels.

### Access to the Fortress

Access to the fortress and panoramic views is **free**. <cite index="31-1">There is a city bus between central Novi Sad and the fortress, or you can walk across the Liberty Bridge — a 15-minute walk.</cite>

**Best moment:** sunset — <cite index="31-1">grill smoke from fortress restaurants, the festival bass in season, and the Danube's colours at sunset create a singular atmosphere.</cite>

### EXIT Festival at the Fortress (July)

<cite index="33-1">Every July, Petrovaradin Fortress transforms into the EXIT Festival stage — 4 days, 40+ stages, 200,000+ attendees from 70 countries, with headliners in electronic, rock, hip-hop and world music.</cite>

<cite index="39-1">EXIT 2026 takes place at Petrovaradin Fortress, Novi Sad, Serbia.</cite>

**EXIT 2026 ticket:** day ticket 50–80 EUR, 4-day pass 150–200 EUR. **Accommodation** in Novi Sad during EXIT sells out 3–6 months ahead — book immediately once you know you're going.

## What to See in Central Novi Sad

### Freedom Square (Trg Slobode)

<cite index="33-1">Freedom Square is the city centre, with the 19th-century Roman Catholic Cathedral of St Mary, the Town Hall and the central fountain</cite> — an elegant Austro-Hungarian square with terraces and cafés. Free entry.

**St Mary's Cathedral** — the 73-metre tower is visible across the city. Impressive Neo-Gothic interior. Free entry.

### Matica Srpska Gallery

<cite index="43-1">Matica Srpska Gallery houses the largest collection of international art in Serbia</cite> — 18th–19th century Serbian painting and European art. ~200–300 RSD (~1.70–2.55 EUR).

### Dunavski Park and Danube Promenade

The riverside park with shaded alleys, fountains and views toward Petrovaradin Fortress — the classic walk of Novi Sad locals. Free.

## Tour to Novi Sad, Sremski Karlovci and Fruška Gora — Wine and Monasteries in One Day

### Sremski Karlovci — The Baroque Town of Treaties

<cite index="38-1">Sremski Karlovci is a baroque town 12 km from Novi Sad, on the Danube bank</cite> — famous as the site where the 1699 Treaty of Karlowitz was signed, by which the Ottoman Empire ceded Transylvania and Hungary to the Habsburgs, reshaping the map of Europe.

**Central square** with four identical houses at each corner and the unique Four-Faced Fountain.

**Bermet** — the local dessert wine, flavoured with herbs, produced in Karlovci for centuries. Buy a bottle directly from local producers: 600–1,200 RSD (~5–10 EUR).

**Getting there:** bus or taxi from Novi Sad, 20 minutes, ~200 RSD (~1.70 EUR).

### Fruška Gora — Monasteries and Wines on the Hills

<cite index="41-1">Fruška Gora is Serbia's oldest national park, 15 km south of Novi Sad</cite> — an 80 km hill range with 16 active Orthodox monasteries (many from the 15th–16th centuries), oak and beech forests and wineries with native varieties.

**Monasteries:** Krušedol, Hopovo, Grgeteg — accessible by car or guided tour.

**Wineries:** Fruška Gora produces Graševina (white), Riesling, Merlot and local varieties. Cellar tasting: 500–1,000 RSD (~4–8 EUR).

## Food and Drink in Novi Sad

### What You Must Eat

**Fisherman's stew (riblja čorba)** — Danube fish soup with paprika and chilli — Vojvodina's river speciality. 400–700 RSD (3.40–6 EUR).

**Kobasice** — Vojvodina smoked sausages with pepper. At any market or traditional restaurant: 200–400 RSD.

**Kajmak** — the rich Serbian fermented cream — with fresh bread or grilled meat. 150–250 RSD.

**Kürtőskalács** — the chimney cake from the Hungarian community — spiral fried dough with sugar and cinnamon. At markets: 300–500 RSD.

### Vojvodina Wine

<cite index="38-1">Vojvodina produces excellent wines on the Fruška Gora hills</cite> — fruity Graševina white, dry Riesling and some local varieties. A good wine glass at a restaurant: 200–400 RSD (1.70–3.40 EUR).

## Practical Tips

**Belgrade → Novi Sad bus** — Lasta Bus Station in Belgrade is the main hub. Don't rely on online booking — tickets are usually purchased at the counter. Frequent departures throughout the day.

**Accommodation during EXIT** — if going in July for EXIT Festival, book 3–6 months ahead. Accommodation prices rise 2–3x compared to the rest of the year.

**The reversed clock** — if you're going specifically to photograph the clock tower, early morning is best — no crowds.

**Multilingual Vojvodina** — in Vojvodina you'll see signs in Serbian, Hungarian, Slovak, Croatian and Rusyn — one of Europe's richest regions for linguistic diversity.
