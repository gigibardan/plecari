Travelling with children is one of life's best experiences — and one that deserves careful attention to insurance. A child who falls ill or gets injured abroad generates enormous costs, pediatric emergencies are more unpredictable than for adults, and finding a paediatrician at 10pm on a Saturday on a Greek island is a nightmare no parent wants to face without a 24/7 assistance line.

This guide covers everything parents need to know: real prices, what the policy covers, pediatric medical costs by destination, common childhood travel illnesses, activity coverage, and the mistakes that can cost you thousands.

## Why Children Need Separate Insurance

Children are **not automatically covered** by a parent's travel insurance. Every person — including babies — needs their own insurance document or must be explicitly included in a family policy. If the parent has a policy and the child doesn't, the child is uninsured.

### Why Children's Insurance Costs Less

Children (0-17) pay **20-30% less** than adults aged 30-45. The reasons:

- Statistically fewer serious cardiovascular and orthopaedic emergencies
- Fewer pre-existing conditions
- Lower risk profile for dangerous activities

**The exception:** children aged 0-2 may carry a **5-10%** surcharge compared to the 3-17 age group, due to greater immune vulnerability (fever, dehydration, allergic reactions to new foods).

### Why It's Still Essential

Although children have a lower general medical risk, pediatric emergencies have specific characteristics that make insurance critical:

1. **Unpredictability** — children get seriously ill suddenly (40°C fever out of nowhere, allergic reactions, severe gastroenteritis)
2. **Rapid dehydration** — small children dehydrate much faster than adults, turning ordinary diarrhoea into a hospitalisation emergency
3. **Need for a paediatrician** — a general practitioner at a resort medical centre isn't always qualified for pediatric emergencies. Insurance with a 24/7 line directs you to hospitals with pediatric departments
4. **Companion accommodation** — if the child is hospitalised, a parent must stay with them. Who pays for the hotel, food, and rebooking the rest of the family's flights?

### Companion Accommodation — Critical Detail

If your child is hospitalised abroad, a parent must stay with them. The costs that accumulate:

- **Accommodation** (hotel near the hospital): €60-150/night
- **Meals**: €30-50/day
- **Flight changes** for the entire family: €200-500
- **Flight for the other parent** coming from home: €150-400

**Potential total for a 5-day hospitalisation:** €600-1,500 just for the companion's expenses. A good policy covers these costs in full.

**How to check:** look for the terms "companion accommodation", "accompanying person", "relative's expenses", or "escort cover" in the policy wording. If it's not mentioned, it's not included.

## Holiday Activities — What's Covered and What's Not

### Activity Risk Classification

| Risk level | Activities | Standard coverage? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Calm** | Beach, hotel pool, city walks, museums | ✅ Yes |
| **Active** | Cycling, easy hiking, surface snorkelling | ✅ Yes (most policies) |
| **Active sport** | On-piste skiing, horse riding, water parks with slides | ⚠️ Requires sport rider |
| **Extreme sport** | Off-piste, diving below 30m, parasailing, bungee | ❌ Excluded standard, separate rider |

### Skiing and Winter Sports with Children

Skiing with children requires special attention to insurance:

| Element | Why it matters | Check in policy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mountain rescue on-slope | Rescue toboggan: €300-1,500 | ✅ "Mountain rescue" included? |
| Helicopter (if needed) | €2,000-10,000 | ✅ "Helicopter evacuation"? |
| Child's sports equipment | Damaged skis/helmet: €200-800 | ⚠️ "Sports equipment"? |
| Unused ski pass | If the accident is on day one: pass lost | ⚠️ "Unused ski pass"? |
| Cancelled ski lessons | If the child can no longer ski | ❌ Rarely covered |

**Winter sports rider cost:** typically €5-15/week per person as an add-on. With EKTA, you select "Active" or "Extreme" level at purchase (included in the price).

### Water Parks and Pools

Water parks with large slides are considered "active sport" by some insurers. If you're planning visits to such parks (Aquapark, water slides), check whether your policy covers them.

**Hotel swimming pool** is considered "calm rest" and is covered as standard.

### Organised Excursions and Group Activities

If you book excursions through agencies (GetYourGuide, Viator, local operators), check:

1. **Does the excursion include insurance?** — some do, but with minimal coverage
2. **What activities are planned?** — a "jeep safari" or "ATV tour" qualifies as active sport
3. **Does your policy cover those activities?** — if not, either buy a rider or skip that activity

## EHIC (European Health Insurance Card) for Children

### Each Child Needs Their Own Card

The child's EHIC is **separate** from the parent's. Children are not included on an adult's card — each child must have their own card issued in their name.

### How to Get the Card for Your Child

The process varies by country, but generally:

1. Contact your national health insurance provider
2. Apply for the child's card using their details (not the parent's)
3. Provide: child's ID or birth certificate + proof of health insurance enrolment
4. The card arrives by post in 7-15 working days

**Cost:** free. **Validity:** typically 2-5 years (varies by country).

### What the EHIC Covers for Children (and What It Doesn't)

The same rules apply as for adults — full details in our [EHIC vs travel insurance guide](/en/asigurari/ehic-vs-travel-insurance):

| Covered ✅ | Not covered ❌ |
| --- | --- |
| Emergencies in public EU hospitals | Private clinics |
| Public pediatric consultations | Medical repatriation |
| Medication (with co-payment) | Mountain rescue / helicopter |
| COVID-19 | Trip cancellation / baggage |
| | Anything outside the EU (Turkey, USA) |

**The formula:** EHIC (free) + private travel insurance = complete protection for your child.

## Ages and Specific Risks — Guide by Age Group

### 0-2 Years (Babies)

**Main risks:** dehydration (from heat or gastroenteritis), fever, reactions to new foods, ear infections (otitis after flying/pressure changes).

**What matters in the policy:**
- Coverage from age 0 (or 2 months — EKTA)
- Repatriation with companion
- Pediatric emergencies (not just general medicine)
- Hospitalisation + parent accommodation

**Tip:** babies under 6 months are more vulnerable to infections. Consult your paediatrician before travelling to tropical countries.

### 3-6 Years (Preschoolers)

**Main risks:** gastroenteritis (dirty hands, new foods), allergic reactions (new foods — seafood, nuts), severe sunburn, pool accidents.

**What matters in the policy:**
- Coverage for "calm" activities (pool, beach)
- Emergency dental (falls, broken teeth)
- Trip cancellation (chickenpox, flu — common at nursery age)

### 7-12 Years (School Age)

**Main risks:** fractures and sprains (intense physical activity), insect/jellyfish stings, water park accidents, skiing accidents.

**What matters in the policy:**
- Active sport rider (if you're skiing, horse riding, visiting water parks)
- Sports equipment (skis, helmet)
- Personal liability (if the child causes an accident on the slope)

### 13-17 Years (Teenagers)

**Main risks:** extreme sports (parasailing, ATV, rented scooter), road accidents (bicycle/scooter), risk-taking behaviour.

**What matters in the policy:**
- Extreme sport rider (if the teenager wants parasailing, jet-ski, etc.)
- Higher personal liability (teenagers can cause more significant damage)
- Scooter/bicycle coverage (check — many policies exclude motorised vehicles)

**Warning:** renting scooters/ATVs to minors (under 18) is illegal in most EU countries. If a minor drives a scooter and has an accident, insurance may reject the claim.

## Camps and School Trips — What to Check

### Does the Camp Include Insurance?

Most organised camps include group insurance in the package price. **But:**

- Coverage may be **minimal** (€10,000-20,000 cap vs €50,000-100,000 on an individual policy)
- **Adventure activities** (skiing, horse riding, climbing) may not be included in the group policy
- **Repatriation** may be missing or limited
- **Cancellation** before the camp may not be covered

### What to Do

1. **Ask the organiser** for a copy of the group insurance policy
2. **Check:** medical cap, covered activities, repatriation included
3. **If insufficient:** buy a supplementary individual policy for your child
4. **Save** on your child's phone: insurer's emergency number, your phone number, policy details

### School Trips Abroad

Schools typically have an obligation to insure students during trips, but coverage may be minimal. Check with the trip coordinator what the group policy covers and supplement with an individual policy if needed.

## Schengen Visa Requirements (Non-EU Children)

If you're applying for a Schengen visa for a non-EU child, the requirements are identical to adults:

- **Minimum €30,000** medical coverage
- Valid for the entire duration of stay
- Coverage in all Schengen countries being visited
- Includes medical emergencies, hospitalisation, repatriation

Embassies check the child's documents **separately** from the parents'. Each child needs their own proof of insurance coverage.

## 10 Common Mistakes Parents Make with Travel Insurance

### 1. "My child is covered on my policy"

No. The child must be explicitly included on the policy or have a separate one. Check the document — if the child's name doesn't appear, they're not covered.

### 2. Not checking companion coverage

If the child is hospitalised, a parent must stay with them. If the policy doesn't cover companion accommodation and transport, you pay €60-150/night out of pocket.

### 3. Forgetting the sports rider

You go skiing and the child falls on the slope. Without a winter sports rider, the insurer rejects the claim. The rider cost: €5-15. The cost of mountain rescue without the rider: €500-1,500.

### 4. No cancellation cover

Children get sick suddenly — chickenpox, flu, ear infection 2 days before departure. Without cancellation cover, you lose all the flight and hotel money (€1,000-3,000 for a family holiday).

### 5. Missing travel consent documents

You're travelling alone with your child. At the border, an officer asks for a notarised consent letter from the other parent. You don't have one. Consequence: you may not be allowed to travel. Holiday cancelled.

### 6. Expired EHIC

You have the child's EHIC, but it expired 3 months ago. At the hospital in Barcelona, they won't accept it. You pay the emergency consultation out of pocket (€150-300).

### 7. Not saving the insurer's emergency number

It's 11pm on Crete, the child has a 40°C fever. You search for the insurer's number — can't find it. You waste precious time. **Save it on your phone BEFORE departure**, in contacts, and take a screenshot for your home screen.

### 8. Not declaring the child's allergies

Your child has a nut/seafood/bee sting allergy. You didn't declare it when buying the policy. Severe allergic reaction on holiday. The insurer may reject the claim on grounds of undeclared pre-existing condition.

### 9. Buying the policy at the airport

Airport counter policies are typically more expensive and with more limited coverage than online ones. And they don't include trip cancellation (logically — you're already at the airport). Buy online, 24-48 hours before departure.

### 10. Assuming "all-inclusive" means everything

No "all-inclusive" holiday package includes private medical insurance. Not even the most expensive resorts. Travel insurance is separate — it must be purchased separately.
