There is a problem with Valletta: it is too small to be taken seriously. 320,000 square meters — smaller than Central Park in New York, smaller than many city parks across Europe. A sovereign European capital you can walk across completely in 20 minutes.

And that scale is precisely what misleads. Valletta is the most densely concentrated assembly of Baroque art, military fortifications, and Knights of Malta history in the world — UNESCO-listed since 1980, repeatedly named one of Europe's best cultural capitals. Behind every street corner, every alley that opens toward the sea, every unlocked church, there are several centuries of compressed story.

Malta itself is a complete surprise for first-time visitors: a 316 km² island with its own language (Maltese — the only Semitic language with a Latin script), a cuisine that blends Italy with North Africa, waters where visibility reaches 30 meters, and a layered history involving Phoenicians, Romans, Arabs, Normans, medieval knights, the British, and beyond.

## Valletta — The Capital That Surprises

### St. John's Co-Cathedral — Caravaggio by the Sea

The Knights' cathedral is deliberately austere on the outside — limestone walls, geometric lines, none of the Baroque exuberance you'd expect. This was calculated. The Knights wanted no one outside to know what they were hiding inside.

What they were hiding: arguably the richest Baroque interior in the Mediterranean. Each of the eight lateral chapels was decorated by a different langues (nationality) of the Order — French, Italian, Spanish, German, English, Portuguese, Aragonese, Provençal. The floor is a mosaic of 375 inlaid marble slabs with inscriptions and heraldic symbols — the knights' tombs, each slightly different, covering the entire nave surface.

And in the Oratory, separated from the rest of the cathedral: **"The Beheading of Saint John the Baptist"** by Caravaggio. The largest canvas he ever painted (3.61 × 5.2 meters). The only painting Caravaggio ever signed — the signature is in the blood in the lower right corner of the composition. It was painted in Malta in 1608, when Caravaggio was a fugitive after killing a man in Rome.

### Upper Barrakka Gardens — The View That Justifies Everything

The bastion gardens, with a shaded colonnade and central fountain, are **free to enter** and offer what is arguably the finest harbour panorama in Europe: Malta's Grand Harbour with the Three Cities (Birgu, Senglea, Bormla) on the opposite shore, golden limestone quays, yachts, ferries, and occasionally enormous cruise ships — all 50 meters below you.

**Saluting Battery:** At noon and 4:00 PM daily, the cannons on the bastion fire a salute. A tradition from the British era, now a tourist spectacle — but a genuine one. Arrive 20 minutes early if you want a spot on the front balcony.

**Barrakka Lift:** If you don't want to climb on foot from the lower harbour, the lift (€1.25 return) takes you directly to the gardens. Useful in summer when climbing steps in 35°C is discouraging.

### Grand Master's Palace

The residence of the Order's leaders since 1571, today housing the Maltese Parliament and Presidency. The historic wings (State Rooms and Armoury) are open to visitors when parliament is not in session.

**Prices:** State Rooms + Armoury ~€10 adults. The inner courtyard — free.

**What not to miss:** The Armoury — 5,000 pieces of weaponry from the Knights' era, including complete 16th–17th century armour in perfect condition. One of the finest collections of medieval weapons in the world.

### Fort St. Elmo — The Peninsula at the City's End

The fort that resisted the Great Ottoman Siege of 1565 for 31 days, with a garrison of 600 men against 48,000 Ottoman soldiers. When it fell, every defender was dead.

Today it houses the National War Museum and stages historical re-enactments in full Knights' costume.

**Prices:** ~€10 adults, includes the National War Museum.

**Tip:** Visit at sunset — the late light on limestone and harbour is exceptional.

### Strait Street — The Road That Watered an Empire

During the British period (1800–1964), Strait Street was the main entertainment strip for Royal Navy sailors — bars, cabarets, dancers. Now revitalised as Valletta's most interesting bar street: so narrow that two people barely pass each other, with coloured balconies nearly touching overhead, jazz live music, Maltese craft beer, and cocktails served from windows.

**Perfect for:** Evening, between 7:00 and 11:00 PM. No admission charge for the atmosphere.

## Blue Lagoon and Comino — The Perfect Turquoise

Comino is a 3.5 km² island with a single permanent resident (a farmer) and a lagoon with the most turquoise water in the Mediterranean. The water is so clear the seabed is visible at 8–10 meters depth. The photographs do not exaggerate.

**Ferry/boat prices 2026:**

| Option | Price |
|---|---|
| Ferry from Malta (Cirkewwa) — return | €15–20 |
| Day cruise from Sliema or Valletta | €35–50 |
| Sunset cruise (2–3h, with wine) | €40–50 |
| Private boat (small group) | €60–100/person |

**Critical warning:** In summer (July–August), the Blue Lagoon is severely overcrowded — hundreds of boats, thousands of people, murky water by midday from boat traffic. **Go early morning or in May/September/October.** A Blue Lagoon visit in August at noon looks nothing like the photographs. In September morning — it does.

**Underrated alternative: St. Peter's Pool** — a natural limestone swimming hole in southeastern Malta, free, no tourist boats, water equally clear. Harder to reach without a car, but completely worth it.

## Gozo Island — Slower and More Authentic Malta

25 minutes by ferry from Cirkewwa (northern Malta), Gozo is the sister island — smaller, greener, less touristic, with a distinct personality. If Malta is Valletta and the Blue Lagoon, Gozo is orange limestone cliffs, stone villages, family vineyards, and the best Fenek (rabbit in wine) in the archipelago.

**What to visit in Gozo:**

**The Citadella (Victoria):** The medieval fortress of Gozo's capital, with the cathedral, museums, and a complete panorama of the island. Fortress entry is free; interior museums €5–8 each.

**Ramla Bay:** Malta's most beautiful beach with russet-red sand, in the north of the island. Free.

**Ggantija Temples:** Megalithic temples 1,000 years older than Stonehenge (3,600 BC) — UNESCO, entry ~€9.

**Xlendi Bay:** A fishing village with a small beach and seafood restaurants at local prices.

**Quad biking or a rental car** is the best way to see Gozo — distances are small but local buses are infrequent.

## Food in Malta — Cheaper Than It Looks

Maltese cuisine is a Mediterranean blend of Arab, Sicilian, and British influences (colonised until 1964). It is not fine dining cuisine — it is port food: filling, fresh, with simple local ingredients and a lot of sea.

### The Essential Dishes

**Pastizzi** — the defining Maltese pastry. Flaky pastry filled with ricotta or mushy peas, baked and served warm. The national snack, sold from pastizzeriji (tiny bakeries with an oven at the front) in every neighbourhood. **Price: €0.35–0.50 each.** The best €0.50 you will spend in any European capital.

**Ftira** — ring-shaped Maltese bread, used as a sandwich. Classic filling: tuna, capers, olives, tomatoes, olive oil. Simple, local, perfect for lunch on the move. **Price: €4–6.**

**Ħobż biż-żejt** — Maltese bread rubbed with concentrated tomato paste and drizzled with olive oil, with capers and cheese. Breakfast or snack. **Price: €2.50–4.**

**Fenek (Fenkata)** — rabbit — Malta's national dish, cooked in Maltese red wine with garlic and herbs, or fried. Unexpectedly delicious, and unexpectedly affordable (€12–16 as a main). Best in inland villages or on Gozo.

**Lampuki** — dolphinfish (mahi-mahi), available September–November, prepared simply grilled or in tomato sauce. Seasonal and local — if you're in Malta in autumn, order it.

**Kinnie** — Malta's own soft drink, made with bitter oranges and aromatic herbs. Unlike anything else. €2–3 at a bar.

### Where to Eat Cheaply

**Is-Suq tal-Belt** (Valletta's covered market) — food stands with prepared meals at local prices, €5–9 for a complete lunch.

**Crystal Palace pastizzeria, Rabat** — considered the best pastizzeria in Malta. One pastizz: €0.40. A constant queue of locals — the best possible sign.

**Café Jubilee, Valletta** — a local favourite, authentic Maltese food at accessible prices.

**Marsaxlokk (Sunday)** — fresh fish and seafood market directly from the boats. Restaurants around the harbour have the best seafood value in Malta.

## When to Visit Malta

| Period | Temperatures | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| December–February | 14–18°C | Minimum prices, zero crowds, authentic Valletta | Blue Lagoon not worthwhile (cold, grey) |
| March–May | 18–25°C | **Excellent**: pleasant warmth, flowers, low prices | Sea still cool (20–22°C) |
| June | 26–30°C | Warm, sea OK (24°C), less crowded than summer | Prices beginning to rise |
| July–August | 32–36°C | **Peak**: sea 27°C, everything open | **Severely overcrowded**, Blue Lagoon impossible at midday, max prices |
| September–October | 26–30°C | **Ideal**: warm sea (25–27°C), smaller crowds, good prices | Occasional rain in October |
| November | 18–22°C | Quiet, low prices, Lampuki on menus | Some water activities wind down |

**Our recommendation:** **May or September** — the perfect compromise. The sea is warm, the Blue Lagoon is visitable without chaos, prices are reasonable, and Valletta is experienced at the right temperature.

### Airport to Valletta
The airport is 8 km from Valletta:
- **Bus X4 or 119** direct to Valletta: €2, ~30 minutes
- **Taxi/Bolt:** €15–20, 15–20 minutes
- **Pre-booked transfer:** €20–30

### Getting around Malta
Malta has a dense **Tallinja** bus network — all routes originating from Valletta as hub:
- Single ticket: **€1.50 (winter) / €2 (summer)**
- 24h pass: **€3** — worth it for 2+ trips
- 7-day pass: **€21** — recommended for a week's stay

**Bolt** works well in Malta and is often faster than the bus for short hops.

**Sliema–Valletta ferry:** €2 return, 10 minutes — the most pleasant way to enter Valletta if you're staying in Sliema.

## Insider Tips — What Locals Know

**1. Pastizzi at the right temperature.** A good pastizzeria runs its oven continuously — pastizzi come out hot every 20–30 minutes. If they're cold on the counter, ask "when's the next batch?" and wait 5 minutes. The difference is enormous.

**2. The Sliema–Valletta ferry beats taxis and buses.** €2 return, 10 minutes on water, with a view of Valletta from the sea. If you're staying in Sliema, it's the default transport to Valletta.

**3. Cruise ships change everything.** When a cruise ship is in port (visible from Upper Barrakka Gardens), Valletta has an extra 5,000–10,000 people. The ship schedule is public — check cruiseportmalta.com if you want to plan quieter days.

**4. Maltese is a real language, actively spoken.** Malta is bilingual (Maltese and English are co-official), but Maltese — the only Semitic language with a Latin script — is used daily. "Grazzi" (thank you) and "Skużani" (sorry/excuse me) are always appreciated.

**5. Cars drive on the left.** Malta inherited British driving conventions. If you rent a car, remember: steering wheel on the right, drive on the left. Particularly important in Gozo where roads are narrow.

**6. Heritage Malta Pass.** If you're visiting 4+ Heritage Malta sites (Ħaġar Qim, Mnajdra, Hypogeum, National Museum of Archaeology etc.), the multi-site card (~€30) is cheaper than individual tickets.

**7. English works perfectly.** Malta was a British colony until 1964, and English is co-official. You don't need any Maltese phrases or translations — all locals speak fluent English, including older generations.
