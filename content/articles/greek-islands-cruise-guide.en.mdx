## Why the Greek Islands by cruise

The Greek Islands are scattered across the Aegean like stepping stones, and cruising is the single most efficient way to see them. In one week, you wake up on a different island every morning — Santorini's volcanic caldera on Monday, Mykonos' windmills on Tuesday, Crete's ancient palaces on Wednesday — without packing, unpacking, catching ferries, or finding hotels. Your ship is your hotel, your restaurant, and your transport all in one.

The Aegean Sea is one of the calmest bodies of water in the Mediterranean, which means even passengers prone to seasickness usually have no trouble. And because the distances between islands are short (2-6 hours of sailing), most cruising happens overnight — you go to sleep, and wake up at a new destination.

## Routes and itineraries in 2026

### Route 1: Classic Greek Islands (7 nights from Athens)

**Typical itinerary:** Piraeus (Athens) → Mykonos → Rhodes → Crete (Heraklion) → Santorini → Piraeus

**Available on:** Costa Fortuna, Celestyal Journey, MSC Lirica

**Best for:** first-timers who want the iconic Greek experience. This route covers the four most famous islands in one week.

### Route 2: Eastern Mediterranean mix (7-10 nights)

**Typical itinerary:** Piraeus → Santorini → Kusadasi (Ephesus, Turkey) → Rhodes → Mykonos → Piraeus

**Available on:** Celebrity Infinity, Norwegian Pearl, Royal Caribbean Explorer of the Seas

**Best for:** travellers who want Greece plus Turkey. Ephesus (accessible from Kusadasi) is one of the best-preserved ancient cities in the world.

### Route 3: Greece + Croatia + Italy (10-12 nights)

**Typical itinerary:** Venice/Ravenna → Dubrovnik → Kotor (Montenegro) → Corfu → Santorini → Mykonos → Piraeus

**Available on:** Norwegian Viva, Celebrity Equinox, MSC Orchestra

**Best for:** those who want the Grand Mediterranean experience. Longer, more expensive, but you see three countries and the Adriatic coast.

### Route 4: Immersive Greece (3-4 nights)

**Typical itinerary:** Piraeus → Mykonos → Kusadasi → Patmos → Santorini → Piraeus

**Available on:** Celestyal (their signature short cruise)

**Best for:** travellers short on time or combining with a land-based Athens stay. Celestyal's smaller ships dock directly at ports where mega-ships must tender.

## Cruise lines compared — honest take

### Best value: MSC & Costa

Both Italian heritage lines offer 7-night Greek Islands itineraries from **$800-1,100 per person** (inside cabin, full board included). MSC has newer ships and kids-sail-free policies. Costa has a slightly more Italian atmosphere with better included food. Both charge service fees on top (~$14/day per person).

### Greece specialist: Celestyal

The only cruise line dedicated exclusively to Greece and Turkey. Smaller ships (1,200 passengers vs 4,000+ on mega-ships) mean they dock at ports the big lines can't reach. Packages often include some shore excursions. From **$700-1,200 per person** for 7 nights, making it competitive despite the specialist positioning.

### Premium experience: Celebrity & Azamara

Celebrity's modern ships, Michelin-inspired dining, and adults-focused atmosphere make it the top choice for couples wanting refinement. Azamara specialises in longer port stays and overnight stops. Both from **$1,500-2,500 per person**.

### Best onboard experience: Royal Caribbean

If you want the Greek Islands AND surf simulators, rock climbing walls, and Broadway shows, Royal Caribbean delivers. Ships are enormous (3,500-5,000 passengers). From **$1,200-1,800 per person**.

### Most flexible: Norwegian

Freestyle cruising means no fixed dinner times, no dress code, no assigned seating. Evening port departures in Greece let you explore islands until late and have dinner on shore. From **$1,000-1,600 per person**.

## Port-by-port guide — what to do at each stop

### Santorini — the one everyone photographs

Ships anchor offshore and you tender to the old port below Fira. From there: **cable car** (€6, 3 minutes — strongly recommended) or walk up 588 steps alongside donkeys. Don't ride the donkeys — animal welfare concerns are well documented.

**With 6-8 hours:** Take the public bus from Fira to Oia (€2.20, 25 minutes). Walk through Oia's blue-domed churches and galleries. Have lunch with a caldera view. Return to Fira for shopping and the cable car back.

**What ship excursions charge:** €80-120 for what you can do independently for €15.

**Insider tip:** Skip Oia if you hate crowds. Instead, visit Akrotiri archaeological site (€12, the "Pompeii of the Aegean") and the Red Beach — both are less visited and extraordinary.

![Oia village in Santorini — blue domes and caldera views](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1613395877344-13d4a8e0d49e?w=900&q=80)

### Mykonos — beaches and windmills

Ships dock at the new port or tender to the old port. Shuttle buses run to town (usually free from the cruise terminal, 5 minutes).

**With 6-8 hours:** Walk through Mykonos Town (Chora) — the five windmills, Little Venice, Paraportiani Church. Then take a water taxi to Platys Gialos or Paradise Beach (€5-8). Have lunch at a taverna by the water.

**Insider tip:** Mykonos is expensive. A coffee in the main square costs €6-8. Walk one street back from the waterfront and prices drop by 30%.

### Crete (Heraklion) — ancient civilisation and amazing food

Ships dock directly — the easiest port. Walk to Heraklion's centre in 15 minutes, or take a taxi to the **Palace of Knossos** (15 EUR entry, 5 km from port).

**This is the one port where a guided tour helps.** Knossos without context is just ruins. With a guide, it becomes the 3,700-year-old capital of Europe's first civilisation — the Minoans, who had running water and flush toilets before ancient Rome existed.

**Insider tip:** Skip the palace cafeteria. Walk to Heraklion's central market (Odos 1866) and eat Cretan dakos (barley rusk with tomato, cheese, olive oil — €4) and grilled octopus (€8).

### Rhodes — medieval time travel

Ships dock at the commercial port, 15 minutes' walk from the Old Town entrance. No transport needed.

**With 6-8 hours:** Enter through the D'Amboise Gate and walk the **Street of the Knights** — the best-preserved medieval street in Europe. Visit the **Palace of the Grand Master** (€8). Get lost in the maze of the Old Town. Have lunch at a taverna in one of the hidden squares.

**Insider tip:** The Old Town is a UNESCO site that's still fully inhabited. 6,000 people live inside the medieval walls. Walk beyond the tourist streets and you'll find residential neighbourhoods unchanged for centuries.

## When to book and when to sail

**Book 6-8 months ahead** for the best prices on shoulder-season dates. Last-minute deals exist but are unreliable — if you want a specific itinerary or cabin type, early booking wins.

**Best months:** May, June, September, October. Pleasant weather, fewer crowds, lower prices.

**Avoid if possible:** First two weeks of August. Peak prices, 35°C+ heat, and ports so crowded that Santorini sometimes limits cruise ship passengers.

## Packing essentials for Greek Islands cruising

**Comfortable walking shoes.** Not sandals — cobblestones, steep paths, and hot stone surfaces require real shoes. Save the sandals for the ship.

**Sun protection.** SPF 50, hat, sunglasses. The Aegean sun is relentless, especially on deck and at exposed archaeological sites.

**A light day backpack.** Water bottle, camera, sunscreen, a layer for air-conditioned museums. Don't carry valuables at port.

**European power adapter.** Even on American-flagged ships, cabin outlets may be European standard.

**An eSIM.** Ship WiFi costs $15-25/day and is painfully slow. A European eSIM ($5-15 for the whole trip) gives you fast data at every port for maps, translation, and sharing photos instantly.
