There is a cliché Croatia has cultivated with skill: everything worth seeing is on the coast. Dubrovnik, Split, Hvar, Brač — the Adriatic coast is spectacular and knows it. As a result, millions of tourists land in Zagreb, spend a night in a hotel and leave for the sea on the first morning bus.

A mistake.

<cite index="104-1">Croatia's capital has a completely different energy to the touristified coast: Austro-Hungarian grandeur, a serious café culture, eccentric museums and a nightlife that Dubrovnik cannot match.</cite> It is a continental European city — closer to Vienna or Budapest in character than to the Mediterranean.

It also has a museum dedicated to failed relationships that is one of the most emotionally affecting places I have visited. And it has the best starting point for the trip to Plitvice Lakes — Europe's most spectacular national park.

## Zagreb at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Croatia — EU member since 2013, Schengen Area since 2023.

**Population:** approximately 800,000 — Croatia's largest city.

**Currency:** Euro (EUR) since 1 January 2023. No currency exchange needed.

**Language:** Croatian. English is well spoken in the tourist area. German is common — Austro-Hungarian influence is strong.

**City structure:** Zagreb divides into the **Upper Town** (Gornji Grad — medieval hilltop core) and the **Lower Town** (Donji Grad — Austro-Hungarian area with wide boulevards and parks), connected by the 66-metre funicular.

**Direct flights from Bucharest:** Wizz Air, OTP→ZAG, ~1h30, from 35 EUR.

**Average budget:** <cite index="99-1">45–80 EUR/day (mid-range) — more affordable than Dubrovnik, similar to Ljubljana.</cite>

## What to See in Zagreb

### Upper Town (Gornji Grad) — The Medieval Core

Gornji Grad is Zagreb's historic zone on a plateau above the city, accessible by funicular or stairs. It is the most authentic and most photographed neighbourhood in the capital.

**St Mark's Square (Trg Sv. Marka)** — the symbolic centre of Gornji Grad, with **St Mark's Church** (Crkva Sv. Marka) — the tiled mosaic roof representing the coats of arms of Croatia and Zagreb is the postcard image of the capital. Building from the 13th century, the current roof from 1882. Access to the square: free.

**Stone Gate (Kamenita Vrata)** — the only medieval gateway remaining from the former fortress walls, dating from the 13th century. It houses a shrine to the Virgin Mary with an icon said to have survived intact a fire in 1731. <cite index="102-1">It attracts both pilgrims and tourists, creating an authentic and intimate atmosphere.</cite> Free.

**Lotrščak Tower (Kula Lotrščak)** — the medieval defence tower from which a cannon is fired every day at noon — a tradition since 1877. Climb for a complete panorama of Zagreb. Entry: 5 EUR.

**Strossmayer Promenade (Strossmayerovo šetalište)** — the leafy walkway along the edge of the Gornji Grad plateau, with benches and panoramic views across the Lower Town and the Medvednica mountains. Free, perfect at sunset.

### The World's Shortest Funicular

<cite index="104-1">Zagreb's funicular has been running since 1890 and connects the Lower Town to the Upper Town over 66 metres — the world's shortest motor-operated cable car.</cite> A ride lasts 64 seconds and costs under 1 EUR. It's also the simplest way to get from Jelačić Square up to Gornji Grad.

### Dolac Market — "The Belly of Zagreb"

<cite index="106-1">Dolac is Zagreb's central market, open daily 7:00–14:00 (13:00 on Sundays), with its famous red umbrellas and fresh produce.</cite> It's where Zagrepčani of all generations have shopped for generations — vegetables, fruit, cheeses, sausages, bread, flowers.

Go down to the lower floor (covered market) for fish, meat and dairy. Eat a pljeskavica or a sarma at one of the places around the market for the most authentic lunch in Zagreb: 5–8 EUR.

### Museum of Broken Relationships

<cite index="105-1">The Museum of Broken Relationships is one of the most moving small museums in Europe — a crowd-sourced collection of objects from ended relationships, each with its own story.</cite>

You didn't come for the cathedral and you'll stay for the exhibit about a woman's garden hose. Or for the axe with which a woman chopped up her unfaithful boyfriend's furniture, each piece documented with a photograph. Or for the wedding dress worn just once.

The collection moves constantly between the comic and the devastating — sometimes within the same object. <cite index="104-1">Entry: 7–8 EUR. Allow 1–2 hours.</cite> Cold beer "colder than your ex" at the museum café — optional but recommended.

### Tkalčićeva Street — Zagreb's Evening Social Life

<cite index="104-1">Tkalčićeva is Zagreb's main bar and café street, with outdoor terraces packed on both sides — the street was once a stream and the buildings on either bank now face each other just metres apart.</cite>

Beer: 3–5 EUR. Coffee: 2–3 EUR. Atmosphere: excellent especially in the evening, when the terrace becomes a single continuous social organism from one end of the street to the other.

### Lower Town (Donji Grad) — Austro-Hungarian Grandeur

Donji Grad is the systematically planned zone of the late 19th century, with wide boulevards, neoclassical buildings and the park system known as the **Green Horseshoe (Zelena Potkova)** — seven interconnected squares and parks in a U-shape around the Lower Town.

**Ban Josip Jelačić Square** — the city centre, with the equestrian statue of Ban Jelačić and the blue trams. Zagreb's meeting point.

**Zrinjevac Park** — the first park in the Green Horseshoe series, with a bandstand, benches and a meteorological display from 1884 (one of the first in the world).

**Zagreb Cathedral** — with its neo-Gothic towers of 108 metres (the tallest building in Croatia), it dominates the capital's skyline. Free entry.

## Day Trip to Plitvice Lakes and Rastoke — Zagreb's Most Popular Excursion

Plitvice is the reason many choose to stay in Zagreb rather than flying directly to Split. <cite index="111-1">Plitvice Lakes is a UNESCO World Heritage Site with 16 turquoise lakes connected by cascades and waterfalls in the middle of ancient beech and fir forest.</cite>

Distance Zagreb → Plitvice: 135 km, 2.5 hours. Guided routes from Zagreb often also include **Rastoke** — a village of watermills at the confluence of two rivers, nicknamed "Little Plitvice."

**Park entry fee:**
- May–October: 20–35 EUR (peak in July–August)
- November–April: 10–15 EUR

**Essential Plitvice tips:**
- Book a guided tour from Zagreb with entry included — avoids queues and availability uncertainty
- Go in the morning (entry at 8:00) for the best light and fewer visitors
- Trainers or light hiking shoes — the trails are on wooden boardwalks, not rough terrain
- Summer: 3–4 hours in the park; autumn: 4–5 hours (autumn colours are spectacular)

## Food and Drink — Zagreb Gastronomy Guide

### What You Must Eat

**Štrukli** — Zagreb and the Zagorje region's absolute speciality. Pasta dough boiled or baked, filled with fresh cottage cheese (skuta) and sour cream. Two versions: kuhani (boiled, served as a main, milder) and pečeni (oven-baked, crispier and more golden). <cite index="106-1">A štrukli at any traditional restaurant: 6–12 EUR.</cite>

**Gablec** — the local term for the mid-morning meal at 10–11 AM, an authentic Zagreb habit. <cite index="106-1">It's the daily menu equivalent — soup + main course — eaten at a neighbourhood restaurant for 8–12 EUR.</cite> Eating gablec is a local ritual.

**Kulen** — the spicy sausage from Slavonia, with paprika and garlic, cold-smoked. Best at Dolac market or a traditional restaurant. 5–8 EUR a portion as a starter.

**Kremšnita** — vanilla cream and puff pastry slice, the Zagreb version of Bled's kremšnita (Slovenia). <cite index="106-1">The most famous is found at Vincek, the patisserie at Ilica 18 — an institution since 1977.</cite> 2–4 EUR a slice.

### Wine and Drinks

**Graševina** — the most widespread white wine from Slavonia (eastern Croatia), fruity and fresh. 3–5 EUR a glass at a restaurant.

**Malvazija** — Istrian white wine, more aromatic, available at several Zagreb restaurants. 4–6 EUR.

**Craft beer** — Zagreb has a growing craft beer scene: Zagreb Beer Factory, Pivnica Pivovara Medvedgrad. Pint: 4–6 EUR.

**Coffee** — <cite index="104-1">Zagreb has a serious coffee culture.</cite> Not a quick espresso — coffee drunk on a terrace, 30–45 minutes, with a friend. Flat white or macchiato: 2–3.50 EUR.

### Where to Eat

**Budget (6–12 EUR/meal):**
- Any neighbourhood restaurant for gablec (10:00–13:00)
- Stands around Dolac Market
- **Pivnica Medvedgrad** — brewery with traditional Croatian food, fair prices

**Mid-range (12–25 EUR/person):**
- **Konoba Didov San** — Dalmatian and Zagreb specialities, authentic atmosphere
- **Restoran Stari Fijaker 900** — traditional Zagreb restaurant, founded in 1900
- Any restaurant on Tkalčićeva with menu displayed at the entrance

**Caution:** <cite index="104-1">restaurants directly on Trg Sv. Marka or the main boulevard charge 30–40% more. One side street and quality increases, price drops.</cite>

## Recommended Itinerary — 3 Days in Zagreb

### Day 1 — Gornji Grad and Evening Life

- **Morning:** Dolac Market (7:00–9:00 — most lively hour), local breakfast
- **10:00–13:00:** funicular to Gornji Grad, St Mark's Church, Stone Gate, Lotrščak Tower
- **Lunch:** štrukli at a Gornji Grad restaurant
- **Afternoon:** Museum of Broken Relationships (2 hours), Galerija Klovićevi Dvori
- **Evening:** terraces on Tkalčićeva, dinner at a konoba

### Day 2 — Plitvice Lakes (Full Day Excursion)

- **8:00–20:00:** guided Plitvice + Rastoke tour with entry included from Zagreb (~48–80 EUR)
- Return in the evening — light dinner in Zagreb

### Day 3 — Donji Grad and Ljubljana (Optional)

- **Morning:** Jelačić Square, Zrinjevac Park, Zagreb Cathedral, stroll along the boulevards
- **Lunch:** gablec at a neighbourhood restaurant

- **Option B:** visit Mirogoj — Zagreb's cemetery-museum with an impressive neo-Renaissance arcade

## Practical Tips

**Zagreb Card** — <cite index="99-1">13 EUR/24h or 18 EUR/72h — unlimited public transport and museum entry.</cite> Calculate whether it works out before buying.

**Trams** are convenient and cheap: <cite index="99-1">single ticket: 0.53 EUR with 30-minute validity, daily pass: 4 EUR.</cite> Route 6 runs through the centre; route 12 goes to the airport.

**Plitvice booking** — tickets to Plitvice sell out quickly in July–August. Book the tour from Zagreb at least 2–3 weeks ahead in season.

**Advent Zagreb** — if you go in December, Advent Zagreb is phenomenal. Ban Jelačić Square, Trg Petra Preradovića (Flower Square), Gornji Grad — all become Christmas markets with lights, mulled wine and spectacular decorations. Awarded best Advent in the world multiple times.

**Safety** — Zagreb is a very safe city. <cite index="98-1">Zagreb maintains high tap water quality standards — you can drink directly from the tap, saving 3–5 EUR/day compared to bottled water.</cite>
