Brașov is Transylvania's most visited city — and rightly so. A perfectly preserved medieval center with Saxon architecture, gothic churches, and cobblestone alleyways, ringed by the Carpathian Mountains and within easy reach of Dracula's Bran Castle, bear sanctuaries, and Romania's best ski resort. It is the kind of city that earns the word "charming" without cliché.

This guide covers where to stay in each part of Brașov and the surrounding area, with honest prices in euros, what to expect from each neighborhood, and the practical logistics international visitors need to plan their trip.

## Understanding Brașov's layout for accommodation

Brașov is compact and easy to navigate. The key areas for tourists are:

**Old Town (Centrul Vechi / Piața Sfatului):** The medieval heart, pedestrianized, with the Black Church (Transylvania's largest Gothic church), Council Square, and dozens of restaurants and cafés. This is where you want to be for atmosphere and convenience. Most hotels here are in renovated Saxon buildings — some date back to the 15th–18th centuries.

**Schei Quarter:** Just south of the Old Town, slightly uphill, quieter. Historically the Romanian quarter (as opposed to the Saxon center). Good guesthouses, fewer tourists.

**Poiana Brașov:** A separate ski resort 12 km away in the mountains, accessible by bus or taxi. Completely different vibe — purpose-built mountain resort with chalets, hotels, and ski facilities.

**Bran / Râșnov / Zărnești:** Villages 25–35 km from Brașov, home to Bran Castle (often called Dracula's Castle), Râșnov Citadel, and the Bucegi-Piatra Craiului mountains. Guesthouses here are cheaper and ideal for hikers.

## Old Town hotels — the best of Brașov's historic center

### Kronwell Brașov Hotel ⭐⭐⭐⭐
One of Brașov's most popular 4-star hotels, consistently rated 9.3–9.5/10 on Booking.com (1,000+ reviews). Located a few steps from Piața Sfatului (Council Square), it combines a restored historic building exterior with modern interiors. Rooftop terrace with mountain views.

**Price range 2027:** €130–200/night (double room, breakfast included in most rates)

**Best for:** Couples and families who want a reliable, polished 4-star experience without surprises.

### Radisson Blu Aurum Brașov ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The most striking hotel in Brașov's center — a bold contemporary design in contrast to the medieval surroundings, with a rooftop infinity pool overlooking the city and the Tampa Mountain cable car visible from upper floors. Consistently rated 9.4+/10. Brașov's most prestigious address for business travelers and honeymooners.

**Price range 2027:** €160–280/night (double room)

**Best for:** Splurge stays, honeymoons, those who prioritize design and the pool experience.

### QOSMO Brașov ⭐⭐⭐⭐
A smaller boutique property (under 30 rooms) with extremely high guest satisfaction — often rated 9.6–9.8/10. Every room is individual in design; the building is a renovated 19th-century townhouse. Exceptional breakfast with local products. Personal service.

**Price range 2027:** €100–160/night (double room, breakfast included)

**Best for:** Boutique hotel lovers who appreciate character over chain-hotel polish.

### Bella Muzica ⭐⭐⭐⭐
One of Brașov's most reviewed hotels on Booking.com (800+ reviews, consistently 9.0+). Located directly on Piața Sfatului (Council Square) — literally the best location in the city, with square-facing rooms offering views of the Black Church. A classic Brașov institution.

**Price range 2027:** €85–140/night (double room)

**Best for:** Location maximalists, first-timers who want to be at the center of everything.

### Casa Chitic ⭐⭐⭐
A charming 3-star in a historic building near the Old Town, consistently praised for its warm, family-run atmosphere. Popular with solo travelers and older couples who prefer character over size.

**Price range 2027:** €65–95/night (double room, breakfast often included)

**Best for:** Budget-conscious travelers who still want Old Town character.

### Aro Palace ⭐⭐⭐⭐
The historic grande dame of Brașov — a 1930s hotel on Bulevardul Eroilor that has hosted celebrities, politicians, and royalty. Recently renovated to combine heritage with modern comfort. Not the hippest option, but unmatched in historic significance.

**Price range 2027:** €100–180/night (double room)

**Best for:** History enthusiasts and those who enjoy large, established properties with a sense of occasion.

## Poiana Brașov — Romania's premier ski resort, 12 km from the city

Poiana Brașov sits at 1,030–1,800 m altitude, with 20 km of ski runs across multiple slopes (beginner to advanced), a gondola, 8 chair lifts, and excellent snow cover from December to March. Summer offers hiking, mountain biking, and a completely different crowd of hikers and families.

**Key hotels in Poiana Brașov:**

### Alpin Resort Hotel ⭐⭐⭐⭐
The largest hotel in Poiana Brașov (300+ rooms), with an indoor pool, spa, and ski-in access to the slopes. Great for families. Rated 8.5–9.0/10.

**Price range 2027:** €140–200/night (double room, ski season, breakfast included)

### Sport Hotel Teleferic ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Located at the base of the gondola — you can ski straight to the door. Modern, sporty interior, good restaurant.

**Price range 2027:** €140–195/night (double room, ski season)

### Silver Mountain Resort ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
The premium option in Poiana Brașov — a newer resort with luxury apartments and hotel rooms, spa, indoor/outdoor pools.

**Price range 2027:** €180–320/night

### Budget option: Vila Zorile ⭐⭐⭐
Simple, clean, family-run accommodation on the edge of the resort.

**Price range 2027:** from €45/night (double room, off-season)

**Should you stay in Poiana Brașov or Brașov city?**
If skiing daily is your main activity, stay in Poiana Brașov — the ski-in convenience is worth it. If you want to combine skiing with Old Town dinners, day trips to Bran, and more restaurant variety, stay in Brașov and taxi/bus up to the resort (15 min, €8–12 taxi).

## Bran, Râșnov & Zărnești — day-trip villages with guesthouses

**Bran** (15 km from Brașov) is where Bran Castle stands — Romania's most visited tourist attraction, marketed as "Dracula's Castle" (the historical connection to Vlad the Impaler is tenuous, but the castle itself is genuinely impressive). Guesthouses in the village are cheaper than Brașov and give you the castle at dawn, before the day-trip crowds arrive.

**Râșnov** (18 km) has its own hilltop citadel (better preserved than most), a quiet town center, and guesthouses from €40–75/night.

**Zărnești** (26 km) is the gateway to Piatra Craiului National Park — home to some of Romania's most dramatic hiking. Small pensions cater almost exclusively to hikers and climbers.

**Price range for village guesthouses 2027:**
- Basic double room with breakfast: €35–65/night
- Upgraded pension (private bathroom, garden): €55–90/night
- Smaller family-run "casa de oaspeți": €30–50/night

### How to get to Brașov

**From Bucharest (Henri Coandă Airport — OTP):**
1. Express train Henri Coandă → Bucharest Nord station: 35 min, €2.50
2. Intercity train Bucharest Nord → Brașov: 2h30, €8–12
3. Total door-to-door: ~3.5 hours. Very comfortable, scenic route through the Prahova Valley and past Sinaia.

**By car from Bucharest:** 166 km on A1 motorway to Comarnic, then DN1 mountain road. 2.5–3 hours. Expect traffic on DN1 on Friday evenings.

**From Budapest:** ~500 km, 5–6 hours by car via Oradea. Overnight train Budapest → Cluj-Napoca → Brașov exists but is slow. Budget flights to Cluj-Napoca or Sibiu (then 1.5–2h drive to Brașov) are often cheaper.

**From Vienna:** ~850 km by car (8–9h) or fly direct to Bucharest/Cluj/Sibiu.

**Flying directly to Brașov:** Brașov International Airport (GHV) opened for commercial flights in 2023, with seasonal connections to some European cities. Check availability — the schedule is still expanding.

## Brașov vs other Transylvanian cities for a base

If you're planning a Transylvania circuit, Brașov is the best starting point — most day trips are within 50 km. But consider:

- **Sibiu** (130 km): More compact, fewer tourists, excellent restaurants, great for a 2-night add-on. Smaller hotel selection but good quality.
- **Cluj-Napoca** (170 km): Romania's second largest city, vibrant student city, TIFF film festival. More urban, less medieval. Good flight connections.
- **Sighișoara** (100 km): The birthplace of Vlad the Impaler — tiny medieval citadel, best as a half-day trip from Brașov rather than a base.

The most efficient Transylvania itinerary: **fly into Bucharest → train to Brașov (3 nights) → drive to Sibiu (2 nights) → drive to Cluj-Napoca (2 nights) → fly home**. Total: 7–8 days.

## What to expect at Romanian hotels: practical notes for international visitors

Romanian hospitality standards have improved significantly in the last decade, particularly in Brașov where international tourism is established. A few things worth knowing:

**Breakfast:** Most 3–4 star hotels in Brașov include or offer breakfast. It's typically a cold buffet — charcuterie (mezeluri), local cheeses (especially telemea, a sheep's milk cheese similar to feta), eggs, pastries, and coffee. Some boutique hotels bake their own bread. Breakfast is rarely a disappointment in Brașov.

**Check-in/check-out:** Standard is 14:00–15:00 check-in and 11:00–12:00 check-out. Most hotels will store luggage if you arrive early. Late check-out (13:00–15:00) is often available for free if the hotel isn't full — just ask.

**Payment:** Credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) accepted everywhere. American Express is hit-or-miss at smaller guesthouses. Romanian lei (RON) is the local currency. ATMs are abundant in the center; avoid the airport currency exchange desks (poor rates). Revolut and Wise work seamlessly in Romania.

**Wi-Fi:** Universally fast and free. Romania consistently ranks among the top countries in the world for internet speed. Don't worry about connectivity.

**Parking:** If you're driving, confirm parking with your hotel before booking. Old Town streets are pedestrianized or restricted — most hotels have arrangements with nearby parking garages (€5–10/day). Street parking in the center is metered and limited.

**Language:** English is widely spoken in Brașov hotels, especially by reception staff under 40. German is also understood (Brașov was historically a Saxon German city known as Kronstadt). French and Italian are spoken at some of the higher-end properties.

**Tipping:** 10% is standard and appreciated in restaurants. At hotels, rounding up the bill or leaving €1–2/day for housekeeping is customary but not obligatory.

## Day trips from Brașov: what to combine with your stay

Part of what makes Brașov such a compelling base is the density of remarkable day trips within 1.5 hours:

**Bran Castle (15 km, 25 min):** Romania's most photographed castle. The "Dracula's Castle" label is marketing — Vlad the Impaler's real castle is elsewhere — but Bran is a genuinely impressive 14th-century fortification with an interesting museum inside. Open daily. Entry: €12–15. Arrive before 10:00 to beat tour groups.

**Râșnov Citadel (18 km, 25 min):** A medieval peasant fortress on a rocky hilltop above the town of Râșnov. Better preserved than most Romanian castles, with an excellent small museum. Entry: €6. Combine with Bran in a half-day easily.

**Peleș Castle, Sinaia (45 km, 50 min):** One of the most extravagant royal palaces in Europe — a neo-Renaissance German-style castle built in 1883 by King Carol I of Romania, with 160 rooms, gilded ceilings, and stained-glass windows. The most impressive interior of any castle in the region. Entry: €7–15 (guided tour required for main palace). Worth the 50-minute drive.

**Libearty Bear Sanctuary, Zărnești (26 km, 35 min):** One of Europe's largest bear sanctuaries, home to 100+ rescued brown bears. A genuinely moving and unique experience — the bears live in semi-wild conditions in a forested reserve. Guided tours only. Advance booking essential in summer.

**Piatra Craiului National Park (26 km, 35 min):** The most dramatic ridge hiking in Romania — a 25 km limestone ridge with trails ranging from easy forest walks to technical alpine routes. The trailheads are in Zărnești. Combine with a Bear Sanctuary visit.

**Sighișoara (100 km, 1h30 by car):** The birthplace of Vlad the Impaler (the historical inspiration for Dracula), with a medieval citadel that is better preserved than Brașov's own. Best as a full-day trip or overnight.

## Brașov city: what to do between accommodation and day trips

So you don't end up only sleeping in Brașov:

**The Black Church (Biserica Neagră):** Transylvania's largest Gothic church (14th–15th century), named after fire damage from 1689. Interior holds the largest collection of Oriental rugs in Europe (donated by merchants in gratitude for commercial privileges). Essential visit: 30–45 minutes.

**Piața Sfatului (Council Square):** The medieval main square, surrounded by historic guild houses. The Council House (1420) is the oldest public building in Brașov. Restaurants and cafés spill onto the square — worth an evening here.

**Tampa Mountain cable car:** A 4-minute cable car ride from the center to 960 m altitude, with a panoramic view over the city and the famous "BRAȘOV" letters (Romania's Hollywood Hills equivalent). Round trip: ~€5. Easy 40-minute walk back down through the forest is enjoyable in good weather.

**Rope Street (Strada Sforii):** One of the narrowest streets in Europe (1.1–1.35 m wide), a medieval fire passage between two buildings. Quirky, photogenic, 2 minutes from Council Square.

**Schei Quarter:** The historical Romanian neighborhood below the Old Town, with the St. Nicholas Church and First Romanian School Museum — the first school to teach in the Romanian language (established 1495). Peaceful alternative to the tourist center.

Brașov punches well above its weight for a city of 250,000 people. The combination of a Gothic medieval center, Carpathian mountain scenery, one of Romania's best ski resorts just up the road, and an efficient rail connection to Bucharest makes it the easiest entry point into Transylvania for international visitors — and one of the most rewarding cities in Central and Eastern Europe, at prices that feel like a decade ago by Western European standards.
