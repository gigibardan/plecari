Travelling with children transforms every decision: the beach is no longer just scenic, it needs shallow water and no currents. The city break is no longer about finding the right restaurant, it is about finding the right pacing. The hotel is no longer about the view, it is about whether there are pool floats and whether the breakfast includes something other than local cuisine a five-year-old will refuse.

This guide is written from the reality of family travel — what each destination actually looks like with children at different ages, how to budget honestly, and the one scheduling rule that makes or breaks a family holiday.

---

## Matching destination to age group

### 0–3 years: predictability, warm water, short flights

Infants and toddlers need routine. A successful holiday at this age is one where the sleep schedule is not completely disrupted, food is accessible, and there are a few quiet hours for the adults.

**What works:** Antalya all-inclusive (structured, diverse food, separate paddling pools), Crete or Rhodes (ultra-shallow beaches). **Maximum flight time:** 3–4 hours.

**What does not work:** intensive city breaks (Paris, Vienna with 10+ km walking), cruises (too much variability), remote destinations with limited medical facilities.

### 3–7 years: waterparks, animals, wonder

The golden age for family holidays. Everything is new, energy is unlimited, enthusiasm is genuine. Waterslides, dolphins, castles, theme parks — all work spectacularly.

**What works:** Antalya (waterpark), Crete (beaches + animals), Budapest (Széchenyi thermal pools with slides), Vienna (Schönbrunn Zoo).

### 8–12 years: adventure, discovery, some context

Children begin to understand history, can walk further, and can appreciate more variety. City breaks become manageable with good pacing.

**What works:** Barcelona (Sagrada Família + Aquarium + beach), London (interactive museums), MSC cruise (freedom on board), Prague (castle + submarine museum).

### 12–17 years: real experiences, less "for children"

Teenagers want authentic experiences — not children's clubs and energetic entertainment teams. Diving, water sports, cultural city breaks, open-air concerts.

**What works:** Dubrovnik (sea kayaking, city walls), Athens (Acropolis with real context), Greek islands (surfing, windsurfing, diving), Vienna (Naschmarkt + galleries).

## The 10 best family destinations — complete with costs

### 1. Antalya, Turkey — unbeatable value for families

Antalya is the destination I recommend as a first instinct for almost any family with children under 12: Turkish all-inclusive hotels are built around families. Daily entertainment teams, children's clubs with structured programmes, waterparks with slides for all age groups, separate paddling pools for under-fives, and a sandy beach with gradual entry that stays at waist height for fifty metres.

**What is included in AI:** hotel waterpark (generally impressive), evening shows, beach sports, mini-golf, theatre, billiards, unlimited food and drinks. You need not leave the resort if you prefer not to.

**Activities outside the hotel:** Land of Legends theme park (€40/person — Turkey's largest), Antalya Aquarium (€25 adults, €15 children — the world's longest underwater tunnel), "Pirates of the Mediterranean" boat trip (€25–35, waterslides from the boat, swimming stops).

**Realistic family budget (2 adults + 2 children, 7 nights AI):**

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Flights (4 tickets) | €400–700 |
| AI hotel 4★ (7 nights, 4 people) | €700–1,200 |
| Airport–hotel transfer | €40–80 |
| Extra excursions (2–3) | €150–250 |
| Family travel insurance | €50–80 |
| **Total** | **€1,340–2,310** |

**Important:** Turkey is outside the EU. The EHIC is not valid — travel insurance is mandatory. Buy an eSIM before departure — provider websites have been blocked inside Turkey since 2025. See: [best eSIM by destination](/en/esim-roaming/best-esim-by-destination) and [Turkey 2026 safety guide](/en/asigurari/turkey-2026-safety-guide).

### 2. Crete — perfect beaches and Greece's most complete island

Crete is the most versatile Mediterranean destination for families. The island is 260 km long — turquoise lagoons with pink sand, mountains with spectacular gorges, and one of the world's most significant archaeological sites (the Palace of Knossos, 3,700 years old).

**Why it works for young children:** Elafonisi and Balos are lagoons with warm 20–25 cm water for hundreds of metres. No currents, no significant waves, no danger — a two-year-old plays in turquoise water on pink sand while you watch without anxiety.

**Activities for children:** CretAquarium Thalassocosmos (€10 adults, €6 children — one of the Mediterranean's largest), Dinosauria Park Gournes (€8/person), Palace of Knossos (€15 adults, free under 6 — told well, it fascinates children over 6+), boat trips to Balos lagoon (€20–25/person).

**Family budget (2+2, 7 nights):** €1,400–2,600 with flights depending on hotel category.

**Best age:** any age — Crete has something for every family configuration.

### 3. Barcelona — urban beach + Gaudí + world-class aquarium

Barcelona works better for families than Rome, Paris, or Amsterdam because it combines a beach (Barceloneta — 20 minutes from the centre by metro) with cultural activities and a pace that does not exhaust children.

**Essential activities for children:** Aquarium Barcelona (€25 adults, €18 children — underwater tunnel, sharks, ocean sunfish), Park Güell (€10 with timed entry — the mosaics fascinate from age 3 upwards), Tibidabo (retro fairground on a mountaintop overlooking the city and sea, €25–35), IMAX at the Aquarium (3D, included with the ticket).

**Practical tip:** avoid July–August for families — 35°C in the city and maximum crowds. May–June or September: 24–28°C, half the queues, 20% lower prices.

**Family budget (2+2, 5 nights):** €1,600–2,800 with flights.

**Pickpocket alert:** Barcelona is Europe's most active city for pickpocketing. Crossbody bags for adults, children carry nothing of value. Read: [travel scams in Europe 2026](/en/city-breaks/travel-scams-europe-2026).

### 4. Budapest — thermal pools with slides at one-third of Vienna's price

Budapest is the guaranteed surprise for families — a city break with excellent tourist infrastructure, prices 40% below Western Europe, and two activities that work perfectly with children: thermal pools with slides and architecture so spectacular it requires no explanation.

**Széchenyi Thermal Baths:** outdoor pools at 36–38°C, slides for children and adults, Olympic-size outdoor basin. €25 adults, €15 children. You spend 4–5 hours without thinking about anything else.

**Activities for children:** the Parliament building (tour adaptable for children over 8), Fishermen's Bastion (fairytale architecture free to enter), Danube Promenade (the Little Prince statue — universally loved by children), and Városliget City Park with lake and ice rink in winter.

**Family budget (2+2, 4 nights):** €800–1,400 with flights.

Read: [Things to do in Budapest](/en/experiente/things-to-do-budapest).

### 5. Vienna — the world's oldest zoo and the most beautiful imperial park

Vienna does not appear on typical "family destinations" lists, but works exceptionally well for families with children aged 5–14, particularly in combination with Schönbrunn and the Prater.

**Schönbrunn Zoo:** the world's oldest zoo (1752), with 700 species. Giant pandas, gorillas, penguins, and one of Europe's most beautiful botanical gardens as the backdrop. €22 adults, €10 children.

**The Prater and Wiener Riesenrad:** the 1897 Giant Ferris Wheel (€14, sees all of Vienna), and the Wurstelprater — a retro amusement park with 250 attractions. Entry to the Prater itself is free; you pay per attraction.

**Naturhistorisches Museum:** dinosaurs, meteorites, the 30,000-year-old Venus of Willendorf — a genuinely spectacular collection with short queues.

**Family budget (2+2, 4 nights):** €1,200–2,200 with flights.

Read: [Things to do in Vienna](/en/experiente/things-to-do-vienna).

### 6. MSC Mediterranean Cruise — children free, non-stop activities

A cruise with children sounds complicated in theory. In practice, it is one of the most efficient family holiday formats: you unpack once, food is included and varied, children have their own daily programme at the kids' club, and you see 4–5 destinations without organising anything.

**MSC and children free:** on most MSC Mediterranean itineraries, children under 12 sail without a supplement in the cabin. A family of four pays effectively for two adults.

**Children's clubs on board:** MSC offers Mini Club (ages 3–6), Junior Club (7–11), Teen Club (12–17) — all included in the price, with multilingual entertainment teams, daily activities, and supervised care.

**Why it works so well with children:** child is happy at kids' club → you are free at the pool → dinner together as a family → wake up in a new port. Repeat for 7 days.

### 7. Greek Islands — Zakynthos and Corfu for families

Not Santorini (difficult terrain, no beach) and not Mykonos (expensive and not designed for families) — for family holidays in the Greek islands, Zakynthos and Corfu are the correct choices.

**Zakynthos:** Navagio Beach (the shipwreck — accessible by boat, €20) is one of Greece's iconic images. The east coast beaches (Tsilivi, Kalamaki) have calm, shallow, sandy water. Bonus: loggerhead sea turtles nesting on the beach — responsibly organised observation trips (€20/person).

**Corfu:** as green as Ireland, as Mediterranean as Greece. Paleokastritsa — one of Europe's most beautiful bays, with turquoise water and yachts anchored in the cove. Best explored by rental car.

### 8. London — free museums that beat any theme park

The Natural History Museum is free, has life-size dinosaur reconstructions and a meteorite collection. The Science Museum is free with a interactive flight simulator. The British Museum is free and has Egyptian mummies.

No European destination offers more free high-quality content for children aged 5–14 than London.

**What costs money:** London Zoo (€30/person), London Eye (€36/person — expensive but worth it with children), Harry Potter Studio Tour (€55/person, 30 km from central London — book months in advance).

**Note:** London is outside the EU since Brexit. Passport required (national ID insufficient for most non-EU nationals), travel insurance essential.

**Family budget (2+2, 4 nights):** €1,200–2,200 with flights.

### 9. Prague — Europe's most compact and safest city break

Prague is arguably the easiest European city break with children aged 7–14: compact (everything within 20–30 minutes on foot), safe, with excellent public transport, and with highlights that do not require lengthy historical explanations to impress.

**Prague Castle and St Vitus Cathedral:** the world's largest castle complex by area. Children over 7 are invariably impressed by the scale and the Gothic cathedral.

**Underground Prague:** the military museum with a real submarine (€6/person), the medieval underground passages in Vinohrady, and the Gothic cellars beneath the Old Town. Prague is a city with three levels — above ground, underground, and on the river (rowboats on the Vltava).

**Family budget (2+2, 4 nights):** €900–1,500 with flights.

Read: [Things to do in Prague](/en/experiente/things-to-do-prague).

### 10. Romania — cheapest and most authentic family adventure

A road trip with children through Transylvania is a holiday they will talk about at school for months: Bran Castle (is Dracula real or not?), Peles Castle (a fairy-tale palace with 160 rooms), a bear spotted at the roadside near Predeal, and traditional food at a mountain guesthouse.

**Family budget (2+2, 7 nights, everything included):** €800–1,400 — the most affordable in the top ten.

## Essential practical advice for families

### The one-activity rule

One attraction in the morning, pool or beach in the afternoon, relaxed evening. How many family holidays have become an exhausted forced march at 35°C with a five-year-old crying at every corner? A family holiday is not a cultural tour — it is recovery for the whole family. Internalise this rule before departure.

### Flights with children

**Seats must be reserved** on Wizz Air and Ryanair — without reservation, your child may be seated separately. €4–8/seat per segment is the cheapest safety cost in the entire holiday.

**Boarding:** families with children under 5 or with pushchairs typically board first at most airlines — check the specific policy before travel.

**Hand luggage for families:** rolled clothing (not folded), nappies in the first accessible compartment, tablet with offline downloads, snacks, and — critically — **one change of clothes for each child in the hand luggage, not the hold bag**. Turbulence and airsickness are real.

### Travel insurance for families

A family policy (2 adults + dependent children) costs €30–60/week — cheaper than four separate policies. Essential checks: (1) trip cancellation included (children get ill the night before departure), (2) accompanying parent covered if child is hospitalised, (3) minimum €30,000 medical coverage.

Read the complete guide: [travel insurance for children 2026](/en/asigurari/travel-insurance-children).

One eSIM with hotspot activated covers the whole family. €10–15 once instead of 4 × roaming charges. Mandatory for Turkey and UK. See: [best eSIM by destination 2026](/en/esim-roaming/best-esim-by-destination).
