Children get ill more suddenly and more unpredictably than adults. A fever that would be manageable at home becomes a medical situation in a foreign country where you do not speak the language, do not know the healthcare system, and are an hour's drive from the nearest paediatric unit. Travel insurance for children is not a luxury — for families travelling outside the EU particularly, it is the single most important thing you buy before a holiday.

This guide covers everything parents need to know: what family policies include, the clause most families overlook, real costs when things go wrong, and how to insure for different types of family trip.

---

## Why children must be explicitly insured

The most common mistake: a parent buys travel insurance, assumes the family is covered, and does not read the policy schedule carefully. Travel insurance does not extend automatically to travelling companions, including children. Every person who is insured must be named on the policy, or specifically included under a defined "family" category.

**What "family policy" means in practice:** most insurers define a family as 2 adults and their dependent children (typically up to age 17 or 18) all travelling together. The children are listed on the same document, under the same premium, and covered by the same limits.

**What is not covered under a parent's individual policy:**
- A child travelling on the same holiday but listed only on the parent's passport photo
- A child who is named as a dependent for tax purposes but not on the insurance document
- A child travelling with a single parent where the other parent holds the only policy

The EHIC (European Health Insurance Card) is a useful baseline for EU destinations — children can obtain their own card free through the national health authority. But it does not cover private clinics, repatriation, non-EU destinations, or any of the scenarios described in this guide. For comprehensive protection: EHIC + private travel insurance together.

## Real medical costs for children abroad

Understanding the potential costs is what makes the insurance decision obvious. These are real cost ranges from 2025–2026 data:

| Scenario | EU (private clinic) | Turkey | USA | Thailand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GP/urgent care consultation | €100–400 | €50–200 | €500–2,000 | €50–200 |
| Chest X-ray + diagnosis | €200–500 | €100–300 | €1,000–3,000 | €100–400 |
| Hospitalisation (per night) | €500–2,000 | €200–800 | €3,000–15,000 | €200–1,000 |
| Surgery (appendicitis) | €8,000–25,000 | €3,000–8,000 | €30,000–80,000 | €3,000–10,000 |
| Medical repatriation (air) | €10,000–30,000 | €8,000–20,000 | €40,000–80,000 | €20,000–50,000 |

**Real scenario:** a 7-year-old develops a high fever in Antalya on day 3 of a 10-night holiday. The resort doctor refers to a private hospital. Diagnosis: severe ear infection requiring IV antibiotics. Two nights in hospital: €800. Doctor's fees: €300. Prescription: €80. Total: €1,180. Travel insurance for the whole family: €25. Without the insurance card presented at admission, the parents pay upfront and claim reimbursement — which can take weeks and may be partially disputed.

## What a family travel insurance policy should cover

### Essential coverage (non-negotiable)

**Emergency medical treatment:** covers consultation fees, hospitalisation, surgery, medication, and any medically necessary treatment arising from an unexpected illness or accident. For children, confirm that the policy explicitly covers paediatric care — some budget policies limit this.

**Medical repatriation:** the cost of transporting the child home for continued treatment when medically appropriate. Air ambulance: €15,000–80,000 depending on destination. This is the coverage that justifies the premium more than anything else.

**Accompanying person costs:** the most commonly overlooked family clause. If a child under a defined age (typically 16 or 18) is hospitalised, a parent must stay with them. The additional accommodation, meals, and return travel costs for the accompanying parent should be covered. Check specifically for:
- Daily accommodation allowance (typical: €80–150/night)
- Duration limit (typical: up to 10–14 nights or until the child can travel)
- Return flight rebooking for the accompanying parent

**Emergency dental treatment:** children lose teeth, crack teeth, and develop dental pain — particularly relevant if the trip involves physical activity. Coverage: €200–500 for emergency treatment is standard.

### Important coverage for families

**Trip cancellation:** if a child is too ill to travel before departure, the non-refundable costs of the holiday (flights, hotel, car rental) are covered. This requires: a medical certificate confirming the child cannot travel, and a policy that explicitly covers trip cancellation.

**Trip interruption:** if an emergency at home (a grandparent hospitalised, a family bereavement) requires the family to return early, the additional return flight costs are covered.

**Baggage cover:** children travel with more luggage than adults relative to trip length. Pushchairs, car seats, and specialist equipment (ski gear, sports equipment) should be declared and covered. Standard baggage cover: €1,000–2,000 per person, with sub-limits per item.

**Activity and sports cover:** standard policies cover swimming and beach activities. Skiing, horse riding, cycling, water sports, and any activity that involves speed or height typically requires a specific endorsement or a policy that includes "hazardous activities." ## Types of family policy: which to choose

### Per-person policies (separate for each family member)

Each family member has their own document with their own limits. Administratively more complex but allows customisation — for example, if a grandparent is travelling with different health requirements.

**Best for:** families where members have different needs, ages, or health conditions requiring different coverage levels.

### Family policy (one document, everyone included)

One policy covering 2 adults and their dependent children. Typically 15–20% cheaper than equivalent separate policies. Administratively simple.

**Best for:** standard family holidays where everyone is travelling together with similar needs.

**Key things to check in a family policy:**
- Age limit for dependent children (most: up to 17; some: up to 21 for students)
- Whether all children must travel with both parents (some policies require this)
- Per-person limits vs per-claim limits — if the policy has a total limit rather than a per-person limit, a major claim for one family member could reduce coverage for others
- Whether the accompanying parent benefit is included (see above)

### Annual multi-trip family policy

One premium covers the whole family for all trips taken in a 12-month period, subject to a maximum duration per trip (typically 30 or 45 days).

**Cost:** approximately €150–250 for a family of four.
**Best for:** families who take 3+ holidays per year — the annual policy pays for itself at the third trip.
**Limitation:** individual trip length is capped. Not suitable for extended trips (sabbaticals, gap years, 3-month stays).

## Schengen visa applications: children's insurance requirements

For non-EU families applying for a Schengen short-stay visa, children have exactly the same insurance requirements as adults. The consulate checks each applicant's documentation separately.

**Minimum requirements:**
- €30,000 minimum medical coverage
- Coverage valid for the entire stay
- Geographic coverage matching the itinerary (all Schengen countries if visiting multiple)
- Explicit inclusion of: emergency medical treatment, hospitalisation, repatriation, repatriation of remains

**What to present:** the insurance policy document (PDF) showing the child's name, coverage dates, coverage amount, and territorial scope. Some consulates also require a separate "Schengen insurance certificate" — a standardised confirmation that the policy meets Schengen requirements. Check the specific consulate's requirements before applying.

**Common mistake:** buying a family policy and submitting only one document without showing that the child is explicitly named as an insured person. The consulate needs to see each applicant listed.

## Destinations and what to verify per region

### EU destinations (France, Spain, Italy, Greece, etc.)

**EHIC:** children can obtain their own EHIC free of charge, providing access to public hospitals. [Compare EHIC vs travel insurance](/en/asigurari/ehic-vs-travel-insurance) — the EHIC alone is insufficient for most family travel scenarios.

**Private travel insurance:** covers private clinics (more accessible in tourist resorts than public hospitals), repatriation, and trip disruption. Essential complement to the EHIC.

**Greece specifically:** see [Greece 2026 holiday guide](/en/city-breaks/greece-2026-holiday-guide) — excellent family destination with good medical infrastructure on major islands.

### Turkey (Antalya, Istanbul, coast)

**EHIC not valid** — Turkey is outside the EU. Private travel insurance is your only medical protection. Antalya's private hospitals in the resort areas are excellent quality — the bills without insurance are significant.

See: [Turkey 2026 safety guide](/en/asigurari/turkey-2026-safety-guide) — full medical and practical preparation for Turkey.

**eSIM for Turkey:** buy before departure. Provider websites are blocked inside Turkey since 2025. See: [how to install an eSIM](/en/esim-roaming/how-to-install-esim).

### USA, Canada, Australia

**EHIC not valid.** Medical costs are the highest in the world. A single night in a US hospital: $3,000–15,000. Repatriation from the US: $40,000–80,000. Family travel insurance with a minimum $1,000,000 medical coverage is the minimum sensible coverage for North American travel.

### Long-haul destinations (Thailand, Bali, South Africa, etc.)

Verify that your insurer covers the specific destination and that medical evacuation is explicitly included. Quality of local healthcare varies enormously; evacuation to the nearest major medical centre (Bangkok, Cape Town) before onward repatriation may be required — a cost typically €15,000–30,000.

## When things go wrong: how to use the insurance

### Step 1: Call the insurer's emergency line first

For non-life-threatening situations, always call before going to a hospital. The insurer directs you to a network hospital where treatment is pre-authorised — no upfront payment, no reimbursement process. For life-threatening emergencies: call 112 (EU) or local emergency services first, then the insurer.

**Before the trip:** save the insurer's 24/7 emergency number as a contact. Include the policy number in the notes field.

### Step 2: For life-threatening emergencies

Call the local emergency number (112 in EU, 911 in USA, 999 in UK). The insurer's involvement begins once the immediate emergency is stabilised. Do not delay emergency care waiting for insurer approval.

### Step 3: Keep all documentation

Every receipt, every medical report, every discharge summary, every prescription. For reimbursement claims (when you pay and claim back): the documentation is the claim. A hospital bill without the supporting clinical documentation is frequently disputed.

### Step 4: Contact the accompanying airline

If a medical situation means early return, many airlines will rebook at no charge with a medical certificate. This is separate from the insurance claim — start the airline process simultaneously.

## The accompanying parent clause in detail

This is the clause that most families overlook and most frequently need. If a child is hospitalised abroad, local law in most EU countries requires parental presence for children under 16. The child cannot be left in hospital alone. The accompanying parent faces:

- Additional hotel nights near the hospital: €80–200/night
- Meals for an extended stay: €40–80/day
- Revised return flight: €200–600 (and sometimes more in high season)
- Loss of pre-paid accommodation at the original destination

A comprehensive family policy covers all of these, typically up to €150/night accommodation and up to 14 additional nights.

**What to check in your policy:** look for "accompanying person benefit," "emergency companion costs," or "child hospitalisation companion cover." If this language is absent from the policy schedule, assume it is not covered.

## Checklist before your family trip

**At purchase:**
- [ ] All children named explicitly on the policy or included under "family" definition
- [ ] Destination(s) covered (check for exclusions)
- [ ] All planned activities covered (swimming, cycling, water sports, skiing if applicable)
- [ ] Trip cancellation included (useful for families — children fall ill before departures)
- [ ] Accompanying person benefit present for children under 16/18
- [ ] Medical limit appropriate for destination (minimum €30,000 EU; €1,000,000+ USA)

**Before departure:**
- [ ] Policy documents downloaded and accessible offline on your phone
- [ ] Insurer's 24/7 emergency number saved in your phone
- [ ] Policy number noted separately from your phone (in your wallet)
- [ ] EHIC cards for all family members (EU trips)
- [ ] Each child's EHIC number noted separately

**At the destination:**
- [ ] Know the local emergency number (112 in EU)
- [ ] Know the nearest hospital with a paediatric department (ask your hotel on day 1)
