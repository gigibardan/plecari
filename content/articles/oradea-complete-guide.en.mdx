There is a question I hear frequently from tourists who have visited Oradea: "Why didn't I know about this city?"

The answer is simple: Oradea was invisible for decades under layers of communist grey. And then, between 2015 and 2026, something rare happened in Romania — a genuine, deep and consistent urban renovation.

<cite index="15-1">Oradea is nicknamed the Art Nouveau Capital of Romania — the historic centre has been restored with pastel façades, Secessionist and Art Nouveau buildings brought back to their original splendour.</cite>

Today, Unirii Square and Republicii Street in Oradea look as they did in 1910 — with Art Nouveau floral ornaments, façades in yellow, peach and light green, wide pedestrian pavements and a European city centre atmosphere that few associated with Romania.

Add to this: <cite index="18-1">the Black Eagle Palace with its coloured glass arcade, the Moon Church with the world's unique astronomical clock, the medieval Fortress and Băile Felix thermal springs 8 km away</cite> — a complete destination for any type of visitor.

## Oradea at a Glance — Essential Facts

**County:** Bihor, north-western Romania.

**Population:** approximately 206,000.

**Geographical position:** 12 km from the Hungarian border. <cite index="15-1">240 km from Budapest, 580 km from Bucharest.</cite>

**Nicknames:** "Little Paris of the Crișul River", "Art Nouveau Capital of Romania".

**Bilingualism:** significant Hungarian community — bilingual signage, dual Romanian-Hungarian culture.

**Airport:** Oradea (OMR) — international, with flights from several European cities.

**Average budget:** 120–200 RON/day (~24–40 EUR) — similar to Iași, more affordable than Cluj.

## How to Get to Oradea from Bucharest

### Direct Flight OTP → OMR

Wizz Air operates direct flights. Duration: ~1h10. 2026 prices: 40–100 EUR return.

**Airport → centre (5 km):** Bolt 15–25 RON, taxi 25–35 RON.

### By Train

InterCity Bucharest → Oradea: ~7–8 hours, 100–160 RON. Long but comfortable.

### By Car

~580 km on A1 + A3, ~6 hours. Good option if combining with Cluj (120 km east of Oradea).

## The Urban Renovation — Romania's Most Ambitious Project

Before visiting individual attractions, the context needs to be understood: <cite index="15-1">Oradea has run Romania's most ambitious urban renovation programme.</cite>

Between 2015 and 2026, the municipality restored over 300 historic buildings in the city centre, recreating the original Art Nouveau and Secessionist façades with the colours documented in the original plans from 1900–1910. The result is a centre that looks like turn-of-the-century Budapest or Vienna — not a reconstruction but an authentic recovery.

<cite index="17-1">Oradea is now considered one of Eastern Europe's most beautiful destinations for Art Nouveau architecture enthusiasts.</cite>

## What to See in Oradea

### Unirii Square — The Renewed Heart

<cite index="15-1">Unirii Square is Oradea's heart, surrounded by the City Hall Tower, the Baroque Roman Catholic Cathedral and impressive restored buildings.</cite>

**City Hall Tower** — <cite index="20-1">climb for spectacular views over the old town and the Crișul Repede River.</cite> Ticket: ~15–20 RON.

**Roman Catholic Cathedral of St Ladislaus** — one of Romania's largest Baroque cathedrals, built in the 18th century. Free entry.

**Artesian fountains** in the centre of the square — active in summer, with evening light shows.

### Black Eagle Palace — The Art Nouveau Gem

<cite index="15-1">The Black Eagle Palace (1907–1909) is a remarkable architectural achievement — a building with a covered commercial arcade topped by a coloured glass vault and Art Nouveau floral ornamentation.</cite>

The arcade connects Republicii Street to Unirii Square — a 100-metre crossing under a glass dome with vegetal and floral motifs in bronze and wrought iron. Free entry, open daily.

<cite index="16-1">It is one of Romania's most photographed locations</cite> — especially in the golden hours when sunlight filters through the coloured glass creating a cathedral-like effect.

### Moon Church — The World-Unique Clock

<cite index="16-1">The Moon Church (1784) on Unirii Square has a completely special astronomical clock — a metal ball that rotates on the clock face in exactly 29.5 days, indicating the moon's phases.</cite>

It is the only clock in the world with this function — indicating the moon's phases on the clock face with a moving ball. Built by craftsman András Nagyváthy, the mechanism is intact and working to this day.

Free access to the exterior; interior accessible during visiting hours.

### Republicii Street — The Open-Air Art Nouveau Gallery

<cite index="16-1">Republicii Street is Oradea's most elegant pedestrian street, with Art Nouveau and Secessionist buildings restored on both sides.</cite>

It is the main promenade for Oradea's residents — and tourists. <cite index="20-1">Every building on Republicii Street has its own architectural story</cite> — Moskovits Palace, Adorján Palace, Stern Palace — each with unique ornamentation.

A complete walk from one end to the other takes 15–20 minutes. Free.

### Oradea Fortress — 1,000 Years of History

<cite index="15-1">Oradea Fortress is a fortification with over 1,000 years of history, today hosting cultural exhibitions and museums.</cite>

Originally built in the 11th century, extended and modified under the Habsburgs in bastion style in the 17th–18th centuries. <cite index="18-1">The Țării Crișurilor Museum in the fortress houses collections of Transylvanian art, dinosaur fossils and ethnographic exhibits.</cite>

Fortress + museum ticket: ~25–35 RON (~5–7 EUR).

### Baroque Palace — The Episcopal Museum

<cite index="16-1">Oradea's Baroque Palace (18th century) is impressive — now a museum complex with an elegant inner courtyard, a bronze and marble statue park and collections of Transylvanian folk art.</cite>

Ticket: ~20 RON.

### Oradea's Synagogues — Jewish Heritage

<cite index="20-1">Oradea had one of Transylvania's largest Jewish communities — the Neolog Synagogue and Sion Synagogue are testimonies to this history.</cite> The Neolog Synagogue (1878) is an impressive Moorish building. Visitable with a small ticket (~10–15 RON).

## Băile Felix and Băile 1 Mai — Thermal Relaxation 8 km Away

### Băile Felix — Romania's Largest Health Resort

<cite index="19-1">Băile Felix is Romania's largest health resort, 8 km from Oradea, with natural thermal waters rich in oligominerals springing at temperatures of 22–38°C.</cite>

The thermal waters have documented therapeutic properties for: rheumatism, arthritis, neurological and gynaecological conditions.

**Main complexes:**
- **Strand Felix** — outdoor pools, entry ~50–80 RON
- **Hotel spa complexes** with indoor facilities — 80–150 RON per day

**Getting there:** regular bus from Oradea, 20 minutes, 3–5 RON. Or taxi, ~50 RON.

### Nymphaea AquaPark — The Largest in the West

<cite index="19-1">Oradea's Nymphaea AquaPark has six pools (indoor and outdoor) of various sizes, five saunas, a Turkish bath, water slides, jacuzzis, beauty salons, spa and two restaurants.</cite>

Entry: ~80–120 RON per day. Hours: daily 10:00–22:00.

## Food and Drink in Oradea

Oradea's cuisine is influenced by its Hungarian proximity — a blend of Romanian and Hungarian character.

**Gulyás (goulash)** — beef stew with paprika and potatoes — the Hungarian speciality present in every Oradea restaurant. 30–50 RON.

**Pörkölt** — meat ragout with paprika, more concentrated than goulash. 35–55 RON.

**Kürtőskalács** — at every street corner in the centre, 15–25 RON.

**Lángos** — fried dough with sour cream or cheese. 10–20 RON.

**Miniș–Măderat wine** — the nearby vineyard, with local Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot.

## Practical Tips

**Black Eagle Palace photography** — best moment: morning 10:00–11:00 when sunlight enters obliquely through the glass vault. In the evening with artificial lighting, equally spectacular.

**Bus to Băile Felix** — the bus line is frequent and cheap (3–5 RON). Not worth a taxi unless you have luggage.

**Bilingualism** — in Oradea you'll hear Romanian and Hungarian in equal measure. Signage is bilingual. Both communities are welcoming.

**Budapest connection** — Oradea is 240 km from Budapest by train (2.5–3 hours). If you're in western Romania, combine with a day in Budapest or Debrecen (50 km into Hungary).
