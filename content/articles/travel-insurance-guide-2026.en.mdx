Most people buy travel insurance in 3 minutes as an afterthought at checkout, or skip it entirely because "nothing's going to happen." Then something does happen — a fracture on Rome's cobblestones, a stomach bug in Turkey, a flight cancelled 12 hours before departure — and the reality of uninsured costs becomes very clear, very fast.

This guide covers everything you actually need to know: what travel insurance covers (and the exclusions that cause most claim denials), how much it costs per destination in 2026, how it compares to the European Health Insurance Card, and the 7 mistakes that cost travellers thousands of euros every year.

## What Is Travel Insurance and Why Does It Matter in 2026?

Travel insurance is a short-term policy that protects you financially against unexpected events during — and in some cases before — a trip abroad. Unlike car insurance or home insurance (which are often legally required), travel insurance is typically optional, which is why so many people skip it.

The risk calculation has changed in 2026. Post-pandemic travel patterns, more extreme weather disruptions, increased flight cancellations, and rising medical costs across Europe and beyond mean the expected cost of being uninsured is higher than it was five years ago.

**What a good travel insurance policy covers:**

- **Medical emergencies abroad** — the core, and the most expensive risk
- **Emergency medical repatriation** — flying you home when you can't travel commercially
- **Trip cancellation** — recovering non-refundable costs if you can't depart
- **Trip interruption** — returning home early due to an emergency
- **Lost, stolen, or damaged baggage** — replacement costs
- **Flight delay** — additional expenses caused by significant delays
- **Personal liability** — if you accidentally injure someone or damage property

**What varies by policy (read before you buy):**
- Pre-existing medical conditions (requires declaration and may cost more)
- Winter sports and extreme activities
- Electronics and high-value items (often sublimits)
- Travel to countries with government-issued "do not travel" warnings

## What's Actually Covered — Section by Section

### Medical Emergencies

The core of any travel policy. Covers hospital treatment, surgery, medications, doctor visits, and in some cases dental emergency treatment, when you need urgent care abroad.

**Minimum cover you should accept:**
- €30,000 for travel within the EU/Schengen area (Schengen visa requirement)
- €50,000–100,000 for Turkey, UK, or non-EU European countries
- €100,000+ for the USA, Canada, or Australia (American medical bills are astronomical — a single ER visit with tests regularly exceeds $10,000)

**Real-world cost examples:**
- Broken leg in Germany: surgery + 3-night hospital stay = €8,000–15,000
- Appendix removed in Italy: €5,000–12,000
- Heart attack in Turkey, private hospital: €10,000–30,000
- ICU admission in the USA: €5,000–15,000 per day

Without insurance, these costs are paid out of pocket.

### Medical Repatriation

If you're seriously ill or injured and cannot travel commercially, you may need a medical evacuation flight back home. This is separate from (and in addition to) the cost of treatment.

Air ambulance from Western Europe: **€10,000–30,000**
Air ambulance from the USA or Asia: **€40,000–100,000+**

The European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) does NOT cover repatriation. This is the single biggest gap that makes "I have the European card, I'm fine" a dangerous assumption.

### Emergency Medical Repatriation of Remains

In the worst case scenario, the cost of returning remains to Romania from abroad: €5,000–15,000, varying by distance and country. Travel insurance covers this entirely, avoiding a devastating additional financial burden on grieving families.

### Baggage Loss, Theft, and Damage

Covers the cost of replacing luggage lost by airlines or stolen during the trip. Standard limits: €500–1,500 total.

**Important sublimits to check:**
- Electronics (phones, laptops): often capped at €200–300 per item
- Jewellery and watches: often excluded or capped very low
- Cash: usually capped at €150–300

If you travel with expensive electronics, check the sublimits carefully — or consider specialist gadget insurance separately.

### Trip Cancellation

Covers non-refundable pre-paid costs (flights, hotels, tours, cruises) if you're forced to cancel your trip for a covered reason BEFORE departure.

**Covered reasons (standard):**
- Sudden illness or injury (documented by a doctor)
- Death of a close family member
- Serious accident
- Court summons or jury duty
- Redundancy (some policies)
- Natural disaster at destination (some policies)

**NOT covered:**
- Changing your mind
- Work commitments
- An event you knew about when you bought the policy
- Fear of travel or pandemic anxiety (without a government travel ban)

For maximum protection, some insurers offer **CFAR (Cancel For Any Reason)** add-ons — typically at 40–50% extra premium, covering 75% of your costs if you cancel for any reason whatsoever. Worth considering for expensive bookings with non-refundable deposits.

### Flight Delay

Covers additional expenses caused by significant delays (usually 4–12 hours, depending on policy): meals, accommodation, transport. Note: this is separate from EU261 compensation (which the airline owes you) — insurance covers expenses the airline doesn't.

### Personal Liability

Covers legal costs and damages if you accidentally injure someone or damage their property. Typically €500,000–2,000,000 coverage. Useful but rarely claimed — included in most standard policies.

**Age premiums:** Over 65 increases premiums by 50–100%. Over 75, many standard insurers won't cover at all — specialist senior travel insurance exists for this.

## European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) vs. Travel Insurance

This is the single biggest misconception in European travel. The EHIC is not travel insurance. Here's the precise comparison:

| | European Health Card (EHIC) | Private Travel Insurance |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Free (apply at CAS/NHS) | €12–80/week |
| Geographic coverage | EU + EEA only | Worldwide |
| Hospitals covered | Public hospitals only | Public + private |
| Medical repatriation | ❌ Not covered | ✅ Included |
| Trip cancellation | ❌ Not covered | ✅ Included |
| Lost/stolen baggage | ❌ Not covered | ✅ Included |
| Winter sports | ❌ Not covered | ✅ With add-on |
| Turkey, UK, USA | ❌ Not valid | ✅ Covered |
| Dental emergency | ✅ At public dentists only | ✅ Included |
| Flight delays | ❌ Not covered | ✅ Included |

**The correct strategy:** Get the EHIC (it's free, takes a few days) and use it as a secondary backup within the EU — some insurers reduce your premium slightly if you have it. But never rely on it as your primary protection.

## What's NOT Covered — The Exclusions That Cause Most Claim Denials

Insurance companies are required to cover what they promise — and they're also legally allowed to exclude what they clearly specify. These are the most common exclusion-related claim denials:

### 1. Undeclared Pre-Existing Conditions

If you have diabetes, hypertension, asthma, a heart condition, or any chronic illness and you don't declare it at the time of purchase, any emergency related to that condition will be denied. Period.

**What to do:** Declare everything. The premium surcharge is usually small (€5–20 extra), and it's far less than the cost of a denied claim.

### 2. Accidents Under the Influence of Alcohol

Fell off a scooter in Bali after 3 cocktails? The insurer has the legal right to deny the claim. This is the most commonly invoked exclusion for denials. Insurers are explicit about this in their conditions.

### 3. Sports Without the Add-On

Standard policies cover everyday activities: swimming, cycling, light hiking, snorkelling. They exclude: skiing, snowboarding, surfing, scuba diving (beyond recreational), bungee jumping, paragliding, and most "extreme" activities. Adding the winter sports or adventure sports rider: €5–20 extra. Without it, a ski accident means you pay everything yourself.

### 4. Planned Medical Procedures

If you travel specifically to get medical treatment abroad (dental tourism, cosmetic surgery, planned procedures), this is entirely excluded from standard travel insurance. These are elective choices, not emergencies.

### 5. Conflict Zones

Travel to countries under government "do not travel" advisories (FCO Level 4, MAE Level 4) is typically excluded. This includes active war zones and some high-risk areas.

### 6. Pandemics (Policy-Dependent)

Coverage for pandemic-related cancellations or medical costs varies widely by insurer and policy. Some cover COVID-19 as a standard illness; others exclude it entirely. Read the specific policy wording.

## 7 Mistakes Travellers Make — and How to Avoid Them

### Mistake 1: "I have the European card — I'm covered"

As detailed above: the EHIC covers only public hospitals in EU countries. It doesn't cover repatriation, baggage, cancellation, or any trip to Turkey, UK, or outside the EU. Thousands of travellers discover this gap in an emergency.

### Mistake 2: "My bank card has travel insurance"

Some premium bank cards (Gold, Platinum) include travel cover — but with critical limitations: medical cover of only €10,000–25,000 (often insufficient), conditions requiring you to have paid the flight with that specific card, activation requirements, and sometimes shorter maximum trip durations. Call your bank before assuming you're covered. Get the details in writing.

### Mistake 3: "I'm only going for a city break — 3 days, nothing can happen"

Duration doesn't reduce risk. A broken ankle on Barcelona's cobblestones, an allergic reaction in Amsterdam, or food poisoning in Prague can happen in 3 hours. Three-day insurance for Europe costs €5–8. The ratio is clear.

### Mistake 4: "I'll buy insurance at the airport"

Airport insurance is typically 40–60% more expensive than buying online. Insurance agents at airports operate with high commission structures. Buy online, ideally the same day you make your first non-refundable booking.

### Mistake 5: "I'm driving to Greece — I don't need it"

Your car insurance (third-party liability) covers the vehicle. Travel insurance covers YOU: your health, your baggage, your trip costs. They are entirely separate products. Romanian drivers in Greece without travel insurance are exposed to the same medical cost risk as everyone else.

### Mistake 6: "I'll figure out the hospital and claim back later"

Wrong approach — and it can invalidate your claim. If you need emergency medical treatment abroad, **call your insurer's 24/7 emergency line first** (the number is on your policy card). They will direct you to network hospitals where payment is direct, and they will authorise treatment. Going directly to a private hospital without prior authorisation may result in the insurer refusing reimbursement.

### Mistake 7: "I'll buy it a few days before, just in case"

Trip cancellation cover works in reverse: you buy it as close as possible to when you make your non-refundable bookings. If you book a €800 non-refundable hotel in February and only buy insurance in April, you've been unprotected for two months. Buy insurance immediately after the first non-refundable cost.

## How to Choose the Right Policy in 5 Minutes

A practical decision tree for most travellers:

**Step 1: Where are you going?**
- EU/Schengen: minimum €30,000 medical cover
- Turkey, UK, non-EU Europe: minimum €50,000
- USA, Canada, Australia: minimum €100,000
- Asia, South America, Africa: minimum €75,000

**Step 2: What activities?**
- Skiing, snowboarding, winter sports: add sports rider
- Scuba diving, paragliding, extreme sports: add adventure rider
- Standard sightseeing, hiking, beaches: no add-on needed

**Step 3: Do you have pre-existing conditions?**
- Yes: declare them at purchase — don't skip this

**Step 4: How much have you paid non-refundably?**
- Under €300: basic policy is fine
- €300–1,000: standard policy with cancellation cover
- €1,000+: comprehensive policy, consider CFAR add-on

**Step 5: How often do you travel?**
- 1–2 trips per year: single trip policies
- 3+ trips per year: annual MultiTravel policy

## What to Do When You Need to Make a Claim

The process matters as much as having the policy:

**1. Call the 24/7 emergency line immediately** (printed on your policy card). Don't go to a hospital first — call the insurer first for emergencies.

**2. Get documentation of everything:**
- Medical: doctor's report, diagnosis, all invoices and receipts
- Baggage: airline's Property Irregularity Report (PIR) and baggage claim reference
- Cancellation: doctor's note, death certificate, official documents depending on reason

**3. Keep ALL receipts.** Every meal, taxi, pharmacy purchase, phone call made because of the insured event.

**4. File the claim as soon as you're home.** Most policies require claims within 30–60 days of the incident. Don't wait.

**5. If your claim is denied:** You have the right to appeal. Insurers must provide the specific reason for denial. If they've denied a legitimate claim incorrectly, the Financial Supervisory Authority (ASF in Romania) handles insurance complaints.

## Travel Insurance and EU261 — How They Work Together

These two protections cover different things and complement each other:

- **EU261** (see our full guide): the airline owes you €250–€600 when THEY cancel or significantly delay your flight. This is separate from insurance — it's a legal obligation on the airline.
- **Travel insurance**: covers YOUR costs when a disruption causes knock-on effects the airline doesn't cover — cancelled hotel nights, non-refundable excursions, additional transport.

The best protection is both: EU261 for the airline's legal obligation, and travel insurance for everything else.

If your flight is cancelled and the airline cites "extraordinary circumstances" (weather, ATC strike) — which means NO EU261 compensation applies — your travel insurance trip cancellation/interruption cover becomes your primary protection.
