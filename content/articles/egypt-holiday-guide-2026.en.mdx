Egypt occupies a unique position in European beach tourism: it's one of the few destinations where you can combine a genuine beach holiday (warm Red Sea, excellent diving, competitive all-inclusive prices) with access to some of the most extraordinary cultural experiences on the planet — the Pyramids, the Valley of the Kings, the temples at Luxor and Karnak. Most travellers do one or the other; the ambitious ones do both in the same trip.

This guide covers everything you need to plan either an Egypt beach holiday or a combined cultural and beach trip in 2026 — with the practical information about visas, insurance, safety, and data that many Egypt guides skip.

## The Two Egypts — Choosing Your Focus

### Egypt Beach Holiday (Red Sea Coast)

The Red Sea coast — Hurghada, Sharm el-Sheikh, Marsa Alam, Dahab — offers all-inclusive beach holidays at prices significantly below comparable Mediterranean alternatives. The sea is warm year-round (26–28°C in winter, 28–30°C in summer), the coral reefs are among the best in the world for snorkelling and diving, and the sunshine is virtually guaranteed.

**Best for:** families wanting a reliable beach holiday at good value, snorkellers and divers, those wanting warm sea outside European summer, couples wanting a resort escape in winter.

### Egypt Cultural Tourism (Cairo, Luxor, Aswan)

The Nile corridor is one of the world's great cultural journeys: the Pyramids of Giza, the Egyptian Museum, the Valley of the Kings, the temples at Karnak, Abu Simbel. These are not just historically significant — they're physically overwhelming. Standing at the base of the Great Pyramid, understanding that it was built 4,500 years ago with 2.3 million limestone blocks, is a genuinely affecting experience.

**Best for:** history enthusiasts, first-time Egypt visitors who want to understand what they're seeing, those who find pure beach holidays unstimulating, and anyone who's wondered "what's it actually like?"

### The Combined Option

Many travellers do 5–7 nights beach (Hurghada) plus 2–3 nights cultural (Cairo). Hurghada–Cairo is 5 hours by road or 50 minutes by flight. This combination is well-served by Egyptian tour operators and gives you the best of both without sacrificing either.

## Practical Essentials Before You Go

### Passport and E-Visa

Egypt requires a full passport (not just EU ID card). Romanian citizens need a visa — the e-Visa is the simplest option:

- Apply at: **visa2egypt.gov.eg**
- Single-entry tourist visa: **$25 USD**
- Multiple-entry: $60 USD
- Processing time: 3–7 business days (apply at least 2 weeks before travel)
- Valid: 3 months from issue date, stay up to 30 days

Alternatively, visa on arrival is available at Cairo International, Hurghada, and Sharm el-Sheikh airports — $25 USD or €25 EUR cash. The e-Visa is preferable as it avoids queuing at the visa counter after a long flight.

### Travel Insurance — Non-Negotiable

Egypt is not in the EU, so the European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) is worthless here. Egyptian private hospitals — which tourists use for any serious treatment — charge upfront:

- Emergency room visit: $100–400
- One night of hospitalisation: $500–2,000+
- Medical repatriation to Romania: $20,000–60,000

At these stakes, it's the most important line item in your Egypt holiday budget.

**Check that your policy specifically covers Egypt** — some standard policies exclude Middle East/North Africa destinations or require a premium add-on. Read the fine print before purchasing.

### eSIM — Buy Before You Land

Roaming in Egypt on a Romanian SIM: €5–15 per day for data. A 7-night holiday = €35–105 in roaming charges.

### Safety Overview for 2026

**Hurghada and Red Sea coast:** Considered safe for tourists. Millions of Europeans visit annually without incident. Crime targeting tourists is low; the main risks are traffic accidents and water safety (rip currents, marine life). Standard resort precautions apply.

**Cairo:** Generally safe in tourist areas. Use ride-hailing apps (Uber and Careem are both active) rather than street taxis. Watch for pickpockets in crowded markets. The tourist areas (Pyramids, Egyptian Museum, Khan el-Khalili bazaar) have visible security presence.

**Sinai Peninsula (Sharm el-Sheikh, Dahab, Taba):** This requires careful consideration. The UK FCO, US State Department, and most EU governments maintain advisories recommending against travel to parts of North Sinai due to terrorist activity. The resort areas of South Sinai (Sharm el-Sheikh, Dahab, Nuweiba) are separated from North Sinai and have security infrastructure — but they're on the same peninsula. Most package holiday tourists visit Sharm without incident; the advisory reflects a genuine, if low-probability, elevated risk. Check your specific government's current advisory and make an informed personal decision.

**Check before booking:** mae.ro (Romania), fco.gov.uk (UK), travel.state.gov (USA).

## The Red Sea Resorts — Compared

### Hurghada

Egypt's largest and most accessible Red Sea resort, with direct charter flights from dozens of European cities. Hurghada's beaches are pleasant but not spectacular — the coral reefs have been somewhat degraded by development and anchoring over decades. The real draw is the water sports, the all-inclusive value, and the access it provides to better dive sites by boat.

**Best for:** families, first-time Egypt visitors, those who want the cheapest package option, people who prefer a larger resort with maximum amenities.

**Beach quality:** Decent sandy beaches at most 4–5 star hotels; some hotels have artificial beaches or jetties. The best snorkelling is reached by boat (glass-bottom boats from €15–25).

**Dive sites:** Reasonable locally, but the best Hurghada diving requires day trips (Abu Nuhas, Giftun Island, Elphinstone). Several excellent dive centres along the marina.

**All-inclusive prices (7 nights, charter flight):**
- 3-star hotel: €350–450/person
- 4-star hotel: €450–650/person
- 5-star hotel: €650–1,000/person

**Day trips from Hurghada:**
- Cairo + Pyramids: €80–250 (coach or fly-drive)
- Luxor: €60–100 (coach day trip, 3h each way)
- Desert quad biking: €35–60
- Glass-bottom boat + snorkelling: €20–35
- Dolphin House snorkelling: €25–40

### Sharm el-Sheikh

More sophisticated and expensive than Hurghada, with better diving, more dramatic mountain scenery (the Sinai mountains behind the resorts are extraordinary), and a more upmarket feel. The Ras Mohammed National Park at the southern tip of the Sinai Peninsula is one of the world's premier diving destinations.

**Best for:** divers, honeymooners, those who want a more polished resort experience, couples without children.

**Beach quality:** Na'ama Bay (the original resort area) has good beaches. Sharks Bay and Nabq Bay to the north have more natural surroundings. The beaches are generally better than Hurghada.

**Dive sites:** World-class. The SS Thistlegorm (WWII wreck, one of the world's top 10 dive sites), Ras Mohammed National Park, the Blue Hole near Dahab, and dozens of wall dives with visibility exceeding 30 metres.

**Prices:** 10–15% higher than Hurghada for equivalent hotels.

**Safety note:** See Sinai section above. Most visitors travel without incident, but the government advisory is worth reading before booking.

### Marsa Alam

280 km south of Hurghada, Marsa Alam is the underdeveloped, under-visited gem of the Egyptian Red Sea. Fewer tourists, pristine coral reefs directly from the beach (no boat required at many spots), and prices lower than both Hurghada and Sharm. Famous for dugong sightings and spinner dolphins.

**Best for:** divers, nature lovers, those who find Hurghada too commercial, travellers who want Egypt without the crowds.

**Limitation:** fewer direct flights than Hurghada, smaller resort infrastructure.

## Cairo — The Cultural Centrepiece

Cairo is one of the world's most intense cities — 22 million people, traffic that makes Rome look calm, the call to prayer echoing across rooftops, and in the middle of it all, the last surviving Wonder of the Ancient World. It's chaotic, exhausting, extraordinary, and unforgettable.

### The Pyramids of Giza

The Great Pyramid of Khufu, the Pyramid of Khafre, and the smaller Pyramid of Menkaure, built between 2560 and 2510 BC. The Sphinx. All of it rising from the desert plateau at the edge of Cairo — and visible from the highway as you approach.

**Practical details:**
- Entry to the Giza complex: $20 USD
- Interior of the Great Pyramid: $20 USD extra (limited tickets, buy online)
- Best time: 8:00 AM opening or 3:00–5:00 PM for golden-hour light and fewer tour groups
- Camel rides and horse carriages: available, negotiate price firmly before mounting (agree on EGP before, or you'll be overcharged substantially)
- Solar Boat Museum (adjacent to the Great Pyramid): $10, displays a 4,600-year-old cedar funeral boat found intact

**The scam to know:** As you enter the complex, men will approach offering to "take you to the back entrance" or "show you a secret angle for photos." These are not official guides — they'll either charge exorbitant amounts later or take you to their friend's shop. Buy official entry, follow the official paths, hire official guides through the ticket office.

The sound and light show (evenings, €15) is atmospheric but not essential if you're visiting during the day.

### Egyptian Museum (Cairo)

The world's largest collection of ancient Egyptian artefacts, housed in a century-old building in central Cairo. Over 120,000 objects, including the treasures of Tutankhamun (the golden burial mask, the throne, the royal mummies) and artefacts from every major pharaonic era.

**Entry:** $15 USD (museum) + $5 USD (Royal Mummies Hall) + $15 USD (Tutankhamun Treasures special exhibition).

**Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM):** The long-anticipated replacement for the old Egyptian Museum, located near Giza, has been opening in phases since 2023. By 2026, most major Tutankhamun artefacts have transferred here. The GEM is the more modern and better-displayed venue — check current status before your visit to see which exhibits are where.

### Khan el-Khalili Bazaar

Cairo's medieval bazaar, dating from 1382. 16 km of covered streets with gold and silver shops, spice stalls, perfume vendors, textile merchants, and coffee houses. The Al-Fishawi Café (open continuously since 1773) is the classic place for mint tea and people-watching.

**The rule:** absolutely everything in Khan el-Khalili is priced for negotiation. Never pay the first price. Start at 30–40% of the asking price and settle at 50–60%. Spices and essential oils are fair-priced by weight; gold and silver are mostly priced by weight too (more transparent).

**When to visit:** late afternoon (16:00–20:00) when the light is softer and the market is at its most atmospheric. Friday mornings are quiet (day of rest).

### Cairo Day Trip from Hurghada — Logistics

If you're based in Hurghada and want to visit Cairo, you have two realistic options:

**By coach (cheaper, exhausting):** 5–6 hours each way through the Eastern Desert. Tour operators offer day trips for €60–100 including guide — you get roughly 3–4 hours at the Pyramids. Barely enough for the complex itself. The double 10-hour coach journey is brutal.

**Fly-drive (recommended):** Air Cairo, EgyptAir, and charter operators offer Hurghada–Cairo routes (45–50 minutes, €60–100 each way). Combined with a car and guide in Cairo: total cost €150–250 but gives you 6–8 hours — enough for the Pyramids, Egyptian Museum or GEM, and lunch. Book through your hotel or via GetYourGuide.

**Overnight option (best experience):** Take an afternoon flight to Cairo, spend a night at a hotel near Giza (€40–80, views of the Pyramids from some rooms), visit the Pyramids at 8:00 AM opening before the crowds, spend the afternoon at the Egyptian Museum or Khan el-Khalili, and fly back to Hurghada the next morning. 2 days/1 night Cairo + 5 nights Hurghada = the ideal Egypt trip.

## Luxor — The Open-Air Museum

At 660 km south of Cairo (1-hour flight or 8-hour train), Luxor is the world's greatest open-air archaeological museum. The city sits on the site of ancient Thebes, capital of Egypt during its most powerful era.

**West Bank (Mortuary temples and tombs):**
- Valley of the Kings: the rock-cut tombs of pharaohs including Tutankhamun and Rameses II. Entry: $18 (3 tombs) + $25 for Tutankhamun's tomb (optional)
- Temple of Hatshepsut: Egypt's greatest female pharaoh, extraordinary colonnaded temple
- Colossi of Memnon: two 18-metre statues rising from the fields, free to view

**East Bank (Temples):**
- Karnak Temple Complex: the largest religious building ever constructed. The Hypostyle Hall — 134 massive columns, each painted with hieroglyphics — is overwhelming. Entry: $15
- Luxor Temple: illuminated at night, extraordinary. Entry: $12

**Practical:** most visitors combine Luxor with Aswan (1-hour train) and optionally Abu Simbel (1-hour flight). The 3-day Luxor + Aswan circuit is the classic cultural Egypt experience. Alternatively, a Nile cruise (4–7 nights, Luxor to Aswan or return) is the most romantic way to experience the temples along the river.

## Snorkelling & Diving — The Red Sea's Main Draw

The Red Sea has some of the most biodiverse coral reefs on Earth, with visibility frequently exceeding 20–30 metres. For non-divers, snorkelling directly from the beach or via glass-bottom boat reveals an extraordinary underwater world.

**Best snorkelling sites from Hurghada:**
- Giftun Island National Park (30-min boat, protected reef, clownfish, turtles): €20–35 day trip
- Abu Nuhas (4 shipwrecks, for experienced snorkellers/divers): €45–80 day trip
- Dolphin House (spinner dolphins present most mornings): €25–45

**Best diving sites from Sharm el-Sheikh:**
- Ras Mohammed National Park: wall diving with hammerheads, barracuda, and hard corals
- SS Thistlegorm (WWII British cargo ship, intact with tanks and motorcycles visible): €80–120 day trip
- Blue Hole (Dahab): iconic wall drop, 30-metre visibility — for experienced divers only

**PADI Open Water course:** Egypt is one of the cheapest places in the world to learn to dive. A full PADI Open Water certification (5 days): €250–350 in Hurghada, including equipment. The same course in the UK or Germany costs €500–800.

## Practical Tips

**Currency:** Egyptian Pound (EGP). $1 ≈ 50 EGP (check current rate — Egyptian pound has fluctuated significantly). Most large hotels, restaurants, and tourist sites accept USD and EUR directly. Carry some EGP for small purchases, street food, and tips. ATMs at airports and hotels dispense EGP.

**Tipping:** Baksheesh (tips) are integral to the Egyptian hospitality economy. Budget €1–3 per service interaction: restaurant (10%), hotel porter (€1–2 per bag), tour guide (€5–10 per day per person), driver (€3–5 per day). Not tipping is noticed and affects service.

**Heat management:** In summer (June–August), temperatures in Hurghada reach 38–42°C. Drink minimum 3 litres of water daily. Avoid direct sun 12:00–15:00. Start outdoor sightseeing at dawn.

**Dress code:** Egypt is a conservative Muslim country. Cover shoulders and knees when outside resort areas — in markets, mosques, and non-tourist streets. Most Egyptians in tourist areas are accustomed to Western dress at beaches and pools, but covering up when leaving the resort shows cultural respect.

**Friday:** Egypt's day of rest. Some businesses and sites have different hours on Fridays. The main prayer time (around 12:00) means crowds gather at mosques throughout the country.

**Water:** Drink only bottled water. Hotel tap water is not potable for tourists without acclimatisation. Most all-inclusive packages include unlimited bottled water.
