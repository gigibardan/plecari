Krakow is the city that makes the impossible seem natural: in the same day you can eat pierogi for 3 EUR on a medieval terrace, visit a 14th-century royal castle and walk the walls of a city that was spared bombardment in both World Wars — preserved, through a miracle of history, from the destruction that erased most European centres.

This intactness is what makes Krakow one of the most extraordinary destinations in Central Europe. <cite index="142-1">It is a medieval metropolis with a UNESCO Old Town, Wawel Royal Castle, the rich Jewish heritage of Kazimierz and the most important proximity to Auschwitz-Birkenau in the world.</cite>

It is at the same time — and this is not a minor detail — one of the most affordable cultural capitals in Europe. <cite index="148-1">The budget for a mid-range traveller in Krakow is 50–70 EUR/day — compared to Munich or Frankfurt, where the same standard costs at least twice as much.</cite>

## Krakow at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Poland — EU member since 2004, Schengen Area. **Does not use the euro.**

**Population:** approximately 800,000 — Poland's second largest city after Warsaw.

**Currency:** Polish Złoty (PLN). **1 EUR = 4.33 PLN** in 2026. Poland does not use the euro.

**Language:** Polish. English is well spoken in the tourist area and by younger generations.

**UNESCO:** Krakow Old Town — World Heritage Site since 1978 (Poland's first UNESCO inscription).

**Direct flights from Bucharest:** Wizz Air and Ryanair, OTP→KRK, ~2h, from 35 EUR.

**Average budget:** <cite index="148-1">50–70 EUR/day (mid-range) — among the most affordable destinations in the EU.</cite>

## The Polish Złoty — Quick Guide

Poland does not use the euro. You'll pay in **PLN (Polish Złoty)**.

**2026 exchange rate:** 1 EUR ≈ 4.33 PLN.

**Quick calculation:**
- 10 PLN ≈ 2.30 EUR
- 50 PLN ≈ 11.55 EUR
- 100 PLN ≈ 23 EUR

**Where to exchange:** <cite index="154-1">PKO BP, Pekao or ING ATMs (best rate). Avoid Euronet — high fees. Look for local bank ATMs.</cite>

## What to See in Krakow

### Main Market Square (Rynek Główny) — Europe's Largest Medieval Square

<cite index="142-1">Krakow's Main Market Square measures 200×200 metres — one of the world's largest medieval squares.</cite> At the centre stands the Cloth Hall (Sukiennice) — a Renaissance building from the 16th century with arcades, where craftsmen sell traditional Polish products on the ground floor and a Polish art museum operates upstairs.

**St Mary's Basilica (Kościół Mariacki)** — the Gothic cathedral from the 14th century with two unequal towers. Every hour on the hour, a trumpeter plays the Hejnał Mariacki from the taller tower — the melody stops abruptly mid-phrase, according to legend because in the 13th century a trumpeter was killed by an arrow while sounding the Tatar invasion alarm. Entry: ~4 EUR.

**Cloth Hall (Sukiennice)** — the Renaissance arcade with amber shops, crafts and souvenirs. Upper floor: National Gallery of Polish Art with 19th–20th century paintings. Gallery entry: ~5 EUR.

**Town Hall Tower** — the only surviving element of the former town hall, offering a panoramic view. Entry: ~3 EUR.

### Wawel Castle — Poland's Symbol

<cite index="140-1">Wawel Castle, perched atop Wawel Hill above the Vistula, was the residence of Polish monarchs for centuries.</cite> It is Poland's most important cultural monument and a UNESCO site since 1978.

**Structure:** the exterior courtyard is free and always open. Separate museums with individual tickets (each 5–15 EUR):
- Royal Apartments — state rooms with original furnishings and tapestries
- Crown Treasury — with royal regalia and Szczerbiec (the coronation sword)
- Armoury — impressive collection of medieval weapons
- Wawel Cathedral — separate entry, crypts with Polish kings' tombs

<cite index="144-1">In 2026, online ticket booking is the recommended standard — walk-up tickets involve significant queues in season.</cite>

**Royal Route** — the walking route from Main Market Square to Wawel Castle (15 minutes): Medieval Barbican → St Florian's Gate → Floriańska Street → Main Square → Grodzka Street → Castle. The route followed by Polish kings at their coronation.

### Kazimierz — The Authentic Jewish Quarter

Kazimierz was for centuries the Jewish town separate from medieval Krakow — a cultural, religious and commercial centre with a Jewish population of 60,000–70,000 before the Holocaust. Today it is Krakow's most vibrant and authentic neighbourhood.

**Synagogues:** seven synagogues function or serve as museums in Kazimierz. Most important: **Old Synagogue (Stara Bożnica)** — Poland's oldest synagogue (15th century), now a museum of Jewish history and culture. Entry: ~10 PLN (2.30 EUR).

**Plac Nowy** — Kazimierz's lively central square, with street food stalls, bars and an atmosphere completely different from the Main Square. <cite index="148-1">Zapiekanki — baguette pizza, Kazimierz's speciality — 2–4 EUR from the circular kiosks in the centre.</cite>

**Film location:** Kazimierz was the main filming location for "Schindler's List" (Spielberg, 1993) and "The Pianist" (Polański, 2002). Guided film tour available.

### Schindler's Factory (Muzeum Fabryki Schindlera)

<cite index="142-1">Schindler's Factory is now a museum of the Nazi occupation of Krakow 1939–1945.</cite> It is not a conventional museum — it is a complex sensory experience that reconstructs daily life under occupation, with personal testimonies, reconstructions and original documents. Entry: ~25 PLN (5.75 EUR). Advance booking recommended.

### Podziemia Rynku — The Underground Medieval City

Below the Main Market Square lies a labyrinth of excavated medieval galleries — foundations of old buildings and commercial structures from the 10th–14th centuries, transformed into an interactive museum. <cite index="154-1">Entry: ~20 PLN (4.60 EUR).</cite> One of Krakow's most original experiences.

## Auschwitz-Birkenau — The Essential Excursion, Booked Weeks in Advance

Auschwitz-Birkenau is 70 km west of Krakow (1.5 hours by tourist coach/minibus). It is the world's largest Holocaust complex — the place where 1.1 million people, the vast majority Jews, were killed by the Nazi regime between 1940 and 1945.

**Essential practical information:**

<cite index="156-1">The guided tour is mandatory for visitors — independent entry is not permitted during high-demand periods (generally May–September between 10:00–15:00).</cite>

<cite index="144-1">Booking 1–2 months in advance is recommended in season. Tickets sell out quickly.</cite>

**Duration:** a complete visit (Auschwitz I + Birkenau) lasts 3.5–4 hours, plus return transport (3 hours total).

**Getting there:**
- **Guided tour from Krakow** (recommended): includes transport and licensed guide, 29–50 EUR
- **Independent:** direct coach from Krakow (Dworzec MDA) → Oświęcim, 2 hours, 15–18 PLN

**Prepare yourself emotionally:** Auschwitz is one of the most overwhelming experiences you can have as a tourist in Europe. It is not a normal tourist visit. Plan your day so you have no other activities afterwards.

## Wieliczka Salt Mine — The UNESCO Alternative

15 km from Krakow, Wieliczka is an active salt mine since the 13th century — a UNESCO site with 300 km of underground galleries and chambers sculpted in salt, including <cite index="149-1">chapels, underground lakes and statues.</cite>

<cite index="149-1">Guided tour ticket: ~30 EUR (140 PLN). Visit duration: 2–2.5 hours.</cite> Temperature in the mine: 14–16°C year-round — bring an extra layer regardless of season.

It is an excellent alternative to Auschwitz for the second day — lighter emotionally, but equally spectacular as an attraction.

## Food and Drink — Krakow Gastronomy Guide

### What You Must Eat

**Pierogi** — Polish dumplings filled with cheese and potato (ruskie), meat and sauerkraut, or blueberries (with sour cream, the sweet version). Poland's national dish. <cite index="154-1">A portion at a local restaurant: 3–5 EUR (14–22 PLN).</cite>

**Żurek** — sour soup made from fermented rye starter with boiled egg and smoked sausage, often served in a hollowed loaf of bread. Polish winter comfort food. <cite index="149-1">2–5 EUR (9–22 PLN).</cite>

**Zapiekanka** — toasted baguette with mushrooms, cheese and ketchup — Polish fast food born in the communist era, Kazimierz's invention. <cite index="148-1">2–4 EUR from the kiosks in Plac Nowy.</cite>

**Obwarzanek krakowski** — the sesame or poppy seed pretzel ring, Krakow's symbol, sold by street vendors with yellow carts. <cite index="148-1">0.50 EUR — the classic Krakow breakfast.</cite>

**Kotlet schabowy** — Polish pork schnitzel, generous portion with potatoes or sauerkraut. 8–14 EUR at a traditional restaurant.

### Bar Mleczny (Milk Bar) — Authentic Ultra-Budget Experience

<cite index="149-1">Bar Mleczny (Milk Bar) are subsidised restaurants inherited from the communist era, with cafeteria-style service and symbolic prices: a full meal (soup + main course) costs 5–7 EUR.</cite> Not touristy — frequented by pensioners, students and locals. The atmosphere is authentic, the food is solid. Best in Krakow: Bar Mleczny Pod Temidą on Grodzka and Milk Bar Tomasza.

### Polish Vodka and Beer

**Vodka** — Poland is vodka's European homeland. Famous brands: Żubrówka (with bison grass — fruity, unique), Wyborowa, Belvedere. A shot at a bar: 5–8 PLN (1.15–1.85 EUR) — among the cheapest in Europe.

**Polish beer** — Żywiec, Tyskie, Lech, Okocim are the classic brands. <cite index="154-1">A pint at a local bar: 18 PLN (4.15 EUR); craft beer in Kazimierz: 28 PLN (6.45 EUR).</cite>

### Where to Eat

**Ultra budget (3–7 EUR/meal):**
- Bar Mleczny (any of the 5–6 in the centre)
- Zapiekanka kiosks in Plac Nowy (Kazimierz)
- Obwarzanek street vendors

**Mid-range (10–20 EUR/person):**
- **Stolarska and Bracka streets** (one or two streets from the Main Square) — same quality, 20–30% cheaper
- **Kazimierz** — restaurants on Szeroka or Józefa, mixing Polish and Jewish-Polish cuisine

**Caution:** <cite index="149-1">restaurants directly on Rynek Główny charge 30–50% more than those one street away for the same dishes.</cite>

## Recommended Itinerary — 3 Days in Krakow

### Day 1 — Old Town and Kazimierz

- **Morning:** Rynek Główny — St Mary's Basilica (listen for the Hejnał on the hour), Cloth Hall, Town Hall Tower
- **Lunch:** pierogi at a restaurant on Stolarska or Bracka (~5 EUR)
- **Afternoon:** Kazimierz — Old Synagogue, Plac Nowy (zapiekanka), galleries and bars
- **Evening:** craft beer in Kazimierz or Polish vodka at an Old Town bar

- **Full day:** guided Auschwitz-Birkenau tour from Krakow (~29–50 EUR, 6–7 hours)
- **Evening:** rest — don't plan activities after Auschwitz

### Day 3 — Wawel Castle and Wieliczka

- **Morning (before 9:00):** Wawel Castle — free exterior courtyard, interior visits (choose 1–2 museums, booked online)
- **Lunch:** traditional Polish restaurant on Grodzka or at a Milk Bar
- **Afternoon:** Wieliczka Salt Mine (2.5 hour tour, ~30 EUR) OR Schindler's Factory (~5 EUR)
- **Evening:** farewell evening on Rynek Główny with local beer

## Practical Tips

**The Złoty** — exchange at local ATMs (PKO BP, Pekao), not at airport kiosks or on the street. Some street vendors exchange EUR/USD with counterfeit notes — refuse any informal exchange.

**Auschwitz — book 4–6 weeks ahead** in season. Tickets sell out. Book at memorial.auschwitz.org or GetYourGuide with guide included.

**Public transport** — trams and buses are cheap (<cite index="148-1">single ticket 5 PLN/1.15 EUR</cite>). Line T1/T2 connects the airport and centre.

**Free on the first Sunday** — some Krakow museums are free on the first Sunday of the month (National Museum, Schindler's Factory). Check in advance.

**Shoes** — Rynek Główny has polished limestone paving — slippery after rain. Sensible shoes, not heels.
