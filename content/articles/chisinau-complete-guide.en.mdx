There is a joke Moldovans tell about Chișinău: "We don't have tourism, we have wine." It's not entirely a joke.

Moldova is the smallest country in Europe by population (under 3 million inhabitants), has the least visited capital on the continent (under 150,000 tourists per year) and produces, per capita, more wine than any other country in the world. Beneath the earth around the capital stretches a labyrinth of underground galleries containing tens of millions of bottles, at a constant temperature of 12–14°C, producing wines of a quality that surprises anyone who held the prejudice that Moldova is somewhere between obscure and irrelevant.

<cite index="270-1">Chișinău is Europe's most underrated capital — a walkable city where Orthodox cathedrals sit alongside Soviet monuments, world-class wine cellars lie just outside town, and green parks cover nearly a third of the urban area.</cite>

Add to this a unique advantage for travellers from Romania: the Bucharest–Chișinău flight takes 1 hour and 10 minutes — shorter than a drive to Brașov — and <cite index="232-1">from October 2026, Wizz Air doubles the frequency to 14 flights per week, meaning two daily flights between the two capitals.</cite>

## Chișinău at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Republic of Moldova — not an EU member (official candidate since 2022), not in Schengen.

**Population:** approximately 690,000 in the municipality — Europe's smallest capital after Vaduz and San Marino.

**Currency:** Moldovan Leu (MDL). <cite index="248-1">1 EUR ≈ 19.15 MDL in 2026.</cite> Moldova does not use the euro.

**Language:** Romanian is the official language (officially named "the Romanian language" since 2023, following a constitutional change). Russian is widely understood and spoken. Romanian speakers from Romania are fully understood without difficulty.

**Visa:** EU citizens do not need a visa — entry with a valid ID, max 90 days.

**Direct flights from Bucharest:** Wizz Air, OTP→RMO, **1h10**, <cite index="232-1">14 flights/week from October 2026.</cite>

**Average budget:** <cite index="264-1">20–45 EUR/day (mid-range) — Europe's most affordable capital.</cite>

## How to Get from Romania to Chișinău

### Direct Flight OTP → RMO (Fastest — 1h10)

<cite index="232-1">Wizz Air operates direct flights Bucharest Otopeni (OTP) → Chișinău (RMO) 7 times per week currently, increasing to 14 frequencies per week (2 per day) from 25 October 2026.</cite>

**2026 prices:** 15–60 EUR return — among the cheapest routes in Wizz Air's portfolio.

**Airport → Chișinău city centre transfer:**
- **Bus 30** → Central Square: 30 minutes, 6 MDL (0.30 EUR) — ultra-cheap
- **Yandex Go / InDrive**: 80–120 MDL (4–6 EUR), 20 minutes — recommended
- **Unlicensed taxis at the airport**: avoid — can charge 10–15x the normal rate

### By Car (Recommended for Flexibility + Wine Tours)

Bucharest → Chișinău: ~460 km, 5–6 hours. Route via **Albița** (main border crossing) or **Sculeni** (near Iași, faster from northern Romania).

**Important:** Moldova is not in the EU. You need valid Green Card insurance for Moldova. Verify your car insurance explicitly covers Moldova.

### By Coach

FlixBus and local operators run Bucharest → Chișinău services. Duration: ~5–6 hours. Prices: 15–30 EUR. A good budget option.

## The Moldovan Leu — Quick Guide

Moldova doesn't use the euro. You'll pay in **MDL (Moldovan lei)**.

**2026 rate:** 1 EUR ≈ 19.15 MDL.

**Quick calculation:**
- 100 MDL ≈ 5.20 EUR
- 500 MDL ≈ 26 EUR
- 1,000 MDL ≈ 52 EUR

**Where to exchange:** exchange offices with "0% commission" signs in central Chișinău (best rates). Check the current rate on **curs.md**. ENERGBANK or Moldova Agroindbank ATMs — low fees.

**Avoid:** airport exchange (5–10% worse rate), Euronet ATMs (high fees), informal street exchange (fraud risk).

## What to See in Chișinău

### Stefan cel Mare Boulevard — The City's Spine

Stefan cel Mare și Sfânt Boulevard is Chișinău's main artery — a wide promenade lined with linden trees that flower in May–June, flanked by eclectic buildings, café terraces and the central park.

**Stefan cel Mare Park** — Chișinău's central park, with the Alley of Classic Writers, fountains and promenade areas. Free. <cite index="249-1">The monument to Ștefan cel Mare, Moldova's national hero, stands at the park entrance.</cite>

**Triumphal Arch** — <cite index="270-1">built in 1840 to commemorate Russia's victory in the Russo-Turkish War of 1828–1829.</cite> The symbolic centre of the city, in the Great National Assembly Square. Free.

**Nativity Cathedral** — <cite index="270-1">a neoclassical construction from 1830, with an impressive design.</cite> Free entry. <cite index="270-1">The Sunday morning service (from 9:00) is the most atmospheric.</cite>

**Cathedral Park** — the park surrounding the cathedral, with old acacia trees, benches and the slow, characteristically Moldovan atmosphere. Locals come to read, play chess or sell hand-knitted goods.

### Central Market — The Commercial Heart

<cite index="270-1">The Central Market is a completely different world from the main boulevard — a vibrant bazaar with practically anything available.</cite> Vegetables, fruit, cheeses, smoked sausages, honey, pickles, clothes, tools. Prices are 30–50% lower than in supermarkets.

Eat a **Moldovan pastry** (plăcintă with cheese, potato or cabbage) at one of the kiosks around the market: 0.50–1 EUR each.

### National Museum of History of Moldova

<cite index="250-1">The ticket costs 25 lei (~1.30 EUR) and gives access to a collection of 265,000 exhibits.</cite> One of the main attractions: a replica of Ștefan cel Mare's sword. For the price of an espresso in Bucharest, you journey through Moldovan history from antiquity to today.

### Soviet Architecture — An Open-Air Museum

Chișinău was almost completely destroyed in World War II and Soviet postwar reconstruction created a special architectural landscape — a mix of Stalinist neoclassical buildings, Khrushchev-era blocks and grand monuments.

**Government House** — the imposing Soviet building on the main boulevard. Photographable from outside.

**Victory Monument and Eternal Flame** — memorial to soldiers fallen in World War II, with the eternal flame burning. Free.

### Orheiul Vechi — The Essential Cultural Excursion

60 km north of Chișinău, the Orheiul Vechi cave complex is Moldova's most important archaeological and spiritual site — a monastic cliff village carved into the limestone rock above the Răut River, with 2,000 years of history.

<cite index="279-1">The complex includes a cliff monastery, Dacian tombs, Roman and Byzantine traces and an unaltered rural landscape.</cite>

**Getting there:** taxi (~35 EUR return with driver waiting) or organised tour from Chișinău (~25–40 EUR per person with guide). Site entry: free or voluntary donation.

## Tour to Cricova and Mileștii Mici — The Two Great Underground Cellars

### Cricova — The Underground City of Wine

<cite index="241-1">Cricova is the world's second largest underground wine cellar, with 120 km of galleries that have transformed it into a real underground city with streets, traffic lights and road signs.</cite>

Founded in 1952, Cricova used the galleries of former limestone mines (the same limestone from which Chișinău was built — the "white city") to create perfect maturation conditions: constant temperature 12°C, humidity 85–90%.

<cite index="241-1">Tours are conducted by electric vehicle through the galleries.</cite> Among the famous visitors who have stored private collections at Cricova: Vladimir Putin, Angela Merkel, John Kerry.

**Practical details:**
- **Standard tour** (without tasting): <cite index="241-1">from 20 EUR</cite>
- **Tour with tasting** (4 wines + 1 sparkling wine, snacks): <cite index="241-1">from 40 EUR</cite>
- **Distance from Chișinău:** 14 km north, ~20 minutes by car
- **Advance booking required** — at cricova.md or through travel agencies
- **Tour duration:** 2–3 hours

### Mileștii Mici — The World's Largest Wine Cellar

<cite index="256-1">Mileștii Mici holds the Guinness World Record for the world's largest wine collection, with 2 million bottles stored in 200 km of underground galleries.</cite>

If Cricova feels like an elaborate underground museum, Mileștii Mici impresses through raw scale. The galleries descend to 85 metres below ground, the temperature is constant, and wines aged 30–40 years sell at prices you won't find anywhere in Western Europe.

**Practical details:**
- **Tour with tasting** (3 wines + 1 dessert wine + snacks): similar prices to Cricova, ~35–50 EUR
- **Distance from Chișinău:** 18 km south-west, ~25 minutes by car
- **Shopping at the cellar shop:** wines from 3–8 EUR per bottle — prices that leave you speechless
- **Booking required** — at milestii-mici.md

### Combined Tour Cricova + Mileștii Mici (Recommended)

<cite index="249-1">The combined tour with private transport from Chișinău to both cellars, with tasting at each, lasts 6 hours and costs 50–80 EUR per person</cite> — the complete Moldovan wine experience in a single day.

## Moldovan Food and Drink

### What You Must Eat

**Mămăligă with cheese and sour cream** — the unofficial national dish — polenta with sheep's cheese (bryndza) and fresh sour cream. At a local restaurant: 2–5 EUR. A simple, dense and absolutely delicious dish.

**Moldovan pastries (plăcinte)** — <cite index="281-1">thin dough filled with cheese, potato, cabbage or meat, fried or baked — classic Moldovan street food at 0.50–1 EUR each.</cite>

**Stuffed vine leaves (sarmale)** — wrapped in pickled vine leaves, a Moldovan speciality distinct from Romanian versions. 5–9 EUR a portion at a restaurant.

**Zeamă** — acidulated Moldovan chicken soup with homemade noodles — the soup that puts you back on your feet after anything. 2–4 EUR.

**Grapes and seasonal fruit** — Moldova produces extraordinary fruit: cherries, plums, peaches, apples, grapes. At the market in season: 0.30–0.80 EUR per kg.

### Moldovan Wine — What to Try

**Feteasca Neagră** — Moldova's native red grape variety, robust, with notes of dark fruit and well-integrated tannin. The signature wine of Moldovan cellars.

**Feteasca Albă** — the local white variety, fresh, lightly floral, perfect with fish and cheeses.

**Rară Neagră** — a rare native variety with a distinctive personality, produced by few wineries.

**Table red wine** — at local restaurants and family wineries: <cite index="270-1">0.80–2 EUR a glass, 3–8 EUR a bottle directly from the cellar.</cite>

**Divin** — Moldovan cognac, produced in Moldova since 1893, exported worldwide. A 5-star bottle at a supermarket: 8–15 EUR.

### Where to Eat in Chișinău

**Budget (2–6 EUR/meal):**
- Cafés and canteens in the centre
- Pastry kiosks near the Central Market
- <cite index="275-1">Student restaurants near the State University</cite>

**Mid-range (6–15 EUR/person):**
- **La Taifas** — traditional Moldovan restaurant with live music, renowned in Chișinău
- **Vatra Neamului** — authentic Moldovan food, rustic interior
- **Andy's Pizza** — local pizza-restaurant chain, popular, fair prices

## Practical Tips for Chișinău

**Yandex Go and InDrive** are the recommended taxi apps in Chișinău — <cite index="247-1">fair prices, transparently displayed, much cheaper than street taxis.</cite> A cross-city ride: 3–6 EUR.

**Language** — Romanian speakers are completely understood in Chișinău, and locals are delighted to hear Romanian. Russian is also useful with older generations.

**Curs.md** — check the daily exchange rate before changing money. Central exchange offices (not the airport) offer the best rates.

**Car insurance** — if driving your own car, verify your Green Card insurance explicitly covers Moldova. Not all policies automatically include Moldova.

**Moldova — EU candidate state** — Moldova obtained EU candidate status in 2022. The European integration process is underway — infrastructure and services are visibly improving.

## Travel Insurance — Moldova Is Not in the EU

**Important:** Moldova is not part of the EU. The European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) **is not valid** in Moldova. You need travel insurance with cover for non-EU countries. Check when booking **EKTA Travel Insurance** that Moldova is explicitly included.
