Bucharest Henri Coandă Airport is one of the most underrated departure points in Europe for budget travel. Wizz Air alone operates 60+ direct routes; Ryanair has expanded aggressively since 2023. The result: an extraordinary number of European city breaks within reach for under €100 return, and some for as little as €30.

This guide covers the 10 best-value destinations reachable directly from Bucharest in 2026 — with real prices, daily budgets, the essential things to see, and exactly how to book the cheapest fare.

## How to Book the Cheapest Flight from Bucharest — The System

Before the destinations, the method. These cheap prices exist, but they're not always visible on the first search.

**Book 6–8 weeks in advance:** This is the sweet spot. Under 4 weeks, prices rise consistently. Over 3 months, prices are standard — no early booking discount. The promotional fares appear most reliably in the 6–8 week window.

**Search on Tuesday or Wednesday:** Airlines typically release and update promotional inventory Monday evening. Tuesday morning searches show the most newly available cheap fares. Friday and Saturday are the most expensive days to search and to fly.

**Fly mid-week:** The same route can vary by €30–70 between a Friday/Sunday departure (premium pricing for weekend travellers) and a Tuesday/Wednesday departure. If your schedule allows any flexibility, mid-week saves significantly.

**Use a flight comparison engine:** Wizz Air and Ryanair don't show each other's fares. A comparison tool shows all airlines simultaneously — sometimes a Turkish Airlines connection via Istanbul is cheaper than a direct Wizz Air fare for the same dates. The search widget above shows real-time prices and routes from Bucharest.

**Be flexible on dates ±3 days:** The calendar view on comparison engines often shows dramatically different prices for the same week. Moving departure from Friday to Wednesday can save €40–60 on the same route.

## 1. Budapest — From €30 Return (1h 20min)

Budapest is the closest thing to a guaranteed cheap flight from Bucharest. Wizz Air operates multiple daily flights; the route is competitive and frequently on promotion. At 1h20, it's shorter than many domestic Romanian train journeys.

**Why it's worth it:** Budapest has extraordinary density of experiences per euro — thermal baths, a spectacular Parliament building, the best ruin bar scene in Europe, Michelin-starred restaurants at half-Western-European prices, and the Danube. It also has some of the cheapest food and accommodation of any Central European capital.

**Daily budget:** €40–60 (meal €8–14, coffee €2–3, accommodation from €30/night for good hostels, €55–80 for 3-star hotels)

**Don't miss:**
- Széchenyi Thermal Baths (€22–30, book in advance in summer)
- Parliament building guided tour (€15, jaw-dropping neo-Gothic interior)
- Ruin bars in the Jewish Quarter (Szimpla Kert — free entry, extraordinary atmosphere)
- Fisherman's Bastion at dawn (free, best panoramic view of the city)
- Danube cruise with dinner (€20–40 via GetYourGuide)

**When to book flights:** Budapest flights from Bucharest are cheap year-round. Christmas markets in December are spectacular and accommodation is reasonable.

**Insider tip:** The Budapest Card (72h, €50) covers unlimited public transport plus free entry to 12 museums and discounts at baths. Worth it if you're visiting 3+ museums.

## 2. Rome — From €40 Return (2h 30min)

Rome needs no introduction, but the prices might surprise you. Wizz Air and Ryanair both fly direct from Bucharest to Rome Fiumicino (Wizz Air) and Rome Ciampino (Ryanair). Direct flight time: 2h30.

**Why it's worth it:** The density of world history per square kilometre is unmatched on Earth. The Colosseum, the Vatican, the Pantheon, the Trevi Fountain, Trastevere — all within walking distance of each other. And unlike many European capitals, Rome's street food and neighbourhood restaurants are genuinely affordable.

**Daily budget:** €50–80 (meals €10–20 at neighbourhood restaurants, coffee €1.50 at the bar standing up, accommodation from €60/night for decent central options)

**Don't miss:**
- Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine Hill (€18–30, pre-book is essential)
- Vatican Museums + Sistine Chapel (€21–40, book weeks ahead in summer)
- Pantheon (€5, in immaculate condition, free-standing for 2,000 years)
- Trastevere neighbourhood at dinner time (walk and eat where locals eat)
- Piazza Navona at dusk (free, beautiful)

**Critical for Rome:** Both the Colosseum and Vatican sell out months in advance in summer. Use [GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/rome-l33/?partner_id=EKAA80H&utm_medium=online_publisher) for skip-the-line access when official sites show sold out — they hold separate pre-purchased allocations.

## 3. Bari — From €40 Return (2h)

Bari is the underrated gem on this list. Capital of Puglia, southern Italy — direct flights from Bucharest for as low as €40 return, and a city that rewards the visitor with extraordinary food, a beautiful old town, and beaches.

**Why it's the best-value Italian destination:** Daily budgets of €35–50 are realistic. A full meal at a trattoria: €10–14. Excellent espresso: €1. Beautiful Baroque architecture, medieval old town (Bari Vecchia with its labyrinthine streets), and Basilica di San Nicola (where St Nicholas — the original Santa Claus — is buried).

**Day trips from Bari (all under 1 hour):**
- Alberobello — UNESCO-listed trulli houses (unique conical-roofed buildings), train €4.50 return
- Polignano a Mare — dramatic cliffs over a crystal-blue sea, train €3 return
- Matera — UNESCO cave city ("Sassi"), oldest continuously inhabited city in the world, 1h30 by bus

**When to go:** Bari is perfect in May–June and September–October. July–August is hot (35–40°C) but still beautiful if you're beach-focused.

**Flights:** Wizz Air and Ryanair both operate direct routes. Bari is also a cruise port — if you're combining with a Mediterranean cruise via GetYourGuide shore excursions, Bari is an excellent base.

## 4. Milan — From €45 Return (2h 40min)

Milan is Italy's fashion and business capital, which sounds expensive — but flights are competitive and the city has significant budget-friendly layers once you get past the luxury boutiques of Quadrilatero della Moda.

**Why it's worth it:** Leonardo da Vinci's Last Supper (Santa Maria delle Grazie), the extraordinary Gothic cathedral (Duomo di Milano — rooftop access €13), the Brera Gallery (Raphael, Caravaggio, Mantegna), and the Navigli canal district for aperitivo culture (€8–12 for a drink that comes with a full buffet of food — the Milanese "happy hour" tradition).

**Daily budget:** €55–80 (Milan is pricier than Rome, but the aperitivo tradition — buy one drink, access a full buffet — saves significantly on dinner)

**Don't miss:**
- Duomo di Milano + rooftop terrace (€13 rooftop, cathedral free)
- Last Supper by Leonardo da Vinci (€15, Santa Maria delle Grazie — book 2–3 weeks ahead minimum)
- Aperitivo in the Navigli district (18:00–21:00, buy a drink, eat for free)
- Pinacoteca di Brera (€15, extraordinary Renaissance collection)
- Castello Sforzesco (free entry to courtyards, €5 for museums inside)

**Day trips:** Lake Como (1h by train, €8 return), Bergamo (50 min by train, €6 return — the medieval upper city is stunning), or Verona (1h15, €11 return — Romeo and Juliet's city).

## 5. Vienna — From €45 Return (2h 10min)

Vienna is Austrian precision meeting Habsburg grandeur. Direct flights on Wizz Air and Austrian Airlines (more expensive) make it easily reachable from Bucharest in 2h10. The city can be expensive but has a strong free-and-cheap layer: the Naschmarkt food market, Schönbrunn Palace gardens (free), and the world's best coffee house culture.

**Daily budget:** €55–80 (meals €12–20, coffee culture where you can sit for hours for one €4 Melange, accommodation from €70/night)

**Don't miss:**
- Schönbrunn Palace (gardens free, palace interior €16–30 depending on tour)
- Belvedere (Upper Belvedere: Klimt's The Kiss, €17)
- Naschmarkt (free to browse, lunch from €8)
- Vienna State Opera house tour or standing-room tickets (€10–13, available 80 minutes before performance — world-class opera for the price of a coffee)
- Kunsthistorisches Museum (€16, extraordinary art collection)
- Prater and the Riesenrad Ferris wheel (€13, vintage 1897 Ferris wheel with panoramic views)

**The coffee house tradition:** Vienna's historic coffee houses (Café Central, Café Landtmann, Café Hawelka) are living institutions where you can sit for 2–3 hours nursing a single coffee and reading newspapers. Nobody will rush you. Budget €4–6 for the experience.

## 6. Athens — From €50 Return (2h 20min)

Athens is one of the most underrated capitals in Europe for value. Direct flights from Bucharest on Wizz Air and Aegean Airlines; the city has low daily costs, extraordinary ancient history, and the best seafood you'll eat anywhere in the Mediterranean.

**Daily budget:** €45–65 (meal at a taverna €10–15, excellent gyros €3–4, accommodation from €50/night for good central options)

**Don't miss:**
- Acropolis (€20, Athens Pass available for multiple sites) — go at opening (8:00 AM) to beat heat and crowds
- Acropolis Museum (€10, extraordinary, modern, right below the hill)
- Monastiraki flea market (free, Saturday is best)
- Plaka neighbourhood (walking, free)
- Seafood dinner in Piraeus or Mikrolimano harbour (€15–25/person, fresh catch)
- Cape Sounion day trip (€14 for the temple of Poseidon on a cliff over the Aegean)

**Island hopping from Athens:** Athens is the gateway to the Greek islands via Piraeus ferry port. Aegina (1h ferry, €9), Hydra (2h, €30), or Poros (2h, €25) all make extraordinary day trips. For longer island stays, see our [Greek Islands guide](/en/cazari/best-greek-islands-2026).

## 7. London — From €50 Return (3h 30min)

London requires a slightly longer caveat: it's the most expensive city on this list for daily costs, and the UK requires a travel insurance policy and an eSIM (EU roaming doesn't apply post-Brexit). But flights from Bucharest are genuinely cheap (Wizz Air to Luton, Ryanair to Stansted), and London's density of world-class free museums is unmatched globally.

**Daily budget:** £60–100 (meals £8–25 depending on where you eat, transport £8.90/day contactless cap, free museums = £0 for most major attractions)

**Don't miss (all free):**
- British Museum (Egyptian mummies, Rosetta Stone, Elgin Marbles)
- Natural History Museum (blue whale, dinosaur hall)
- Victoria & Albert Museum (fashion, design, decorative arts)
- Tate Modern (world-class contemporary art, South Bank)
- National Gallery (Vermeer, van Gogh, Turner)

**Essential London logistics:** Use contactless bank card or phone for all Tube and bus travel (daily cap £8.90 zones 1–2). UK ETA required (£10, apply online). Travel insurance with UK cover essential (EHIC not valid post-Brexit). eSIM from [Airalo](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3) or [Saily](https://saily.tpo.mx/HfN66bwk) saves €50–100 vs carrier roaming.

## 8. Istanbul — From €55 Return (2h)

Istanbul is the closest major city to Romania geographically — just 2 hours by direct flight — and offers extraordinary value combined with some of the most visually dramatic sights in the world. The Blue Mosque, Hagia Sophia, the Bosphorus, the Grand Bazaar — all concentrated within walking distance of each other.

**Daily budget:** €35–55 (Istanbul is excellent value — full restaurant meal €6–12, accommodation from €30/night for decent hotels in Sultanahmet, Turkish tea everywhere for €0.50)

**Don't miss:**
- Hagia Sophia (now a mosque, free entry, remove shoes, cover hair — extraordinary architecture)
- Blue Mosque (free, active mosque, requires appropriate dress)
- Topkapi Palace (€20, 600 years of Ottoman history, extraordinary treasury)
- Grand Bazaar (free to browse, 60 covered streets, 4,000 shops)
- Bosphorus cruise (€10–15 for public ferry TK Tours, or evening dinner cruise via GetYourGuide)
- Galata Tower (€10, views over both the European and Asian sides)

**Essential Istanbul logistics:** Travel insurance is essential — Turkey is non-EU, EHIC doesn't apply, and private hospital costs are high. eSIM is critical — buy before arriving in Turkey, as eSIM provider websites are blocked from within the country since 2025.

## 9. Barcelona — From €55 Return (3h 10min)

Barcelona combines beach access, extraordinary architecture, world-class food, and a vibrant urban culture. Direct flights on Wizz Air and Ryanair. The city is more expensive than Eastern European capitals but comparable to Rome.

**Daily budget:** €55–80 (meals €12–20 at neighbourhood spots, coffee €2–3, accommodation from €65/night)

**Don't miss:**
- Sagrada Família (€26–36, Gaudí's unfinished masterpiece — book weeks ahead)
- Park Güell (€10, Gaudí park with views; free areas in surrounding park)
- Gothic Quarter (free, walking)
- La Boqueria market (free to browse, lunch from €8)
- Barceloneta Beach (free)
- Montjuïc cable car + castle (€15 return cable car, €5 castle)

**Gaudí package:** Barcelona's major Gaudí sites (Sagrada Família, Casa Batlló, Casa Milà/La Pedrera, Park Güell) each sell out independently. Book all of them through [GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/barcelona-l45/?partner_id=EKAA80H&utm_medium=online_publisher) as skip-the-line when official sites show limited availability.

## 10. Paris — From €60 Return (3h 10min)

Paris is the most expensive destination on this list but consistently one of the most searched routes from Bucharest. Wizz Air flies to Beauvais (90 km from Paris — note the bus transfer adds £17 and 75 minutes); other options to Orly or CDG via code-shares are pricier but more central.

**Daily budget:** €60–95 (meals €12–20 at neighbourhood bistros, transport €16.90/day Navigo, accommodation from €80/night for decent central hotels)

**Don't miss:**
- Eiffel Tower (€26–35 to summit, or free to stand underneath)
- Louvre (€22 — first Sunday of the month is FREE, but expect 2-hour queues)
- Notre-Dame Cathedral (undergoing final phases of renovation from 2019 fire — exterior viewing is possible)
- Montmartre and Sacré-Cœur (free, extraordinary neighbourhood)
- Musée d'Orsay (€16, Impressionists — Monet, Renoir, Degas, Van Gogh)
- Galleries Lafayette rooftop (free, panoramic views)

**Paris budget tip:** The first Sunday of each month, the Louvre is free (€0 instead of €22). But queues form from early morning and can reach 2 hours. Alternatively, book a skip-the-line Louvre ticket through GetYourGuide to combine morning entry with a guide.
