There are places that take your breath away before you have time to understand why. Ohrid is one of them.

<cite index="46-1">Ohrid is North Macedonia's most-visited destination — a UNESCO-listed lakeside town on the northeastern shore of Lake Ohrid, one of Europe's oldest and deepest lakes, 34 km long, 14 km wide and over 300 metres deep.</cite>

Nicknamed the "Balkan Jerusalem" because <cite index="51-1">Ohrid once had 365 Orthodox churches — one for each day of the year</cite>, the town layers 3,000 years of civilisation on the shore of a lake 3–5 million years old. The Greeks built the theatre in the 2nd century BC. The Romans left their marks. Byzantium covered the rocks with frescoes. Stefan Dušan crowned kings in the medieval fortress. And through all this time, the lake remained unchanged — with the same 20-metre transparency and the same endemic species that exist nowhere else on Earth.

<cite index="48-1">Ohrid holds one of the rarest distinctions in European tourism: it is a double UNESCO World Heritage Site, listed for both its cultural monuments and its natural environment.</cite>

## Ohrid at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** North Macedonia — not in the EU, not in Schengen.

**Population:** approximately 55,000.

**Currency:** Macedonian Denar (MKD). 1 EUR ≈ 61.5 MKD.

**UNESCO:** Double — cultural and natural heritage, since 1979/1980.

**Lake Ohrid:** <cite index="46-1">one of the world's oldest lakes (3–5 million years), maximum depth 288 m, water visibility 20+ metres.</cite>

**Flights:** Ohrid has a small seasonal airport (OHD), but more accessible via Skopje (SKP, 3 hours by bus) or Tirana (TIA, 2.5 hours by bus).

**Average budget:** 30–55 EUR/day — similar to Skopje.

## How to Get to Ohrid from Romania

### Option 1 — Via Skopje (Recommended)

**Flight OTP → SKP** (Wizz Air, ~1h45, 30–80 EUR) + **bus Skopje → Ohrid** (3 hours, 400–600 MKD/6.50–10 EUR). Multiple daily buses.

### Option 2 — Via Tirana (Shorter Distance)

**Flight OTP → TIA** (Wizz Air, ~1h45, 35–90 EUR) + **bus Tirana → Ohrid** (2.5 hours, ~10–15 EUR). Tirana is geographically closer — if the flight price is similar, this is the better option.

## Lake Ohrid — Europe's Oldest Lake

<cite index="49-1">Lake Ohrid, known in antiquity as Lihnid, is one of Europe's oldest and deepest lakes — a natural and cultural treasure shared by Albania and North Macedonia.</cite>

**Why it is unique:**
- Estimated age: 3–5 million years (one of the few ancient lakes in the world, alongside Baikal and Tanganyika)
- Maximum depth: 288 metres
- Water visibility: up to 22 metres — the clearest water in the Balkans
- Endemic species: <cite index="48-1">Ohrid trout (Salmo letnica) is a legally protected endemic species that exists nowhere else on the planet</cite>
- Water temperature: 22–24°C in July–August, 18–20°C in September

## What to See in Ohrid

### The Old Town — A Layering of Civilisations

<cite index="46-1">Ohrid Old Town works perfectly for walking — the vast majority of attractions are within walking distance.</cite>

**Ancient Theatre of Ohrid** — <cite index="51-1">a Greek-Roman theatre dating from the 2nd century BC, only discovered in 1980 during construction works. Capacity: 4,200 seats. Today it hosts performances during the Ohrid Summer Festival.</cite> Entry: ~100–150 MKD (~1.65–2.45 EUR).

**Tsar Samuel's Fortress (Samuil's Fortress)** — <cite index="46-1">the medieval fortress dominating Ohrid from the hilltop, with restored walls and a complete panorama of the lake and town.</cite> Built in the 10th century, extended later. Entry: ~100 MKD (~1.65 EUR).

**Old Bazaar** — the Ottoman market with craftsmen, jewellery shops, cafés and traditional restaurants. Free.

### Church of St John at Kaneo — Ohrid's Defining Image

<cite index="51-1">The Church of St John at Kaneo is a 13th-century Orthodox church dramatically built on a cliff above Lake Ohrid — one of the most photographed images in North Macedonia and throughout the Balkan region.</cite>

<cite index="46-1">Entry: ~100 MKD (~1.65 EUR).</cite> Accessible on foot from the Old Bazaar (10–15 minutes) or by boat from the lake (the view from the water is even more spectacular).

### Church of St Sophia — The Best-Preserved Medieval Frescoes

<cite index="46-1">St Sophia in Ohrid is one of North Macedonia's most important Byzantine churches, with exceptional 11th-century medieval frescoes.</cite> Entry: ~150 MKD (~2.45 EUR).

### Bay of Bones (Zaliv na Koskie)

<cite index="46-1">The Bay of Bones is a reconstruction of a Palaeolithic lake dwelling — wooden houses on platforms above the water, reconstructing life 3,500 years ago.</cite> Based on artefacts found on the lake bottom.

Ticket: ~150 MKD (~2.45 EUR). Accessible by boat or on foot from Ohrid Old Town (20 minutes).

## Boat Tour on Lake Ohrid — Bay of Bones and St Naum Monastery

### St Naum Monastery — The Pearl at the Albanian Border

<cite index="46-1">St Naum Monastery is 29 km south of Ohrid, on the Albanian border, on the lake shore in the middle of wild nature.</cite> Founded in the 9th century by Saint Naum — the disciple of Cyril and Methodius, creators of the Cyrillic alphabet.

Near the monastery: crystal springs feeding the lake, with enormous carp visible through the transparent water — a remarkable natural spectacle.

**Getting there:**
- **By boat** from Ohrid (2 hours on the lake, 200–400 MKD return) — the most spectacular experience
- **By shuttle bus** (45 minutes, ~200 MKD)

Monastery entry: free.

### Ohrid Summer Festival (July–August)

<cite index="51-1">The Ohrid Summer Festival hosts classical music, theatre and dance performances in the Ancient Theatre and other Old Town venues.</cite> Tickets: 200–1,000 MKD (~3.25–16 EUR) depending on the performance. One of the Balkans' most beautiful cultural festivals — classical music under the open sky with the lake as backdrop.

## Food and Drink in Ohrid

### Ohrid Trout — The Most Important Culinary Dish

<cite index="48-1">Ohrid trout (pastrmka) is a legally protected endemic species available in limited quantities on restaurant menus. Grilled or baked, it costs 700–1,200 MKD (~11.40–19.50 EUR) depending on size and location. If it is genuinely Ohrid trout (not farmed rainbow trout), it has a flavour and texture that justifies the price.</cite>

**Warning:** many restaurants serve rainbow trout (farmed) at the price of endemic trout. Ask explicitly: "Дали е охридска пастрмка?" (Is it Ohrid trout?).

### Tavče Gravče

<cite index="48-1">Beans slow-baked in a clay pot with onions, peppers and herbs — Macedonia's national dish. 300–500 MKD (5–8 EUR). Available everywhere, consistent quality, genuinely good.</cite>

## Practical Tips

**Old Town accommodation** — book in advance in July–August (peak season). <cite index="46-1">Private apartments in Old Town: ~40–100 EUR per night in 2026.</cite> Advantage: you're 5 minutes from all attractions.

**Summer heat** — Ohrid is hot in July–August (32–36°C). Early morning is best for visiting attractions; swimming and relaxation in the afternoon.

**Winter** — <cite index="48-1">Ohrid is a warm-season destination and largely closes outside summer.</cite> Don't plan a visit outside season without verifying hotels and restaurants are open.

**Lakeside fish** — carp and other species in Lake Ohrid are extremely close to the shore and visible with the naked eye through the transparent water. Don't feed the fish — it's prohibited.
