If Krakow is medieval Poland and Warsaw is Poland that rose from the ashes, Gdańsk is the Poland that changed the world.

This is not an exaggeration. In August 1980, workers at the Lenin Shipyard in Gdańsk launched a strike that gave birth to the Solidarity trade union — the first independent organisation from the Communist Party in the Soviet bloc. Ten years later, Lech Wałęsa, the leader of the Gdańsk strike, became Poland's first democratically elected president, and communism in Eastern Europe fell like a line of dominoes.

But Gdańsk is not only a place of contemporary history. <cite index="185-1">With its tall, colourful gabled houses, lazy river and cobbled streets, the Old Town looks more akin to the Netherlands than the rest of Poland.</cite> It is a Hanseatic port faithfully reconstructed after World War II destruction, with a northern elegance you won't find anywhere else in Poland.

Add the world's largest castle 45 minutes away by train, the finest sand beach at Sopot 15 minutes away and the best excuse in Europe to buy authentic amber — and you have one of the most complete destinations in Central Europe.

## Gdańsk at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Poland — EU member, Schengen Area. Does not use the euro.

**Population:** approximately 490,000 — Poland's third largest city.

**Currency:** Polish Złoty (PLN). 1 EUR ≈ 4.33 PLN.

**Language:** Polish. English is well spoken in the tourist area.

**Location:** on the Baltic Sea coast, at the mouth of the Vistula — Poland's most important port.

**Trójmiasto metropolitan area:** Gdańsk + Sopot + Gdynia — three cities connected by the SKM train, all accessible in the same city break.

**Direct flights from Bucharest:** Wizz Air, OTP→GDN, ~2h15, from 40 EUR.

**Average budget:** <cite index="192-1">35–70 EUR/day (mid-range) — similar to Krakow and Warsaw.</cite>

## What to See in Gdańsk

### Długa Street and Długi Targ — the Heart of the Old Town

<cite index="188-1">The promenade from the Golden Gate to the Green Gate along Długa and Długi Targ is Gdańsk's Royal Route — the path along which Polish kings entered the city.</cite>

**Golden Gate (Złota Brama)** — the main entrance to the Old Town from the 17th century, with Renaissance arcades and allegorical statues. Free from outside.

**Town Hall (Ratusz)** — the Gothic 14th-century building with an 80 m tower, symbol of Hanseatic commercial power. <cite index="188-1">Entry ~20 PLN (4.60 EUR).</cite>

**Neptune's Fountain** — the baroque bronze from 1633 in the centre of Długi Targ Square — the most photographed spot in Gdańsk.

**Artus Court (Dwór Artusa)** — the medieval assembly hall of Hanseatic merchants, with wall paintings and a monumental 16th-century stove. <cite index="188-1">Entry ~20 PLN.</cite>

**Green Gate (Zielona Brama)** — the gate at the end of the Royal Route, with views over the Motława River and the restored granaries.

### Mariacka Street — the Amber Street

Mariacka Street is one of Poland's most photogenic — a medieval lane with stone steps at every entrance, flanked by sculptures and flower pots. On both sides: dozens of amber workshops and galleries.

<cite index="188-1">Gdańsk is the world capital of amber — this region produces 70–90% of the world's Baltic amber production.</cite> Jewellery starts from 20–30 PLN (5–7 EUR) for simple pieces, up to hundreds of EUR for pieces with inclusions (fossil insects). <cite index="188-1">Negotiating from 50% of the asking price is normal and accepted.</cite>

### St Mary's Basilica — The World's Largest Brick Church

<cite index="188-1">St Mary's Basilica is one of the world's largest Gothic brick churches, with a capacity of 25,000 people. Entry is free.</cite> The tower (400+ steps) offers exceptional panoramic views: <cite index="188-1">~8.50 PLN (2 EUR).</cite>

### Medieval Crane (Żuraw) — Symbol of Maritime Power

<cite index="188-1">The Crane (Żuraw) from 1444 was the largest medieval crane in Europe — used for centuries to load ships.</cite> Today it is a maritime museum with Motława views. <cite index="188-1">Entry ~23 PLN (5.30 EUR).</cite>

### Motława Waterfront — The Sunset Promenade

The promenade along the Motława River, from the Crane to Zielony Most bridge, is Gdańsk's most beautiful evening route. The medieval grain warehouses (spichrze) have been converted into restaurants and hotels — an exceptional sunset scene.

## Old Town Walking Tour — Legends and Facts in 3–6 Hours

### European Solidarity Centre — Where History Changed

<cite index="188-1">The European Solidarity Centre (ECS) preserves the history of the 1980 strike in moving interactive exhibitions explaining how shipyard workers changed the world.</cite>

Built right next to the former Gate No. 2 of the Lenin Shipyard — where in August 1980 Lech Wałęsa announced the strike and led negotiations with communist authorities — the building itself is a remarkable architectural work, clad in oxidised rust-coloured steel (corten) evoking ships.

**Sala BHP** (inside the ECS) — the hall where the Gdańsk Agreement was signed on 31 August 1980, legalising Solidarity. One of the most important rooms in post-war European history.

<cite index="199-1">Ticket: ~34 PLN (7.85 EUR). Free on Mondays.</cite>

### Museum of the Second World War

<cite index="188-1">The museum presents World War II from a uniquely Polish perspective, emphasising civilian suffering and resistance — often overlooked in Western narratives.</cite>

<cite index="185-1">It includes a life-size Warsaw street from the interwar period, a Sherman tank and a detailed Holocaust section.</cite> <cite index="199-1">Ticket: ~30 PLN (6.90 EUR). Free on Tuesdays.</cite>

**Westerplatte** — 7 km from the centre, the peninsula where Germany fired the first shots of World War II on 1 September 1939. <cite index="188-1">Monument and small museum on the site of the Polish defensive battle.</cite> Bus or pleasure boat (~10–15 PLN).

## Sopot — The Elegant Baltic Resort

Sopot is 15 minutes by SKM train from Gdańsk (5–8 PLN) and is completely different from the industrial port — an elegant seaside town with Tsarist-era and Art Nouveau architecture, fine sand beach and Europe's longest pier.

**The Pier (Molo)** — <cite index="199-1">entry ~12 PLN (2.75 EUR) in season (May–September).</cite> 511 metres into the Baltic Sea, with views of the bays and toward Gdynia. At the end: café and complete coastal panorama.

**Monte Cassino Street (Bohaterów Monte Cassino)** — Sopot's main pedestrian promenade, with restaurants, cafés and shops. In the evening it becomes the centre of the Trójmiasto's nightlife.

**The beach** — fine sand, free, with sunbeds available (~15–25 PLN per pair). Baltic water: 18–22°C in July–August.

## Day Trip to Malbork Castle — The World's Largest

<cite index="191-1">Malbork Castle of the Teutonic Knights is the world's largest castle by area, built in the 13th–15th centuries in red Gothic brick.</cite> UNESCO World Heritage Site. Located 43 km from Gdańsk.

**Getting there:** PKP Intercity train from Gdańsk Główny → Malbork, 24–35 minutes. <cite index="191-1">Train ticket: 25–35 PLN (5.75–8 EUR) one-way. Castle ticket: 70–80 PLN (16–18 EUR) with audio guide. Total excursion: ~30–40 EUR per person.</cite>

**Plan 3–4 hours** for the full route through the castle's three sections (High Castle, Middle Castle, Lower Castle).

## Food and Drink — Gdańsk Gastronomy Guide

### What You Must Eat

**Baltic fish** — marinated herring, smoked sprats, grilled cod are Gdańsk's coastal specialities. At any restaurant on the Motława waterfront: 10–20 EUR a portion.

**Pierogi** — omnipresent, as throughout Poland. 3–5 EUR a portion.

**Żurek** — fermented rye soup. 2–5 EUR.

**Rye bread with butter and smoked fish** — classic port breakfast. At the local market: 3–5 EUR.

### Goldwasser — Gdańsk's Golden Liqueur

<cite index="192-1">Goldwasser is a traditional liqueur produced in Gdańsk since 1598, with 22-karat gold flakes in suspension.</cite> One of Europe's oldest liqueurs. Flavour: complex, aromatic, slightly sweet with herbal notes. Drink cold, neat.

A bottle (0.5L): 35–60 PLN (8–14 EUR) in city centre shops or at the Gdańska Distillery.

### Craft Beer in Gdańsk

Gdańsk has a rapidly growing craft beer scene. Recommended brewpubs: Browar Gdańsk, Piwoteka Narodowa. Craft pint: 15–22 PLN (3.50–5 EUR).

### Where to Eat

**Budget (5–10 EUR/meal):**
- Bar Mleczny Turystyczny — Polish food at canteen prices
- Local pierogarnie on Chlebnicka Street

**Mid-range (10–25 EUR/person):**
- Restaurants on Długi Targ — touristy but convenient
- Restaurants on the Motława waterfront — slightly cheaper, river views

**Caution:** <cite index="199-1">the most crowded restaurants on Długi Targ charge 30–40% more than those on adjacent streets for the same quality.</cite>

## Practical Tips

**SKM train** connects Gdańsk–Sopot–Gdynia every 10–15 minutes. <cite index="199-1">24-hour ticket: 22 PLN (5 EUR) — excellent if visiting all three cities.</cite>

**Bus to Westerplatte** — line 106 from Old Town takes ~30 minutes. Alternatively, the pleasure boat from Motława (slower but more scenic).

**Amber** — verify authenticity: rub between fingers (becomes electrostatically charged), put in salt water (it floats). Fair price: 20–50 PLN for simple jewellery; pieces with fossil inclusions: 100–500+ PLN depending on rarity.

**St Dominic's Fair** (usually 1–3 August) — Poland's oldest fair, since the 13th century. Book accommodation months ahead if visiting during this period.
