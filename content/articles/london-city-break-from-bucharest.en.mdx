London is one of those cities where everyone arrives with high expectations and still comes away surprised. The scale, the cultural depth, the food scene, the sheer density of world-class free museums — it earns its place on every European bucket list. ## Before You Book — Two Things Romanian Travellers Often Miss

**1. The UK ETA (Electronic Travel Authorisation)** — introduced in 2024, this is required for all Romanian and EU citizens visiting the UK. It costs £10, takes 10 minutes to apply for online (on the UK government website), and is linked digitally to your passport. Apply at least 72 hours before you fly. Airlines check ETAs at boarding — without one, you won't board.

**2. The EHIC doesn't work in the UK** — the European Health Insurance Card lost its validity in the UK after Brexit. If you get sick or injured in London, NHS treatment for tourists can cost thousands of pounds. Travel insurance with UK medical cover is not optional — it's essential. At €15–25 for a week, it's less than a London pub dinner.

## Getting Around London

The London Underground (the Tube) is your main tool. In 2026, the absolute best way to pay is **contactless with your bank card or phone** — tap in and tap out, exactly like a card payment. TfL's daily cap means:

- Zones 1–2 (covers all major tourist sites): **£8.90 cap Monday–Friday, £5.25 Saturday–Sunday**
- No need to buy individual tickets; once you hit the cap, all further journeys are free that day

Never buy single paper tickets. A single Zone 1 journey costs £5.60 on paper; contactless caps your whole day at £8.90.

**Bolt and Uber** are widely available and reasonably priced for late nights or when you're carrying luggage. Surge pricing applies during peak hours and after West End shows end (around 11pm). Avoid street taxis (black cabs) for longer journeys — fares are metered and expensive. Fine for short hops if you insist on the classic London experience.

**Walking:** Central London is far more compact than it appears on a map. British Museum to Covent Garden: 10 minutes on foot. Tate Modern to Borough Market: 5 minutes. Notting Hill to Holland Park: 15 minutes. Walk whenever the map shows under 20 minutes — you'll see more and save Underground fare.

## 3-Day London Itinerary from Bucharest

### Day 1 — The Classic: Westminster, South Bank & Borough Market

**Morning (9:00):** Start at **Westminster Bridge** for the postcard view — the Houses of Parliament, Big Ben (Elizabeth Tower, which completed its renovation), and the Thames all in one frame. Free.

Walk across the bridge to the **South Bank** along the Thames. This riverside walk is free, atmospheric, and connects most major South London attractions.

**Midday (11:00):** **Borough Market** — London's most famous food market, open Tuesday–Saturday. This is where you have lunch. Budget £10–15: a salt beef bagel from Monty's Deli, a wheel of cheese from Neal's Yard, fresh pasta from one of the Italian stalls. It's also just south of London Bridge, next to Southwark Cathedral (free to enter, beautiful).

**Afternoon (13:30):** Walk west along the Thames to **Tate Modern** (the old Bankside Power Station, free entry for the permanent collection, world-class contemporary art). Then cross the Millennium Bridge on foot — great views — to **St Paul's Cathedral**. Entry costs £24 but the exterior and surroundings are worth seeing for free. Continue east to the **Sky Garden** at 20 Fenchurch Street — free panoramic views from the 35th floor (booking required in advance online, free).

**Evening:** Head to **Shoreditch** for dinner. This is London's best food neighbourhood for variety and value: Vietnamese, Korean BBQ, authentic Mexican, Israeli, and everything in between, all at non-tourist prices. Budget £15–25 for dinner.

### Day 2 — Museums: British Museum, V&A & Kensington

**Morning (9:00):** **British Museum** — one of the world's great museums, entirely free. The permanent collection includes the Rosetta Stone, Elgin Marbles, Egyptian mummies, Sutton Hoo helmet, and artefacts from every civilisation on earth. Minimum 2 hours; could spend a whole day.

**Midday:** Walk or take the bus south to **Kensington**. Have lunch at a pub around South Kensington station (a pint and a pie: £12–18) or grab something cheaper at Whole Foods on Gloucester Road.

**Afternoon (14:00):** **Victoria & Albert Museum** (V&A) — the world's greatest museum of design, craft, and the decorative arts. Fashion, furniture, ceramics, jewellery, textiles, photography — all free. More manageable than the British Museum; extraordinary quality. Alternatively, **Natural History Museum** is directly next door — the blue whale skeleton in the main hall alone is worth the trip (free).

**Evening:** **South Kensington to Chelsea** walk (15 minutes) for the King's Road. Then head to **Covent Garden** (30 min by Tube) for street performers, cocktail bars, and dinner options at all price points. If you want a West End show, this is the night — book tickets in advance through official channels, or try the TKTS booth on Leicester Square for same-day discounts (genuine discounts on unsold seats, up to 50% off).

### Day 3 — East London & Notting Hill

**Morning (9:00):** **Camden Market** — London's iconic alternative market, open daily. Three interconnected market areas: Stables Market (vintage, crafts, street food), Camden Lock (canal views, international food), Buck Street Market (new clothing, accessories). Food stalls open from 10:00 — breakfast here is excellent and varied. Budget £8–12.

From Camden, it's a 10-minute walk (or one Tube stop) to **Primrose Hill** — a small park with the best panoramic view of London's skyline, completely free, and less crowded than Observatory Hill in Greenwich.

**Afternoon (13:00):** Take the Tube to Notting Hill Gate. **Portobello Road Market** (Saturday is best, but there are stalls most days). Antiques, vintage clothing, fresh food. The neighbourhood itself — white-painted townhouses, Westbourne Grove boutiques, Electric Cinema (London's oldest working cinema) — is worth an hour of wandering.

From Notting Hill, walk south 20 minutes to **Kensington Gardens and Hyde Park** — 250 acres of parkland, the Serpentine Gallery (free), and in summer the Lido. Free.

**Evening:** Head to **Soho** for your last London dinner. Berwick Street Market (until 17:00) for snacks; then dinner on one of the side streets around Old Compton Street. London's theatre and live music scene is concentrated here — if you haven't seen a show yet, this is your last chance.

## Food in London — Eating Well Without the Tourist Tax

London's food scene is genuinely world-class, and you don't need to spend £50/head to experience it. The key is knowing where to avoid tourist pricing and where to find extraordinary food at normal prices.

**Market eating (budget £8–15 per meal):**
- Borough Market — the gold standard for gourmet street food
- Broadway Market (Saturdays) — east London's best neighbourhood market
- Maltby Street Market (Saturdays) — smaller, less crowded than Borough, excellent quality
- Berwick Street Market (Soho, weekdays) — fresh produce and cheap lunch spots

**Neighbourhood restaurants (budget £15–25 per person):**
- **Shoreditch / Bethnal Green:** the most diverse restaurant neighbourhood in the city
- **Brixton Village:** covered market with restaurants from every corner of the world
- **Peckham:** south London's creative food hub, brilliant value
- **Dalston / Hackney:** Turkish grills, Vietnamese, Ethiopian — all at local prices

**What to eat in London (must-tries):**
- Full English breakfast (London greasy spoon "caff"): £6–9, extraordinary value
- Salt beef bagel (Brick Lane or Borough): £7
- Fish and chips (proper chip shop, not tourist trap): £10–14
- Roast dinner at a gastropub on Sunday: £16–22, genuinely excellent
- Pad Thai at Kingsland Road (Shoreditch's "Pho Mile"): £9–12

**Where to avoid tourist pricing:** anywhere on Oxford Street, around Buckingham Palace, near Leicester Square for dinner (tourist menus at non-tourist prices). One block away from any major attraction, prices normalise dramatically.

## London in Context — What's Changed in 2026

A few practical updates for 2026 visitors:

**The UK ETA is now standard:** all Romanian/EU visitors need it. Apply on the official UK government website (gov.uk/eta). Takes 10 minutes, costs £10, valid for 2 years or until passport expiry.

**Contactless travel is fully mature:** Apple Pay, Google Pay, and any contactless bank card all work seamlessly on the entire TfL network. No Oyster card needed unless you prefer a physical card.

**Museum opening times:** most major museums now open 10:00–18:00 daily (some until 21:00 on Fridays). The British Museum's Great Court opens earlier (9:00).

**West End shows:** book online well in advance for best prices. TKTS booth on Leicester Square sells genuine discounts (up to 50% off) for same-day and next-day shows. Major productions sell out months in advance.
