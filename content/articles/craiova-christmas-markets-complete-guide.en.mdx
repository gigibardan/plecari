# Craiova Christmas Market: Complete Guide — Europe's Largest and Most Spectacular Winter Wonderland

There is a city in southern Romania that has quietly pulled off one of the most remarkable transformations in European Christmas culture. A place that most Europeans had never heard of a decade ago now hosts what many experts and visitors call **Europe's largest Christmas market** — a sprawling 280,000-square-meter winter wonderland that has left travel journalists and tourists from London to Tokyo searching for superlatives.

That city is **Craiova** — the capital of Oltenia, in southern Romania.

Ranked **2nd best Christmas destination in Europe** by European Best Destinations in 2024, described by Secret London as "just 3 hours from London and home to Europe's largest Christmas market," and covered by Travel and Tour World, Travelbook Magazine and dozens of international publications, Craiova's Christmas market has become a phenomenon — one that is simultaneously Romania's greatest Christmas secret and, for those in the know, its most exciting winter destination.

## Why Craiova? The Rise of an Unlikely Christmas Capital

### From Modest Market to European Phenomenon

Craiova had a Christmas market before — a modest one, comparable to dozens of markets across Romania. What changed everything was a decision to transform the event into something truly extraordinary: a large-scale spectacle worthy of international attention.

The investment in decorations, lighting infrastructure, artificial snow systems, unique attractions and professional organization produced results faster than anyone expected. What started as local ambition became a genuinely world-class event.

**The numbers that left Europe speechless:**
- **280,000 m²** of total area — equivalent to 39 football pitches
- **Romania's largest ice rink** at 1,800 m²
- Thousands of decorated chalets and hundreds of thousands of LED lights
- **A Flying Santa** — a unique installation sending Santa Claus flying above the crowds in his sleigh
- Scheduled artificial snowfall — several times a day, regardless of weather
- A **Christmas train** circling all four themed worlds
- A **40-meter panoramic Ferris wheel**
- Multiple concert stages with daily performances

### International Recognition

**European Best Destinations**, the organization that annually ranks the continent's best holiday markets, placed Craiova's Christmas market **2nd in all of Europe in 2024** — ahead of markets in Germany, Austria and France that have been running for centuries.

The international press followed. Headlines from London to Berlin announced the discovery:
- "Europe's Largest Christmas Market Is Just 3 Hours From London" — *Secret London*
- "Craiova: Home to Europe's Largest Christmas Market" — *Travelbook Magazine*
- "Romania Transforms Into a Magical Winter Wonderland" — *Travel and Tour World*
- "Hardly Anyone Knows This City — Yet It Has Europe's Largest Christmas Market" — *Vienna.at*

The story is consistent: Craiova is Europe's greatest Christmas secret, and those who go there come back amazed.

## The 4 Themed Worlds — What Makes Craiova Unique in Europe

The concept of **four distinct themed worlds**, each with its own visual identity, music and food offering, is what truly separates Craiova's Christmas market from anything else in Europe.

### World 1: Christmas Town (The Central Hub)

The heart of the market, occupying the main area of Piața Mihai Viteazul. This is where you find:

**Traditional craft chalets** — hundreds of beautifully decorated wooden chalets with garlands, lights and Christmas ornaments, selling:
- Handmade Christmas ornaments and decorations
- Traditional Oltenian pottery and ceramics
- Folk costumes and embroidered textiles
- Hand-painted icons on wood and glass
- Natural cosmetics and locally-sourced wellness products
- Unique artisan Christmas gifts

**The giant central Christmas tree** — a 20+ meter fir tree decorated with thousands of ornaments and LED lights, the visual symbol of the entire market.

**The main stage** — with daily concerts including:
- Traditional Romanian carols (colinde) performed by local choirs
- Pop and rock concerts by well-known Romanian artists (weekends)
- Ballet and dance performances by Craiova's schools
- Children's puppet theatre
- Classical Christmas music recitals

### World 2: The Snow Realm

This is where Craiova has genuinely revolutionized the concept of Christmas markets. Professional snowmaking equipment transforms this zone into a **genuine winter landscape**, regardless of the actual temperature outside.

**Scheduled artificial snowfall** — 2-3 times per day, at announced times, the snowmaking machines release real snow over the zone. Children and adults can play in the snow even when the temperature is 10°C above zero.

**Romania's largest ice rink:**
- 1,800 m² of artificial ice — one of the largest in southeastern Europe
- Can accommodate hundreds of skaters simultaneously
- Skate rental available on-site
- Skating instructors for beginners
- Special morning sessions for young children

**Winter playground** — sledding, artificial snow toboggan, igloo structures for children, snowman building sessions.

### World 3: Fairytale Land

Primarily designed for families with children, but with something for everyone:

**Santa's House** — an elaborately themed space where children can:
- Meet Santa Claus (Moș Crăciun) and Mrs. Claus in person
- Leave letters in the special Christmas mailbox
- Receive gifts and sweets
- Take professional Christmas photographs with the full set

**The Flying Santa** — Craiova's most unique attraction and one that exists nowhere else in Europe in this form. At scheduled intervals, a spectacular installation makes Santa Claus actually fly above the crowd in his sleigh pulled by reindeer. The visual effect is extraordinary, especially after dark.

**The Christmas train** — a narrow-gauge miniature train that circuits through all four worlds, giving visitors a complete tour of the market. Full circuit: approximately 20 minutes. Perfect for young children and for getting your bearings.

**Traditional carousels** — a classic Christmas carousel for young children and a larger ride for older kids.

**Creative workshops** — children can participate in:
- Making Christmas ornaments
- Glass painting
- Clay modeling
- Decorating Christmas biscuits
- Writing Christmas cards

### World 4: The Gastronomic Village

The most aromatic zone of the entire market — a festival of winter tastes with emphasis on Oltenian cuisine and Christmas specialties.

**Mulled wine (vin fiert)** — Oltenian mulled wine is celebrated across Romania. The price of a glass in Craiova is **10-15 RON (2-3€)**, significantly cheaper than 5-8€ in German or Austrian markets. Recipes vary between stands: cinnamon, cloves, oranges, star anise, local spices.

**Warm beer with lemon** — a less sweet alternative to mulled wine, beloved by locals.

**Cocoșei** — traditional Romanian fairground sweets made from colored caramelized sugar in the shape of roosters and hearts. 10-15 RON each.

**Chimney cake (kurtoș)** — pastry cone baked over open fire.

**Oltenian sausages and mici** — the hot, savory meats specific to Oltenia: mici (skinless grilled meat rolls) and artisanal pork sausages with garlic and regional seasonings.

**Sarmale** — traditional Romanian stuffed cabbage leaves filled with pork and rice, the ultimate Romanian Christmas comfort food.

**Cozonac and traditional pastries** — Romanian Christmas sweet breads with walnuts and lokum, honey cakes and holiday cookies.

**Price guide for food and drink:**
- Mulled wine: 10-15 RON (2-3€) per glass
- Cocoșei: 10-15 RON (2-3€) each
- Mici or sausage: 8-12 RON (1.5-2.5€) each
- 2 sarmale: 15-20 RON (3-4€)
- Cozonac slice: 10-15 RON (2-3€)
- Hot chocolate: 10-15 RON (2-3€)
- Pretzels: 10-12 RON (2-2.5€)

> **Bottom line on food prices:** Craiova is 50-70% cheaper than Christmas markets in Germany, Austria, France or UK for comparable food and drinks.

## Concerts, Events and Special Occasions

Craiova's Christmas market is not just a shopping and eating destination — it's a festival running for weeks. Every weekend features concerts and shows; the key periods (pre-Christmas and New Year) intensify dramatically.

### Regular Events

**Lights-on ceremony** (opening night) — a spectacular event with fireworks, concerts and the magic moment when all the market's lights are switched on simultaneously.

**Flying Santa shows** — every evening, usually at 18:00 and 20:00, Santa Claus performs his flying show above the square. The exact schedule is announced daily at the main stage.

**Weekend concerts** — well-known Romanian pop and rock artists perform on the main stage every Saturday and Sunday evening. Previous editions have featured Smiley, Andra, Carla's Dreams, Irina Rimes, Horia Brenciu and others.

**Traditional carols** — every evening before the main concert, choir groups from Craiova and from Oltenian villages perform traditional Romanian Christmas carols (colinde). This is one of the most authentic and moving experiences at the market.

**Christmas Eve** (December 24) — special extended programme with concerts until midnight and a lights-and-sound show at midnight.

**New Year's Eve** — Craiova organizes a major New Year's celebration in Piața Mihai Viteazul, with concerts, fireworks at midnight and an open-air street party that draws tens of thousands.

## Getting to Craiova

Craiova is located approximately **230 km south-west of Bucharest**, with excellent transport connections by train, bus and road. For international visitors, it also has its own airport.

### By Train — The Recommended Option

**The train is the best way to travel** from Bucharest to Craiova, for several reasons:
- No parking headaches in a busy city
- You can drink mulled wine without worrying about driving
- Craiova train station (Gara Craiova) is 10-15 minutes on foot or by taxi from the city center

**Routes and journey times:**
- **Intercity/Interregio (IR):** approximately 2h15m — 2h30m — the fastest trains
- **Regional trains:** approximately 3 — 3.5 hours; more stops, slightly cheaper
- **Approximate prices:** 30-80 RON (6-16€) per person one-way, depending on train class and type

**Departures from Bucharest Gara de Nord:**
Frequent services throughout the day. For a day trip, the most convenient departures are early morning (06:00-09:00), arriving in Craiova by 09:00-11:00.

**Booking:** Buy tickets in advance at **cfrcalatori.ro** or the **CFR** app — trains fill up fast during Christmas weekends.

**Return:** Last trains to Bucharest usually depart between 20:00 and 22:00 — check the CFR timetable for your specific date.

### By Bus/Coach — The Budget Option

Comfortable coach services connect Bucharest and Craiova frequently and cheaply.

**Main operators:**
- **Atlassib** — one of Romania's largest coach companies
- **FlixBus** — international operator covering the route
- **Other local operators** — departing from Autogara Militari or Autogara Filaret in Bucharest

**Journey time:** approximately 2.5-3 hours (depending on A1 motorway traffic)
**Price:** 25-45 RON (5-9€) per person — significantly cheaper than train
**Bookings:** at **autogari.ro**, **flixbus.ro** or at the bus station counter

**Departures:** Coaches depart from **Autogara Militari** (metro: Preciziei or by bus) and **Autogara Filaret** (near Piața Unirii). Frequency: 1-3 departures per hour during peak times.

> **Practical tip:** During Christmas weekends, buy your ticket 2-3 days in advance — seats fill up rapidly.

### By Car — The Flexible Option

For families or groups of 3+, driving may be the most practical option.

**Main route:** Bucharest → A1 motorway → Pitești → DN65 → Craiova (approximately 230 km)
- **Via A1 motorway (to Pitești) + national road DN65:** approximately 2.5-3 hours in normal traffic
- **Note:** There is a section on national road between Pitești and Craiova; check road conditions in winter

**Parking in Craiova:**
- **Piața Mihai Viteazul** is generally closed to traffic during the market — do not plan to park there
- **Parking alternatives:**
  - Underground parking at Mall Electroputere — 10-15 minutes walk to the square
  - Street parking on Strada Unirii and Calea București
  - Parking at Teatrul Național Craiova
- **Parking costs:** 3-5 RON/hour in paid car parks
- **Tip:** Arrive before 10:00 on weekends to find parking more easily

### By Plane — For International Visitors

Craiova has its own international airport — **Craiova Airport (CRA)** — with regular services from several European cities.

**Regular and seasonal routes:**
- **Bucharest (Henri Coandă / Otopeni) → Craiova:** Tarom, approximately 45 minutes
- **London Luton → Craiova:** Wizz Air, approximately 3 hours
- **Italian cities → Craiova:** charter and scheduled seasonal services
- **Spain → Craiova:** seasonal charter flights

**From the airport to city center:** taxi or Uber, approximately 20 minutes, 30-50 RON (6-10€).

**Flying from London:** With Wizz Air's direct Luton-Craiova route at approximately 3 hours, Craiova is genuinely one of the most accessible Eastern European Christmas destinations from the UK — often cheaper and faster than flying to Vienna, Prague or Kraków.

## Craiova — A City Worth Discovering

Craiova is not just a pit stop for the market. It is a city of **300,000 people** with a rich history, stunning museums, beautiful parks and a vibrant cultural scene. If you're making the trip, allow time to explore.

### The Jean Mihail Palace — Art Museum

**The most beautiful palace in Craiova** and one of the most impressive buildings in southern Romania. Constructed in the early 20th century for the Mihail family by Jean Mihail (a wealthy Oltenian businessman), the palace now houses the **Craiova Art Museum**.

The collection includes:
- **Works by Constantin Brâncuși** — the legendary Romanian sculptor was born in neighboring Gorj county, and the museum holds authentic pieces. This alone is worth the visit.
- Paintings by Theodor Aman, Nicolae Grigorescu and Ioan Andreescu — the great masters of Romanian painting
- European art from significant collections
- Decorative arts and period furniture

Even if you're not a museum enthusiast, **the palace itself is worth visiting** for its magnificent neo-Renaissance architecture with Baroque influences.

**Opening hours:** Tuesday-Sunday, 10:00-18:00
**Ticket:** approximately 20 RON (~4€) adults, 10 RON (~2€) students

### Museum of Oltenia — Regional History in Depth

**The oldest museum in Craiova** (founded 1915) and one of Romania's largest regional museums. The complex spans several buildings in the city center and includes sections covering:

**Natural Sciences** — documenting Oltenia's flora, fauna, geology and paleontology.

**Archaeology and History** — with artifacts from:
- The Dacian period (Dacia malvensis region)
- Roman occupation (Craiova falls within former Roman province Dacia)
- Medieval Oltenia
- Numismatic and medieval art collections

**Ethnography** — the most authentic window into traditional Oltenian life:
- Oltenian folk costumes with characteristic embroidery
- Horezu pottery (Horezu is only 60 km from Craiova)
- Traditional musical instruments
- Reconstructions of traditional interiors

**Opening hours:** Tuesday-Sunday, 10:00-18:00
**Ticket:** approximately 15-20 RON (~3-4€) adults

### Nicolae Romanescu Park — One of Europe's Most Beautiful Parks

Recognized as one of **Europe's most beautiful parks at the 1900 Paris Universal Exhibition**, Parcul Nicolae Romanescu is Craiova's most beloved green space.

Covering approximately **90 hectares**, the park features:
- Romantic alleys with century-old trees
- Lakes with rowing boats (seasonal)
- The iconic suspension bridge — a symbol of the park
- A stone viaduct traversing the park
- Restaurants and terraces (seasonal)

**In winter:** The park is stunning covered in snow. Locals come for winter walks; children sled down the park's hills. It's a world away from the market's buzz.

### Casa Băniei — A 17th Century Monument

One of Craiova's oldest surviving buildings, dating from the **17th century**. Casa Băniei (House of the Banat) is a representative example of Brâncovenesc architectural style and served as the administrative headquarters of the Banat of Oltenia.

Today it houses a museum of folk art and culture, with exhibits on traditional Oltenian crafts.

### The Botanical Garden — Tropical Warmth in Winter

One of Romania's largest botanical gardens, located a short distance from the city center. Its **15 hectares** contain thousands of plant species from all climatic zones.

**In winter:** While not the flowering season, the **tropical greenhouses** offer a warm, lush escape from the cold outside — a perfect 30-minute interlude between market visits.

**Winter hours:** 09:00-16:00
**Ticket:** approximately 10-15 RON (~2-3€)

### Universitatea Craiova Stadium

For football fans: **Stadionul Ion Oblemenco** is one of Romania's most modern arenas, opened in 2017, home to **CS Universitatea Craiova**. The atmosphere at a home match is legendary, and Craiova supporters are among the most passionate in the country.

## Oltenian Cuisine — What to Eat in Craiova

Oltenia has a distinctive, robust and aromatic cuisine with Balkan influences. You cannot visit Craiova without tasting local specialties.

### At the Market

**Oltenian mulled wine (vin fiert)** — wine from the Drăgășani vineyards (one of Romania's oldest, documented since the Geto-Dacian era) mulled with cinnamon, cloves, oranges and spices. More wine-forward and drier than mulled wines from northern Romania. 10-15 RON (2-3€) per glass.

**Cocoșei** — fairground sweets from colored caramelized sugar, one of the most quintessentially Romanian market experiences.

**Local mici** — the Romanian version of skinless grilled meat rolls, from local recipes with garlic, thyme and paprika.

### At Local Restaurants

**Ciorbă de burtă** (tripe soup) — the Oltenian version, with generous sour cream and crushed garlic, served with pickled hot pepper. One of the finest in Romania.

**Ciorbă de fasole cu afumătură** (bean soup with smoked meat) — bean soup with smoked pork ribs, served with pickles. A winter staple.

**Tochitura olteană** — a mix of pork offal (liver, kidneys, heart) and meat with crushed garlic sauce (mujdei), served with polenta and fried egg. The quintessential Oltenian dish.

**Zacuscă** — a spread made from roasted eggplant, peppers and tomatoes, more intense than versions from other regions.

**Oltenian pickles** — cucumbers, green tomatoes, hot peppers and sauerkraut, homemade style with distinctive local character.

**Drăgășani wine** — Craiova is only 60 km from the **Drăgășani wine region**, one of Romania's oldest. The white and red wines, especially indigenous varieties (Feteasca Regală, Crâmpoșie, Negru de Drăgășani) are unmissable. Many restaurants serve them by the glass.

## Day Trips from Craiova

Craiova is an excellent base for exploring Oltenia — one of Romania's most beautiful and least-traveled regions.

### Horezu Monastery and Pottery (60 km, 1 hour)

**Horezu Monastery** is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, built by Constantin Brâncoveanu in 1690-1697. It is the most representative example of **Brâncovenesc architecture** — the style specific to Wallachia and Oltenia that blends Byzantine, Baroque and Oriental elements.

**Horezu pottery** — the town of Horezu is world-famous for its traditional ceramics: hand-painted clay vessels with characteristic geometric and floral motifs in the distinctive colors (white, terracotta, green and brown). Horezu pottery has centuries of tradition and holds a Protected Geographical Indication.

Visiting Horezu, you can buy directly from the craftspeople — the most beautiful souvenirs from Oltenia.

### The Olt Gorge and Cozia Monastery (80 km, 1.5 hours)

The Olt Gorge is one of the most spectacular landscapes in southern Carpathia: the Olt River has cut through the Căpățânii and Cozia mountains, creating a dramatic gorge followed by both the road and railway.

Along the Olt banks: **Cozia Monastery** (14th century, first-class historical monument), thermal baths at Căciulata, Arnota Monastery and Bistrița Monastery.

### Strehaia Medieval Fortress (70 km, 1 hour)

Strehaia was one of the residences of **Mihai Viteazul** (Michael the Brave) — the first ruler to unify Wallachia, Moldova and Transylvania in 1600. The medieval fortress of Strehaia, though partially ruined, retains the atmosphere of its medieval era.

### The Danube (80 km, 1.5 hours)

Craiova is relatively close to the Danube — the great river forming the border with Bulgaria. Bechet is the crossing point to Bulgaria and offers spectacular views along the Danube bank. In years with severe frost, the frozen Danube is a rare natural spectacle.

## Craiova vs. Other European Christmas Markets — An Honest Comparison

Craiova has earned international recognition, but how does it honestly compare to other famous Christmas markets?

### Where Craiova Wins

**Scale:** 280,000 m² cannot be matched by any other Christmas market in Europe. Nuremberg, Vienna, Strasbourg, Prague — all are much smaller by area.

**Price:** Mulled wine at 2-3€ vs. 5-8€ in Germany or Austria. Food, generally speaking, is 2-3 times cheaper than in Western Europe.

**Originality:** The Flying Santa, scheduled artificial snowfall, the concept of four themed worlds — these are innovations that exist nowhere else in this combination.

**Space:** Compared to Nuremberg or Vienna where you might queue 30 minutes for mulled wine on a weekend, Craiova is more spacious and manageable.

**Atmosphere:** Romanians are genuinely warm and welcoming hosts. The market has an authenticity that emerges from real local enthusiasm rather than managed tourism.

### Where Other Markets Still Lead

**Historical authenticity:** Nuremberg's Christkindlesmarkt dates from 1628; Strasbourg's Christkindelsmärik from 1570. Craiova cannot compete with centuries of tradition.

**Artisan quality:** German and Alsatian markets have craftspeople with multi-generational quality in specific categories (wooden ornaments, glass blowing, candles).

**Medieval architectural backdrop:** Vienna, Prague, Strasbourg have medieval city centers that add incomparable visual beauty to their markets.

**Conclusion:** Craiova wins the category "size, spectacle, value for money." Western markets win on "tradition, craft quality and architectural backdrop." Both are worth visiting — fortunately, Craiova is significantly closer and cheaper for most visitors who've already done the Western circuit.

## Practical Tips for the Perfect Visit

### What to Wear

**Craiova in winter:**
- Average November temperatures: 5-10°C
- Average December-January: -5 to 5°C
- Possibility of snow and sub-zero temperatures
- Wind can make the cold feel sharper

**Clothing recommendations:**
- Layered clothing (thermal base layer, mid-layer sweater, waterproof outer jacket)
- Hat, gloves and scarf — essential for evenings
- Waterproof boots with anti-slip soles — critical on ice
- Small backpack for market purchases

### Photography

The best time for photographs: **1-2 hours after sunset** (in December, approximately 16:30-18:30). Market lights are at maximum, there's still some natural light on the horizon, and the crowd is building.

Best vantage points:
- Main market entrance with the central tree in the background
- Ice rink from above (Ferris wheel offers a great perspective)
- Illuminated chalets against the night sky
- Flying Santa — have your camera ready

### Timing Your Visit

- **Morning (10:00-12:00):** Less crowded, ideal for artisan shopping and ice skating
- **Lunch (12:00-15:00):** Maximum visitors; avoid on weekends if you dislike crowds
- **Evening (17:00-21:00):** Most magical — lights are on, concerts begin, atmosphere peaks

**Busiest periods:**
- Weekends throughout December (especially December 5-24)
- December 23-26
- December 30-31 and January 1-2

**Quietest:** Monday-Thursday, outside the festive weeks.
