There is a moment when you understand why Mostar survived the war — not as a physical place, but as an idea. On 9 November 1993, HVO soldiers dynamited the Old Bridge (Stari Most) — the elegant limestone arch built in 1566 by the Ottoman architect Mimar Hayruddin, which had connected the Bosnian bank to the Croatian bank above the emerald-green Neretva for 427 years.

<cite index="53-1">The reconstruction of the Old Bridge (1999–2004) received UNESCO recognition in 2005 as a landmark achievement in heritage protection and a symbol of human solidarity following the bridge's destruction in November 1993.</cite>

It is not a simple reconstruction. It is an act of intention — the declaration that certain things are too important to disappear permanently. <cite index="56-1">Today, crossing the Old Bridge is free, and you can watch professional divers leaping 24 metres into the green Neretva below — a tradition dating back to the 17th century.</cite>

And beyond Stari Most, Mostar is an entire Ottoman universe: <cite index="59-1">the Kujundžiluk bazaar with craftsmen, mosques with minarets, white limestone houses and cafés perched over the river.</cite> A few kilometres away: Blagaj with Europe's largest karst spring, Počitelj with intact medieval architecture and Kravice Waterfalls — a perfect natural amphitheatre.

**Country:** Bosnia and Herzegovina — not in the EU, not in Schengen.

**Population:** approximately 105,000.

**Language:** Bosnian, Croatian, Serbian (practically the same language, three political names). English is well spoken in the tourist area.

**UNESCO:** Old Bridge Area of the Old City — since 2005.

**Nearest airport:** Sarajevo (SJJ), 130 km / 2.5 hours.

**Average budget:** <cite index="53-1">50–80 EUR/day (mid-range) — more affordable than the Croatian Adriatic coast.</cite>

## Stari Most — The Old Bridge

<cite index="55-1">Crossing Stari Most is completely free — it is a public thoroughfare accessible 24/7.</cite> The bridge arcs 21 metres above the Neretva, connecting the Bosnian neighbourhood (Kujundžiluk) to the Croatian bank.

### The History of Destruction and Reconstruction

Built in 1566 by Mimar Hayruddin (a student of Sinan, architect of the Süleymaniye Mosque in Istanbul), the Bridge stood for 427 years. On 9 November 1993, it was brought down by HVO Croatian forces during the Bosnian War.

<cite index="53-1">The reconstruction (1999–2004) used the same limestone from the original quarries and authentic medieval techniques, documented through old photographs and plans. In 2004, Prince Charles attended the reopening. UNESCO inscribed the area in 2005.</cite>

### Tara and Halebija Towers

The two towers at either end of the bridge house the Stari Most Museum. <cite index="55-1">Museum + towers ticket: 8–12 KM (~4–6 EUR). Hours: 9:00–18:00 in season.</cite>

### The Bridge Divers

<cite index="59-1">Professional divers (Mostarski Ronilački Klub) perform 24-metre jumps into the Neretva on afternoons from June–September, after collecting donations from spectators on the bridge — typically ~25 EUR from all spectators combined.</cite>

<cite index="56-1">The diving tradition dates from the 17th century — a rite of initiation for young men of Mostar.</cite> The International Diving Championship takes place on 27 July — hundreds of spectators watch divers from around the world.

## Kujundžiluk — The Ottoman Bazaar

Kujundžiluk is the Old Town's main lane — a narrow cobblestone street with jewellers' shops (beaten copper, ivory, silver), craftsmen's workshops, tea houses and cafés. The atmosphere is authentic — craftsmen work visibly in their workshops, not selling imported souvenirs.

**Recommended purchases:** beaten copper džezve and cups, decorative plates, silver filigree jewellery, baklava.

**Price note:** <cite index="56-1">negotiation is accepted and expected at Kujundžiluk — the initial price may be 30–50% above the real value.</cite>

## Koski Mehmed Pasha Mosque — Panoramic View from the Minaret

<cite index="56-1">Koski Mehmed Pasha Mosque (entry 10 KM/~5 EUR, includes the minaret) offers one of Mostar's best views.</cite> The minaret provides a complete panorama over Stari Most, the Neretva and the Old Town — far more spectacular than the bridge towers.

## Crooked Bridge (Kriva Ćuprija) — Older than Stari Most

<cite index="56-1">Kriva Ćuprija (the Crooked Bridge), built in 1558 — eight years before Stari Most — was probably a test structure built by Hayruddin to perfect the techniques he would use at the main bridge.</cite> Located in the Lučki neighbourhood, 10 minutes' walk from Stari Most. Free access and far less crowded.

## Tour from Mostar: Blagaj, Počitelj and Kravice Waterfalls — Herzegovina's Best Day

### Blagaj Tekke — Dervish House at Europe's Largest Karst Spring

<cite index="64-1">Blagaj is one of Bosnia's oldest towns, with a 16th-century Ottoman Tekke (dervish house) built directly at the source of the Buna River — cold, clear water emerging from 200 m cliffs.</cite>

The Buna is the largest karst spring in Europe with constant flow: <cite index="63-1">43 cubic metres per second.</cite> The water is so transparent that visibility reaches 10+ metres.

**Entry:** free. Tekke: 2–3 KM (~1–1.50 EUR). <cite index="63-1">A Bosnian coffee on the Buna riverbank — at 10°C year-round — is one of Bosnia's most memorable culinary experiences.</cite>

**Distance:** 12 km from Mostar, 20 minutes by car or taxi.

### Počitelj — The Medieval Town Frozen in Time

<cite index="63-1">Počitelj is an Ottoman medieval town on the Neretva bank, with defensive walls, a 16th-century mosque and the Džebhana tower.</cite> The grey limestone architecture, climbing up the hillside behind the river, is one of Bosnia's most photogenic images.

**Distance:** 30 km from Mostar. Entry: free.

### Kravice Waterfalls — Herzegovina's Natural Swimming Pool

<cite index="59-1">Kravice Waterfalls are a karst phenomenon — a natural travertine amphitheatre 25 metres high and 120 metres wide, with a natural turquoise swimming pool.</cite>

<cite index="63-1">Entry: 10 KM (~5 EUR) in season.</cite> Swimming is permitted and popular. Best time: early morning or late afternoon — at midday in July–August, large crowds.

**Distance:** 40 km west of Mostar.

## Food and Drink in Mostar

**Ćevapi** — Bosnian grilled meat rolls with somun bread. 5–8 KM (~2.50–4 EUR). Best at local restaurants in Kujundžiluk, not the tourist-facing riverside restaurants.

**Burek** — meat, cheese or spinach pastry. 2–4 KM (~1–2 EUR) at a bakery.

**Neretva river fish** — trout and eel, local specialities. 12–20 KM (~6–10 EUR).

**Bosnian coffee** — served in a džezva with Turkish delight. 2–3 KM (~1–1.50 EUR).

**Where to eat:** <cite index="56-1">restaurants in Kujundžiluk are 30–50% more expensive than those on adjacent streets for the same dishes.</cite> Walk into the residential neighbourhoods Staro Santica or toward the local market for authentic prices.
