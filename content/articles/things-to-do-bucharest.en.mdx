## Why Bucharest surprises everyone who gives it a chance

Bucharest is the European capital nobody plans to visit — and everyone wishes they'd spent more time in. It has the world's heaviest building, nightlife that genuinely rivals Berlin, a food scene where €10 buys a feast, and it's the gateway to Transylvania (Peleș Castle is 90 minutes away by train).

The city earned the nickname "Little Paris" in the 1930s for its Belle Époque architecture and café culture. Then communism reshaped it brutally — Ceaușescu demolished a fifth of the historic centre to build the Palace of Parliament. What survived, and what's grown since 1989, creates a layered city unlike anywhere else.

## Top experiences

### Palace of Parliament — the building that defies belief

The world's heaviest building. 365,000 tonnes. 1,100 rooms. 1 million m³ of marble. 3,500 tonnes of crystal chandeliers. Built by Ceaușescu with forced labour, demolishing 7,000 homes and 27 churches in the process. The scale is genuinely staggering — you can't comprehend it from outside.

**Tip:** the rooftop terrace tour (€15) adds panoramic city views — worth the upgrade.

### Communist history walking tour

A guided walk through Bucharest's communist-era landmarks: Revolution Square (where Ceaușescu gave his final speech in 1989), the former secret police headquarters, the bullet holes still visible on buildings, and the stories of the revolution.

**[Book on GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/bucharest-l215/?partner_id=EKAA80H)** (€15-25, 2-3 hours). This is the experience that transforms Bucharest from "interesting city" to "unforgettable city."

### Old Town food experience

The Old Town (Centrul Vechi) isn't just bars — it's increasingly a food destination. The essential Romanian dishes to try:

**Mici** (grilled minced meat rolls): €3-5 for a plate. Romania's national street food. Best at Obor Market or any terrace with a grill smoking.

**Sarmale** (cabbage rolls): €6-8. Slow-cooked minced pork and rice wrapped in fermented cabbage leaves. **Lacrimi și Sfinți** does the best version in Bucharest.

**Ciorbă de burtă** (tripe soup): €4-5. Sounds challenging, tastes incredible. The acid-cream-garlic combination is addictive.

**Papanași** (fried doughnuts): €4-5. With sour cream and jam. The perfect ending.

**[Food tours](https://www.getyourguide.com/bucharest-l215/?partner_id=EKAA80H)** (€30-50, 3 hours) combine 5-7 tastings with local history — the most delicious way to understand Romania.

### Therme Bucharest

30,000 m² of indoor and outdoor thermal pools, 10 waterslides, a palm-tree relaxation area, and saunas. 30 minutes from the city centre by shuttle bus. **Entry: €25 (weekday), €30 (weekend).**

Perfect before an evening flight — the shuttle connects to the airport area.

### Street art + Uranus neighbourhood

The neighbourhood partially demolished by Ceaușescu (to build the Palace of Parliament) has become a canvas for street artists. Walking through Izvor/Uranus you see giant murals on the buildings that survived — art as memorial. No ticket needed, just walk and look.

### Cișmigiu Gardens + Herăstrău Park

**Cișmigiu** (1847): the oldest public garden in Bucharest. Lake with rowing boats, chess players under the trees, and winding paths. Perfect for a morning coffee stop.

**Herăstrău** (now King Michael I Park): Bucharest's largest park, surrounding a lake. Bike rental (€3/hour), boat rides (€5), and inside it: the **Village Museum** — 272 traditional Romanian houses brought from across the country. €3 entry, 1-2 hours, one of Europe's best open-air museums.

## Nightlife — Bucharest's secret weapon

The Old Town has 120+ bars within walking distance. Prices are absurd (by Western standards): beer €2.50, cocktails €5-7, clubs €5-10 entry.

**Rooftop start:** Linea / Closer to the Moon — 360° city views, cocktails €7-9.

**Ruin bars:** Expirat (underground + terrace), Eden Garden (courtyard), Control (indie).

**Clubs:** Gaia, Nuba, Kristal — open until 6 AM. Smart casual dress code.

**The vibe:** Bucharest nightlife has genuine energy — it's not manufactured for tourists. Romanians go out late (arrive after 11 PM) and stay out until sunrise. Join them.

## Day trips to Transylvania

### Peleș Castle + Sinaia (90 min by train, €8)

Often called the most beautiful castle in Europe. Neo-Renaissance, 160 rooms, Murano glass, hand-carved walnut. €10-20 entry. Closed Mon-Tue. Combine with Pelișor Castle and Sinaia Monastery.

### Brașov (2.5 hours by train, €10)

Medieval city centre, Black Church (largest Gothic church between Vienna and Istanbul), Tâmpa Mountain cable car. Full day trip possible. Add Bran Castle ("Dracula's Castle", 30 min from Brașov) if time allows.

## Getting around

**Metro:** 5 lines, €0.50/trip. Covers major areas. Clean and efficient.

**Bolt/Uber:** €0.40/km — a ride across the entire city costs €3-4. Safer and cheaper than street taxis. [Why Bolt beats taxis](/en/asigurari/travel-scams-europe-2026).

**Airport:** Express Bus 783 to Piața Unirii: €1, 40-50 minutes. Bolt/Uber: €8-12, 25-40 minutes.

**Walking:** the historic centre is compact — Old Town to Revolution Square: 5 minutes. Old Town to Herăstrău: 30 minutes or 2 metro stops.
