Sofia is the capital that reveals itself slowly. It doesn't bombard you with unmissable monuments — it has one immediately recognisable landmark (Alexander Nevsky Cathedral with its gilded domes), and the rest discloses itself at walking pace: 2nd-century Roman ruins inside the central metro station, thermal mineral springs beside an Orthodox cathedral, a forested mountain 30 minutes from the center, and a food scene where a complete meal costs less than a cappuccino in Paris.

Bulgaria adopted the euro on January 1, 2026 — an important detail for trip planning. No currency exchange, cards work directly, and prices are now in euros. But the local economy hasn't suddenly jumped to Western European levels: Sofia remains Europe's most affordable capital, with €35–60 per day covering accommodation, food, and attractions comfortably.

## Alexander Nevsky Cathedral — The Golden Symbol of Slavic Orthodoxy

Built between 1882–1912 in memory of Russian soldiers who fell in the Russo-Turkish War of Liberation of Bulgaria (1877–1878), Alexander Nevsky Cathedral is the largest Orthodox cathedral in the Balkans and one of the largest religious buildings in Eastern Europe — 5,000 people inside, 12 bells, a dome covered in 24-karat gold leaf.

The main nave is **free to enter** — Byzantine mosaics, icons, chandeliers, and an atmosphere of particular solemnity. It is not a museum; it is a functioning cathedral with daily services.

**The Crypt (National Gallery of Medieval Art):** beneath the cathedral, Bulgaria's most important collection of medieval icons — icons from the 13th–19th centuries, some brought from across Bulgaria after Communist-era deconsecration.

| Category | Price |
|---|---|
| Main nave | **Free** |
| Crypt — Icon Gallery | ~€5 (10 BGN) |
| Photography inside the crypt | ~€5 extra |

**Tip:** Visit the nave early morning (8:00–9:30 AM) when candles are lit and morning light comes through the stained glass. The atmosphere is completely different from midday when tour groups fill the space.

**Alexander Nevsky Square** surrounding the cathedral hosts every weekend an **antiques and icons market** — the best place in Sofia for browsing and authentic purchases (old icons, coins, Communist-era objects, ceramics).

## Roman Serdica Ruins — The Ancient Metropolis Under the Metro

Serdica was a major Roman city in the Eastern Empire — so important that Emperor Constantine the Great seriously considered making it the imperial capital before choosing Constantinople. "Serdica is my Rome," he is said to have remarked.

The 2nd–4th century AD Roman ruins are visible in several places in central Sofia:

**Serdica Complex in the Metro (Serdika Station):** The most spectacular — a Roman street, thermal baths, and defensive walls visible through the metro station glass and accessible on a viewing platform. **Completely free, 24/7.** You descend to the metro and are literally above a 2nd-century AD metropolis.

**Serdica Complex at street level (The Largo):** Ruins beside the Sheraton Hotel and Presidential Administration — visible from the street, free access.

**St. George Rotunda:** The best-preserved Roman monument in Sofia — a red-brick rotunda from the 4th century AD, successively converted to a Christian baptistery, an Ottoman mosque (with frescoes whitewashed over), and finally an Orthodox church. Recently restored medieval frescoes are remarkable. **Free entry.**

## St. Sofia Cathedral and the City's History

St. Sofia Cathedral is 14 centuries older than Alexander Nevsky — built in the 6th century AD on the site of earlier Roman and early Christian structures. It gave the city its name (Sofia derives from Hagia Sophia — Holy Wisdom — not from a king or ruler).

Beneath the cathedral: **Necropolis with Roman sarcophagi** from the 4th century — visitable with a separate ticket.

**Cathedral entry:** ~€3. Underground necropolis: ~€3.

## Boyana Church — Bulgaria's Medieval Caravaggio

8 km from central Sofia, in the suburban Boyana district, stands one of the most important rarities of European medieval art: **Boyana Church, UNESCO since 1979.**

The frescoes from 1259 (the second layer of paintings) are considered a direct precursor of the Italian Renaissance — 50 years before Giotto and 150 years before Van Eyck. The anonymous Boyana painter depicted characters with individual expressions, genuine emotions, and three-dimensionality — completely unusual for medieval art of the period. There is a portrait of Bulgarian king Kaloyan and his wife Desislava with a portraiture accuracy that Western Europe would not achieve until much later.

**Prices and logistics — IMPORTANT:**

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| Adult price | ~€5 (10 BGN) |
| Entry system | **Small groups at fixed intervals, 10 minutes** |
| Booking | **Mandatory online** — boyanachurch.org |
| Photography inside | **Prohibited** |
| How to get there | Taxi (~€8), bus 64, or organised tour |

**Critical tip:** Book your time slot at least 1–2 weeks ahead in peak season. Maximum groups of 8–10 people, 10 minutes inside. It sounds brief — it's enough if you know what you're looking for.

## Vitosha Mountain — Forest 30 Minutes from the Center

Vitosha is the mountain dominating Sofia's southern skyline — visible from every street in the center. It is a 27,000-hectare nature park at the city's edge, with conifer forests, glacial lakes, and Cherni Vrah peak at 2,290 meters.

**Why it's special:** you can be in the city center at 10:00 AM, take the gondola up the mountain, and be at the peak by noon. This is literally possible — the Simeonovo gondola takes 20 minutes.

**Gondola prices (Simeonovo):**

| Category | Price |
|---|---|
| Adult return | ~€7–9 |
| Children | Reduced |

**Walking route** from Dragalevtsi district: 3–4 hours return, free, with views over Sofia. Perfect for hiking in summer, skiing in winter at Aleko (cheapest ski slopes in Europe — daily pass ~€20–30).

## Food in Sofia — Europe's Best Value

Bulgaria is arguably the place in Europe where you eat best relative to price. Not because the food is "mediocre but cheap" — but because local Bulgarian food is authentic, fresh, quality ingredients, at prices that make any comparison with Western Europe feel absurd.

**The golden rule:** English-only menu with photos = tourist restaurant, double prices. Handwritten menu in Bulgarian = local mehana, fair prices. Follow the locals.

### The Essential Dishes

**Shopska salad** — Bulgaria's national salad: tomatoes, cucumber, peppers, onion, topped with a mound of grated sirene cheese (similar to feta). **€3–4 at a local restaurant.** No Bulgarian lunch exists without shopska.

**Banitsa** — filo pastry filled with cheese (or spinach, or pumpkin), baked or fried. The national breakfast. **€1.50–2.50** at a banitsa shop. With Bulgarian yoghurt (genuinely the best in the world — *Lactobacillus bulgaricus* is endemic to Bulgaria) = complete breakfast for €3.

**Kebapche** — grilled minced meat sausages, seasoned with cumin. Portion with chips and shopska: **€6–8.**

**Kavarma** — clay pot slow-cooked meat stew. The Bulgarian version of a goulash — complex, aromatic. **€7–10.**

**Tarator** — cold yoghurt soup with cucumber and garlic, perfect in summer. **€2–3.** The most refreshing soup in Europe.

**Bob chorba** — Bulgarian-style bean soup, usually with seasonal vegetables. The daily food of rural Bulgaria. **€3–5.**

**Rakia** — plum or grape brandy, home-produced across Bulgaria. The national drink. One glass at a restaurant: **€1.50–3.** Never refuse when a Bulgarian offers you one — it is a sign of hospitality.

**Mavrud** — Bulgaria's endemic red wine, a grape variety grown exclusively around Plovdiv. Robust, tannic, with notes of plum and leather. **€3–5 a glass** at restaurants, **€5–12 a bottle** at shops.

### Where to Eat Cheaply and Well

**Mehana** (traditional tavern): carved wooden furniture, folk music, authentic food. **€6–12 per meal.** The best ones are in the Lozenets and Studentski Grad neighbourhoods, not near Alexander Nevsky.

**Zhenski Pazar Hall** (Women's Market): covered market with fruit, vegetables, cheeses, and cooked food stalls. **€3–5 lunch.**

**Self-service cafeterias** in the center: Bulgarian food at canteen prices. **€3–5 full meal.**

### Approximate food budget

| Meal type | Cost per person |
|---|---|
| Banitsa + yoghurt (breakfast) | €2–3 |
| Shopska + kebapche at a local mehana | €6–10 |
| Full meal at mid-range restaurant (3 courses + wine) | €12–20 |
| Tourist restaurant near Alexander Nevsky | €15–25 |
| Rakia (one glass) | €1.50–3 |

## Day Trips from Sofia

### Rila Monastery — UNESCO 120 km Away

Bulgaria's largest Orthodox monastic complex and one of the most important religious sites in Eastern Europe. Founded in the 10th century by St. John of Rila (Bulgaria's first saint), expanded and restored across the centuries.

**What not to miss:**
- **Exterior frescoes** of the main church — vivid colours, biblical scenes, angels and demons — painted in the 19th century by Zahari Zograf
- **Raphael's Cross** — an 81cm wooden cross with 140 biblical scenes carved at microscale, over 12 years, by a monk who lost his eyesight in the process
- **Hrelyo's Tower** (14th century) — the only surviving medieval original element
- The cemetery and monastic cells — freely accessible

**Logistics:**

| Option | Price | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Organised tour with guide (from Sofia) | €25–35/person | Full day |
| Rental car | €40–60/day for the car | Flexible |
| Bus + local taxi | ~€15–20/person | Full day |

**Monastery entry:** Free (courtyard and church). Monastery museum: ~€3.

### Plovdiv — Europe's Oldest Continuously Inhabited City

Plovdiv is older than Rome, Athens, and Jerusalem — continuously inhabited for at least 6,000 years. **European Capital of Culture 2019**, with visible effects: renovated museums, the Kapana arts district (with galleries and hipster cafés), and a fully functioning 2nd-century AD Roman amphitheatre that hosts concerts in summer.

**How to get there:** Bus from Sofia: 2 hours, €7–8 return. Train: 2h15, similar price. Full guided day tour from Sofia: €23–35.

**What to visit:**
- **Old Town (Stari Grad):** 18th–19th century Revival houses, cobbled alleys, craft shops
- **Roman Amphitheatre:** functioning, with performances in summer. Entry ~€3
- **Kapana district:** galleries, cafés, cocktail bars, nightlife
- **The seven hills:** complete city panorama

### Bansko — Skiing in the Pirin Mountains

160 km from Sofia, the Bansko ski resort has the lowest ski prices in Europe in season (December–March). Daily lift pass: **€20–35** (vs €50–80+ in Austria). In summer, Bansko is the base for hiking in Pirin National Park (UNESCO).

## Getting to Sofia

### Flights
Sofia Airport (SOF) has direct connections from many European cities. From Bucharest, the flight is just ~50 minutes — one of Europe's shortest international routes. Prices from €30–60 return.

### Airport to city center
Sofia Airport is 10 km from the center:
- **Metro Line M4** directly airport–center: **€1**, 20 minutes — cheapest airport transfer in Europe
- **Taxi OK Supertrans or Yellow Taxi:** €10–15, 20 minutes
- **Avoid unregulated taxis** at the exit — classic tourist scam

### Getting around Sofia
The metro has 4 lines covering the main attractions. **Single ticket: €0.82.** 24h pass: ~€2.50. Trams and buses complete the network.

**Bolt** works excellently in Sofia — cheaper than traditional taxis.

## Insider Tips — What Locals Know

**1. The metro from the airport costs €1.** The cheapest airport transfer in Europe — 4 stops directly to the center. No other European capital has a direct airport-center metro for €1.

**2. Airport taxis are a trap.** Avoid any driver approaching you at the exit. Use Bolt from the app or go to the official OK Supertrans and Yellow Taxi stands (official numbers are displayed). Correct price to center: €8–12.

**3. Bulgaria adopted euros January 1, 2026 — but locals still think in lev.** At markets or older mehanas, you may hear prices in BGN (1 BGN = €0.51, or roughly: 2 BGN = €1). You're not being cheated — it's linguistic inertia.

**4. Rakia is drunk at room temperature, no ice.** Asking for ice in your rakia earns a look from older Bulgarians. Not a strict rule, but it marks you as a tourist.

**5. Bulgarian yoghurt is genuinely different.** Lactobacillus bulgaricus is endemic to Bulgaria — yoghurt produced elsewhere with these cultures tastes different from the local product. Have a plain yoghurt with honey for breakfast and you'll understand why Danone and Yoplait exported the bacteria but couldn't export the taste.

**6. Boyana requires a booking.** Do not show up without a reserved slot — entry is only permitted in small groups at fixed intervals. Official site: boyanachurch.org.

**7. The Sunday antiques market is better than the shops.** Next to Alexander Nevsky, every Sunday, the best antiques, icon art, and Communist-era objects market in the Balkans unfolds. Prices negotiable, authenticity variable — but the atmosphere is worth it even without buying anything.
