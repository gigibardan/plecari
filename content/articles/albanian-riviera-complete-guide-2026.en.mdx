Albania is the secret everyone already knows about but few have actually tested. For five years, every travel article has proclaimed the Albanian Riviera the "next Croatia" or "Greece 20 years ago." They're right — but not for the reasons they keep repeating. Albania isn't interesting just because it's cheap (though it is, dramatically so). It's interesting because 30 minutes by ferry from Corfu, 2 hours from Ioannina, and a day's drive from most of Central Europe, you have a 120 km coastline where the water is as clear as the Cyclades, the cliffs are as dramatic as Croatia, and a dinner of grilled fish on a terrace above the sea costs €8.

The Albanian Riviera is the coastal strip between Vlorë (north) and Saranda (south), along the Ionian Sea. It's a landscape of mountains falling directly into the sea — Llogara Pass at 1,027 m altitude, then switchbacks descending to beaches hidden between white limestone cliffs. It's raw, wild, developing at dizzying speed, and that's exactly what makes it fascinating: in 2026, Albania is in a moment of transition where you can still catch the authentic version before it becomes fully commercialised.

## Ksamil — "The Maldives of Europe"

15 km south of Saranda, three small islands (reachable by swimming or pedal boat) with white sand and unreal turquoise water — the photos don't lie, but they don't tell the whole story either.

**The reality:** Ksamil has experienced a tourism explosion. The island beaches are small and in summer (July–August) they're overcrowded — 10:00–16:00 is chaotic. Beach bars have multiplied, and on some beaches you pay €5–8 for a sunbed (a lot for Albania). Illegal construction along the coast is a visual problem.

**How to make the most of it:** Come in June or September — you get the Ksamil you see in photos. The small pebble beaches between the main ones (accessible via short paths) are much quieter. Mirror Beach and Pasqyra Beach are excellent alternatives to the three islands. Or take a boat from Ksamil to beaches inaccessible by land — €10–15 return.

**Butrint:** 15 km south of Ksamil, the UNESCO archaeological site of Butrint — a small ancient city with a Greek theatre, Byzantine basilica, baptistery with mosaic floor, and Venetian castle, all in a subtropical lagoon landscape. One of the Mediterranean's most underrated archaeological sites. Entry: €10. Guide: recommended (€15–20/group). Allow 2–3 hours.

## Dhermi and Drymades — Riviera Elegance

Dhermi is the Riviera's most upscale point — beach clubs (Folie Marine, Drymades Inn), cocktails at €6–8, and an atmosphere closer to Hvar than rural Albania. The beach is white pebble with turquoise water between dramatic cliff walls covered in vegetation.

**Drymades:** 3 km north of Dhermi, a longer and wilder beach. More relaxed beach bars, camping, and a "hippie meets Riviera" vibe. At the southern end, Drymades Inn has an infinity pool with sea views — sunset cocktail €6.

**Access:** From the main SH8 road (after Llogara Pass), a 7 km secondary road descent — paved but steep. Any car manages, but steady nerves and good brakes help.

## Himarë — The Authentic Centre

Himarë is a small town with a harbour, a pebble beach in the centre, street-side tavernas, and a Byzantine castle on the hill with a Riviera panorama. It's less commercialised than Saranda or Dhermi and much more authentic — locals here also speak Greek (the Greek minority in southern Albania), and the food is a Greek-Albanian fusion.

**Porto Palermo:** 8 km south of Himarë — a bay with an 18th-century Venetian fortress on a headland, crystal water, zero facilities. One of Albania's most beautiful beach-plus-history combinations. Fortress visit: €2.

**Llamani Beach:** 5 km south — white pebble, emerald water, dramatic cliffs. Accessible via a short path from the road. Few people.

## Gjipe — The Wild Beach

Gjipe is the beach for those willing to work a little for the reward. Accessible only via a 30-minute path descending through a spectacular canyon (or by boat from Dhermi/Himarë, €10–15 return), Gjipe is a tongue of white pebble between two 100 m cliff walls with turquoise water and zero construction. A minimalist beach bar operates in summer, but otherwise it's wild.

## Blue Eye (Syri i Kaltër)

25 km from Saranda on the road to Gjirokastër, Blue Eye is a karst spring where underground water emerges from an unknown depth with a force producing 6,000 litres/second. The result: a circular pool of hypnotic blue — deep cobalt in the centre, transitioning through turquoise and emerald to clear at the edges. Temperature: a constant 10°C regardless of season.

The water is COLD. Seriously cold. You can enter but you'll exit in 30 seconds. Worth it for the photograph and the sensation of being in a unique place. From the parking area, a 15-minute walk through an oak forest. Restaurant at the entrance with decent food and good prices (€5–10/dish).

Entry: €1. Best time: 09:00–10:00 (perfect light, few tourists).

## Food — Mediterranean at Impossibly Low Prices

**Tavë kosi:** Lamb baked with yoghurt — Albania's national dish. The meat melts with tenderness, the yoghurt forms a golden crust on top. €4–6/portion.

**Byrek:** Albanian pie — thin pastry sheets with cheese (djathë), spinach, meat, or potato. At any bakery: €0.50–1/piece. The perfect breakfast.

**Fresh fish:** At coastal tavernas, fish is from this morning. Grilled sea bream/sea bass: €5–10. Mussels/clams: €4–6. Prices are literally half of Greece's.

**Where to eat:**

**Haxhi (Saranda, on the promenade):** Fresh fish with Corfu views. €8–12/person.

**Mare Nostrum (Himarë):** Beachfront taverna, excellent seafood. €7–12/person.

**Mulliri (Ksamil):** Fish with feet in the sand. Generous portions, island views. €6–10/person.

**Taverna Antigoni (Gjirokastër):** Traditional Albanian cuisine in an Ottoman house. Legendary tavë kosi. €5–8/person.

## Essential Tips

**Pay cash.** Albania runs predominantly on cash. Currency: Albanian Lek (ALL). 1 EUR ≈ 100 ALL (approximately stable). Many restaurants and hotels accept euros (at a slightly worse rate), but don't rely on cards. ATMs work in Saranda, Himarë, Vlorë — withdraw cash on arrival. ATM fee: ~€2–3/withdrawal.

**You need an eSIM.** Albania is NOT in the EU — European roaming doesn't apply. An [Airalo eSIM](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3) (€5–10 for a week's data) is the simplest solution.

**Passport, not ID card.** While Romanian/EU ID cards are theoretically accepted at Albanian border crossings, a passport is firmly recommended — especially if you want to take the Saranda–Corfu ferry (crossing from non-EU Albania to EU Greece, where ID cards can create complications).

**Gjirokastër deserves a night.** Don't just drive through it on the way to Saranda. The UNESCO old town — with Ottoman stone houses on a hill beneath a massive fortress — is spectacular. The bazaar, the castle (€2), and the view across the Drinos valley. Food is excellent and costs almost nothing (€3–5/dish at Taverna Antigoni).

**Llogara Pass (1,027 m): don't bypass it, cross it.** The road from Vlorë to Dhermi climbs spectacularly through Llogara National Park — 800-year-old pine forests, wild goats on the road, and at the summit, a restaurant with a panorama of the Riviera below. The descent to Dhermi is one of the Mediterranean's most beautiful switchback roads.

**Illegal construction is the reality.** Albania is developing fast, and urban planning regulation lags behind. On the Riviera you'll see concrete skeletons next to paradise beaches. It's part of the experience — it's not polished Croatia, but it's authentic and alive.
