There is a moment when the bus descending from the Montenegrin mountains takes a bend and suddenly the Bay of Kotor appears — and you understand why UNESCO declared it a World Heritage Site.

It isn't just a view. It's something that stops your breath: a deep fjord-like bay with perfectly still water, surrounded by mountains that fall straight into the sea, with a white-grey medieval town caught between cliff and water, with fortress walls climbing the hillside behind it like a miniature Great Wall of China.

Kotor is the best-preserved medieval town on the Adriatic — more authentic than Dubrovnik, less crowded (for now), cheaper and with a completely distinct character. It belonged to Venice for several centuries, then to Byzantium, the Habsburg Empire, Yugoslavia and, since 1992, Montenegro — and every layer of history is visible in its architecture.

The only real risk: cruise ships. In July–August, 5–7 per day discharge 4,000 passengers each into the Old Town between 09:00 and 17:00. If you avoid those hours or travel in shoulder season, Kotor is one of the most spectacular small cities in Europe.

## Kotor at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Montenegro — not in the EU, but uses the euro. Not in Schengen.

**Currency:** Euro (EUR) — no currency exchange needed.

**UNESCO:** Old Town of Kotor and the Bay of Kotor — World Heritage Site since 1979.

**Population:** approximately 13,500 in the Old Town — a small city, but intensely visited.

**Language:** Montenegrin (practically Serbian with some nuances). English is spoken well in the tourist area.

**Visa:** Romanian citizens do not need a visa for Montenegro — entry with a valid ID card (max 90 days).

**Nearest airport:** Tivat (TIV) — 30 minutes from Kotor. Second: Podgorica (TGD) — 1.5 hours. Third: Dubrovnik (DBV, Croatia) — 2 hours.

## How to Get from Romania to Kotor

### Option 1 — Flight OTP → Tivat (Most Direct, Seasonal)

Wizz Air operates OTP → TIV flights in season (May–September), approximately 1h30. Transfer Tivat → Kotor: taxi (~15 EUR, 30 minutes) or local bus (~1.50 EUR, 45 minutes).

### Option 2 — Flight OTP → Dubrovnik + Transfer (Year-Round)

Wizz Air and Ryanair fly year-round to Dubrovnik (DBV). Transfer Dubrovnik → Kotor: bus (~10–15 EUR, 2.5 hours) or car (~1.5 hours). Advantage: Dubrovnik + Kotor combined in the same trip, 2–3 days each.

### Option 3 — By Car

Bucharest → Kotor: ~900 km, 9–10 hours (via Serbia or Bulgaria, Montenegro). A good option for a Balkan road trip — combine with Belgrade, Budva and possibly Albania.

## When to Go — The Cruise Ship Problem

This is the number one piece of information you need to know about Kotor in 2026.

In July–August, between 5 and 7 cruise ships anchor daily in the Bay of Kotor. Each carries 2,000–4,000 passengers. They all come ashore between 09:00 and 17:00 into an Old Town with an area of 0.5 km². The result: a city of 13,500 residents simultaneously receives tens of thousands of visitors.

| Period | Temperature | Cruise ships | Prices | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **May – June** | 20–26°C | Rare | -20% | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Ideal |
| **July – August** | 30–35°C | 5–7/day, 09:00–17:00 | +30% | ⚠️ Avoid daytime (go early morning/evening) |
| **September – October** | 20–27°C | Rare | -15% | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Ideal |
| **November – March** | 8–14°C | None | -40% | ⭐⭐⭐ Quiet, some restaurants closed |

**The essential tip:** if you go in July–August, enter the Old Town before 08:30 or after 17:30. Kotor in the evening and at night — when the ships have left and the day-trippers are at their hotels — is magical.

## What to See in Kotor

### The Old Town (Stari Grad) — The Medieval Labyrinth

Kotor's Old Town is a network of narrow streets, small squares and medieval buildings enclosed by fortress walls on three sides and the sea on the fourth. You can cross from one end to the other in 10–15 minutes — but you can wander for hours in the labyrinth without getting bored.

**Main entrance:** The Sea Gate (Morska vrata, 1555), with a Latin inscription above it. You pass through it every time you enter or exit — if you get lost, walk toward the sound of the sea.

**Arms Square (Trg od Oružja)** — the Old Town's centre, with the Clock Tower (1602). It's the locals' meeting point and has the best café terraces.

**St Luke's Square (Trg Sv. Luke)** — the smaller, quieter square, with a church from 1195. Kotor's cats often gather here.

**Cathedral of Saint Tryphon (Katedrala Sv. Tripuna)** — the Romanesque cathedral from 1166, the town's most important monument. It has survived earthquakes and bombardments over the centuries. Entry: 3 EUR.

**Maritime Museum (Pomorski muzej)** — in the 18th-century Grgurina Palace. Kotor was one of the most important naval centres of Venetian Adriatic. Entry: 5 EUR.

**Cat Museum** — a pleasant curiosity, dedicated to Kotor's famous cats, in the former St Clare's monastery. Entry: 1 EUR.

### The City Walls and San Giovanni Fortress

This is the climb that defines a Kotor visit. The medieval walls begin in the Old Town and ascend the rock face behind the city to San Giovanni Fortress (265 m elevation), with 1,350 steps, a height gain over approximately 1.5 km.

**The view from the top:** the red rooftops of the Old Town, the mirror-still bay, the surrounding mountains and, in the distance, the open sea — one of the most spectacular views on the Adriatic.

**Practical details:**
- Official ticket: 8–10 EUR (2026)
- **Free hack:** leave before 08:00 — the ticket booths aren't open at that hour
- Climb duration: 45–90 minutes depending on fitness and stops
- Essential: water (at least 1.5 litres), trainers or trekking shoes, not sandals
- **Temperature:** in July–August, climb before 8:00 or after 17:00 — on bare rock at midday it's 40°C

**Photography tip:** don't stop at the first viewpoint (Chapel of Our Lady of Remedy, halfway up). Continue to the fortress at the top — the view from there is something else entirely.

## Kayaking on the Bay of Kotor — A Different Perspective on the Fortress

### The Bay of Kotor — The Mediterranean's Deepest Bay

The Bay of Kotor (Boka Kotorska) is often described as "the southernmost fjord in Europe" — technically debatable, but visually perfectly justified. At 28 km long and a maximum depth of 60 metres, surrounded by mountains that descend directly into the water, the bay has a Nordic aesthetic in the southern Mediterranean.

**Driving the bay loop** (100 km, 2–3 hours with stops): the road winds along the water through baroque sleeping villages. Best perspective: from Vrmac Hill, the road climbing above Kotor.

**The Verige ferry** (crossing the bay's narrow point between Kamenari and Lepetane): 4 EUR per car, 10-minute crossing. Saves 50 km if doing the full loop.

### Perast and Our Lady of the Rocks — The Unmissable Visit

Perast is a village of 300 souls on the inner bay, with nearly abandoned Venetian palaces and one of Europe's most spectacular views: two twin islands in the middle of still water — Sveti Đorđe (the natural island, Benedictine monastery) and Gospa od Škrpjela (the artificial island with the Church of Our Lady).

**Gospa od Škrpjela** was built by local sailors from 1452 — every time they returned safely from a voyage, they sank a stone or an old boat near a rock where legend said an icon had appeared. Over five centuries, the island grew to its current dimensions. It holds a church from 1630 with 68 votive paintings by Tripo Kokolja and a collection of objects donated by sailors over the centuries.

**Getting to Perast:** bus from Kotor (~1.50 EUR, 20 minutes) or taxi (~10 EUR).
**Boat to Gospa od Škrpjela:** 5–6 EUR return, departing from Perast's waterfront.

### The Blue Cave and the Yugoslav Submarine Base

Two attractions accessible only by boat, absent from most standard guides:

**Blue Cave** — a sea cave with luminous turquoise water, lit by natural light filtering through the rock, where you can swim in underwater illumination. Accessible by speedboat in ~1 hour from Kotor.

**Yugoslav Submarine Base** — a tunnel cut into the rock, built secretly during the Cold War to shelter Yugoslav submarines. Today you can enter the tunnel by boat — a unique and slightly eerie experience.

Combined tours (Blue Cave + Our Lady of the Rocks + Submarine Base, 3–4 hours): 25–45 EUR per person.

## Food and Drink — Kotor Gastronomy Guide

### What You Must Eat

**Black risotto with cuttlefish (crni rižoto)** — risotto coloured with cuttlefish ink, with an intense sea flavour and striking colour. Kotor's absolute speciality. 10–15 EUR at a restaurant.

**Fresh fish** — at any konoba (local tavern) near the port. Sea bass, dorada and bream on the grill: 15–25 EUR per portion, depending on weight. Always ask the price BEFORE ordering — fish is weighed and charged per kg.

**Škampi buzara** — shrimps cooked in garlic and white wine sauce, an Adriatic speciality. 12–18 EUR.

**Njeguški cheese and pršut** — smoked cheese and ham from Montenegro's mountains, produced around Njeguši. Perfect as an aperitif with local wine. 6–10 EUR a portion.

**Burek** — present in Montenegro too. At local bakeries outside the Old Town: 1–2 EUR a slice.

### Where to Eat

**Inside the Old Town (higher prices, 12–25 EUR/person):**
- **Cesarica** — restaurant with an interior terrace, excellent local food
- **Konoba Scala Santa** — one of the most appreciated local restaurants
- Any konoba on Arms Square (more touristy but convenient)

**On the seafront outside the walls (lower prices, 8–16 EUR/person):**
- Restaurants in the Dobrota neighbourhood, a 5-minute walk, serve the same dishes at 20–30% less
- Taverns in Muo and Prčanj on the bay shore

**Important tip:** inside the Old Town, restaurants on the main thoroughfares charge tourist prices. Turn into a side street and prices drop immediately.

### What to Drink

**Vranac** — Montenegro's reference red wine, made from the local Vranac grape. Robust, fruity, good with meat and fatty fish. 3–6 EUR a glass at a restaurant.

**Krstač** — the local white wine, rarer but excellent with seafood. 3–5 EUR.

**Mulberry rakija (murva)** — the local favourite spirit in Kotor, from mulberries. More delicate than Serbian šljivovica. 2–4 EUR a shot.

**Coffee** — the coffee culture is Adriatic: dense espresso, drunk slowly on a terrace with a water view. 1.50–2.50 EUR.

## Recommended Itinerary — 3 Days in Kotor

### Day 1 — Old Town and City Walls (First Half of the Day)

- **06:30:** quick breakfast + climb the walls (before the ticket booths open)
- **09:00:** descend, Cathedral of Saint Tryphon, Arms Square, Cat Museum
- **Lunch:** konoba in the Old Town or on the seafront outside the walls
- **Afternoon:** walk around the bay, swimming if weather permits (simple pebble beaches 10 minutes away)
- **Evening (after 17:30):** Old Town returns to the locals — the most beautiful hour

### Day 2 — Perast and Our Lady of the Rocks

- **Morning:** bus to Perast (1.50 EUR, 20 minutes)
- **09:30–12:00:** walk along Perast's waterfront, boat to Our Lady of the Rocks (5–6 EUR)
- **Lunch:** Restaurant Conte or another tavern in Perast
- **Return to Kotor:** free afternoon, optional walk along Škaljari street
- **Evening:** black risotto at a konoba in the Old Town

### Day 3 — Boat Tour or Lovćen National Park

- **Option A:** speedboat tour (Blue Cave + Our Lady of the Rocks + Submarine Base, 3–4 hours, 25–45 EUR)
- **Option B:** car excursion to Lovćen National Park and Njegoš Mausoleum (spectacular hairpin bends, 360° panorama, 3 EUR park fee + 8 EUR mausoleum)
- **Option C:** excursion to Budva (30 km, bus 6 EUR) — the old Budvan town and beaches

## Practical Tips for Kotor

**The cats** — don't feed them restaurant food. Locals feed them with special food and are possessive of the tradition. Pet them freely — they're used to people.

**Parking** — in peak season, parking near the Old Town costs 1–2 EUR per hour and fills up fast. Park at the periphery and walk in (the Old Town is 100% pedestrianised).

**Water temperature** in the bay: 22–25°C in July–August, 19–22°C in June and September. Pebble beaches immediately nearby (Dobrota, Muo) — not fine sand. Sand beaches: Budva (30 km).

**Fish price guide:** always ask the price per kg before ordering. A 400g dorada at 28 EUR/kg means 11.20 EUR — reasonable. At some tourist restaurants, the same fish can be listed at 45–50 EUR/kg.

**Montenegro is not in the EU** — the EHIC is not valid. You need travel insurance.

## Kotor vs. Dubrovnik — Which Do You Choose?

| Criterion | Kotor | Dubrovnik |
|---|---|---|
| **Authenticity** | Less touristified | Extremely touristified |
| **Prices** | 30–40% cheaper | Expensive (Croatia is in EU) |
| **Crowding** | High but manageable | Overwhelming in peak season |
| **Scenery** | Unique — bay + mountains | Superb — open sea |
| **Old Town** | Authentic Venetian | Perfectly restored Venetian |
| **Activities** | Bay, kayak, submarines, Lovćen | Walls, Elaphite islands, Cetinje |
| **Best for** | Those wanting authenticity + value | Those wanting Instagram-perfect |

The ideal combination: 2–3 days Kotor + 2–3 days Dubrovnik, connected by bus (~10–15 EUR, 2–2.5 hours).
