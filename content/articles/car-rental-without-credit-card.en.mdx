You land after a 3-hour flight, collect your bags, and walk to the car rental desk. The agent asks for your credit card. You hand over your Revolut card. She shakes her head. "We require a credit card, I'm afraid."

This scenario plays out daily in airports across Europe. The fix is straightforward — but it has to happen before you leave, not at the counter.

---

## Why rental companies require credit cards: the actual reason

A credit card allows the rental company to perform a **pre-authorisation** (hold) rather than an actual charge. The deposit — typically €200–2,000 depending on the car category and your insurance level — is blocked on the card without being withdrawn from your account. When you return the car undamaged and with a full tank, the hold is released within 3–14 days.

**The debit card problem:**

| Aspect | Credit card | Debit card |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit mechanism | Blocked (not withdrawn) | Withdrawn immediately |
| Impact on available funds | None if released | Reduces bank balance |
| Recovery of additional charges | Company can charge card directly | Legally and practically complex |
| Acceptance at major chains | ✅ Almost universal | ❌ Rarely, with conditions |
| Acceptance at local operators | ✅ Yes | ✅ Frequently |

The debit card issue is legal and operational. The money disappears from your account the moment the hold is placed, and recovering additional amounts (for damage discovered later) is significantly more complicated than with a credit card.

## Major rental chain policies in 2026

### What each chain actually does

**Sixt:** accepts debit Visa/Mastercard at select locations in a few countries, with conditions: minimum age 25, advance booking (not same-day), additional security deposit of €200–500, and typically not available during peak season. Not reliable as a primary strategy.

**Enterprise / National:** operates a specific debit card rental programme in the UK (requires proof of address and sometimes a credit check). In continental Europe: extremely rare and inconsistent.

**Hertz / Avis / Budget / Europcar:** credit card required at the vast majority of European locations. Isolated exceptions at a few airports in Spain and Italy with a mandatory cash deposit of €500–1,000 — must be confirmed in writing before arrival, not assumed.

**Goldcar, OK Mobility, Record Go** (mid-tier Mediterranean operators): more flexible; debit accepted at some locations with higher deposit requirements. Worth checking per-location.

**The rule:** never assume. Even if a chain has a debit-acceptance policy in theory, implementation varies by country, by airport, by season, and by individual agent. **Get written confirmation before booking.**

## Digital bank cards: Revolut, Wise, N26

The most-asked question — and the answer requires precision.

### Revolut Standard / Plus
Virtual or physical **debit card**. Issued by Revolut Bank, carrying Visa or Mastercard branding. Major chains treat it identically to any other debit card — declined. Local companies may accept it.

### Revolut Metal (premium subscription, ~€13/month)
Physical embossed Mastercard. Marginally better odds than Revolut Standard at some companies, but still classified as a **debit card** by rental company systems. Not a guaranteed solution.

### Revolut Credit Card
A true credit product available in some European markets. Works exactly like any credit card at rental companies. Check whether this product is available in your country and whether you have applied for it — it looks similar to the debit card in the app but is a completely different product type.

### Wise Visa Debit
Debit card. Same treatment as any debit — declined at major chains, potentially accepted at flexible local operators.

### N26 Mastercard
Debit Mastercard. Same behaviour as Wise.

**Practical conclusion:** if you have a Revolut Credit Card or a traditional bank credit card (any major bank), you are covered. If you have only digital debit cards, you need an alternative strategy. The Revolut Credit Card is the best path if you want to stay within the digital banking ecosystem.

## Real alternatives if you have no credit card

### Alternative 1: Cash deposit at local companies

Many local operators (particularly in Greece, Turkey, Bulgaria, and Romania) accept a cash security deposit of **€300–1,500** instead of a card hold. The cash is physically retained for the rental duration and returned at vehicle return.

**How to arrange this:**
1. Identify local operators through GetRentacar or Google Maps search at your destination
2. Contact them before booking — ask explicitly if cash deposit is accepted in lieu of credit card
3. Obtain written confirmation (email or messaging app)
4. Bring the exact cash amount on collection day; get a receipt

**Risk:** not all local companies offer this. Without written advance confirmation, do not rely on it.

### Alternative 2: Second driver's credit card

If someone travelling with you has a credit card, the deposit can be placed on their card — even if the booking is in your name.

**At the counter:**
- Both people must be physically present
- The credit card holder is added as an additional driver on the contract
- Additional driver fee: €3–10/day (sometimes waived)
- The credit card holder assumes responsibility for the deposit

This is the cleanest solution when travelling as a couple or group where at least one person has a credit card.

### Alternative 3: Third-party excess waiver — eliminates the deposit

Purchasing a full excess waiver from a specialist provider before your rental (insurance4carhire.com, RentalCover.com, or similar) at €3–8/day typically:
- Reduces the rental company's excess to zero
- In many cases, causes the company to reduce or eliminate the pre-authorisation hold entirely
- Works regardless of whether your main card is credit or debit

This approach works best confirmed in advance — contact the rental company and confirm that with a zero-excess policy in hand, they will reduce or remove the deposit requirement.

### Alternative 4: Urban car sharing (city destinations only)

In major European cities, car sharing services work with debit cards and apps — no large security deposit:

| Service | Cities | Accepted payment | Approx. cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| ShareNow | Berlin, Hamburg, Munich, Milan, Vienna, Brussels | Debit/credit | €0.29/min |
| Zity | Madrid, Paris | Debit/credit | €0.23/min |
| Citybee | Vilnius, Riga, Tallinn, Warsaw | Debit/credit | €0.19/min |
| GreenGo | Bucharest | Debit/prepaid | €35/day |
| Sixtshare | Selected German cities | Debit/credit | €0.25/min |

**Limitation:** car sharing is designed for urban use. You cannot take a ShareNow vehicle to the beach 80 km away. For city exploration, it is an excellent credit-card-free option. For beach holidays or touring, it does not work.

### Alternative 5: Transfer + Bolt/Uber

For beach resorts, island destinations, or ski resorts where you primarily travel from accommodation to the beach and back: calculate whether you actually need a rental car. Airport transfer (€40–80) + daily Bolt/Uber trips can cost less than a week of car rental when you factor in the complications of not having a credit card.

## Destinations with the most flexible local markets

If you prioritise payment flexibility, these markets consistently have local operators who work with debit or cash:

**Greece — islands:** Crete, Rhodes, Corfu, Zakynthos have hundreds of local operators charging €20–40/day for small cars, frequently accepting debit Visa/Mastercard or cash deposits of €200–400. Search Google Maps for "rent a car [island name]" — local operators rarely appear prominently on aggregator platforms. See: [car rental in Greece 2026](/en/inchirieri-auto/car-rental-greece-2026).

**Turkey — coast and resorts:** Antalya, Bodrum, Marmaris have intensely competitive local markets with prices of €15–30/day. Debit and sometimes cash routinely accepted. If you are visiting Antalya: [Antalya holiday guide 2026](/en/cazari/antalya-all-inclusive-guide-2026) includes transport context.

**Bulgaria — Black Sea coast:** Sunny Beach, Varna, Nessebar offer highly flexible local operators at €15–25/day, debit or cash accepted widely.

**Romania:** the most permissive market. Almost all local companies in Bucharest, Cluj, Constanța and Brașov accept debit cards, bank transfers, or cash. Prices €25–50/day with full insurance included. See: [Romania road trip guide](/en/inchirieri-auto/romania-road-trip-transylvania).

**The trade-off with local companies:** the contract may be less standardised, the vehicle potentially older, and dispute resolution more complex than with an international chain. Document the vehicle thoroughly at collection.

## What to inspect at collection (local companies especially)

When renting from a local company to avoid the credit card requirement, be more thorough with documentation than you would be with a major chain.

**Photograph before driving away:**
- All four body panels (front, rear, both sides)
- All four wheels and alloys
- Interior — seats, steering wheel, floor, dashboard
- Fuel gauge display (screenshot from the instrument panel)
- Email all photos to yourself immediately — the timestamp is your evidence

**Review in the contract:**
- What the included CDW actually covers (bodywork? glass? tyres? theft?)
- The excess amount you would pay in case of damage (can be €0 or €2,000)
- Fuel policy — full-to-full is correct; anything else requires careful reading
- Mileage: unlimited or capped (and the per-kilometre charge above the cap)
- Late return penalties
