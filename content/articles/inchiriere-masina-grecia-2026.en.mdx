On the large Greek islands, a rental car transforms a holiday entirely. Instead of being tied to your resort or the limited local bus network, you reach hidden beaches, mountain villages, and attractions that most tourists never see. On Crete or Rhodes, this is not a luxury — it is the difference between a standard beach holiday and a genuinely interesting one. Here is everything you need to know for 2026.

## Documents required at the counter

EU citizens need three things:

**Valid EU driving licence (category B).** Your national licence is sufficient — no international driving permit needed. Greece is in the EU and EU licences are mutually recognised.

**Passport or national ID card.** Most rental companies prefer a passport. Some specifically require it rather than an ID card, especially for international chains. Bring both to be safe.

**Credit card in the driver's name.** The company will place a hold (pre-authorisation) on your card for the security deposit — typically €200–1,500 depending on the car category and the excess level on your insurance. This is not charged; it is blocked and released when you return the car in good condition.

Bring the booking confirmation (email or print) and arrive with time to inspect the car before driving away.

## What changed in 2026 — new traffic rules

Greece introduced significant changes to its road traffic laws effective January 2026. The most important for drivers:

**Urban speed limit reduced to 30 km/h.** In most town and village zones, residential areas, and pedestrian-heavy areas, the limit is now 30 km/h rather than 50 km/h. This applies to the majority of built-up areas across the islands. Enforcement is stricter and fines are higher than before.

**Other rules in force:**
- Outside built-up areas: 90 km/h on national roads, 130 km/h on motorways
- Seat belts mandatory on all seats
- Mobile phone use prohibited without hands-free
- Blood alcohol limit: 0.5‰ (0.2‰ for drivers with less than 2 years of licence)
- Children under 12 must use an appropriate child seat

The 30 km/h zones are the change most likely to affect tourists — they are not always clearly signposted at the entry to every village. When in doubt on a narrow island road passing through a settlement, slow to 30.

## Island-by-island: where a car is essential, useful, or unnecessary

**Crete — essential.** Greece's largest island at 250 km long. Without a car, you are effectively confined to your resort and its immediate area. The best beaches — Elafonisi (pink sand, turquoise water), Balos (lagoon accessible only by boat or 4WD road), Preveli (palm-lined gorge), and Falassarna — are only reachable by car or expensive organised tours. The Samaria Gorge hike requires driving to the trailhead. Rent a car.

**Rhodes — very useful.** The medieval old town of Rhodes City and Lindos can be reached by bus, but the western and southern beaches, the Valley of the Butterflies, and the inland villages require a car. A rental for 3–4 days of your week gives you access to a completely different island.

**Corfu — useful with caution.** Corfu's roads are narrow and heavily used in summer. A small car (not an SUV) is genuinely easier to park and manoeuvre. The north of the island — Kassiopi, Sidari, Paleokastritsa — is most worth exploring by car. The old town of Corfu City is best on foot.

**Zakynthos — recommended.** The famous Navagio Beach (Shipwreck Beach) is only accessible by boat, but the rest of the island's best spots (Keri Lake, Marathonisi Island viewpoint, the blue caves coast road) require a car. Buses are infrequent.

**Lefkada — recommended.** Connected to the mainland by a bridge, so you can drive your own car. The beaches on the west coast (Porto Katsiki, Egremni) are spectacular but only reachable by car on steep, winding roads.

**Thassos — optional.** Small enough to be circled by scooter. A car helps but is not essential if you are based near the main towns. Accessible by ferry from Kavala (mainland), so you can bring your own car.

**Mykonos — unnecessary.** Parking in Mykonos Town is effectively impossible in summer. ATV and scooter rentals are the island's transport system. The island is small enough that everything is reachable by the local bus or a short ATV ride.

**Santorini — unnecessary.** The caldera villages (Oia, Fira, Imerovigli) are connected by foot paths along the cliff edge. Cars are used by locals but tourists find parking close to impossible. The local bus (KTEL) is adequate and cheap.

## Prices: what to expect in 2026

Prices vary significantly by season, island, and category:

| Category | May–June / September | July–August |
|---|---|---|
| Small (Hyundai i10, Fiat 500) | €25–40/day | €40–70/day |
| Compact (VW Golf, Toyota Corolla) | €35–55/day | €55–90/day |
| Small SUV | €50–80/day | €80–130/day |
| Automatic (add to above) | +€15–25/day | +€15–25/day |

**Airport surcharge:** Collecting from the airport adds 10–15% to the total. If you are willing to take a short taxi to a city-centre location, you save this fee.

**Young driver surcharge (under 25):** €5–15/day extra on top of the base rate.

**Book 4–6 weeks ahead** for the best prices. On popular islands in July–August, cars genuinely sell out — not as a sales tactic but as a physical reality. Walk-up prices at island airports in peak season are consistently 40–60% higher than pre-booked rates.

## The CDW excess problem — the most expensive "included" feature

Almost every rental includes CDW (Collision Damage Waiver) in the base price. This sounds like full protection but it is not — CDW comes with an **excess**: the amount you personally pay if the car is damaged, regardless of fault. This excess is pre-authorised (blocked) on your credit card at collection.

| Rental company insurance level | Your excess |
|---|---|
| Basic CDW (standard included) | €500–1,500 |
| Super CDW (counter upsell, €15–25/day) | €0 |
| Third-party excess waiver (pre-purchased online) | €0 |

The counter Super CDW at €15–25/day for a week costs €105–175 extra. A third-party excess waiver from a specialist (insurance4carhire.com, RentalCover, iCarhireinsurance) covers the same thing for €5–8/day, or €35–56 for the week. Always buy the third-party version before arriving — the counter staff will push their product but you are not obligated to buy it.

## At collection: the photograph routine

This single habit prevents the majority of damage disputes:

1. Before touching the car, walk around it and photograph every panel — all four sides, the roof, the bonnet, the boot, and both wing mirrors
2. Photograph the interior (any stains on seats, condition of dashboard)
3. Photograph the fuel gauge showing it is full
4. Email all photos to yourself immediately — the timestamp is your legal proof
5. Have the agent note any existing damage on the contract before you sign

On Greek islands in summer, rental cars are used intensively. Pre-existing scratches and scrapes are common. Without your own timestamped photos, you cannot prove which damage was there before you drove away.

## Parking on Greek islands — the colour code

Greek road markings determine parking rules:

- **White markings:** free parking (sometimes with a time limit — check signs)
- **Blue markings:** paid parking (pay at a nearby meter or kiosk)
- **Yellow markings:** no parking at any time

In village centres and old towns, parking is often scarce and the streets are too narrow for anything larger than a small hatchback. Plan to park at the edge of town and walk in — most Greek village centres are pedestrianised or effectively so.

## Fuel policy — always choose full-to-full

You will encounter two main policies:

**Full-to-full:** You collect the car with a full tank and return it full. You pay only for what you used, at petrol station prices. This is the correct and transparent option — always choose it.

**Pre-purchase fuel:** You pay upfront for a full tank at the company's price (higher than the petrol station), and return the car empty. You never manage to return it exactly empty, so you always lose paid fuel. Avoid this.

Find a petrol station within 2–3 km of the drop-off point as you navigate back to return the car. Returning with a full tank from a proper petrol station rather than the company's surcharge is always cheaper.

## Which islands have the best local rental companies

On larger islands, local Greek companies often offer significantly lower prices than the international chains, with comparable car quality:

**Crete:** Look for companies based in Heraklion, Chania, or Rethymno city centres — not the airport. Local companies like Auto Motor Sport, Alegria, or Simply Crete regularly offer rates 20–30% below Hertz or Europcar for the same category.

**Rhodes:** The city centre has dozens of local operators along Ierou Lohou street. Negotiate in low season (May, October) — prices are genuinely flexible.

**Corfu:** Airport road companies are mostly international chains. Town-based companies around Corfu Town (Kerkyra) are cheaper.

Whichever company you use — local or international — the documentation rules and the photograph routine remain exactly the same.

## Getting to Greece and travel logistics

Flights from Bucharest to Heraklion (Crete), Rhodes, or Corfu run throughout summer — direct charters from May to October. For the best fares, read [how to find cheap flights](/en/zboruri/cum-gasesti-bilete-avion-ieftine).

Greece is in the EU — EU roaming applies, no eSIM needed. Your Romanian mobile plan works without extra charges.

Travel insurance is not legally mandatory for Greece, but highly recommended. The European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) covers emergency treatment at public hospitals, but not repatriation, private clinics, or trip cancellation.
