There are few cities in Europe that move you before you understand why. Sarajevo is one of them.

It isn't the most beautiful city on the continent — not the most organised, not the cleanest. But it has something no other European city has: a historical and cultural density you feel physically, at every street corner. Here, within a few square kilometres, an Ottoman mosque from 1531, a Serbian Orthodox cathedral, a Catholic cathedral, a Sephardic synagogue and a Jesuit chapel coexist — all within 100 metres of each other. That is why it is called "the Jerusalem of Europe."

Here, in 1914, on the Latin Bridge over the Miljacka River, the Bosnian student Gavrilo Princip fired two shots that killed Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary and his wife Sofia. The assassination triggered the diplomatic crisis that led to the First World War — 20 million dead.

And here, between 1992 and 1996, the longest siege of a city in modern history took place — 1,425 days, longer than the Siege of Leningrad in World War II.

Sarajevo isn't just a city break — it's an experience. And it is, additionally, Europe's most affordable capital: you can live well on 25–50 EUR per day.

## Sarajevo at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Bosnia and Herzegovina (BiH) — not in the EU, not in Schengen.

**Population:** approximately 275,000 in the municipality (440,000 in the metropolitan area).

**Currency:** Convertible Mark (BAM or KM). **1 EUR = 1.96 KM** — practically 2 KM to 1 EUR. Bosnia does not use the euro.

**Language:** Bosnian, Serbian and Croatian (practically the same language, three political names). English is spoken well in the tourist area by younger generations.

**Religion:** 51% Muslim (Bosniaks), 31% Orthodox (Serbs), 15% Catholic (Croats), 3% other. This diversity is visible and audible — the call to prayer, church bells and city sounds mix in the same space.

**Visa:** EU citizens do not need a visa for BiH — entry with a valid ID card (max 90 days).

**Airport:** Sarajevo International Airport (SJJ) — 12 km from the centre.

**Average budget:** 35–65 EUR/day (mid-range) — **Europe's most affordable capital**.

## The Convertible Mark — Quick Guide

Bosnia doesn't use the euro. You'll pay in **KM (Convertible Mark)**.

**Fixed rate:** 1 EUR = 1.956 KM (fixed exchange rate, doesn't fluctuate).

**Quick calculation:**
- 5 KM ≈ 2.55 EUR
- 10 KM ≈ 5.10 EUR
- 50 KM ≈ 25.50 EUR

**Where to exchange:** BBI Bank or Raiffeisen ATMs (best rate, low commission). **Avoid** Euronet (fixed fee ~5 EUR per withdrawal) and airport exchange.

Many places accept cards — hotels, larger restaurants, shops. But the **Tunnel of Hope, burek bakeries, markets and taxis** are cash-only in KM.

## What to See in Sarajevo

### Baščaršija — The Ottoman Bazaar, Heart of the City

Baščaršija (pronounced "Bash-char-shee-ya") is Sarajevo's old Ottoman quarter, built in 1462, with narrow cobblestone streets, craftsmen at work in small shops, džezva cafés and the smell of Bosnian coffee mixing with grilled meat.

**Sebilj Fountain** — Sarajevo's symbol, an octagonal wooden and metal fountain at the centre of Baščaršija. The pigeons surrounding it have their own legend: whoever drinks from it will inevitably return to Sarajevo.

**Kazandžiluk Street (Coppersmiths' Street)** — a narrow lane specialising in hand-beaten copper objects: džezve (coffee pots), decorative plates, glasses. The craft is alive — you can watch artisans at work. A coffee pot: 10–30 EUR depending on size and engraving quality.

**Gazi Husrev-beg Mosque** — the most important mosque in Bosnia, built in 1531, with a courtyard fountain. Free entry for visitors (outside prayer times). Remove shoes at the entrance; women will be offered a headscarf.

**Clock Tower (Sahat Kula)** — the only functional lunar clock in the world, showing time based on the sun's position rather than a time zone. Built in 1667.

### Latin Bridge — Where the First World War Began

The Latin Bridge (Latinska ćuprija) is an elegant Ottoman bridge over the Miljacka River. On 28 June 1914, precisely on this bridge, the Bosnian student Gavrilo Princip fired two shots that killed Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary and his wife Sofia. The assassination triggered the diplomatic crisis that led to the First World War — 20 million dead.

Today, a small museum at the end of the bridge documents the event. Entry: free. The floor has markings showing the exact spots where Princip stood and where the Archduke's car stopped.

**Local perspective:** in Sarajevo, Princip is viewed ambiguously — a liberation hero for some, an assassin who brought catastrophe for others. Local guides explain both perspectives with remarkable honesty.

## Walking Tour of Sarajevo — East Meets West in 2.5 Hours

### The Austro-Hungarian Zone — When the Empire Modernised Sarajevo

A few minutes' walk from Baščaršija, you cross an invisible line and enter Austro-Hungarian Sarajevo — a completely different architectural zone, with neoclassical and neo-Moorish buildings constructed after 1878, when the Habsburg Empire administered Bosnia.

**Sarajevo City Hall (Vijećnica)** — a neo-Moorish masterpiece built in 1894–96, with a striped yellow and red façade. It was the National Library of Bosnia before being deliberately burned in 1992 (2 million books and manuscripts destroyed). Restored and reopened in 2014 — free entry, the interior with its arcade galleries is impressive.

**Eternal Flame (Vječna Vatra)** — a monument at the main boulevard intersection, the flame lit in 1946 in memory of World War II victims. Never extinguished.

**Sacré-Cœur Cathedral** (Catholic, 1889) and the **Orthodox Cathedral** (1868) — less than 200 metres from each other, in the same street as the Gazi Husrev-beg Mosque and the Sephardic Synagogue. No other European city has this density of different religious buildings.

### The Tunnel of Hope (Tunel Spasa) — The Essential Experience

The Tunnel of Hope was the only connection between besieged Sarajevo and the outside world during the Bosnian War. Hand-dug by soldiers and civilians in 1993, under the UN-controlled international airport runway, the 800 metres of tunnel allowed the passage of food, weapons and people for almost 3 years of siege.

Today it's a museum: you visit the Kolar family house (the tunnel entrance was in their basement), a preserved section of the original tunnel, and a video exhibition about the siege. <cite index="62-1">The ticket costs ~8 EUR — arguably the most meaningful money you'll spend in Sarajevo.</cite>

**Getting there:** taxi/Bolt (~8–10 EUR, 20 min) or bus 32 (~1 EUR, 40 min). Located near the airport, on the city's outskirts.

**Important:** the museum is cash-only in KM. Bring banknotes.

## The Siege Tour with a Survivor Guide — The Experience That Changes Your Perspective

### Trebević — The Abandoned Olympic Bobsled Track

Mount Trebević hosted the bobsled run at the 1984 Winter Olympics. Today, the track is abandoned, covered in graffiti and vegetation — a place with a singular atmosphere, between sporting triumph and war ruin (the mountain was occupied by Serbian forces during the siege, the track used as a military position).

The abandoned Olympic bobsled track consistently appears on lists of the world's most beautiful abandoned places.

**Getting there:** cable car from the city centre (6 EUR return) or by car/taxi. Walking: 2–3 hours.

### Gallery 11/07/95 — Srebrenica

<cite index="62-1">Gallery 11/07/95 is a modern space dedicated to the memory of the Srebrenica genocide, using photography and video to document the events of July 1995.</cite> It's an emotionally difficult but necessary and powerful visit. Entry: ~5 EUR.

### War Childhood Museum

An internationally award-winning museum presenting the Bosnian War through the eyes of the children who lived it — each object displayed (a toy, a notebook, a dress) comes with the personal story of the child who owned it. <cite index="62-1">It is delicate, moving and profoundly impactful.</cite> Entry: ~5–6 EUR.

## Food and Drink — Sarajevo Gastronomy Guide

### What You Must Eat

**Ćevapi** — grilled minced beef sausages served in somun flatbread with onion and kajmak or pavlaka (sour cream). Sarajevo claims to have the best ćevapi in the Balkans — and it's probably right. A portion of 10 pieces with somun: 5–7 EUR. Best at the specialist restaurants in Baščaršija, not fast food.

**Burek** — filo pastry pie with meat (burek), cheese (sirnica) or spinach (zeljanica), baked in large trays and sold by the spiral. At a local bakery (buregdžinica): 1.50–2.50 EUR a portion. Classic Sarajevo breakfast with milk (mlijeko).

**Klepe** — dumplings filled with meat, boiled in water, served with sour cream and garlic. 6–10 EUR at a traditional restaurant.

**Bosanski lonac** — slow-cooked beef and vegetable stew, Bosnia's comfort food. 7–12 EUR.

**Bosnian baklava** — different from Turkish or Greek: less sweet, with walnuts or pumpkin seeds, moderately syruped. 0.80–1.50 EUR a piece.

### Bosnian Coffee — The Obligatory Ritual

Bosnian coffee is served in a small copper džezva, with water and rahat (Turkish delight). It's not espresso, not filter — it's a unique preparation, brewed directly in the pot. The ritual: pour the coffee from the džezva into a fildžan (small handleless cup), hold the rahat in your mouth and sip the slightly bitter coffee over it.

<cite index="63-1">Price: 0.80–1.50 EUR — among the cheapest coffees in Europe.</cite> Don't rush it — in Sarajevo, coffee is a 30–45 minute social pause, not a quick drink.

### Where to Eat

**Budget (4–10 EUR/meal):**
- Any **buregdžinica** (burek bakery) — look for the sign
- **Aščinica** — traditional restaurants with pot-cooked food, no written menu, whatever was cooked today
- **Restoran Željo** (Baščaršija) — Sarajevo's most famous ćevapi, queue at lunchtime

**Mid-range (10–20 EUR/person):**
- **Restoran Inat Kuća** — "The House of Spite", in the building moved stone by stone as a protest against the construction of City Hall in 1895. Good food, great story.
- **Meze** — modern restaurant with interpretations of traditional Bosnian cuisine
- Any **kafana** with wood fires in Baščaršija

### What to Drink

**Sarajevsko pivo** — the local beer, brewed in Sarajevo since 1864. Blonde, slightly bitter, perfect with ćevapi. At a bar: 2–3 EUR a pint.

**Pear rakija (kruška)** — the preferred local spirit. 1.50–2.50 EUR a shot.

**Bosnian coffee** — already described. Obligatory.

## Practical Tips for Sarajevo

**Tourist tax** — there's a city tax of ~1 EUR per night. Not large, but cash-only at many small hotels.

**Landmines** — **inside the city there is no risk**. But if hiking in the mountains around Sarajevo (Bjelašnica, Trebević, Igman), stay strictly on marked paths. Mine maps are available at tourist information centres.

**Tipping** — not obligatory, but rounding up the bill or 5–10% at restaurants is appreciated. At small cafés, not expected.

**Religious respect** — when visiting the mosque, remove shoes and dress modestly (covered shoulders, trousers/skirt below the knee). Headscarf available at the entrance for women.
