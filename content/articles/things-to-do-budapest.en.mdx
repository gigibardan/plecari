## Why Budapest is everyone's favourite surprise

Budapest doesn't try to be Paris. It doesn't compete with Rome. What it does is combine thermal bath culture (120+ natural hot springs beneath the city), the most photogenic riverfront in Europe (the Danube between Buda and Pest at night), a ruin bar scene that invented its own genre of nightlife, and prices that make you feel like you've hacked travel.

Every person who visits Budapest says the same thing: "I didn't expect it to be THIS good."

## Thermal baths — Budapest's signature

Budapest sits on a geological fault with 120+ natural hot springs. Bathing culture here goes back to Roman times and peaked under Ottoman rule (500 years ago). Today, the thermal baths are the city's defining experience.

### Széchenyi (the essential)

The largest medicinal bath in Europe. 18 pools (3 outdoor, 15 indoor), temperatures from 26°C to 38°C, slides, and a chess-playing-in-the-pool tradition that's uniquely Hungarian.

**Entry:** €25 (locker), €28 (cabin). Weekend: €2-3 more. [Book skip-the-line on GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/budapest-l29/?partner_id=EKAA80H) to avoid the 30-minute ticket queue.

**Tips:** go in the afternoon (locals are there in the morning, tourists arrive after lunch). Bring your own towel (rental €5). The outdoor pools are the Instagram shots — find them through the main building, past the indoor thermal pools.

### Rudas (the atmospheric one)

Ottoman-era bath (1550) with octagonal pool under a domed ceiling pierced with star-shaped skylights. The rooftop pool (open weekends and evenings) has the best panoramic view of any bath — the Danube, Parliament, and Chain Bridge all visible from 40°C water.

**Entry:** €22-28. Evening sessions (Fri-Sat) have a party atmosphere.

### Gellért (the elegant one)

Art Nouveau interior (1918) with ornate mosaics, marble columns, and a wave pool. The most beautiful bath architecturally but also the most expensive (€28-32) and popular with tour groups.

## Buda — the castle side

### Fisherman's Bastion

Neo-Gothic terrace with the most photographed view of Budapest: the Parliament building across the Danube. Free access to the lower terrace (the best views). Upper tower: €4. Go at sunrise for empty terraces and golden light.

### Matthias Church

800-year-old church with a psychedelic tiled roof (Zsolnay ceramic tiles, a Hungarian speciality). €8 entry. The interior is surprisingly colourful — geometric patterns painted on every surface.

### Buda Castle + National Gallery

The castle complex houses the Hungarian National Gallery (€12) and the Budapest History Museum (€8). The funicular from the Chain Bridge to the castle (€6 return) is scenic but the walk up is free and only 15 minutes.

## Pest — the energetic side

### Parliament Building

Europe's third-largest parliament building. The interior is as spectacular as the exterior — 40 kg of gold covering the ceiling and walls. **Guided tour: €12** (EU citizens €6). Book at [GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/budapest-l29/?partner_id=EKAA80H) or the official site — tours in English run multiple times daily but fill up.

### Danube promenade + night cruise

Walk along the Pest embankment at dusk and watch Buda's castle, Fisherman's Bastion, and Gellért Hill illuminate in sequence. Then take a [1-hour evening cruise](https://www.getyourguide.com/budapest-l29/danube-cruise-t69093/?partner_id=EKAA80H) (€10-20, some include a drink) — the illuminated Parliament from the water is unforgettable.

### Shoes on the Danube Bank

Memorial to Jewish victims shot into the Danube during WWII. 60 pairs of iron shoes along the embankment. Powerful, free, and unmissable.

### Great Market Hall (Nagyvásárcsarnok)

Budapest's largest market: ground floor is fresh produce, paprika, salami, lángos (fried dough with sour cream and cheese, €3-4). Upper floor: souvenirs and food stalls. Free entry. Open Mon-Sat.

## Ruin bars — Budapest's invention

In the early 2000s, entrepreneurs opened bars in abandoned buildings in the Jewish Quarter. The result: a nightlife genre that doesn't exist anywhere else. Mismatched furniture, street art walls, bathtubs as seats, and cheap drinks.

**Szimpla Kert** — the original and still the best. Multi-room maze of weirdness. Beer €2.50-3.50, cocktails €5-8. Gets crowded after 10 PM; go at 7-8 PM for a more relaxed experience. Sunday morning: farmer's market inside the bar.

**Instant-Fogas** — the biggest ruin bar complex. 26 rooms across two buildings. More club-oriented than Szimpla.

**Élesztő** — craft beer focus (20+ taps, mostly Hungarian breweries). Quieter, more local, excellent beer quality.

## Where to eat

**Goulash** — the national soup (not stew — in Hungary it's a soup). Best at: **Bors GasztroBar** (€4-5, modern twist), **Kéhli Vendéglő** (traditional, €6-8), or any étkezde (workers' canteen, €3-5).

**Lángos** — fried dough with sour cream and cheese. €3-4 from market stalls or street vendors. The Central Market Hall version is classic.

**Chimney cake (kürtőskalács)** — spiral pastry rolled in sugar/cinnamon/walnut. €3-4. Originally from Transylvania.

**Full restaurant meal:** €10-15 with wine at a local vendéglő. **Hungarikum Bisztró** (tourist-friendly but good quality), **Menza** (retro canteen style, excellent value).

## Getting around

**Metro:** 4 lines. M1 (yellow, historic, UNESCO) runs under Andrássy Avenue. Single ticket: €1.50. 24h pass: €6.50.

**Tram 2:** runs along the Pest embankment — the most scenic urban tram in Europe. Same ticket.

**Walking:** Pest side is flat and walkable. Buda side involves hills (Gellért, Castle) — take the bus or funicular.

**Airport:** bus 100E to Deák Ferenc tér (central): €3.50, 35 minutes.
