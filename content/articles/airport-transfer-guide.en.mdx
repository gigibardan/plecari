The first hour after landing is where most travel budgets take their hardest hit. You're tired, possibly confused, carrying luggage — and that's exactly when unofficial taxi drivers, expensive hotel shuttles, and overpriced kiosks are waiting. A 10-minute plan before departure saves €30–100 and avoids the stress of making decisions on the spot.

This guide covers the transfer options at Europe's most-visited airports, with real 2026 prices and journey times for each.

## The Universal Rule — Apply Everywhere

**Step 1:** Does the airport have a direct metro or train to the city centre? If yes: take it. It will be the cheapest, usually the fastest (no traffic), and requires zero negotiation or decision-making.

**Step 2:** If no metro/train exists or it doesn't run at your arrival time: use Uber or Bolt (have the app installed before landing, have your eSIM active). Open the app from arrivals, see the exact price before ordering, and wait in a designated pickup area.

**Step 3:** If ridesharing isn't available or you prefer a human: use the official taxi stand outside the terminal (not inside — never inside). Official taxis have set colours, meters, and official licences. Never get into a vehicle with someone who approached you unprompted.

**The unofficial taxi scam** operates at virtually every major European airport. Individuals in the arrivals hall (sometimes wearing fake lanyards or airport-branded clothing) offer "immediate taxi service." By the time you arrive, the price has multiplied. In some cities (Naples, Istanbul old town, Athens in the past) this is endemic. The solution is always the same: ignore them, walk past, go to the official stand or use an app.

## Rome — Fiumicino (FCO) & Ciampino (CIA)

Bucharest travellers arrive mostly at Fiumicino (Wizz Air) and Ciampino (Ryanair).

### Fiumicino (FCO) — Leonardo da Vinci Airport

| Option | Journey Time | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leonardo Express (direct train) | 32 min | €14 | FCO → Roma Termini, every 15–30 min |
| Regional train FL1 | 45–60 min | €8 | FCO → Trastevere, Ostiense, Tiburtina — cheaper, no Termini |
| Official taxi | 40–60 min | **€50 flat rate** | Fixed price to any point inside the Aurelian Walls |
| Uber/Bolt | 40–60 min | €45–60 | Similar to taxi; useful for non-central destinations |
| Private transfer | 40–60 min | €55–80 | Pre-booked, name on sign at arrivals |

**Recommendation:** Leonardo Express if you're staying near Termini or central Rome (30 min, €14 — best value). Regional FL1 if you're staying in Trastevere or near Ostiense (€8, adds 20 minutes). Official taxi only if you're in a group of 3–4 splitting the flat €50.

**Important:** Rome taxis have a legitimate flat rate of €50 to any destination inside the ancient walls. This is fixed by city law — the meter doesn't run. If a driver quotes you more or says the flat rate "doesn't apply" for some reason, get out.

### Ciampino (CIA) — Low-Cost Airport

Bucharest travellers typically arrive at CDG (Charles de Gaulle) or Beauvais (budget routes, Wizz Air).

### Charles de Gaulle (CDG)

| Option | Journey Time | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| RER B (suburban train) | 25–45 min | ~€11.80 | → Châtelet-Les Halles, Gare du Nord; every 10–15 min |
| Le Bus Direct | 45–75 min | €17 | → Champs-Élysées, Montparnasse; more comfortable |
| Official taxi | 30–60 min | **€55 fixed (Right Bank), €62 (Left Bank)** | Fixed rate by law |
| Uber | 30–60 min | €45–70 | Varies with traffic |

**Recommendation:** RER B is the best option for most travellers — €11.80, runs until midnight (night bus after), and connects to central Paris efficiently. The fixed taxi rate is genuinely fair for groups of 3–4 splitting the cost.

**Important:** Paris taxis have legally fixed rates from CDG (€55 to Right Bank arrondissements, €62 to Left Bank). These are real fixed rates established in 2014, not negotiated prices. The meter won't run on these routes.

## Private Transfers — When They Make Sense

Pre-booked private transfers (through [GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/?partner_id=EKAA80H&utm_medium=online_publisher), Welcome Pickups, or Kiwitaxi) cost 30–100% more than taxis but offer predictability, fixed pricing, and a waiting driver.

**When private transfers are worth it:**

| Situation | Reason |
|---|---|
| Late-night arrivals (after midnight) | Public transport stopped; uncertain taxi availability |
| Family with young children + strollers | Metro + luggage + tired children = stress |
| Group of 4+ people | Cost per person approaches or beats taxi when split |
| City with unreliable taxi infrastructure | Naples, some Turkish cities, smaller airports |
| Business travel with tight schedule | Can't afford delays or uncertainty |
| First visit to a destination with complex transport | Reduces cognitive load on arrival |

**When private transfers are NOT worth it:**

Direct metro or train exists, it's daytime, you're a solo traveller or couple, and you're comfortable with apps. In these cases, a €6 Piccadilly Line ride to central London beats a €55 private transfer in every way except comfort.

## Universal Tips — Apply at Every Airport

**Have your eSIM active before landing.** The moment the wheels touch down, you want data — to open Uber, check the train schedule, confirm your hotel address. Install your [eSIM from Airalo](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3) at home and it activates automatically at destination. This single step eliminates most transfer-related stress.

**Screenshot your hotel address before landing.** In case your data doesn't activate immediately, have the address (in local script if possible) saved offline. Show it to the taxi driver or use offline Google Maps.

**Have local currency for emergencies.** Most European cities accept cards everywhere, but having €20–30 in local cash means you can take a bus or metro even if your card fails. Don't exchange at the airport — ATM rates inside the terminal are still better than currency exchange booths, and city ATMs are even better.

**Never exchange money at the airport exchange desks.** Rates are typically 10–15% worse than at city ATMs. Withdraw from an ATM (or better, pay everything by card with Revolut/Wise for 0% exchange fee).

**Ignore everyone who approaches you inside the terminal.** Hotel shuttle? Walk to the taxi stand. "Better price" taxi? No. "Free transfer to the hotel"? Absolutely not. The official options are always outside, marked, and regulated.

**For night arrivals at unfamiliar airports:** pre-book a private transfer for the first trip. For all subsequent trips from that airport, you'll know the layout and can use public transport.

## Airport Transfer Decision Tree

Use this at any airport in Europe:

1. **Is there a direct metro, tram, or train to the city centre?** → Take it. Always the best value.

2. **No direct rail, or it doesn't run at my arrival time?** → Open Bolt or Uber from the arrivals hall (have eSIM active). Order a car from the designated pickup area.

3. **Neither app available or I prefer not to?** → Walk past the terminal exits to the **official taxi stand** (marked with the city's official colours). Never the drivers inside.

4. **Late night, family, or heavy luggage?** → Pre-booked private transfer through GetYourGuide or Welcome Pickups, organised before departure.
