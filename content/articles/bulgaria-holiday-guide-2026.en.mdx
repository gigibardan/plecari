Bulgaria's Black Sea coast has been Romania's most popular beach destination for decades — and 2026 brings two significant changes that make it even more practical. Bulgaria adopted the euro in January, eliminating the last friction of currency exchange. And with Romania and Bulgaria both fully in Schengen since 2025, the land border crossing that once meant hour-long queues is now as seamless as driving between any two EU countries.

Add steadily improving hotel infrastructure, prices that remain the best value in Europe for all-inclusive beach holidays, and the UNESCO World Heritage city of Nessebar 5 minutes from the main resort — and Bulgaria's case for your summer holiday is stronger than ever.

## What Changed in 2026 — Two Things Every Visitor Should Know

**1. Bulgaria adopted the euro.** From 1 January 2026, the Bulgarian lev is gone. Prices are in euros, ATMs dispense euros, and you pay with euros everywhere. No more mental arithmetic on the exchange rate, no more confusion at the supermarket, no more currency exchange fees.

**2. Schengen border crossing.** Romania and Bulgaria completed full Schengen accession in 2025. The land border crossing — which previously meant document checks, queues of 30–90 minutes, and occasional uncertainty — is now open. You drive through the same way you'd cross between Austria and Germany.

For the large number of Romanian (and now broader European) families who drive to Bulgaria each summer, these two changes remove the last sources of friction from an already accessible destination.

## The Black Sea vs The Mediterranean — What's Actually Different

Before choosing between Bulgaria and Mediterranean alternatives, it helps to understand what the Black Sea coast actually offers.

**Sea conditions:** The Black Sea is calmer than the Mediterranean, with smaller waves and gentler currents. This makes it significantly better for young children and less-confident swimmers. The shallow gradient at most Bulgarian beaches (particularly Albena and Sunny Beach) means the water stays knee-deep for 30–50 metres before dropping — a genuine safety advantage for families.

**Water temperature:** Slightly cooler than the Mediterranean in June (20–22°C vs 23–25°C) but comparable in July–August (24–27°C). The lower salinity (about one third of Mediterranean salinity) makes it easier to swim in — you need to work harder to float.

**Weather:** Slightly cooler than Greece and Turkey in peak summer — typically 28–33°C rather than 35–42°C. Less extreme heat risk, more comfortable for outdoor exploration. The consistent afternoon sea breeze on the Bulgarian coast is a feature, not a bug.

**Jellyfish:** A genuine issue in August in some years. The moon jellyfish (Aurelia aurita) appears periodically in Black Sea waters — harmless but unpleasant. Not every year, not every beach, but worth knowing. Local hotels post daily jellyfish alerts.

**Price:** All-inclusive in Bulgaria is 30–50% cheaper than Turkey and 40–60% cheaper than Greece for comparable hotel categories. This is the most significant practical difference.

## The Resorts — Which One Is Right for You

### Sunny Beach (Slanchev Bryag) — Biggest, Liveliest, Most Complete

Bulgaria's largest resort, with 8 km of fine sandy beach, approximately 350 hotels, multiple aquaparks, beach clubs, and the most concentrated nightlife on the Bulgarian coast.

**The beach:** Wide, gently shelving, with organised sections (sunlounger and parasol: €8–12/day) and free sections interleaved. The sand is genuinely fine — not the coarse gravel you find in parts of Greece. Water entry is gradual, excellent for children.

**Who it's for:** Families wanting maximum facilities and entertainment, young adults, groups who want nightlife options, first-time beach holiday visitors who want everything in one place.

**Aquaparks:** Action Aquapark (Bulgaria's largest, €30 adults/€18 children) is the main attraction. Several hotels also have their own waterparks included in the all-inclusive price — check before booking if this matters to you.

**Nightlife:** Bedroom Beach Club, Cacao Beach, Nessebar Fort Club — parties until sunrise, beer from €2–4, cocktails from €5–8. Not what you're there for if you have young children; exactly what you're there for if you don't.

**All-inclusive packages:** 7 nights, 4-star hotel, charter flight: €400–600/person. Coach transfer: €280–450/person. Early booking (before March) brings these down 20–30%.

**Nessebar is 5 minutes away.** Bus: €1. Taxi: €5. The UNESCO old town is the essential evening excursion from Sunny Beach — mandatory, not optional. More on this below.

### Sozopol — Most Beautiful, Most Atmospheric

A 2,600-year-old town built on a rocky peninsula jutting into the Black Sea, with two beaches flanking it and an old town of Byzantine churches, wooden-balconied houses, and small art galleries. Sozopol is to the Bulgarian coast what Dubrovnik is to the Adriatic — intimate, artistic, genuinely beautiful.

**Who it's for:** Couples, families who want culture alongside beach, travellers who find Sunny Beach too loud, those who appreciate architecture and history.

**Beaches:** Harmani Beach (organised, fine sand, facing north — calmer waves), Kavatsite (to the south, wilder, less organised), and several small coves accessible by water taxi (€5–8 return).

**Restaurants:** Sozopol has some of the best fish restaurants on the Bulgarian coast. Fresh catch, grilled or baked, €10–18 for a full meal with wine on a terrace over the sea. Reserve in advance in August.

**Apollonia Arts Festival:** Held each September, Apollonia transforms Sozopol into a hub for theatre, music, and contemporary art performances. If you're flexible on timing, September in Sozopol coincides with this and with significantly lower prices and smaller crowds.

**Accommodation:** More independent (self-catering apartments and boutique hotels), fewer large all-inclusive complexes. Apartment from €30–50/night, boutique hotel from €60–120/night.

**Distance from Sunny Beach:** 30 km south. Easy day trip by car or taxi (€20–25 one-way); less convenient without a car.

### Nessebar Old Town — Day Trip Essential

Nessebar isn't really a resort — it's a UNESCO World Heritage Site that happens to be adjacent to Sunny Beach, connected by regular bus (€1) and a 5-minute taxi ride (€5).

The old town sits on a narrow peninsula connected to the mainland by a thin isthmus. It has been continuously inhabited for 3,000 years — Thracians, Greeks, Romans, Byzantines, and Bulgarians have all left their mark. The result: 40+ churches in various states of preservation, medieval fortifications, wooden-balconied 19th-century houses, and a genuinely atmospheric warren of cobblestone streets.

**What to see:** Church of St Stephen (16th-century frescoes, exceptional state of preservation), the Old Basilica (roofless, beautiful ruins), the Archaeological Museum (the finds from excavations, €3), and the harbour — where you should eat dinner. Fresh fish at a harbour-front restaurant (Black Sea bream, mussels, grilled squid): €12–18 for a full meal.

**When to go:** Evening (18:00–22:00) when the day-trippers from cruise ships have left and the light is golden. Midday in July–August is extremely crowded with cruise passengers.

### Golden Sands (Zlatni Pyasatsi) — Premium Option Near Varna

18 km north of Varna (Bulgaria's second city on the Black Sea), Golden Sands is a smaller, quieter resort aimed at a slightly older demographic — couples, families seeking less animation. The beach is narrower but the sand quality is excellent, and there's a protected natural park immediately behind the hotels.

**Who it's for:** Those who find Sunny Beach too large and loud, couples, over-40s.

**Price:** 10–15% higher than Sunny Beach for equivalent hotels.

**Varna connection:** Golden Sands is essentially a suburb of Varna. The city has excellent museums (Archaeological Museum with the world's oldest gold treasure), a beautiful cathedral, sea garden, and good restaurants — easy day trip.

### Albena — Purpose-Built Family Paradise

Albena is a planned resort with no through-traffic (cars park at the perimeter, the resort is pedestrian-only). The beach is wide and very shallow for a long distance — perfect for children under 6. There are dedicated children's facilities, playgrounds, animation teams, and a medical centre on-site.

**Who it's for:** Families with very young children (under 6). The no-traffic design makes it uniquely stress-free.

**Price:** Similar to Sunny Beach or slightly higher for the family-specific facilities.

## How to Get There

### By Car (Most Popular for Romanian Families)

| From | Distance | Time | Route Options |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bucharest | ~400 km | ~5 hours | Giurgiu-Ruse bridge, or coastal via Vama Veche-Durankulak |
| Cluj-Napoca | ~750 km | ~8–9 hours | Via Bucharest or directly south through Bulgaria |
| Timișoara | ~650 km | ~7–8 hours | Via Belgrade or via Bucharest |
| Brașov | ~490 km | ~6 hours | Via Bucharest |

**Route options:**
- **Giurgiu-Ruse (Danube bridge):** Most direct from Bucharest. Bridge crossing, no fee. Then A2 motorway through Bulgaria to Burgas.
- **Coastal route:** Via Romanian Black Sea coast (Constanța, Mangalia, Vama Veche) then Durankulak border crossing into Bulgaria. More scenic, similar time from Bucharest, popular with families stopping at Romanian beaches en route.

**Parking:** Most hotels have free parking or €3–5/day. Sunny Beach town centre: €5–10/day.

**Car rental for day trips:** If you're arriving by coach or flight, renting a car for 1–2 days gives access to Sozopol, Nessebar, Varna, and the smaller coves that the coach excursions miss. [GetRentacar](https://getrentacar.tpo.mx/C6z4XHxU) aggregates Bulgarian rental companies with competitive pricing.

### By Coach

Daily departures from Bucharest, Brașov, Cluj-Napoca, Iași, Timișoara, Ploiești, and 10+ other Romanian cities. Price: €30–60 return. Duration: 6–8 hours with rest stops. Most popular with families and older travellers who don't want to drive.

**Advantage:** door-to-door (hotel pickup in some cities), no driving stress.

**Disadvantage:** fixed schedule, longer journey time, no flexibility once there.

### By Charter Flight

Charter flights from multiple Romanian airports direct to Burgas (Sunny Beach area) or Varna (Golden Sands/Albena area). Flight time: 45 minutes. Flights are typically sold as part of package holidays through tour operators.

**Stand-alone flights:** Available on comparison engines — Bucharest to Burgas from ~€80 return. Airport transfer to resort: €10–15 by shuttle, included in most packages, or taxi (~€30).

## Beyond the Beach — Day Trips and Excursions

A week on the Bulgarian coast doesn't have to be 7 consecutive beach days. The surrounding area has genuinely interesting destinations within easy reach.

### Nessebar Old Town (Already Covered Above)

5 km from Sunny Beach, evening required. Don't skip it.

### Sozopol

30 km south of Sunny Beach. Half-day or full-day excursion, or base yourself here instead. Most spectacular on a warm September evening.

### Varna — Bulgaria's Cultural Capital on the Sea

85 km north of Sunny Beach (1h15 by car or excursion bus). Bulgaria's second largest city and main Black Sea port. Worth a day:

- **Archaeological Museum:** Houses the world's oldest processed gold treasure (Varna Gold, ~6,500 years old). €5 entry. Genuinely extraordinary.
- **Sea Garden:** 8 km of promenade, fountains, aquarium, and planetarium along the coast.
- **Cathedral of the Assumption:** Bulgaria's largest cathedral, impressive Neo-Byzantine architecture.
- **Varna Fish Market:** Fresh Black Sea catch, smoked sprat, Black Sea mussels — the best seafood shopping on the coast.

### Burgas and the Burgas Lakes

35 km from Sunny Beach. Bulgaria's third city on the Black Sea, with a smaller, more local feel than Varna. The Burgas Lakes complex (four interconnected lakes behind the city) is an important bird sanctuary — flamingos, pelicans, and 300+ species during migration season.

### Aquaparks

**Action Aquapark** (Sunny Beach): Bulgaria's largest, €30 adults/€18 children. Full day's entertainment.

**Kavatsite Aquapark** (Sozopol area): Smaller, less crowded, good alternative.

### Boat Trips and Water Activities

Coastal boat trips (2–3 hours, €15–25) depart from Nessebar and Sunny Beach harbours throughout the day. Routes typically include a swimming stop at a sea cave or secluded cove. Jet skiing, parasailing, banana boats, and paddleboard rentals are available at all organised beaches.

Day trips to Sozopol and Nessebar by boat (rather than road) are scenic and popular — enquire at your hotel or via [GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/sunny-beach-l99070/?partner_id=EKAA80H&utm_medium=online_publisher).

## Practical Information for 2026

**Currency:** Euro (since January 2026). Cards accepted everywhere at hotels and restaurants in resorts. Cash useful for smaller amounts at beach vendors and in village shops.

**Documents:** EU national ID card is sufficient. No passport needed.

**Language:** Bulgarian resort staff speak Romanian and English fluently. Menus in tourist areas are multilingual.

**Health:** Bulgaria is EU, EHIC (European Health Insurance Card) works at public hospitals. Still recommend travel insurance (from [EKTA](https://ektatraveling.tpo.mx/uU7jZ5jK), ~€12/week) for repatriation, cancellation, and luggage cover.

**Data/Mobile:** EU free roaming applies — your Romanian SIM works at no extra cost. If your plan has data limits, a Bulgarian eSIM from [Airalo](https://airalo.tpo.mx/mYekFAo3) provides guaranteed fast data from €3–5.

**Tourist tax:** Bulgaria's resort municipalities charge a small tourist tax (€1–2/night) in some hotels — typically included in package prices. Lower than equivalent taxes in Barcelona, Rome, or Amsterdam.

**Food outside the hotel:** A full meal at a local restaurant in Nessebar or Sozopol (shopska salad, grilled meat or fish, beer): €10–18. Quality is good but variable — read Google reviews for specific restaurants.

**Best time to visit:** June and September offer the best combination of pleasant weather (26–30°C), manageable crowds, and lower prices. July–August is peak season with maximum energy and maximum cost. Early September coincides with Apollonia Festival in Sozopol.

## The Honest Verdict

Bulgaria won't give you the volcanic sunsets of Santorini or the ancient marble of Athens. What it gives you is a relaxed, affordable, family-friendly beach holiday in an EU country with Euro currency, seamless border crossing, fine sand, calm warm water, and a UNESCO heritage city 5 minutes away.

For families with children, budget-conscious travellers, and anyone who values the practical over the spectacular, Bulgaria's Black Sea coast remains the best-value beach holiday option reachable from Central and Eastern Europe.
