# Budapest Christmas Markets: Complete Guide 2026 — Central Europe's Most Beautiful Advent

There's a rare consensus in the travel world: when it comes to Christmas in Central Europe, **Budapest stands alone**. Not because of one spectacular market, but because an entire city transforms — the neo-Gothic Parliament reflected in the Danube, the illuminated Buda Castle on the hill, St. Stephen's Basilica surrounded by artisan chalets, and the smell of kürtőskalács drifting from Vörösmarty tér.

**Budapest has won** the title of Europe's best Christmas market multiple times in European Best Destinations polls and has consistently appeared in every serious ranking of European Christmas destinations. And deservedly — because you're not dealing with a single location but with an **Advent system** spread across all of Pest and Buda.

**This guide covers:**
- The 4 major Budapest Christmas markets (Vörösmarty, Basilica, Buda Castle, Városliget)
- Christmas attractions beyond the markets (Parliament, thermal baths, ice rink)
- How to get to Budapest (flights, bus, train, car)
- Where to stay and how to save money
- Hungarian Christmas food — what you absolutely must taste
- 3-day itinerary and full budget

## Why Budapest Is Central Europe's Unofficial Christmas Capital

Before diving into practical details, it's worth understanding why Budapest stands out from competitors like Vienna, Prague or Strasbourg.

**Architecture.** Budapest has an extraordinary concentration of neo-Gothic, neo-Baroque and Jugendstil (Art Nouveau) buildings that look absolutely spectacular illuminated at Christmas. The Hungarian Parliament — the largest neo-Gothic building in continental Europe — is literally the most photographed thing in Budapest during the Advent season. St. Stephen's Basilica with its neoclassical facade transformed into a projection screen for 3D light shows every evening is unique in Europe.

**Diversity.** Budapest has **4 large markets** with distinct personalities, plus dozens of smaller ones. You can move from the elegant Vörösmarty tér (oriented towards premium design and craftsmanship) to the rustic Buda Castle (medieval atmosphere) in 15 minutes by tram.

**Value for money.** The Hungarian forint (HUF) makes Budapest **significantly cheaper** than Vienna or Paris. A warm mulled wine (forralt bor) costs 600-800 HUF (€1.5-2), a kürtőskalács 500-700 HUF (€1.3-1.8), a good restaurant dinner 3,000-5,000 HUF (€8-13). Compared to Vienna or Paris, Budapest costs 30-40% less.

**Thermal baths.** Nowhere else in Europe can you combine a Christmas market visit with an afternoon in a 19th-century thermal palace. Széchenyi or Gellért in winter are experiences unique to Budapest.

## Vörösmarty tér Christmas Market — The Heart of Budapest's Advent

### General Description

**Vörösmarty tér** (Vörösmarty Square) in the heart of Budapest is Hungary's oldest and most famous Christmas market — running **since 1999** and growing continuously in scale and reputation.

The square is named after Hungarian poet Mihály Vörösmarty, whose white marble statue stands in the centre of the square transformed for the Advent season. Surrounded by the elegant late 19th and early 20th century buildings of central Pest, the square becomes in November-December a market with white and gold wooden chalets decorated with Hungarian folk motifs.

**2026 Dates:** Approximately **14 November — 31 December 2026**

**Access:** Metro M1 (Vörösmarty tér), at the top end of Váci utca (Budapest's main pedestrian street)

### What You'll Find at Vörösmarty

**Arts and crafts:** Vörösmarty is renowned for the **superior quality of its artisan products**. Hungarian craftspeople — ceramicists, embroiderers, woodcarvers, jewellers — display and sell genuine handmade products. You can find:
- Traditional Hungarian ceramics (Herend, Zsolnay, Hollóháza — brands with century-old traditions)
- Folk embroidery (Hungarian flower motif)
- Hand-painted wooden figures
- Silver jewellery with ethno-Hungarian motifs
- Traditional hand-crafted wooden toys
- Handmade Christmas decorations

**Gastronomy:** The market has an excellent food section with Hungarian specialities:
- **Kürtőskalács** (chimney cake) — cylindrical sweet pastry cooked on a wooden spit over an open fire and rolled in sugar, cinnamon or walnuts. It is the gastronomic symbol of Budapest at Christmas.
- **Lángos** with sour cream and cheese — the deep-fried dough disc, a Hungarian street food icon
- **Forralt bor** (Hungarian mulled wine) — different from German Glühwein, lighter and more fragrant
- **Pálinka** (Hungarian fruit brandy) — an absolute tradition, available in dozens of flavours
- **Kolbász** (Hungarian sausages) on the grill
- **Mézeskalács** (honey gingerbread) decorated by hand — beautiful and delicious

**Entertainment:** Vörösmarty tér has a central stage with **daily live concerts** — Hungarian folk music, carol choirs, children's shows and classical Advent music concerts.

**The gingerbread wall:** An iconic element of the market is the **giant mézeskalács wall** covering an entire building in the square — hundreds of coloured gingerbreads forming a giant edible Christmas mosaic.

## Advent at St. Stephen's Basilica — Europe's Best Christmas Market?

### Why It's Special

If Vörösmarty tér is the classic heart of Budapest's Christmas, **Advent at St. Stephen's Basilica** is its revelation — and many travel experts consider it **the most beautiful Christmas market in Europe**.

Located in the square in front of **St. Stephen's Basilica** — Budapest's most important cathedral, built in neoclassical style between 1851 and 1905 — the market enjoys an extraordinary architectural setting. The basilica's facade becomes every evening a screen for a spectacular **3D light mapping show** projecting images, animations and Christmas stories onto its surface. The show lasts about 10 minutes and repeats at set times.

**2026 Dates:** Approximately **21 November — 1 January 2027**

**Access:** Metro M3 (Arany János utca) or M1 (Bajcsy-Zsilinszky út), 5 minutes on foot

### What the Basilica Offers vs Vörösmarty

**Award-winning gastronomy:** The Basilica market has a **more sophisticated and contemporary food selection** compared to Vörösmarty. Beyond the Hungarian classics, you'll find:
- **Foie gras on toast** with fruit jam
- **Craft beer artisan** with traditional Hungarian ingredients
- **Dobos cake slices** (classic Hungarian caramel layered cake) in portable form
- **Artisan cheeses** from Hungarian rural regions
- Wines from the Tokaj, Eger and Villány regions
- **Premium pálinka** from the most renowned distilleries

**Chalet design:** The Basilica has uniform white, minimalist chalets, with emphasis on the product rather than the decor — the result is a market with a more elegant appearance than Vörösmarty.

**The 3D light show:** This is the unique element. The **video mapping** show projected onto the facade of St. Stephen's Basilica, with symphonic music or classical carols, is one of Europe's most spectacular visual experiences at Christmas. It repeats several times each evening. **Free.**

**Practical tip:** Come to the Basilica **after nightfall** (17:00-18:00) to catch the light show in its full glory. The exact times of the light show are posted on the market's official website.

## Budavári Advent — Christmas at Buda Castle

### The Medieval Atmosphere

**Budavári Advent** is the Christmas market at **Buda Castle** — the medieval palace complex on the Buda hill to the west of the Danube. It is a completely different setting from the two Pest squares: instead of wide boulevards and neoclassical architecture, you have the narrow lanes of the castle district with reconstructed Baroque and medieval buildings.

**2026 Dates:** Approximately **21 November — 23 December 2026**

**Access:** The funicular (Sikló) from Chain Bridge Square (paid, ~2,000 HUF/person) or bus 16/116 from Deak Ferenc tér

### What Makes Budavári Special

**The location.** The market is organised in front of the **Royal Palace** (Budavári Palota) and in the Lion Court, with direct panoramic views towards Pest and the Danube. During the day the view is enchanting; in the evening, with the illuminated Parliament and Chain Bridge in front of you, it becomes fairy tale.

**Traditional crafts.** Compared to Vörösmarty (more commercialised), Budavári has a more artisan profile with emphasis on **traditional Hungarian rural crafts** — blacksmiths, potters, woodcarvers working live in front of visitors.

**Ice rink.** Budavári has a **small ice rink** — more decorative and romantic compared to the giant one in Városliget, but with the extraordinary backdrop of the Royal Palace.

## Városliget Ice Rink — Central Europe's Largest Outdoor Ice Rink

**Városligeti Műjégpálya** (the City Park Artificial Ice Rink) is **Central Europe's largest outdoor ice rink** — approximately 10,000 m² of artificial ice, in operation since 1870 (with breaks), making it one of the oldest public ice rinks in the world.

**Location:** Városliget (City Park), at the end of Andrássy út

**2026 Dates:** Opening around 1-7 November, operating until February 2027

**Approximate prices:** ~2,500-3,500 HUF (€6-9)/adult, ~1,500-2,000 HUF/child; skates included in price

**The architectural setting** is exceptional — the rink is surrounded by **Vajdahunyad Castle** (Vajdahunyadvára), a fantastic replica built in 1896 for Hungary's millennium, which combines different architectural styles from all the Carpathian regions. At night, with the castle illuminated and Christmas music in the air, it is one of Budapest's most photographed places in winter.

**The Városliget market:** Alongside the ice rink, there is a small Christmas market with food and craft stalls, more modest than Vörösmarty or the Basilica, but the atmosphere is exceptional.

## Christmas Attractions Beyond the Markets

### The Illuminated Hungarian Parliament

The **Hungarian Parliament** (Országház) is, without doubt, one of Europe's most photographed buildings at Christmas. Built between 1885 and 1904 in neo-Gothic style, with 691 rooms and 20 km of internal staircases, it is the largest parliament building in Europe by floor area.

At night, illuminated in yellow and gold, reflected in the Danube, it is an unforgettable visual spectacle. The **Danube Promenade** (Duna korzó) on the Pest bank is the ideal spot for photographs — described by Napoleon Bonaparte as "the pearl of the Danube", Budapest looks just as good as he imagined.

**Interior tours** of Parliament are available during the Christmas season; they include the parliamentary hall with St. Stephen's Crown (the national jewel). Tickets: ~8,000-10,000 HUF (€20-25) for non-EU tourists.

### Chain Bridge — The Christmas Bridge

**Széchenyi Lánchíd** (Chain Bridge) — inaugurated in 1849, the first permanent bridge over the Danube in Budapest — is spectacularly illuminated at Christmas. Walking across it at sunset, with Buda Castle ahead and Parliament behind, is one of Budapest's must-do winter walks.

### Andrássy út — The Christmas Boulevard

**Andrássy út** — Budapest's grand Haussmann-style boulevard, compared to the Champs-Élysées — has elegant Christmas lights along its entire 2.3 km length, from Deak tér to Hősök tere (Heroes' Square).

An evening stroll along Andrássy út, with Christmas music from open cafes and decorated shopfronts, is one of Budapest's most beautiful Advent experiences.

### Thermal Baths — The Unique Combination

Budapest has **over 50 thermal springs** and some of Europe's most beautiful public baths, built in various styles: Turkish-Ottoman (Király, Veli Bej), neo-Baroque (Széchenyi, Gellért), Art Nouveau.

**Széchenyi Baths** in Városliget — built in 1913 in neo-Baroque style, with 3 outdoor pools and 15 indoor — are a fantastic experience in winter: steam rising from the warm water (+36-38°C) under a December sky, snow on the yellow palace roof. You can **bathe in the outdoor pool in winter** — one of Budapest's most talked-about tourist experiences.

**Prices:** ~8,000-12,000 HUF (€20-30)/person — reasonable for 3-4 hours of relaxation.

**Recommendation:** Bath visits early in the morning (8-10:00) or in the evening (after 17:00) are quieter. Weekends are crowded.

## How to Get to Budapest

Budapest is excellent connected to the rest of Europe, with a major international airport and good overland connections.

### By Plane — The Fastest Option

**Budapest (BUD — Ferenc Liszt International Airport) direct flights:**
- From most Western European cities: budget airlines (Wizz Air, Ryanair, easyJet) from €20-80 one way, 2-3 hours
- From further afield: full-service airlines (British Airways, Lufthansa, Air France) from €100-300

**Airport to city centre:**
- **Metro M3** (new extension): 30 minutes to city centre, ~900 HUF (€2.3) — **cheapest option**
- **Minibus 100E:** 30 minutes, ~2,400 HUF (€6)
- **Taxi:** 8,000-12,000 HUF (€20-30) — much more expensive but comfortable with luggage

### By Car

Budapest is well situated for road trips from Central and Eastern Europe. If you're driving:
- **From Vienna:** 2.5 hours on the M1 motorway
- **From Prague:** 5 hours
- **From Warsaw:** 7-8 hours
- **From Bucharest:** 8-10 hours via Nădlac border crossing

**Hungarian motorway vignette (e-matrica):** Mandatory for motorways. Buy online at motorway.hu or at the border: approximately 5,400 HUF (€14) for 10 days.

### By Bus

**FlixBus and other operators** run direct services to Budapest from many European cities. Often cheaper than flying for nearby destinations.

### By Train

Budapest is a major rail hub with good connections from Vienna (3 hours), Prague (7 hours), Berlin (10 hours) and other Central European cities. The Eurorail/Interrail pass covers Hungary.

## Where to Stay in Budapest — Neighbourhood Guide

Budapest is divided into two: **Pest** (east, flat, urban) and **Buda** (west, hilly, quieter). For Christmas, **Pest is recommended** — close to all the major markets.

### Best Areas

**District V — Belváros (Inner City):** Directly by Vörösmarty tér and St. Stephen's Basilica. Most expensive, but most convenient for Advent.

**District VI — Terézváros:** Andrássy út, the State Opera, Art Nouveau cafes. Excellent balance of price and location.

**District VII — Erzsébetváros (Jewish District):** Famous ruin bars (Szimpla Kert etc.), young and cosmopolitan vibe. Lower prices than the centre.

**District VIII — Józsefváros:** Museums, National Garden, accessible prices. Good for tighter budgets.

## Hungarian Christmas Food — What You Must Taste

### At the Markets

**Kürtőskalács (Chimney Cake)** — this is the absolute symbol of Budapest at Christmas. A cylindrical sweet pastry prepared from dough wrapped around a wooden cone rotated over charcoal embers, covered with sugar and optionally cinnamon, walnuts, cocoa or almonds. Crispy on the outside and soft inside, with the distinctive caramelised aroma — not to be missed. Price: 500-800 HUF (€1.3-2).

**Forralt bor (Hungarian Mulled Wine)** — lighter and more fragrant than German Glühwein, Hungarian mulled wine uses local wines (Egri Bikavér, Villányi) with cinnamon, cloves, oranges and sugar. Some versions include pálinka for extra warmth. Price: 600-900 HUF (€1.5-2.3).

**Lángos with sour cream and cheese** — the deep-fried dough disc, covered with thick sour cream and grated cheese (or various toppings). The beloved Hungarian street food, available at every market. Price: 700-1,200 HUF (€1.8-3).

**Pálinka** — Hungarian fruit brandy with protected designation of origin. Basic varieties: apricot (barackpálinka), plum (szilvapálinka), pear (körtepálinka). Quality pálinka (5-10 years old) is an entirely different world from ordinary varieties. Price: 400-800 HUF (€1-2)/tasting at a stand.

**Mangalica kolbász** — sausages from mangalica pork (an old Hungarian breed with marbled meat and healthy fat), grilled with mustard and bread. Mangalica has become fashionable again in European gastronomy — fattier but more flavoursome than regular pork. Price: 1,000-1,500 HUF (€2.5-4).

### Traditional Hungarian Dishes

**Gulyás** — the rich beef soup with vegetables, sweet paprika and caraway, served in a bowl or cauldron. This is the original Hungarian gulyás — a soup, not a stew. In a good restaurant: 1,800-2,800 HUF (€4.5-7).

**Pörkölt** — the Hungarian stew closer to what most people outside Hungary call "goulash" — meat braised with onions, red paprika and spices, served with nokedli (home-made noodles). Price: 2,500-3,500 HUF (€6.3-9).

**Halászlé (Fisherman's Soup)** — the spicy Danube fish soup, traditional in Hungary especially at Christmas and New Year, prepared from carp and other fish with hot peppers and paprika. The Szeged version (spiciest) and Baja version (milder) are most renowned.

**Dobos torta** — the chocolate caramel layered cake with thin sponge layers and chocolate buttercream, invented by József Dobos in 1884. The symbol of Hungarian patisserie.

**Rétes (Hungarian Strudel)** — strudel with apples, cherries, poppy seeds or curd cheese is present in every Budapest patisserie. Excellent with a warm winter tea.

## 3-Day Budapest Christmas Itinerary

### Day 1 — Festive Pest

**09:00-10:00** — Breakfast at one of the historic central cafes. **Gerbeaud** (on Vörösmarty tér, since 1858) is the most famous — expensive but iconic. Alternative: Ruszwurm (in Buda Castle, since 1827).

**10:00-13:00** — Vörösmarty tér market: crafts, kürtőskalács and forralt bor. Admire the gingerbread wall.

**13:00-14:30** — Lunch at a traditional restaurant: gulyás or pörkölt with nokedli.

**14:30-16:00** — Andrássy út: stroll, shopfronts, the Opera House (exterior). If you have time and interest, visit Szépművészeti Múzeum (Museum of Fine Arts) — free on certain days.

**16:30-17:30** — Hősök tere (Heroes' Square) and Városliget — ice rink.

**18:00-20:00** — **St. Stephen's Basilica at sunset and 3D light show.** This is the day's highlight.

**20:00** — Dinner and ruin bar in District VII.

### Day 2 — Royal Buda and Thermal Baths

**09:00-11:00** — Széchenyi Baths in the morning (quieter). Outdoor pool in winter — an unforgettable experience.

**11:30-13:00** — Lunch in Városliget and a stroll.

**14:00-17:00** — Buda Castle: funicular, Royal Palace, Hungarian National Gallery (Danube views!), Fisherman's Bastion and Matthias Church. Budavári Advent market.

**17:00-18:30** — Danube Promenade in Pest: cross Chain Bridge, photographs of the illuminated Parliament.

**19:00** — Special dinner with Hungarian specialities — a recommended restaurant in District V or VI.

### Day 3 — Jewish District, Shopping and Farewell

**09:00-11:00** — Jewish District: Dohány Synagogue (Europe's largest), Tree of Life Memorial, indie cafes.

**11:00-13:00** — Váci utca (pedestrian street) for souvenir shopping.

**13:00-14:30** — Central Market Hall (Nagy Vásárcsarnok) — Budapest's most beautiful market hall, built in 1897. Two floors with Hungarian products, cured meats, cheeses, paprika. The 1st floor has cheap good restaurants.

**14:30-16:30** — Free time: National Museum, Erzsébet Park or hotel relaxation.

**17:00-19:00** — Final visit to the Basilica for the light show.

**19:00+** — New Year's Eve or Christmas dinner at one of Budapest's celebrated restaurants.

## Practical Tips

### Currency

Hungary is not in the Eurozone — the **national currency is the Hungarian Forint (HUF)**. €1 ≈ 395-400 HUF (fluctuates).

- **Don't exchange into euros first** — exchange directly at Budapest (exchange offices in the centre, banks)
- **Best rates:** Independent exchange offices in the centre, not banks or the airport
- **Bank cards** are accepted everywhere

Hungary is in the EU — the **European Health Insurance Card** (EHIC) is valid for EU citizens. A supplementary travel insurance is still recommended for major medical emergencies, lost luggage and flight cancellations.

## Budapest vs Other European Christmas Markets — How Does It Compare?

If you're weighing Budapest against other European Christmas destinations, here's an honest comparison:

### Budapest vs Vienna

Both cities are exceptional for Christmas. Vienna has the more polished, operatic atmosphere — the Rathaus market with its ice skating rink and the Schönbrunn Palace market are iconic. But Vienna is **40-50% more expensive** than Budapest for accommodation, food and drinks. If budget matters, Budapest wins easily. If money is no object and you want the most storybook European Christmas, they're neck and neck.

**Winner for value:** Budapest
**Winner for tradition:** Vienna (marginally)
**Winner for thermal baths:** Budapest (no contest)

### Budapest vs Prague

Prague is consistently ranked among Europe's most beautiful Christmas cities, with its Old Town Square market and the backdrop of Prague Castle. However, Prague's Christmas markets have become **more commercial and tourist-heavy** in recent years. Budapest's markets — especially the Basilica — maintain a stronger local feel with higher artisan quality.

**Winner for artisan quality:** Budapest
**Winner for gothic atmosphere:** Prague
**Winner for price:** Similar — Prague is slightly cheaper than Budapest for some items

### Budapest vs Strasbourg

Strasbourg's Christkindelsmärik (the oldest in France, since 1570) offers an authentic Alsatian experience that Budapest can't replicate. But Strasbourg has fewer large-scale attractions surrounding the market itself.

**Winner for Christmas tradition depth:** Strasbourg
**Winner for overall trip content:** Budapest (thermal baths, architecture, more markets)

### Budapest vs Nuremberg

Nuremberg's Christkindlesmarkt is one of the world's most famous markets — opened by the Christkind herself each year, known globally for Lebkuchen and wooden decorations. It's pure German Christmas tradition. Budapest is more diverse in its offer.

**Winner for single best market atmosphere:** Nuremberg
**Winner for city-wide Christmas experience:** Budapest

## Day Trips from Budapest at Christmas

Budapest is ideally positioned for day trips to other charming Hungarian cities that have their own Christmas traditions.

### Eger — Wine and Baroque Charm (130 km)

**Eger** is Hungary's wine capital, home to the famous Bull's Blood red wine (Egri Bikavér), with a beautifully preserved Baroque city centre. At Christmas, Eger's Dobó tér (the central square) hosts a small but charming market.

The drive takes about 1.5 hours from Budapest (via M3 motorway). Alternatively, direct trains run from Budapest-Keleti station (1.5-2 hours, ~3,500 HUF return).

**What to do in Eger:** The Eger Castle (Egri Vár) with its wine cellar tours and tasting rooms in the underground "Valley of Beautiful Women" (Szépasszonyvölgy). Perfect in December — underground cellars maintain constant temperature and hot wine flows freely.

### Győr — The Hungarian Baroque Capital (120 km)

**Győr** (pronounced "Djeur") is considered Hungary's most beautiful Baroque city — its compact old town, built on a confluence of three rivers, has an extraordinary collection of Baroque churches and palaces.

Győr hosts the **Advent Győr** Christmas market in the city's old town, with Hungarian crafts and regional food specialities.

The drive takes about 1 hour from Budapest (via M1 motorway towards Vienna). Direct trains run from Budapest-Keleti (1.5 hours).

### Sopron — The Most Faithful City (220 km)

**Sopron**, near the Austrian border, is Hungary's best-preserved medieval city — it was nicknamed "the most faithful city" after voting to remain in Hungary (rather than join Austria) in a 1921 referendum.

Its Christmas market, in the medieval Fő tér (Main Square), surrounded by centuries-old buildings, is one of Hungary's most atmospheric. The proximity to Vienna (60 km) means Austrian influence in the food and decorations.

The drive from Budapest takes about 2.5 hours (via M1). The trip is best done by car as rail connections are slower.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Budapest at Christmas

### Is Budapest expensive at Christmas?

Compared to other major European Christmas destinations, **Budapest is significantly affordable**. A beer in a bar costs €1.5-3, a restaurant lunch €5-10, a 3-star hotel €80-130/night. Vienna or Paris typically cost 40-60% more for similar quality.

### What is the currency in Hungary?

Hungary is in the EU but **not in the Eurozone**. The currency is the **Hungarian Forint (HUF)**. €1 = approximately 395-410 HUF (fluctuates). Some tourist-area shops accept euros but at poor exchange rates — it's better to use HUF.

### Is Budapest safe for tourists?

Hungary and Budapest are **very safe** for tourists by European standards. Normal precautions apply (watch for pickpockets in crowded areas like markets, metro stations, tourist buses). Emergency number: 112.

### What's the weather like in Budapest in December?

Budapest in December has **typical Central European winter weather**: temperatures range from -5°C to +7°C (23-45°F). Snow is possible but not guaranteed (Budapest averages about 5-10 snowy days per December). Rain and overcast skies are more common than snow. Pack warm, layered clothing — the Christmas market visits involve a lot of standing outdoors.

### Do the thermal baths stay open during Christmas?

Yes! The thermal baths are **open year-round**, including Christmas and New Year. Most (Széchenyi, Gellért, Lukács) operate on Christmas Day and New Year's Day with slightly reduced hours. Check the official websites for exact holiday schedules.

### Can I combine Budapest with Vienna or Prague at Christmas?

Absolutely. Budapest–Vienna by train takes 2.5-3 hours (Railjet service, 6-8 trains daily). Budapest–Prague takes 7 hours by direct train. Many travellers combine Budapest + Vienna (3 days each) or Budapest + Bratislava (1 hour by train) for a comprehensive Central European Christmas trip.

### Is English widely spoken in Budapest?

In tourist areas, hotels, restaurants and the Christmas markets, **English is very widely spoken**. Outside the tourist circuit, Hungarian (a notoriously difficult language) is the norm. Learning a few words — *köszönöm* (thank you), *kérem* (please), *jó napot* (good day) — is appreciated.

### What souvenirs should I buy in Budapest?

The best authentic Budapest souvenirs:
- **Herend or Zsolnay porcelain** (expensive but world-class quality)
- **Embroidered Hungarian tablecloths and pillowcases** (kalocsa or matyo folk motifs)
- **Quality pálinka** from renowned distilleries
- **Hungarian paprika** (sweet, hot or smoked) — invaluable in the kitchen
- **Tokaji wine** — the golden wine of Hungarian kings, with Aszú being the premium variety
- **Hand-painted wooden decorations** from the Christmas markets
