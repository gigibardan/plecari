The scenarios in this guide are not hypothetical. Medical emergencies happen to fit, young travellers on first trips. Passports disappear. Flights are missed. Natural disasters redirect carefully planned itineraries. The difference between a bad experience that becomes a story you tell later and a genuine crisis is almost always the same thing: knowing what to do in the first 30 minutes.

This guide covers every major travel emergency in sequence — what to do first, who to call, and how to protect yourself financially.

---

## Before any emergency: the five things to do before departure

These five actions take 20 minutes total and make every scenario below significantly easier to handle:

**1. Save your insurer's emergency number in your phone**
It is on your policy document, in the insurer's app, and on the back of any physical insurance card. Save it as a contact called "TRAVEL EMERGENCY" — capital letters so it appears first when you start typing in a moment of stress.

**2. Photograph your passport and email it to yourself**
Both sides. Email to yourself and to one trusted person at home. If your passport is lost or stolen, this photo is your primary evidence of identity at the consulate. Without it, the process takes significantly longer.

**3. Note the address and phone number of your country's embassy in your destination**
Search "[your country] embassy in [destination]" — the result is always the official government website. Save the address and emergency phone number. Embassies have after-hours emergency lines for genuine crises (passport loss, arrest, medical repatriation).

**4. Save your bank's international fraud line**
The number on the back of each card. If your cards are stolen, you call this number to block them before the thief uses them. Bolt or Uber apps, Google Pay, and Apple Pay are also worth setting up as backup payment methods.

**5. Share your itinerary**
Email your accommodation details, flight numbers, and rough daily plans to a trusted person at home. If you cannot be reached for 48 hours, they know where to start looking.

## Medical emergency abroad

A medical emergency is the highest-stakes scenario. The decisions made in the first hour determine both outcomes and costs.

### Immediate danger to life

**Call 112** (universal EU emergency number) — or the local equivalent:
- Turkey: 112
- UK: 999 or 112
- USA/Canada: 911
- Australia: 000
- Morocco: 15 (medical), 19 (police)

112 works from any mobile phone, even without a SIM card, credit, or data. If your phone is dead, ask anyone nearby to call.

### Non-life-threatening medical situation

**Call your insurer's emergency line first.** This is critical and counterintuitive: the instinct is to go to the nearest hospital. But:

- The insurer directs you to a network partner hospital where they pay directly — you pay nothing out of pocket
- If you go to a non-partner private hospital without pre-authorisation, the insurer can refuse or reduce reimbursement
- The insurer's emergency operator speaks multiple languages and can also communicate with the hospital on your behalf

**What to say when you call:**
- Your name and policy number (in the insurer's app before departure)
- Your location — as precise as possible
- Your symptoms or situation
- Your phone number in case you lose connection

**The insurer then:**
1. Directs you to the nearest appropriate facility
2. Contacts the hospital and pre-authorises treatment
3. Arranges payment directly with the hospital
4. Assigns a case manager who monitors your situation

### If you pay out of pocket (partner hospital not available)

Keep every document:
- Medical report or discharge summary
- All receipts for treatment, medication, tests
- Prescription copies
- Taxi receipts to and from the hospital
- Hotel receipts if your stay was extended due to illness

Submit a reimbursement claim on return. Most insurers have an online claims portal. Reimbursement typically arrives within 14–30 days.

### Medical repatriation

If you are seriously ill or injured and need to return home for treatment, your insurer organises this:
- Air ambulance (if required): €15,000–80,000 — covered in full
- Commercial flight with medical escort: covered
- A family member's flight to accompany you: covered by most comprehensive policies

Do not try to arrange repatriation yourself — the insurer has specialist logistics teams for this. Call them, describe the situation, and let them manage it.

### What the EHIC covers (and does not)

The European Health Insurance Card gives you access to public hospitals in EU countries under the same conditions as local residents. It does NOT cover:
- Private hospitals (where most tourist-area urgent care happens)
- Medical repatriation
- Non-EU destinations (Turkey, UK, USA, Asia, etc.)

For non-EU destinations, travel insurance is your only protection. For EU destinations, travel insurance covers the gaps the EHIC leaves. See the full comparison: [EHIC vs travel insurance](/en/asigurari/ehic-vs-travel-insurance).

## Passport lost or stolen abroad

Losing your passport abroad is stressful but solvable. The process is well-established.

### Step-by-step: lost or stolen passport

**Step 1 — File a police report (within hours)**

For a stolen passport: go to the nearest police station and file a theft report. For a lost passport: file a loss report. You need this document for:
- Applying for an Emergency Travel Document
- Travel insurance claims
- Proving the original passport is no longer usable (prevents fraudulent use)

In most EU countries, you can file a police report online. In Turkey: Jandarma (rural) or Polis (urban), both reachable via 155.

**Step 2 — Contact your country's embassy or consulate**

Find the nearest location before you travel (saved from your pre-departure preparation). Call their emergency line — most embassies have 24/7 emergency services for genuine passport loss situations.

For Romanian citizens abroad: the Consular Department of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs operates a permanent emergency line. Contact via mae.ro → "Cetățeni români în dificultate."

For UK citizens: contact the FCDO on +44 207 008 5000 (24/7).

For US citizens: contact the nearest US Embassy or +1 202 501 4444.

**Step 3 — Apply for an Emergency Travel Document**

Bring to the embassy:
- Police report
- Two passport-size photos (available at local photo shops, pharmacies, or printing services)
- Proof of your original passport — the photo on your phone counts
- Proof of your travel plans (flight booking)
- Payment for the document fee (€50–80 approximately)

Processing time: typically 24–48 hours in normal circumstances. In acute situations, some embassies issue same-day documents.

**Step 4 — Replace your travel insurance documentation**

If your physical insurance card was also stolen, access your policy digitally (most insurers have apps). Contact the insurer and they will resend documentation by email.

### Preventing passport theft

The best prevention: keep your passport at the hotel safe and carry only a photo of it and your national ID for daily use. Most EU countries accept national ID for identification within the EU — you only need the physical passport at the airport or at accommodation check-in.

## Phone and wallet stolen

Theft of phone and/or wallet is the most statistically common travel emergency, especially in high-tourist areas (metro stations, outdoor markets, beach areas).

### Immediate steps (within the first hour)

**Block your cards immediately:**
- Revolut: one tap in the app (from a borrowed phone) → freeze card
- Bank card: call the international fraud line (saved before departure)
- PayPal, Apple Pay, Google Pay: log in and suspend from any browser

**Change passwords:** if your phone is taken unlocked, the thief has access to your email, banking apps, and social media. Change email password first (via another device) — this locks out everything connected to it.

**File a police report:** required for insurance claims and for blocking the stolen phone via IMEI (the IMEI number is on the original box if you have it, or on the SIM slot area of most phones — take a photo before travel).

**Lock your phone remotely:**
- iPhone: iCloud.com → Find My → Mark as Lost
- Android: myaccount.google.com → Find My Device → Lock

This prevents access to the phone even if the passcode is bypassed.

### Practical access to money after card theft

- Google Pay / Apple Pay on a borrowed phone: if you set up digital wallets before travel, you can pay using a borrowed phone logged into your account
- Western Union or MoneyGram: someone at home can transfer money — you collect with ID at a local outlet
- Your country's embassy: some embassies can provide emergency funds or facilitate transfers for genuinely stranded citizens

### Does travel insurance cover phone theft?

Many standard travel insurance policies include a baggage/personal effects section that covers stolen items — typically with a maximum claim per item (€200–500 for phones) and a policy excess. You need the police report and evidence of ownership (receipt, or the phone's serial number). File the claim on return.

## Missing your flight or connection

Missing a flight has two completely different causes with completely different remedies.

### Missed because of the airline (delay, cancellation, overbooking)

The airline is responsible. Under EU Regulation 261/2004:
- Re-routing to your destination on the next available flight at no cost
- Meals and refreshments during the wait
- Hotel accommodation if overnight stay required
- Compensation of €250–600 depending on distance (for delays over 3 hours at final destination)

Do not buy a new ticket yourself — ask the airline to rebook you. Full details: [EU261 flight delay and cancellation rights](/en/zboruri/flight-delay-passenger-rights).

### Missed because of you (overslept, traffic, misjudged time)

The airline is not responsible. Your options:
- Contact the airline immediately — some will waive fees for documented emergencies (hospital certificate, police report)
- Buy a new ticket — check all airlines on the route, not just the original one
- Check your travel insurance — some policies cover missed departures if the cause was a covered event (accident en route to airport, medical emergency)

**AirHelp** can assess whether any compensation is owed if the miss was caused by a prior airline delay: [check eligibility](https://airhelp.tpo.mx/Etx51IpP).

### Missed connection on a single booking

If you miss a connection because the first flight was delayed and both flights are on the same booking, the airline rebooks you automatically and owes you care (meals, hotel if overnight) and potentially compensation. Do not buy a new ticket — go to the airline's service desk.

If you had two separate bookings: you are on your own for the second flight.

## Natural disaster or civil unrest at your destination

### Before the situation develops: monitoring

**UK FCDO:** travel.fco.gov.uk — regularly updated travel advisories per country with specific risk ratings.

**US State Department:** travel.state.gov — similar system, updated in real time during crises.

**Romanian MAE:** mae.ro → "Atenționări de călătorie" — advisories for Romanian citizens.

Subscribe to email alerts from your government's foreign affairs ministry — they send updates when the situation in a destination changes.

### If you are caught in a developing emergency

**Register with your embassy:** many embassies have registration systems for citizens abroad — useful for being contacted and evacuated in a crisis. Some countries mandate this for high-risk destinations.

**Follow official government advice:** not social media rumours. In a genuine crisis, the FCDO, State Department, or equivalent is your authoritative source.

**Contact your insurer:** travel insurance typically covers emergency evacuation in cases of natural disaster, civil unrest, or terrorist incident — this is a separate benefit from medical evacuation. Check your policy for "catastrophe evacuation" or "political evacuation" language.

**If evacuation is announced:** follow official routes and timings. Do not improvise. Embassies coordinate evacuation logistics and will contact registered citizens.

### If you can't return on your original flight

Airlines typically waive rebooking fees during declared disasters or government-ordered evacuations. Document everything. Your travel insurance covers additional accommodation and transport costs when your return is disrupted by a covered event.

## Legal trouble abroad

### Minor incidents (fine, minor dispute)

Contact your hotel for local guidance — staff in tourist hotels deal with these regularly and often speak the language. Most minor incidents (overstayed parking, minor traffic violation, noise complaint) are resolved directly with local authorities.

### Arrest or detention

**Contact your embassy immediately.** This is the embassy's primary consular function. They cannot get you released, but they can:
- Ensure you are treated according to local law and international standards
- Provide a list of local lawyers
- Contact your family at home
- Monitor your case

Do not sign any document in a language you do not understand before consulting a lawyer. You have the right to consular access in virtually every country.

**Your insurer may cover legal costs:** some travel insurance policies include a legal liability or legal expenses section — check your policy for this.

## Accident or injury (not medical emergency level)

If you are involved in a road accident, fall, or injury that requires documentation:

1. Document everything: photos of the scene, the other party's details (if applicable), witness contacts
2. File a police report even for minor incidents — required for insurance claims
3. Seek medical assessment even if you feel fine — some injuries manifest hours later, and having a medical record from the day of the incident protects your insurance claim
4. Contact your insurer and your travel insurance within 24–48 hours

## The travel emergency kit: what to prepare before departure

**On your phone (offline, in photos or notes app):**
- Photo of passport (front and back)
- Photo of travel insurance policy document and policy number
- Travel insurer's 24/7 emergency number
- Your home country's embassy address and phone in each destination country
- Your bank's international fraud line (each card)
- Your flight booking references and airline contact numbers
- Accommodation booking references and addresses

**Physical backup (in a different bag from your main wallet):**
- One bank card (keep one card physically separate from the rest)
- €100–200 cash (different from your main wallet)
- Printed copy of your travel insurance with emergency number

**Apps to install before departure:**
- Your insurer's app (policy accessible offline)
- Your bank app (instant card blocking)
- Revolut or Wise (instant freeze, digital payments from any device)
- Google Maps with offline maps downloaded for your destination
- Find My (iPhone) or Google Find My Device activated
