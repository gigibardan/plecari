Madrid is not the city you visit for any single monument. It doesn't have an Eiffel Tower, a Colosseum, or a Sagrada Familia dominating everything. Madrid is the city you visit for how it makes you feel — alive, restless in the good sense, and with the feeling that every side street could contain something extraordinary. Most of the time, it does.

It's the capital of a kingdom that ruled half the world. It has three of the most important art museums on the planet, within 10 minutes' walk of each other. It has a food culture that turns a simple plate of jamón into a ritual. And it has a daily schedule that would shock any northern European: lunch at 14:30, dinner at 22:00, going out at midnight, home at 04:00. Madrid doesn't rush. Madrid is lived.

## The Art Triangle — Three Museums, One Kilometre

If you were to do a single thing in Madrid, it would be the Paseo del Prado — a tree-lined boulevard where, within walking distance, you'll find three of the world's most important museums.

**Museo del Prado:** The world's most important collection of European classical art (14th–19th century). This isn't exaggeration — the Prado rivals the Louvre and Uffizi, but with a concentration the others lack. Here you'll find Velázquez's Las Meninas (considered by many the greatest painting in art history), Hieronymus Bosch's Garden of Earthly Delights (a 1500 triptych that looks like a medieval psychedelic vision), Goya's Black Paintings series (painted directly on his house walls in the isolation of his late madness), and works by El Greco, Rubens, Titian, and Rembrandt. Entry: €15. Free Monday–Saturday 18:00–20:00 and Sunday 17:00–19:00 — crowded, but worth it on a tight budget. Allow minimum 3 hours. The audio guide (€6) is excellent.

**Museo Reina Sofía:** Modern and contemporary art. The centrepiece: Picasso's Guernica — a 3.5 × 7.8-metre painting depicting the Nazi bombing of the Basque town of Guernica in 1937. It's one of the most powerful anti-war works ever created, and standing before it is overwhelming. The rest of the collection: Dalí (including The Great Masturbator), Miró, Juan Gris, Tàpies. Entry: €12. **Free** Mondays and Wednesday–Saturday 19:00–21:00, Sunday 12:30–14:30.

**Museo Thyssen-Bornemisza:** A private collection turned public that fills the gaps left by the other two — Impressionism, Expressionism, Pop Art, American art. A Caravaggio next to a Hopper next to a Rothko. Entry: €13. Free Mondays 12:00–16:00.

**Paseo del Arte pass:** €32 for all three — saving ~30%. Available at any of the three ticket offices.

**Practical tip:** Don't attempt all three on the same day. Prado in the morning (opens at 10:00, arrive 09:45), Reina Sofía the next afternoon (free from 19:00), Thyssen on day three.

## Neighbourhoods — Where You Stay Defines What You Experience

Madrid is a city of neighbourhoods, each with a distinct personality. Where you choose to stay isn't just a question of price — it's a question of experience.

**Malasaña:** The neighbourhood that defined Spanish counterculture in the '80s (La Movida Madrileña — Almodóvar, Alaska, Mecano). Today it's the city's bohemian epicentre: speciality coffee shops (Toma Café, HanSo Café), vintage stores on Calle Velarde, cocktail bars on Calle Espíritu Santo, and a palpable creative energy. Plaza del Dos de Mayo — the neighbourhood's centre — is where locals drink beer on benches with friends at sunset. Accommodation: €80–150/night 3-star hotel. Metro: Tribunal, Noviciado.

**La Latina:** Madrid's oldest medieval quarter, with narrow streets descending toward the Manzanares river. Cava Baja — the street with the most tapas taverns per square metre in Madrid. Sunday morning, El Rastro — the flea market stretching across dozens of La Latina streets that draws half of Madrid. Accommodation: €70–130/night. Metro: La Latina.

**Lavapiés:** The most multicultural quarter — Indian, Chinese, African, Arab, everything. Extraordinary street food (Madrid's best curry is here, not in a fancy restaurant), underground art galleries, small theatres. Rough around the edges but authentic and vibrant. Accommodation: €60–110/night. Metro: Lavapiés.

**Chueca:** Madrid's LGBTQ+ quarter, with excellent restaurants, terraces, and a contagious positive energy. Mercado de San Antón — a market with a rooftop tasting floor — is a better alternative to San Miguel. Accommodation: €90–170/night. Metro: Chueca.

**Sol / Gran Vía:** Ultra-central, noisy, touristy. Good as a reference point, not as a base. Gran Vía is "Madrid's Broadway" — musicals, art deco cinemas from the 1920s, fast-fashion stores. Sol is kilometre 0 of Spain. Both areas buzz at night but are full of international chains.

**Salamanca:** Elegant, expensive, orderly. Serrano and Velázquez boulevards are lined with luxury brands. For those who want quiet and quality shopping. Accommodation: €150–300/night. Metro: Serrano, Velázquez.

## Food — The Central Ritual of Madrid Life

Madrid doesn't have a local cuisine the way Barcelona or Seville do. Madrid has everything — it's the capital, and the cuisines of all Spanish regions converge here. But it has a few dishes of its own and a way of eating that is a social ritual.

**Jamón ibérico:** Spanish cured ham — de bellota (from acorn-fed pigs, aged 36–48 months) is considered the finest cured meat in the world. A plate of jamón ibérico de bellota at a good bar: €18–25. A plate of jamón serrano (the more accessible version): €8–12. Do not skip this experience.

**Cocido madrileño:** A chickpea stew with beef, pork, chorizo sausage, morcillo (bone marrow), and vegetables, traditionally served in three courses (sopa, legumbres, carnes). It's Madrid's winter dish — heavy, satisfying, glorious. The best: La Bola (since 1870, cocido cooked in clay pots, €23), Malacatín (since 1895, La Latina, €21).

**Bocadillo de calamares:** A sandwich of fried calamari rings in white bread — simple, cheap (€3–4), and surprisingly good. Madrid is the landlocked city that worships squid. The best: Bar La Campana (Plaza Mayor), El Brillante (near Atocha station).

**Churros con chocolate:** They're not doughnuts. They're thin fried dough sticks served with thick hot chocolate you dip them into. San Ginés (open since 1894, near Sol) is the institution — open 24/7, the queue at 03:00 after a night out is part of the Madrid experience.

**Tortilla de patatas:** The Spanish omelette with potatoes and onion. Simple, perfect. Every bar has one. The best ones are in nameless bars on side streets.

**Where to eat — selectively:**

**Mercado de San Miguel** (near Plaza Mayor): Beautiful but touristy and expensive. Go for a beer and one tapa, not a full meal.

**Mercado de San Fernando** (Malasaña): The locals' market. Diverse food stalls, real prices, no selfie sticks.

**Casa Lucio** (Cava Baja 35): Famous for huevos rotos (broken eggs on jamón and fried potatoes). Kings, presidents, and bullfighters have eaten here. Reserve 2–3 days ahead. €20–35/person.

**Lateral** (multiple locations): Modern tapas, reasonable prices (€4–8/tapa), relaxed atmosphere. Good for a first contact with tapas.

**StreetXO** (Serrano 52): If you want something experimental — Asian-Spanish fusion from David Muñoz (3 Michelin stars at DiverXO). Gourmet street food at €8–15/dish.

## Flamenco — Raw Emotion

Flamenco isn't Spanish — it's Andalusian. But Madrid is the city that adopted it and transformed it into a performing art without losing its soul. A good flamenco show hits you physically — the footwork (zapateado), the guitar oscillating between tenderness and fury, and the voice (cante jondo) expressing a pain you don't need to understand verbally to feel.

**Casa Patas** (Cañizares 10): Madrid's most respected tablao. Small, intimate room, artists 2 metres from you. €25–40 show + drink. If you see one flamenco show in your life, make it Casa Patas.

**Corral de la Morería** (Morería 17): The world's oldest tablao, open since 1956. First-class artists. €45–55 with drink, €80–120 with dinner. More formal, more "show," but impeccable quality.

**Cardamomo** (Echegaray 15): Shows at 21:00 and 22:30, pure energy. €35–45. Popular, book online.

**Avoid:** The tablaos on Gran Vía promising "authentic flamenco dinner show" at €70+ — these are diluted shows for tourist coaches.

## Retiro and Green Spaces

**Parque del Retiro:** 125 hectares of green in Madrid's centre. Former royal hunting grounds, opened to the public in 1868. The central lake with rowing boats (€6/45 min) is the Sunday classic. Palacio de Cristal — an 1887 glass-and-iron conservatory now used as an exhibition space by Reina Sofía — is one of Madrid's most beautiful spaces, and entry is free. The Rosaleda (rose garden) blooms May–June.

**Templo de Debod:** An authentic 2nd-century BC Egyptian temple, donated to Spain by Egypt in 1968 for helping save the Aswan temples. Set on a hill in the Cuartel de la Montaña park, the sunset panorama toward Casa de Campo and the Sierra de Guadarrama is one of Madrid's finest. Free.

**Madrid Río:** The linear park along the Manzanares river, built over the buried M30 motorway. 10 km of paths, modern bridges, sports facilities, and beach bars in summer. Ideal for jogging or cycling.

## Day Trips

**Toledo** (30 min by AVE, €13–25 return): The "City of Three Cultures" — Christian, Jewish, Muslim — on a hill above the Tagus river. The Gothic cathedral (Spain's second largest), Santa María la Blanca and El Tránsito synagogues, Cristo de la Luz mosque (from 999 AD), and El Greco's masterwork (The Burial of the Count of Orgaz at Santo Tomé church). Compact, can be visited in 6–8 hours. Best approach: arrive on the first AVE (08:20), return on the last (22:20).

**Segovia** (90 min by La Sepulvedana bus, €8–12 return): The 1st-century Roman aqueduct (163 arches, 28 m tall, built without cement — still standing after 2,000 years), the Alcázar (the castle that inspired Disney's Cinderella), and cochinillo asado (roast suckling pig, the city's speciality). Mesón de Cándido restaurant (since 1786) carves the cochinillo with a plate in front of you — traditional, theatrical, delicious.

**El Escorial** (60 min by bus 661/664, €4–5): King Philip II's 16th-century monastery-palace — a granite colossus combining palace, monastery, library, and royal pantheon. Every Spanish monarch from Charles V onward is buried here. Entry: €12.

## Getting Around

**Metro:** 13 lines, excellent coverage, clean, safe. Single ticket: €1.50–2.00 (depending on zones). Tourist pass: €8.40/day, €14.20/2 days, €18.40/3 days (includes unlimited metro + bus). Available at machines in every station. Schedule: 06:00–01:30.

**Bus:** Supplements the metro. Night lines (búhos) run 00:00–06:00 — essential for nightlife.

**Taxi:** White with a red stripe. Base fare: €2.50 + €1.10/km. Centre to airport: fixed rate €33. Uber and Cabify both operate legally.

**From the airport:** Metro Line 8 (Nuevos Ministerios, 30 min, €4.50–5 with airport supplement). Exprés Aeropuerto bus (Atocha/Cibeles, 40 min, €5, 24/7). Fixed-rate taxi: €33.

## Practical Tips

**The Spanish schedule is real.** Restaurants serve lunch 13:30–16:00 and dinner 21:00–23:30. If you walk into a restaurant at 19:00, it's either empty or serving only tourists. Adapt: breakfast 09:00–10:00, tapas at 13:00, lunch at 14:30, tapas at 20:00, dinner at 21:30. It's more pleasant than it sounds.

**Menú del día:** Spain's best budget secret. At lunchtime, nearly every restaurant offers a fixed menu with 3 courses + bread + drink for €12–16. Excellent quality, generous portions. Available lunch only, Monday–Friday.

**Madrid nightlife is serious.** Beer on a terrace: 20:00. Pre-drinks (cañas y tapas): 22:00. Cocktails: 00:00. Clubs: 01:30–02:00. Leave the club: 05:00–06:00. Churros at San Ginés: 06:30. It's a marathon, not a sprint. You don't have to do the full programme — but if you want to, Madrid will support you.

**Pickpockets:** Active on Line 1 (Gran Vía–Sol) and Line 3 (Sol–Moncloa) at rush hour, in Sol, and around the Prado. Backpack in front, phone in an inner pocket. Never place your phone on a terrace table — passing scooters will grab it as they go.

**Tap water** is excellent in Madrid — it comes from the Sierra de Guadarrama and is superb. Don't buy bottled water.

**Sunday: El Rastro + La Latina.** The best Sunday programme: the El Rastro flea market (09:00–15:00, hundreds of stalls across La Latina's streets), then tapas on Cava Baja. All of Madrid is there.
