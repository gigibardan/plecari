## Why Rome hits different

Rome is the only city where you can stand inside a 2,000-year-old arena that held 50,000 spectators, eat the best pasta of your life for €12, toss a coin into a Baroque fountain, and finish the day with gelato on a piazza that hasn't changed since the Renaissance — all in one afternoon. No other city layers history, food, and atmosphere quite like this.

## Colosseum, Roman Forum & Palatine Hill

The Colosseum is Rome's number one attraction and the queue reflects it: 2-4 hours without advance tickets. **Do not show up without a ticket.** [Book skip-the-line on GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/rome-l33/colosseum-roman-forum-palatine-hill-priority-entrance-t740387/?partner_id=EKAA80H) (€18-35, includes Forum and Palatine, free cancellation) at least 2 weeks ahead.

**Practical info:** combined ticket covers Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine Hill. Start at the Forum (less crowded entrance on Via dei Fori Imperiali), cross to the Palatine (panoramic views), then walk down to the Colosseum. Total time: 3-4 hours.

**Tip:** the underground level and third tier (top ring) are available on special tickets for €9 extra — worth it for the view and the hypogeum where gladiators waited.

## Vatican Museums & Sistine Chapel

The Vatican collection is so vast you could spend days. Most visitors have 2-3 hours. The Sistine Chapel is at the END of the museum route — don't rush the galleries just to get there, but don't linger so long that you're exhausted when you arrive.

**[Book Vatican tickets](https://www.getyourguide.com/rome-l33/skip-the-line-vatican-museums-sistine-chapel-t62214/?partner_id=EKAA80H) weeks ahead** — the official site often sells out, and the queue without tickets wraps around St. Peter's Square (3+ hours). GetYourGuide guided tours include skip-the-line access and cost €25-45.

**St. Peter's Basilica** is free to enter (separate from the museum) but has its own security line (30-60 min). The dome climb (€8-10, 551 steps) is the best panoramic view in Rome.

**Tip:** Wednesday morning the Pope holds a general audience in St. Peter's Square — the Vatican Museums are quieter than usual.

## The historic centre — free and priceless

### Pantheon

The best-preserved Roman building in the world (126 AD). The unreinforced concrete dome is STILL the largest in the world. Free entry (timed reservation required since 2023, book on the official site or [GetYourGuide](https://www.getyourguide.com/rome-l33/?partner_id=EKAA80H)). Arrive at 8:30 AM for the light beam through the oculus.

### Trevi Fountain

Toss a coin over your left shoulder (tradition says you'll return to Rome). Visit at 7:00 AM for photos without 500 people in the frame. After 10:00 AM, it's a mosh pit.

### Piazza Navona

Bernini's Fountain of the Four Rivers. Best experienced in the evening — the piazza fills with street performers, artists, and the light on the Baroque facades is magical. Skip the overpriced restaurants ON the piazza — walk one block to eat.

### Spanish Steps

Iconic but... you can't sit on them anymore (€250 fine since 2019). Walk up for the view, then continue to Villa Borghese park.

## Where to eat (the real guide)

### Trastevere — Rome's food neighbourhood

Cross the Tiber and get lost in Trastevere's cobblestone alleys. This is where Romans eat. **Da Enzo al 29** (queue expected, no reservations — the cacio e pepe is legendary, €10-12). **Tonnarello** (carbonara, amatriciana, huge portions, €10-14). **Suppli** (fried rice balls, €2-3 each — the best street food in Rome).

### Pizza al taglio (by the slice)

Rome's fast food is pizza sold by weight. **Bonci Pizzarium** (near Vatican, the most famous — €4-6 for a generous serving). **Antico Forno Roscioli** (Campo de' Fiori area, traditional Roman bakery). Point at what looks good, they cut and weigh it.

### Coffee rules

Coffee at the bar (standing): €1-1.50. Coffee sitting at a table: €3-5 (the table surcharge is legal and traditional). Cappuccino is a morning drink only — ordering one after 11:00 AM marks you as a tourist.

### Gelato

Look for "produzione propria" (made on site). If the colours are neon, it's industrial. Good gelato costs €3-4 for two scoops. **Fatamorgana** (multiple locations, creative flavours), **Giolitti** (historic, since 1890), **Come il Latte** (near Termini).

## Getting around

**Metro:** 2 lines (A and B), covers major sites. Single ticket: €1.50. 24h pass: €7. Buy at machines in stations (cash or card).

**Walking:** the historic centre is compact — Colosseum to Trevi is 20 minutes on foot. Walking is the best way to discover Rome.

**Bolt/Uber:** works in Rome, cheaper and safer than street taxis. Avoid unlicensed taxi drivers at Termini and the airports. [Why Bolt/Uber beats taxis](/en/asigurari/travel-scams-europe-2026).

**Airport transfer:** Fiumicino to Termini: Leonardo Express train (€14, 32 min). Ciampino to Termini: bus (€6-8, 40 min).

## Don't miss (and don't bother)

**Don't miss:** sunset from Pincio Terrace (free, above Piazza del Popolo), aperitivo in Monti neighbourhood (drink + buffet €8-12), Sunday morning at Porta Portese flea market (free entry), Aventine Hill keyhole (free, 5-minute queue, peek at St. Peter's dome perfectly framed).

**Don't bother:** Mouth of Truth (30-min queue for a 5-second photo with a drain cover), restaurant meals on Piazza Navona or Campo de' Fiori (tourist trap pricing), "gladiator" photo ops outside the Colosseum (they demand €5-20 after the photo).

**Pickpockets:** active around Termini station, on the metro, and at tourist hotspots. Phone and wallet in front pockets. [Full scam guide](/en/asigurari/travel-scams-europe-2026).

**Water:** Rome has 2,500+ public drinking fountains (nasoni). The water is excellent — carry a reusable bottle and refill for free.
