There is a phrase that Warsaw tour guides tell tourists at the start of every tour: "Practically everything you see around you was built after 1945."

Warsaw was 85% destroyed in World War II — first in the bombardments of 1939, then in the 1944 Uprising and the Nazi reprisals that followed, then systematically, building by building, by German soldiers who received orders to erase the city from the map. When the Red Army liberated Warsaw in January 1945, it found a field of ruins.

<cite index="172-1">Warsaw was known as "the Pearl of the North" — a magnificent city with historic tenements, an advanced sewerage system, rapid growth, style and refinement. This magnificent city was demolished down to the last wall and the last brick.</cite>

What happened next is, in itself, a remarkable act of historical resilience: Poles rebuilt Warsaw from memory, using paintings, photographs, drawings and old plans. Today's Old Town is a faithful reconstruction — so well executed that UNESCO inscribed it on the World Heritage List in 1980, as an exceptional example of historical reconstruction.

Warsaw is not a city to contemplate — it is a city to understand.

## Warsaw at a Glance — Essential Facts

**Country:** Poland — EU member since 2004, Schengen Area. **Does not use the euro.**

**Population:** approximately 1.86 million — Poland's largest city.

**Currency:** Polish Złoty (PLN). 1 EUR ≈ 4.33 PLN in 2026.

**Language:** Polish. English is well spoken in the tourist area.

**UNESCO:** Old Town — World Heritage Site since 1980 (as an example of historical reconstruction).

**Direct flights from Bucharest:** Wizz Air and Ryanair, OTP→WAW or OTP→WMI, ~2h, from 35 EUR.

**Average budget:** <cite index="169-1">50–70 EUR/day (mid-range) — similar to Krakow.</cite>

## How to Get from Romania to Warsaw

### Direct Flight (Recommended)

Wizz Air operates flights OTP → **WAW** (Chopin Airport, 10 km from centre). Ryanair often flies to **WMI** (Modlin, 40 km north — ModlinBus costs 30–35 PLN).

**2026 prices:** 35–100 EUR return.

**Chopin Airport → city centre transfer:**
- **SKM S2/S3 train** → Central Station: 20 minutes, 4.40 PLN (1 EUR) — <cite index="173-1">the cheapest and fastest option</cite>
- **Bolt/Uber**: 30–45 PLN (7–10 EUR), 20–25 minutes

## What to See in Warsaw

### The Old Town (Stare Miasto) — The Miraculous Reconstruction

<cite index="180-1">Walk through the Old Town and pay attention to the plaques on buildings — many show what the same building's ruins looked like in 1945.</cite> It is not a typical medieval neighbourhood — it is a testament to a people's will to reclaim their identity.

**Old Town Market Square (Rynek Starego Miasta)** — the central square of the Old Town, with coloured houses rebuilt identically to the originals. The Warsaw Mermaid (Syrenka) — the bronze statue of the mermaid, the city's symbol, stands at the centre.

**Royal Castle (Zamek Królewski)** — former residence of Polish kings, deliberately destroyed by the Nazis in 1944 and rebuilt in 1971–1984 from donations by Poles worldwide. <cite index="162-1">Ticket: 60 PLN (~14 EUR). Free on Wednesdays.</cite> Online booking recommended.

**Barbican and Medieval Walls** — the 15th-century defensive structure, one of the few that survives partially original (later reconstructed).

**St John's Cathedral** — the Gothic cathedral from the 14th century, where Polish kings were crowned. Free entry.

### Warsaw Uprising Museum — Poland's Most Important Museum

<cite index="177-1">The Warsaw Uprising of 1944 was the largest urban revolt of resistance forces against Nazi occupation — 63 days of fighting in which 200,000 Polish civilians and fighters lost their lives.</cite>

The Warsaw Uprising Museum is not a conventional museum. It is a total experience — reconstructions of the sewage networks the fighters moved through, video testimonies from survivors, original objects and an emotional architecture designed by the architect who won the international design competition.

<cite index="175-1">"You can crawl through a replica sewer. It is a heavy, emotional museum and the best-curated history museum I have ever visited."</cite>

**Practical details:** ticket ~34 PLN (7.85 EUR). <cite index="169-1">Free on Mondays.</cite> Hours: daily 8:00–18:00, Fridays until 20:00. <cite index="180-1">Book a timed-entry ticket through GetYourGuide or the museum site — at midday in July the queue can last 45 minutes.</cite>

### POLIN Museum — Europe's Best Museum 2016

POLIN (Muzeum Historii Żydów Polskich) documents 1,000 years of Jewish life in Poland — from the first medieval communities to the Holocaust and the present, across 8 interactive multimedia galleries.

<cite index="175-1">"Interactive, moving and architecturally stunning. Plan 3 hours."</cite>

The building itself is an architectural monument — a glass wave structure designed by architect Rainer Mahlamäki, winner of the European Museum of the Year 2016.

**Practical details:** ticket ~35 PLN (8 EUR). <cite index="169-1">Free on Thursdays.</cite> Location: in the former Jewish Ghetto neighbourhood, next to the Ghetto Heroes Monument.

### Palace of Culture and Science — "Stalin's Gift"

<cite index="172-1">Locals hate the Palace of Culture and Science but climb it anyway.</cite> It is Warsaw's most controversial building — a 237-metre skyscraper built by the USSR as a "gift" to the Polish people in 1952–1955, in monumental Stalinist style.

The panoramic terrace on the 30th floor offers the best view of Warsaw. <cite index="172-1">Entry: ~28–30 PLN (6.50–7 EUR).</cite>

### Łazienki Park and Chopin Concerts

<cite index="172-1">Łazienki Park is Warsaw's largest green space — a former royal estate with palaces, lakes and free-roaming peacocks.</cite>

**Chopin Concerts** — <cite index="173-1">every Sunday at 12:00 and 16:00, May–September, live Chopin music concerts are held next to the Chopin Monument. Entry is free.</cite> One of Europe's most valuable free cultural experiences.

**Palace on the Island (Pałac na Wyspie)** — the neo-Baroque palace on an artificial island. Entry: ~55–76 PLN (13–17.50 EUR). Free on Fridays.

### Praga — Former Rough District, Now Hipster

Praga is the neighbourhood on the right bank of the Vistula — <cite index="170-1">the only Warsaw district that survived World War II relatively intact, preserving pre-war architecture that contrasts sharply with the left-bank reconstructions.</cite>

**Neon Museum (Neon Muzeum)** — a Cold War treasure trove: <cite index="175-1">a collection of communist-era neon signs, eerie and beautiful in equal measure.</cite> Entry: ~25 PLN (5.75 EUR).

**Ząbkowska Street** — the epicentre of Praga's nightlife, with bars in the courtyards of communist-era blocks.

## Food and Drink

### What You Must Eat

**Pierogi** — obligatory, as in Krakow. In Warsaw, best found in Kazimierzówka or Praga. 3–5 EUR a portion.

**Żurek** — sour rye soup with boiled egg and sausage. 2–5 EUR at a Bar Mleczny, 5–8 EUR at a restaurant.

**Kotlet schabowy** — pork schnitzel with sauerkraut. 8–14 EUR.

### Bar Mleczny — Ultra-Cheap and Authentic

<cite index="169-1">Bar Prasowy and Bar Bambino are Warsaw's best Milk Bars for an authentic meal at 2–5 EUR.</cite>

### Where Else to Eat

**Hala Koszyki** — <cite index="175-1">a restored market hall with dozens of restaurants and food stalls under one roof — the modern version of a food market, perfect for a varied lunch.</cite>

**Powiśle** — the neighbourhood on the Vistula riverbank, with bars and restaurants more authentic than the Old Town. Beer on the Vistula boulevards at sunset: under 10 PLN.

**Praga** — <cite index="174-1">the bars on Ząbkowska and in the inner courtyards are where locals go in the evening — no tourist markup.</cite>

### Polish Vodka and Beer

**Polish vodka** — a shot at a bar: 5–8 PLN (1.15–1.85 EUR). Avoid the "shot bars" in the Old Town aimed at stag parties — <cite index="174-1">the real nightlife is in Powiśle and Praga.</cite>

**Polish beer** — Żywiec, Tyskie. At a bar: 10–18 PLN (2.30–4.15 EUR).

## Recommended Itinerary — 3 Days in Warsaw

### Day 1 — Old Town and Warsaw Uprising Museum

- **Morning:** Old Town Market Square, Royal Castle (or free visit if Wednesday), Barbican
- **Lunch:** Bar Mleczny (3–5 EUR)
- **Afternoon:** Warsaw Uprising Museum (2–3 hours, ~8 EUR or free on Mondays)
- **Evening:** walk along the Vistula boulevards, beer at sunset

### Day 2 — POLIN, Ghetto and Palace of Culture

- **Morning:** Ghetto Heroes Monument → POLIN Museum (~8 EUR or free Thursday, 3 hours)
- **Lunch:** Hala Koszyki
- **Afternoon:** Palace of Culture — panoramic terrace (~7 EUR), Nowy Świat street
- **Evening:** Powiśle — bars by the Vistula

### Day 3 — Łazienki Park and Praga

- **Morning:** Łazienki Park — Palace on the Island, free peacocks
- **Sunday (May–September):** Chopin Concert at 12:00 (free!)
- **Lunch:** restaurant in Praga
- **Afternoon:** Neon Museum, Ząbkowska Street, art galleries
- **Departure:** airport

## Warsaw vs. Krakow — Which Is Right for You?

| Criterion | Warsaw | Krakow |
|---|---|---|
| **Medieval beauty** | Reconstructed (post-1945) | Authentic (unaffected by WWII) |
| **Museums** | Superior (Uprising, POLIN) | Good (Schindler, Wawel Castle) |
| **Cultural life** | More dynamic, more cosmopolitan | More touristy, more romantic |
| **Budget** | Similar | Similar (~5% cheaper) |
| **Auschwitz access** | 3 hours by train | 1.5 hours by coach (more practical) |
| **Classic excursion** | Żelazowa Wola (Chopin birthplace) | Wieliczka (Salt Mine) |
| **Best for** | Historical depth + museums | Medieval aesthetics + Auschwitz |

**The ideal combination:** Warsaw (2–3 days) + Krakow (2–3 days) in the same extended city break, by Intercity fast train (2.5 hours, ~30 EUR).
